## File an Online Police Report

The Online Reporting System lets you submit a report immediately and print a copy for free. Online filing is for you if all of these are true: this is not an emergency; there is no evidence to be collected; the incident occurred within the Orange city limits; there are no known suspects; it did not occur on a state freeway; and you have a valid email address. If this is an emergency, call 9-1-1.

Report types available online: Custody/Visitation Court Order Violation; Harassing Phone Call; Information Scam; Lost Property; Non-Injury Hit & Run Traffic Accident; Non-Injury Single Vehicle Property Damage Only Traffic Accident; Non-Injury Traffic Accident; Supplemental Report; Theft; Vandalism; Vehicle Burglary/Theft From Vehicle. The system is not available for Injury Traffic Accidents or Hit and Run Traffic Accidents with complete suspect or suspect vehicle information. For questions about using the system, call (714) 744-7416.

After submitting, you are issued a temporary police report number. Once the report is reviewed and approved, you are issued a permanent police report case number. Reports not meeting the online approval criteria will not be approved and you will receive an email explaining the reason(s). If your incident is not listed, call (714) 744-7444 (non-emergency) to file a report. Freeway incidents go to the California Highway Patrol office nearest you. Crimes outside the City of Orange go to the law enforcement agency for that jurisdiction.

## Request a Copy of a Police Report

Copies are available from the Records Bureau counter, Monday through Friday between the hours of 8 a.m. and 6 p.m and Saturday from 8 a.m to 2 p.m. Reports will only be released to parties involved in the incident. Bring a valid form of I.D. and complete the Request for Release of Police Records Information form. Fees may apply. Requests must be reviewed for eligibility and may take up to 10 business days to process.

You may request a report if you are a victim of a crime; an authorized representative of the victim (with a signed waiver); the insurance carrier against whom a claim has been made or may be made; or a person who suffered bodily injury or property damage because of an incident, including a traffic collision. If you have been arrested and have not appeared in court, the Records Bureau will not release the report until the case is adjudicated. Reports involving a juvenile (under 18) require authorization of the Presiding Judge of the Juvenile Court. Parents may receive certain reports (e.g., traffic collision reports). Others must petition Juvenile Court directly; the Records Bureau has the form.

Records Bureau: 1107 N. Batavia St., Orange, CA 92866, (714) 744-7380.

## Vehicle Release

Vehicle releases may be obtained from the Records Bureau counter, Monday through Friday between the hours of 8 a.m. and 6 p.m. and Saturday from 8 a.m. to 2 p.m. The registered owner must be present with a valid government issued identification card. If the registered owner is unable to visit, a notarized authorization letter must be submitted designating a specific person who may have the vehicle released to them.

Requirements: the vehicle must have current registration; a valid government issued identification card (driver's license, ID card, passport); a "Moving Permit" issued by the Department of Motor Vehicles if the vehicle needs smog certification or for other applicable reasons; a valid licensed driver must be present; legal owners must present required repossession paperwork as stated in California Vehicle Code 14602.6(1)(E)(3).

## Alarm Registration and False Alarm Fees

The Orange Police Department has a Burglary Alarm Registration form, required to be on file for any alarm installation for your home or business. Complete one form for each alarm system and/or each location equipped with an alarm. An Alarm Appeal Petition form is available for appealing false alarm charges. The form is returned with your Customer Number and the Orange Municipal Codes.

False alarm fees: 1st call is free; 2nd call is free; 3rd call is $150; 4th call is $200; each additional call is $200.

## Hate Crimes

The Orange Police Department states that all reported cases of hate crimes will be fully investigated. California Penal Code 422.55 defines a hate crime as a criminal act committed, in whole or in part, because of actual or perceived disability, gender, nationality, race or ethnicity, religion, sexual orientation, or association with a person or group with one or more of these characteristics. Criminal acts include, but are not limited to, vandalism, physical violence against the person of another, criminal threats, and theft. California's hate crime laws are codified in Penal Code Sections 422.55, 422.6, 422.7, and 422.75.

## Paying or Disputing a Parking Citation

Pay by mailing the citation with payment to "City of Orange, Citation Processing Center, P.O. Box 10479, Newport Beach, CA 92658-0479." Only checks or money orders are accepted through the mail. You can also visit www.CitationProcessingCenter.com to pay with a credit card.

To contest, make a copy of the citation, write a letter explaining why you are contesting it, include supporting documents, and mail it to the same address. You can also submit a written appeal online at www.CitationProcessingCenter.com.

Lost citation: come to the Traffic Bureau counter, open Monday through Thursday from 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. Provide the license plate number of the vehicle and the date the citation was issued. The fee for a replacement citation is $6.00.

To register as a new permit holder with the Citation Processing Center, upload the required documents: a government issued ID and current utility bill, or for Chapman University students, the enrollment verification letter, government issued ID, and utility bill. ## Building Security Ordinance and Crime Prevention Through Environmental Design

Orange Municipal Code 15.52 (Building Security Ordinance, adopted in 1979) outlines specific security standards for all new commercial and residential construction in the city. The Crime Prevention Unit takes part in Staff Review, Plan Check and on-site inspections.

Crime Prevention staff are available for consultation at any stage of development and for assessments of existing properties. The four key principles of CPTED are Natural Surveillance, Access Control, Territorial Reinforcement and Maintenance. To schedule an appointment or ask questions, contact the Crime Prevention Bureau at (714) 744-7555.
