The Traffic Division (Public Works Department) handles Traffic Engineering, which designs, analyzes, and plans for traffic capacity and safe movement, and Traffic Operations, which builds and maintains traffic signals, streetlights, roadway striping, traffic control signs, and pavement markers. ## Visitor RV Street Parking Permit

Visitors who wish to park RVs on the street must obtain a permit from the Orange Police Department. The permit is good for seven consecutive days, but the RV must be parked in the street adjacent to the home being visited. It cannot be parked in front of someone else's home. Six permits are allowed per year.

Source: https://www.cityoforange.org/our-city/departments/public-works/traffic-division

## Streetlight Outages and Traffic Signals

The Traffic Engineering & Operations Division fixes and maintains the traffic signals and streetlights throughout the City of Orange. The Division conducts a streetlight check regularly to locate and repair any malfunctioning streetlights. To report a streetlight that is not working properly, use Orange 24/7.

## Residential Neighborhood Traffic Management Program (RNTMP)

The Residential Neighborhood Traffic Management Program (RNTMP) was established to help mitigate drivers using neighborhood streets at excessive speeds to avoid congested roads. The City Council adopted RNTMP by resolution in July of 2001.

## Traffic Counts and Traffic Flow Map

Traffic count data for the Traffic Flow Map is collected by mechanical traffic counters placed on the street for a 24-hour period during a typical weekday. The map represents a weekday average daily traffic volume; weekend and holiday counts are not included. The page links to the City of Orange Traffic Flow Map and the 2024 Traffic Count Book.

## Traffic Management Center (TMC)

Orange County's Traffic Management Center is the hub behind the City's Intelligent Transportation System (ITS), a camera and traffic data system that lets specialists monitor traffic and regulate its flow. TMC controls over 150 traffic signals to adjust timing and improve vehicular flow based on real-time traffic conditions.

## Trails and Bikeways

The City of Orange has a network of recreational and equestrian trails and street and off-street bikeways. Equestrian and multi-purpose trails are maintained by Homeowner Associations and volunteers. The Santiago Creek Bike Trail (SCBT) is a 6-mile paved trail running alongside Santiago Creek and links to Grijalva, Yorba, and Hart Parks. The page links to the Santiago Creek Trail Access Map, Santiago Creek Trail Rules, the City of Orange Bikeways Master Plan, and the City of Orange Recreational Trails Master Plan.
