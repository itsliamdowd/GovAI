## Neighborhood Traffic Calming

The City addresses concerns about traffic congestion, pedestrian and bicycle safety, speeds and cut-through traffic volumes with the "Five E's": Education, Encouragement, Enforcement, Engineering, and Evaluation. The page says the most successful solution combines measures from all five, and that the final solution takes time because measures are proposed and refined through trial and error. Engineering tools include signage, markings, signals, pathways and other traffic calming improvements. Low-cost examples are posting signs, modifying signal timing, and painting crosswalks or bike lanes. Enforcement techniques include traffic stings targeted at pedestrian safety and speed feedback trailers.

To report a safety concern about pedestrian, bicycle, or motorist activity, call the Traffic Engineering Division at (619) 691-5026 or email trafficsafety@chulavistaca.gov.

## Pedestrian & Bicycle Safety

The City has created suggested school route maps for pedestrians and bicyclists, mainly within a 1-mile radius of these Chula Vista middle schools: Bonita Vista, Castle Park, Chula Vista, Eastlake, Hilltop, and Rancho Del Rey.

The City is certified as a bronze-level "Bicycle-Friendly Community" by the American League of Cyclists. The Active Transportation Plan was updated in 2020 and is a blueprint for a network of bike lanes and routes. The "Bike Chula Vista" Bike CV Map & Guide, Map Only, and Guide Only are available as PDFs. Bicycle Safety and Pedestrian Safety brochures on existing traffic laws are available in English and Spanish (PDF).

## Crosswalks

Crosswalks are marked (white or yellow painted markings) or unmarked. Most of the City's crosswalks are unmarked. Marked crosswalks are found at intersections with significant pedestrian travel, including schools and signalized or stop-controlled intersections. By law, drivers must yield to pedestrians who have entered a marked or unmarked crosswalk.

Safety tips from the City include looking both ways, making sure all traffic stops before crossing, never crossing between parked cars, and using the push button signal and crossing with the "Walk" signal only.

## Traffic Signals — Synchronization, Left Turns, and Installation

Signals are synchronized to get the greatest number of vehicles through with the fewest stops. A side-street driver may therefore wait longer so the busier street keeps moving.

Protected left turn signals show a green arrow, then a yellow arrow, then a red arrow, which means left turns are not permitted. Protected/Permissive signals show a green arrow, then a yellow arrow. When the yellow arrow disappears, a regular green light appears and drivers may turn left when it is safe.

State and federal "warrants" must be met before a signal can be installed. The criteria include vehicular and pedestrian traffic flow, the need to interrupt the major flow for side-street vehicles and pedestrians, special conditions such as curves and hills, accident history, and proximity to schools. Not all intersections that meet the criteria are signalized, and installations are subject to fund availability. Traffic signal evaluations are performed once a year.

## Traffic Signs — Speed Limits, Stop Signs, and "Children at Play" Signs

The non-posted speed limit for all residential streets in California is 25 miles per hour and can be enforced. The California Vehicle Code restricts where lower limits can be posted. 15 and 20 mph signs are allowed along narrow streets and near schools, parks, and senior centers.

The City does not install "Slow - Children at Play" signs on public streets. The State of California and the Federal Highway Administration do not recognize them as traffic control devices.

Stop signs are installed only after a location has been studied and only when warranted under City guidelines.

Questions: Traffic Engineering Division, (619) 691-5026.

Source: https://www.chulavistaca.gov/departments/engineering/traffic-engineering/traffic-control-information

## Potholes and Street Maintenance

The Street Maintenance Section maintains asphalt and concrete structures (streets, sidewalks, curbs and gutters) and provides litter removal and weed/trash abatement. Crews handle reactive repairs such as pothole repair, minor street reconstruction, major street reconstruction (less than 550 square feet), curb line asphalt repairs, lateral cut resurfacing, and skin patching/capping. Larger proactive work, such as surface treatments and resurfacing/overlays, is done through Capital Improvement Projects.

Pothole Hotline: (619) 460-BUMP, or (619) 460-2867. Other Public Works issues: (619) 397-6000.

Source: https://www.chulavistaca.gov/departments/public-works/operations/street-maintenance

## Street Sweeping

SCA of CA, L.L.C. has been contracted, effective July 1, 2022, to sweep City streets, center islands/medians, centerlines and designated parking lots. The Residential schedule is Monday to Friday, 7 a.m. to 4 p.m. The Commercial schedule is Monday to Friday, 12 to 6 a.m. Special sweeping may be scheduled as directed by Public Works.

Frequencies by class:
- Class A, residential streets: once every two months
- Class B, business/commercial streets: once every two months
- Class C, commercial streets: once every two weeks
- Class D, center islands and medians: once every two months
- Class E, center lines: once every two months
- Class F, non-curbed streets: once every two months

To report street sweeping issues, call Public Works at (619) 397-6000. The page links to a lookup for your sweeping day/time and an interactive map.

## Traffic Signal and Street Light Repairs

Chula Vista has 300+ traffic signals and 10,000+ street lights. Technicians respond to traffic signal emergencies within one hour of notice and evaluate other signal malfunctions within 48 hours. Street light repairs are generally completed within three to five working days.

The City does not maintain street lights on private streets. Contact the street's homeowners association to report an outage there. Street lights in subdivisions still under construction are the developer's responsibility, so direct requests to the developer's representative and/or the homeowners association.

To report a malfunctioning traffic signal, street light, or illuminated street name sign outage, call (619) 397-6000, report via ACT Chula Vista, or submit a request online. Provide the street name and address (and whether the pole is in front of, across from, or between addresses), or the intersection street names for a signal. Also include any detail about the malfunction and your name and contact number. For an emergency outside normal business hours, call (619) 691-5151.
