## Building Inspections: Scheduling, Canceling and Results

Inspections are required for all permits to confirm construction is in accordance with approved plans and applicable codes and standards. Covered activities include, but are not limited to, new construction, demolition, remodels, fences, walls, patios, water heaters, water line repairs, electric meter reset, and photovoltaic permits. The page lists typical inspections for these permit types: New/Alteration Residential, Utility, Commercial/Tenant Improvement, and PV Solar. Include all applicable inspection types in your request.

Scheduling, canceling or obtaining results of inspections can be done online at the Citizen Access website, or by phone using the automated Permit Inspection Line at (619) 409-5434. If you need assistance, call (619) 409-5868. You need your permit number to make a request. Request the inspection as soon as possible, because inspections are limited each day. Requests received after the daily limit has been reached are made for the next available business day. The service goal has been next-day inspections, but the city is experiencing high volumes and is transitioning to a new permitting software system.

The city can't give a specific time of day for an inspection. For an unusual scheduling issue or other concern, contact your inspector the morning of your inspection at (619) 409-5868 between 7:30 and 8:30 a.m. The inspector may be able to give a time window or make other arrangements.

## Zoning Information and eZONING

The City of Chula Vista Zoning Code (CVMC Title 19) dictates what uses are allowed and how land can be developed (e.g. setbacks, height, parking) within the City's jurisdiction. Zones specify a category of uses, such as single-family residential, multifamily residential, commercial, and industrial. The City's zoning is shown on the City's official land use map.

For zoning questions, contact the Development Services Department at (619) 585-5621 or visit the DSD Counter at 276 Fourth Av, Building B. Chula Vista, CA 91910. For complete regulatory information about your property or use, call (619) 585-5621 or email planning@chulavistaca.gov.

## Environmental Review (CEQA) for Projects

Environmental review under CEQA is required for all proposed public and private activities requiring discretionary approval by the City, including City projects, unless determined to be exempt. Activities requiring federal approval or involving federal funding are subject to NEPA review, and both processes can be conducted jointly. The process is established by the Environmental Review Procedures of the City of Chula Vista, CEQA (1970), and NEPA (1969).

Process steps:
- **Pre-application review:** Applicants submit project information early. The Environmental Review Coordinator (ERC) determines whether the project is clearly exempt from CEQA or requires a Preliminary Environmental Review application.
- **Preliminary Environmental Review:** The application must be filed simultaneously with the discretionary permit application(s). It must include the technical reports, documents and other information the ERC needs, and requires a deposit account to cover city staff review time. Average processing time to conduct an initial study and complete a Negative Declaration or Mitigated Negative Declaration is approximately four to six months once the application is submitted.
- **Initial study outcomes:** The ERC determines that a Negative Declaration, a Mitigated Negative Declaration, or an Environmental Impact Report (EIR) is required.
- **EIR:** The applicant completes an EIR application and creates a deposit account with the city to reimburse all city consultants providing environmental analysis. The city sends a Notice of Preparation, and an environmental consultant selected by the ERC may prepare the EIR.
- **Public review of Draft EIR:** The minimum review period is 30 days and the maximum is generally 60 days. When the Draft EIR is sent to the State Clearinghouse, the review period must be at least 45 days, though the Clearinghouse may approve a shorter period (not less than 30 days). The Resource Conservation Commission (RCC) may consider the draft EIR and make a recommendation on its adequacy.
- **Final EIR:** The City must respond in writing to all comments received on the Draft EIR and certify the Final EIR before approving the project.

## Historic Preservation — Alterations, Designation and Mills Act

The City's Historic Preservation Program (HPP) includes a screening process to determine historical significance of potential historical resources that are 45 years or older, have not previously been surveyed or designated as historic, and are proposed for alteration or demolition. City staff use a Historical Resource Eligibility Determination Form for the evaluation.

A Certificate of Appropriateness (COA) is required for alterations to historical resources that require a building permit. In most circumstances, if the alteration conforms to the Secretary of the Interior's Standards for the Treatment of Historic Properties, a COA may be issued.

If the HPC or City Council designates a resource as historic, it is assigned a historic number and added to the Chula Vista Register of Historical Resources (Local Register). A historic plaque may be purchased by the property owner.

The City may enter into Mills Act Agreements with owners of designated historical resources. The owner agrees to preserve, restore or rehabilitate the resource in exchange for a reduction of property taxes.

## Researching Building Records and Requesting Copies

The Building Division of the Development Services Department maintains copies of building permits, inspection records, building plans and specifications for permits it issued, and these are available to the public upon request. Per Section 6253 of the California Public Records Act, a request must reasonably describe an identifiable record or records.

Building permits and inspection history for permits issued since 1992 are available online through Citizen Access. For permit information prior to 1992, contact the City by email or by phone at 619-409-5868. These records may have been digitally archived and can be researched for you by staff, but records are sometimes not on file.

Once a building permit has passed final inspection, the permit, inspection history and plans become public record. To request building permit research, provide for the property: Assessor's Parcel Number, Address, Development, Project, Type of documents requested (permit, inspection record, plans, etc.), and your Name, Address and Phone Number. Allow up to 10 working days to process the request.

Requests for copies of building plans must be accompanied by a completed Form 4553 Application for Duplication of Official Copies of Building Plans and a completed Form 4554 Affidavit. The division must request written permission to duplicate plans from the certified, licensed or registered professional and the current registered owner. The process takes 30 - 60 days, so "immediate" duplication of plans is not available except via court order or upon request of a state agency. Under California Health and Safety Code Section 19851 and federal copyright law, building plans can be inspected but not copied without permission of the professional who signed the document and the original or current property owner (and the copyright owner).

## New Business — Other Permits and Licenses Required

Depending on the nature of your business, you may need permits or licenses from other government agencies. Per CVMC 5.02.050, the license applicant has the sole and continuing responsibility of assuring that the business operations comply with applicable laws, including building and zoning ordinances. By approving and issuing a business license, the City is not certifying that the proposed business operation is compliant with applicable laws. Contact a Business License Representative to confirm requirements for your specific business.

Businesses regulated under NPDES Permit 2014-0057-DWQ must demonstrate enrollment in the State Industrial General Storm Water Permit in order to receive a City business license. All businesses with alarms must obtain a Security Alarm Permit: (877) 503-6727.

## Habitat Conservation (MSCP) — Contact

The City of Chula Vista MSCP Subarea Plan, approved on May 13th, 2003, implements the MSCP Subregional Plan within the City's jurisdiction and forms the basis for federal and state incidental "take" permits for 86 plant and animal species within the City. As of January 13, 2005, the City was issued a Section 10(a)(1)(b) Incidental "Take" Permit by the Wildlife Agencies (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and California Department of Fish and Game). This permit gives the City authority to allow a landowner or other public or private entity to impact sensitive species covered under the City's MSCP Subarea Plan.

For more information regarding the City's MSCP, contact the Development Services Department, Mayra Medel, Principal Planner, at (619) 409-5806.

## General Plan

The Chula Vista City Council approved an updated General Plan on December 13, 2005. It looks out to the year 2030 and is the first comprehensive update of the City's General Plan since 1989. It identifies how land will be used, how needs for transportation, parks and other public facilities and services will be met, and how natural resources and the community's heritage will be protected.
