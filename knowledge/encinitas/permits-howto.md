## Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) Building Permit — Steps

To get ADU building permit approval, start by consulting City staff in person, by email, or by phone. Also consult the water district for the property, which is either the San Dieguito Water District or the Olivenhain Municipal Water District.
- Land Development & Building (Planning): planning@encinitasca.gov, 760-633-2710
- Land Development Engineering: developmenteng@encinitasca.gov, 760-633-2609
- Building: building@encinitasca.gov, 760-633-2730
- Fire Prevention: fireprevention@encinitasca.gov, 760-633-2820

Permit materials include:
- Project plans (site plan, floor plans, elevations, roof plans, structural, electrical, plumbing, etc.)
- Grading plan (if applicable)
- Stormwater Intake Form
- Housing Development Tracking Form
- Current Grant Deed with a legal description
- Preliminary Title Report (6 mos. or less old)
- Geotechnical report (if addition or new construction is 500 sq. ft. or greater in size)

All permit applications are submitted through the Customer Self-Service Portal (CSS). Building technicians check for a complete submittal, then route the project for review. They email the water form, sewer form, and school fees form to the applicant of record. The applicant must get signatures, pay any required fees, and upload the final forms through CSS before the building permit is issued. Review time is approximately 15 working days.

The fees for plan review and inspections are waived, except for required State fees, postage fees (if a coastal development permit is required), and the recordation of a covenant with associated San Diego County Recorder fees. If the ADU is combined with a remodel or addition to the primary residence, submit under the primary scope (e.g., Residential Addition) with the ADU in the project description. Questions on permit type: building@encinitasca.gov.

## ADU Coastal Development Permit (Coastal Zone)

In the Coastal Zone, a coastal development permit (CDP) is required for ADUs and JADUs that:
- are not completely contained in the existing primary structure,
- include increases in habitable area,
- include conversion of non-habitable space, or
- are considered detached residential units.

The ADU/JADU application is submitted through CSS for a building permit, and the Land Development & Building reviewer creates a CDP record. The applicant uploads a plan set with only the title sheet, site plan, floor plans, elevations, and roof plan, plus the Grant Deed and Preliminary Title Report. The reviewer emails a sign, which must be printed on brightly colored 11-inch X 17-inch paper and posted in public view. The applicant uploads a completed, signed certificate of posting form with a pdf photo of the posted sign to the CDP record in CSS. A postage fee is collected before staff issue the legal notice.

After the Notice of Decision is signed, the applicant must return an original wet-signed, notarized indemnification agreement to the reviewer. A Notice of Final Action (NOFA) is sent to the California Coastal Commission. If the property is in the Commission's appeal jurisdiction, the Commission sets a 10-business day appeal period once it receives the NOFA. The building permit cannot be issued until the CDP has been issued and all other conditions or corrections are addressed.

## SB 9 Projects (Two-Unit Development and Urban Lot Split) — How to Apply

SB 9 requires cities to ministerially approve (no public hearing) up to two residences on an existing single-family residential zoned property and the split of an existing single-family lot into two parcels, if requirements are met. SB 9 projects are reviewed through a ministerial process. An Administrative Coastal Development Permit is required within the Coastal Zone. Only properties in the "Coastal Commission Appeal Zone" are appealable to the Coastal Commission.

Applicants submit a Planning Application with the required SB 9 Supplement and any other required supplements. Email planning@encinitasca.gov with questions and to schedule an in-take appointment once ready to submit. An Urban Lot Split Owner Occupancy Affidavit is also available as a resource. SB 9 applications and their status can be viewed on the City's SB 9 Project Dashboard.

Only properties zoned for single-family residential development (RR, RR-1, RR-2, R-3, R-5, R-8, and RS-11) that meet the location requirements of Government Code Section 65913.4(a)(6)(b)-(k) qualify. Use the City's e-zoning map to find zoning.

## SB 9 Covenant and Lot Split Requirements

SB 9 projects require execution and recordation of a covenant that:
- prohibits non-residential uses on the site;
- prohibits short-term rentals (less than 30 days stay);
- bars any parcel created from an urban lot split from being split again;
- requires ongoing compliance with all SB 9 requirements and restrictions;
- requires that access to the public right-of-way be maintained;
- requires that all required parking be maintained.

Urban lot split rules:
- Each parcel must be at least 1,200 square feet.
- New parcels must be approximately equal in size, with one parcel no smaller than 40% of the original lot's size.
- No more than two parcels may be created.
- Existing residence(s) must be located fully on a split parcel.
- The applicant must provide a signed affidavit of intent to occupy one of the housing units as their principal residence for a minimum of three years.

Two-unit development rules:
- Up to two primary dwelling units and two accessory dwelling units may be proposed on a lot.
- An existing residential unit occupied by a tenant within the past 3 years may not be demolished or altered.
- New units may be built as close as four feet from a side or rear property line.

## SB 35/SB 423 Streamlined Affordable Housing Approval — Process

SB 35/SB 423 allows eligible multifamily and mixed-use affordable housing projects to apply for streamlined, ministerial (no public hearing) approval. These projects are not permitted in certain areas of the Coastal Zone, including parcels zoned for single-family, within the Coastal Appeal Jurisdiction, areas vulnerable to sea level rise, within 100 feet of a wetland, estuary, or stream, or on prime agricultural land.

Steps:
1. Confirm eligibility using the checklist in the SB 35/SB 423 Supplemental Application.
2. Submit a Notice of Intent in the form of the SB 35/SB 423 Supplemental Application.
3. Tribal consultation: within 30 days the City gives formal notice to each Tribe, each Tribe has 30 days to accept, and the City initiates consultation within 30 days of acceptance.
4. Submit the Supplemental Application, the City of Encinitas Planning Application, the State Density Bonus Application Supplemental (if applicable), and Architectural Plans. These can be submitted to the Land Development Division.
5. Ministerial review: staff determine completeness and conflicts with objective standards within 60 days for projects of 150 or fewer units and 90 days for more than 150 units. Action on the application, including design review, must be completed in 90 days for 150 or fewer units and 180 days for more than 150 units. A noticed public hearing is scheduled at the Planning Commission for objective design review.
6. Submit a building permit before the three-year approval expiration. A one-time, one-year extension is possible if the applicant shows significant progress.

A denial may be appealed by the applicant to the City Council. CEQA review is not required.

## Cannabis Business Registration and Location Check (Measure H)

Registration and application materials for retail and non-retail cannabis businesses are on the City's Cannabis (Measure H) page. Retail materials include Procedures and Guidelines, Flowchart, Annual Renewal Form, and Change of Ownership Form. Non-retail materials include Procedures and Guidelines, Registration Form, Application Form, Financial Responsibility and Indemnification Agreement, and Geographic Barrier Form. The governing code is Encinitas Municipal Code Chapter 9.25 - Cannabis Activity.

The City's online mapping tool has separate maps for cannabis retail and for all other (non-retail) uses. The maps show allowable zoning districts, 1,000' buffers for day care centers, playgrounds, and schools, and 600' buffers for youth centers. Only sensitive uses in existence prior to October 20, 2025 were used for the buffers. The applicant is responsible for demonstrating that a proposed location meets all buffer requirements based on sensitive uses in existence at the time of application, which City staff will verify.

Retailers must maintain a 1,000-foot separation from sensitive uses, defined as a day care center, playground, other retailer, or school. Contact: Evan Jedynak, Senior Planner, (760) 633-2686, ejedynak@encinitasca.gov.
