# Housing & Rent Control

## Rent Control — Not in Effect in Newport Beach

Newport Beach does not have a rent control ordinance. The City's Housing Crisis Act page states: "Newport Beach does not have rent control ordinance and therefore this provision is not applicable."

## Housing Crisis Act (SB 330) — Protected Units, Relocation and Right of First Refusal

Protected units are units that are or were within the past 5 years either: (1) subject to a recorded covenant, ordinance, or law restricting rents to levels affordable to low- or very low-income households; (2) occupied by low- or very-low income households; or (3) subject to a local rent control ordinance, or withdrawn from the rental market under the Ellis Act within the last 10 years. Before the City issues a demolition permit for any number of units, the property owner must complete an income verification form to determine whether the units are "protected."

Existing residents must be allowed to remain until 6 months before construction begins. Displaced residents must be provided relocation benefits and a right of first refusal for a comparable unit in the new project at an affordable rent for a minimum term of 55 years. The law applies to all cities, including charter cities such as Newport Beach. Questions: Planning Counter 949-644-3204, or CDD@newportbeachca.gov.

## Housing Crisis Act — SB 8 and AB 1218 Updates

AB 1218 (Chapter 754, Statutes of 2023) expanded the replacement requirements for protected units to nonresidential developments. SB 8 extended the temporary prohibition on net loss of residential units for an additional 5-year period, concluding on January 1, 2030. For example, during this prohibition a residential duplex cannot be demolished and replaced with a single-unit dwelling, because that would be a net loss of one unit. Replacing the duplex with a new single-unit dwelling plus an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) or Junior Accessory Dwelling Unit (JADU) is permissible.

## Landlord/Tenant Dispute Mediation

Fair housing enforcement and landlord/tenant mediation services are available for tenants, realtors, apartment owners and managers, lending institutions and other interested parties within the City. Effective July 1, 2011, the City contracted with the Fair Housing Foundation for these services, which are provided free of cost. Each year the City allocates CDBG funds for this purpose as required by law.

## Fair Housing — Discrimination Protections

Federal and State laws prohibit discrimination in the sale, rental, leasing, negotiation, advertising, and financing of housing based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, handicap, familial status, marital status, age, disability, ancestry, sexual orientation, source of income or any other arbitrary reason. The laws also prohibit acts of hatred, violence and harassment. Each year, the City allocates CDBG funds for this purpose as required by law.

## Housing Choice Voucher Program (Section 8)

The City participates with the Orange County Housing Authority in the "Housing Choice Voucher Program." Participants can use the rental assistance in a variety of rental dwellings and locations. An individual or family is generally required to pay approximately 30% of adjusted monthly income toward rent and utilities. The Orange County Housing Authority pays the remaining portion of the rent to the property owner under an approved contract.

For program information, contact the Orange County Housing Authority at (714) 480-2700. Landlords can place a vacancy on the Orange County Housing Authority's rental list by calling (714) 480-2866. The page lists these owner benefits: low turnover, timely and dependable rental payments, direct deposit, and free annual property inspections.

## Affordable Rental Housing and Home Purchase Programs

The City has had policies in effect since the mid-1980s requiring affordable housing in association with all new residential developments. Most of these units were provided within new or existing apartment projects. Owners of such properties have entered into agreements with the City to keep rents affordable in a specified number of rental units within each property.

The City does not currently have any available programs to assist with the purchase of a home within the City. The page points to California Housing Finance Agency (Cal HFA) State and Federal programs, which make low-interest loans available with smaller down payments.

## Newport Senior Apartments (Bayview Landing)

Bayview Landing is at the corner of Jamboree Road and Back Bay Drive. It provides 120 affordable rental units for tenants aged 62 and older. Twenty-four units are offered to tenants whose annual income does not exceed 50% of Area Median Income, and 95 units to those whose annual income does not exceed 60% of Area Median Income. The unit mix is 96 one-bedroom units and 24 two-bedroom units (including the manager's unit).

## Inclusionary Housing

Inclusionary housing is a requirement placed upon developers to provide a minimum percentage of lower- and moderate-income dwelling units within new residential developments. The City's Regional Housing Needs Allocation (RHNA) of 4,845 new housing units breaks down as follows: 1,456 very low-income, 930 low-income, 1,050 moderate-income, and 1,409 above-moderate income. The City has engaged Keyser Marston Associates, Inc. to evaluate a Citywide Inclusionary Housing program, including a recommended minimum inclusionary percentage and a recommended in-lieu fee a developer could pay instead of building units on-site.

## Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs and JADUs)

ADUs and JADUs are additional independent living facilities, for one or more persons, located on the same parcel as a primary residence or residences. They are now permitted where residential uses are allowed, including single-family lots. Eligible projects are: a new detached ADU added to an existing residence; a new attached ADU/JADU added to an existing residence; conversion of existing residential floor area, including a garage, to an ADU/JADU; or legalization of an existing unpermitted ADU or JADU that meets all current requirements of the Municipal Code, including the California Building Code.

The City Council adopted Ordinance No. 2022-5 (Title 20, Planning and Zoning, ADU Regulations) and Ordinance No. 2022-6 (Title 21, LCP Implementation Plan, ADU Regulations) on March 8, 2022. A temporary fee waiver for ADUs applies, but not to a new ADU/JADU that is part of a new residential development project (i.e., a new home). The City's ADU resource website is newportbeachca.gov/adu.

## Housing Element and State Housing Targets

The City of Newport Beach Housing Element was adopted by the City Council on September 13, 2022, and certified by the California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) as compliant on October 5, 2022. The Housing Element is part of the General Plan and outlines how the City plans to meet its state-mandated Regional Housing Needs Allocation (RHNA). For the 6th Cycle (2021–2029), the RHNA target is 4,845 new units, of which 3,436 must be affordable for very-low-, low-, and moderate-income households.

## Homelessness — City Actions and Shelter Access

City staff, including police officers, work with the City's outreach and case management contractor PATH to connect homeless individuals with emergency housing, mental health treatment and health care, and benefits counseling. The City has two full-time positions dedicated to homelessness: a police officer and a homeless services manager. Newport Beach partners with the City of Costa Mesa for shelter beds at the Costa Mesa Bridge Shelter. The Bridge Shelter provides temporary housing and case workers who specialize in addiction and mental health counseling, job search, and accessing permanent housing.

The City has an anti-camping ordinance. It bans sleeping in tents and cars, blocking access to public rights-of-way, leaving personal items unattended, and more.

Source: https://www.newportbeachca.gov/trending/community-issues/homelessness/city-action

## Homelessness — When Residents and Businesses Should Call

The City asks residents and businesses to call the NBPD Non-Emergency Line at 949-644-3717 for any of these:
- Threats or threatening behavior.
- Setting up tents or other unpermitted temporary structures (shade structures with open sides are allowed in parks and beaches).
- Blocking access to businesses, private property or public restrooms.
- Leaving personal items unattended on public property.
- Sleeping in restrooms.
- Sleeping overnight in cars.
- Using public fountains, drinking faucets, restroom sinks or sprinklers for personal hygiene or to wash dishes or clothes.
- Trespassing on private property.

## Good Giving Program

The City discourages direct donations to homeless individuals. It asks the public to donate to the Good Giving fund, which helps people stay housed and employed. The page lists these organizations: Mercy House (housing and comprehensive supportive services); Serving People In Need (SPIN OC) (helps homeless families with children and the working poor); Chrysalis (pathways to self-sufficiency for homeless and low-income individuals); and Share Our Selves (SOS) (onsite outreach medical programs, food and clothing).

## Homeless Outreach and Crisis Response Services

City of Newport Beach staff count the number of people experiencing street homelessness once a month. PATH, a social services provider, provides street outreach services five days a week throughout Newport Beach and reports outreach contacts to the City each month. The Be Well mobile crisis response team operates in Newport Beach under contract 12 hours a day, 7 days a week to respond to mental and behavioral health crises. The mobile unit is staffed with mental health specialists and EMTs and works closely with the City's police and fire departments.
