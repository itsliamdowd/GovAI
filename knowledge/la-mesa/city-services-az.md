## Household Hazardous Waste & E-Waste Collection Events

Household hazardous waste (HHW) is any material (gas, liquid, or solid) from a home that may pose a health threat to people, animals, or the environment if handled or disposed of improperly. Examples include chemicals labeled "poison," "toxic," "flammable," "corrosive," or "reactive," electronic devices, batteries, medical sharps, paint, motor oil, and products containing mercury (including fluorescent bulbs). HHW should never be thrown into the trash or poured down the sink, sewer or storm drains, and improper disposal is illegal.

The City offers several free collection events each year for incorporated City of La Mesa and Lemon Grove residents. HHW can be dropped off by appointment only, with a limit of 15 gallons, 125 pounds, or five monitors and/or TVs. 2026 events take place from 9 a.m. to 12:45 p.m. on these Saturdays: Jan. 17, Feb. 14, March 28, May 2, June 6, July 25, Sept. 19, Oct. 17, and Dec. 5.

To make an appointment, call EDCO at (619) 287-5696 ext. 4270, book a time slot online on EDCO's HHW webpage, or email lmhhw@edcodisposal.com. Include your name, address, and telephone number. To cancel an appointment, email LMhhw@edcodisposal.com. Not all hazardous waste materials are accepted at City of La Mesa events.

## Lithium-Ion Batteries — Proper Disposal

Do not put lithium-ion batteries in curbside trash or recycling bins. Crushing or jostling can cause them to combust, and lithium-ion fires cannot be put out with water. In California, it is illegal to dispose of electronics and these batteries in household trash and recycling bins. All batteries and electronic waste must be disposed of at household hazardous waste facilities or events.

## ProduceGood Backyard Fruit Donation

ProduceGood is a food recovery organization that collects excess fruit from backyard trees at no cost and donates it to food pantries, shelters, and other organizations that feed food insecure people in San Diego. It is insured, provides tax receipts, and coordinates the details. Volunteers pick and deliver the fruit.

As of March 5, 2026, a Mexican Fruit Fly quarantine ordered by the California Department of Food and Agriculture (CDFA) is in effect in La Mesa and surrounding neighborhoods. Residents are urged not to move any fruits or vegetables from their property, and ProduceGood is not permitted to send volunteers to glean fruit at this time. Residents can still sign up to become growers, and those in the queue will be contacted when the quarantine is lifted. Backyard fruit is still safe to consume or process at the property of origin. Unused fruit should be double-bagged in plastic bags and put in the garbage bin.

## Mandatory Commercial Organics Recycling (SB 1383)

La Mesa Municipal Code Chapter 7.30 Mandatory Organics Recycling and Edible Food Recovery went into effect on April 21, 2022. It mandates enrollment in the City's three-container collection services (gray landfill bin, blue recycling bin, and green organic waste bin) for all residential and commercial (including multi-family dwellings) customers. These services are currently provided by EDCO. Specified commercial edible food generators must recover the maximum amount of surplus edible food and establish an agreement with a food recovery organization for donations.

EDCO is available for one-on-one onsite consultations to set up organics recycling service. Per SB 1383, starting January 1, 2022, all single-family residential dwellings (less than five units), commercial businesses, and multi-family residential dwellings (five or more units) are required to subscribe to recycling and organics recycling service.

## Commercial Self-Haul of Organic Waste

Commercial businesses and multi-family residential dwellings that self-haul must submit a Self-Haul Form to maintain compliance. Self-hauling is transporting your waste (organic waste and/or recyclable materials) off your property yourself instead of using EDCO collection services. It also includes business back-hauling, when a business transports these materials to a destination it owns and operates using its own employees and equipment. Per State law and La Mesa Municipal Code 7.30.090, customers who self-haul organic waste must ensure it is kept out of the landfill. City staff and EDCO review the forms.

## Shared Collection Service for Commercial and Multi-Family Properties

La Mesa Municipal Code 7.20.110 allows separate properties to share refuse collection services if all of the following are met:
- A property owner, property manager, or property owners' association subscribes on behalf of all properties sharing the service and pays the service charges.
- The subscribing party takes responsibility for all aspects of the service, including corrective actions if a shared container is determined to be a "contaminated container," as defined in 7.30.020.
- EDCO is informed of and approves which properties share which service(s).
- The properties are contiguous, and solid waste does not have to be moved on or across a public street from the point of generation to a collection container.
- Gray landfill, blue recycle, and green organic waste containers are reasonably convenient and accessible to all occupants and tenants sharing the service.

## De Minimis Waiver for Commercial Businesses

Commercial businesses may be eligible for a "de minimis" waiver, considered and approved case-by-case and monitored by the waste hauler and City staff. A business is eligible if its solid waste collection service meets one of these:
- Service is 2 cubic yards or more per week and the business generates less than 20 gallons per week of organic waste.
- Service is less than 2 cubic yards per week and the business generates less than 10 gallons per week of organic waste.

Solid waste collection service includes the total volume of landfill, recycling, and organic waste bins provided by EDCO for the property, plus the total volume of all non-containerized cardboard and yard materials collected.

To request a waiver, download, fill out, and submit the "de minimis" Waiver Form. Explain how it was identified that the property meets the selected condition, attach pictures to support the form, and state any existing alternate service (such as paper shredding and collection services, landscaping companies that haul away trimmings, and food donation).

## Statewide Plastic Bag Rules (SB 1053)

Effective January 1, 2026, SB 1053 prohibits stores defined under State law (including supermarkets, large retailers with pharmacies, and certain convenience stores) from providing plastic carryout bags. Retailers may only offer post-consumer recycled paper bags for a minimum charge of 10 cents per bag, which the store retains. Compliant bags must contain a minimum of 40% post consumer recycled material unless otherwise stated in SB 1053. By January 1, 2028, all paper bags provided must contain at least 50% post-consumer recycled materials.

## California Refund Value (CRV) on Wine, Liquor and Juice Containers

Effective January 1, 2024, wine and liquor bottles and plastic juice bottles are subject to a CRV of 5¢ (if under 24 oz.) or 10¢ (if over 24 oz.). Wine and liquor bag-in-boxes, paperboard cartons, plastic pouches, and multi-layer pouches are subject to a CRV of 25¢. These containers can be brought to a bottle redemption center for payment.
