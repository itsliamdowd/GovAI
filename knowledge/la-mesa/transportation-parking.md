## E-Bike Ordinance — Warning Period and Citations

The City Council formally adopted an e-bike ordinance on Tuesday, May 12, 2026. It is part of the San Diego Electric Bicycle Safety Pilot Program (AB 2234) and is intended to improve safety, reduce collisions, and increase awareness of responsible e-bike use. Anyone operating a Class 1 or Class 2 e-bike within La Mesa city limits must be at least 12 years old.

From June 11 through Aug. 10, 2026, officers will focus on education and awareness by issuing warnings rather than citations. Beginning Aug. 11, 2026, violations may result in a $25 citation. Families may have the citation waived by completing an approved e-bike safety training course within 120 days. The pilot program remains in effect through Jan. 1, 2029, unless extended by state law.

## E-Bike Classes — Speed, Age, and Helmet Requirements

- Class 1: pedal assist, maximum speed 20 mph, minimum age 12, helmet required under 18, not allowed on sidewalks.
- Class 2: throttle, maximum speed 20 mph, minimum age 12, helmet required under 18, not allowed on sidewalks.
- Class 3: pedal assist, maximum speed 28 mph, minimum age 16, helmet required for all ages, not allowed on sidewalks.

An e-bike is an electric bicycle equipped with fully operable pedals and an electric motor of less than 750 watts. Electric bicycles do not need registration, licensing or insurance like a car or motorcycle. The same rules of the road that apply to human-powered bicycles apply to all types of e-bikes.

## E-Motorcycles Are Not E-Bikes

E-motorcycles, like Sur-ron, Talaria, 79-Bike, Rawrr Mantis, and Stark, are not considered e-bikes by law. They do not have functioning pedals and are entirely powered by the motor. Riders must have a Drivers License with a "motorcycle endorsement." E-motorcycles must be registered just like a car, and you must have proof of insurance. Most e-motorcycles are not street legal without modifications (including adding turn signals, mirrors and license plate holders).

## E-Bike Rules of the Road

- Ride in the same direction as traffic.
- Stay as far to the right as practicable when riding on a roadway, except when turning, passing, or avoiding hazards.
- Stop at stop signs and red lights.
- Always yield to pedestrians in crosswalks.
- Give an audible signal when passing pedestrians on shared-use paths.
- Do not carry passengers unless the e-bike is specifically designed and equipped to do so.
- Never ride under the influence of alcohol or drugs.

Hand signals: left turn, left arm extended straight out; right turn, right arm extended straight out, or left arm bent upward; stop, left arm bent downward.

The City encourages riders and families to participate in an approved Electric Bicycle Safety and Training Program.

## Traffic Signals — Coordination and Fiber Optic Network

The City of La Mesa Department of Public Works obtained grants from the Federal Congestion Management and Air Quality program to interconnect and coordinate traffic signals with fiber optic cable. Currently, 53 of the 57 signals are connected to each other and to a central computer at City Hall.

The central computer tells the signals what times of the day and days of the week to start coordination and when to turn green, allowing a stream of green lights along a corridor while traveling at the speed limit. The program can also increase green light time when traffic is heaviest.

When communication between a signal or series of signals and the central computer is disrupted or cut off, or a signal is preempted by an emergency vehicle or trolley, the signals continue to operate but are no longer coordinated. A preempted signal will take several minutes to reorganize itself and get back into coordination.

## Downtown Village Parking — Meter Rates and Permit Pricing (Effective January 1, 2026)

Effective January 1, 2026, parking meter rates in Parking District One increase from $0.75 per hour to $1.50 per hour for all meters, including the Lemon Avenue Municipal Parking Lot, which uses a QR code-based permit system.

Tiered parking permits for employees of businesses in Parking District One are no longer available. One parking permit is offered for access to all seven designated permit parking areas at a cost of $80 for a quarterly permit and $288 for an annual permit.

## Downtown Village Parking Permit Program — How to Buy a Permit

The Downtown Village Parking Permit Program was established to improve management of the supply of municipal parking downtown and to provide businesses and employees with affordable parking. Permit holders are exempted from parking meter fees and two- or four-hour parking time limits in designated permit parking areas.

Permits are purchased through a web-based system. Review the How to Buy Permits information sheet, then visit www.paymycite.com to purchase your permit. The system includes an annual permit option and a feature for printing a temporary permit to display until your permit sticker arrives in the mail. A Map of Designated Permit Areas and Program Policies & Procedures are available on the page.

## Parking Permit Display and Replacement

Permit placards must be properly displayed on the front dashboard at all times. Failure to display the placard may result in issuance of an expired parking meter citation. Forgotten, lost or misplaced parking permits do not qualify as a reason to dismiss a valid parking citation. If the placard is not displayed, the vehicle has no parking privileges and must pay the parking meter and observe posted time limitations.

To purchase a replacement for a lost or misplaced placard, call Phoenix Group at 888-300-9915. Lost or stolen permits incur a replacement charge of $25 for each quarterly permit and $50 for each annual permit.
