## Fire Hazard Severity Zone Maps

On Monday, March 24, 2025, the California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection (CAL FIRE) released updated Fire Hazard Severity Zone maps. These maps define areas where state fire safety regulations apply and clarify the responsibilities of local and county fire departments in fire response. A Fire Hazard Severity Zone (FHSZ) is a geographic area classified by the State as having a "Moderate," "High," or "Very High" wildfire risk, based on factors such as vegetation, terrain, weather patterns, and fire history. CAL FIRE primarily uses these zones to inform building codes and wildfire mitigation strategies in high-risk areas.

California Government Code Section 51178 requires the State to identify "Moderate" and "High" zones in addition to "Very High" fire hazard areas. The last Local Response Area map, published in 2007, only contained "Very High" hazard zones. The updated maps may include new areas that were not previously identified by the State Fire Marshal. Government Code Section 51179 does not allow local agencies to decrease the level of Fire Hazard Severity Zone or to remove areas from the recommended map, but does allow a local agency to include additional areas not identified by the State Fire Marshal.

## Fire Hazard Severity Zone Maps — Public Review and Adoption Timeline

California Government Code Section 51178.5 requires local agencies to make the information available for public review and comment. ## Complaint of Police Conduct — How to File

Residents have the right to make a complaint against a police officer(s) for any improper police conduct, using the online Complaint of Police Conduct form (Investigation/Complaint Form). California law requires the agency to have a procedure to investigate complaints of community members, and complainants have a right to a written description of this procedure. Civilian complaints and any reports or findings related to complaints must be retained by the agency for at least five years. The form asks for name, personal phone number, address, location of occurrence/incident, date, time, incident number (if available), what the complaint resulted from (field interview, investigation, arrest, citation/ticket, traffic stop, radio call, or other), a brief description of the incident, and any witnesses. Photographs, videos, documents, or a citation/ticket can be attached.

## Complaint of Police Conduct — Mediation Option

Mediation is available as an alternative method to resolve complaints that qualify. It is a voluntary, confidential process in which the community member and the involved officer take part in a guided conversation facilitated by a trained, neutral mediator. Not all complaints qualify for mediation. Complainants indicate on the form whether they are open to learning more; if the complaint is eligible, a staff member will contact the complainant with more information.

## Complaint of Police Conduct — Review Procedure and Findings

After the form is submitted, a representative from the La Mesa PD will contact the complainant to ask for more details. The La Mesa PD reviews every complaint. The Community Police Oversight Board determines if an audit by the Independent Police Auditor is necessary; if the auditor conducts an audit, the Oversight Board may make recommendations to the Chief of Police. If the Chief of Police determines that an employee violated department policies or procedures, appropriate corrective action is taken.

The complainant receives written notification of the findings of any formal complaint. Possible findings are: Unfounded (the alleged act did not occur); Not Sustained (the investigation produced insufficient evidence); Exonerated (the alleged act occurred, but was justified); Sustained (the accused employee committed all or part of the alleged conduct). If a complaint is sustained, the Chief of Police determines whether the employee will be disciplined and/or receive additional training. Discipline may include reprimand, suspension, demotion or termination. State law does not allow the release of the specific action taken against a public safety employee.
