## Building Permit for a New or Relocating Business

Generally, if the prior use of the building was the same as the proposed future use and no construction or alterations to existing building systems (structural, electric, plumbing, mechanical, etc.) are planned to accommodate the new tenant, a building permit may not be needed. Certain businesses, such as restaurants and automotive uses, must be formally reviewed and will require a tenant improvement permit even if no work is being done.

Before expanding, the City suggests talking to a contractor, engineer, architect or other design professional, along with City staff members. For answers to specific questions, contact the Building Division, 619.667.1176.

## Plans Required for a New Tenant

Generally, plans are required whenever it is determined that a building permit is required for a new tenant to occupy a building or tenant space. If no work is taking place but a permit is required, a Site Plan and a Floor Plan may be all that are necessary to show the location and number of parking spaces and the intended uses of each room or space within the building.

When additions or alterations to the building are proposed, or the intended use presents special fire or life/safety concerns, additional plans may also be required. These plans must clearly show the extent of the work and its compliance with the applicable laws, codes and ordinances.

## Homeowner Permits — When a Permit Is Needed

A construction permit is needed for all new construction. In many cases, a permit is needed for repair or replacement of existing fixtures, such as replacing windows. A plumbing, electrical or mechanical permit may be needed for any addition or changes to a building's existing system; for example, moving or adding an electrical outlet requires a permit.

A construction permit is not needed for wallpapering, painting or similar finish work; fences six feet high or lower and not enclosing a pool; platforms, decks and walks 30 inches high or less over grade or not over basement; and in several other cases. Reviews may still be required from other agencies, so check before building. For plumbing, mechanical and electrical work, replacement or repair of fixtures (such as changing water faucets or replacing switches) does not normally require a permit. Replacing a water heater or adding a permanently wired light fixture does require a permit.

For specific questions, call the Building Division, 667.1176.

## Permit Processing Time and Penalties for Unpermitted Work

Permit issuance periods vary. Some projects can be fully permitted over-the-counter, meaning a return trip won't be needed. Some projects require that plans be left for additional review.

If a permit, when needed, is not obtained before construction, you have violated city codes and regulations and will be subject to citations and/or penalties. You'll be required to obtain permits for the work and it must pass inspection, or you'll have to return the structure or site to its original condition.

## Who Can Obtain a Permit and Do the Work (Owner-Builders)

Contractors licensed by the State of California or their agents may obtain permits. Homeowners may also obtain permits. The City recommends dealing only with a contractor licensed by the State of California. The Contractors State License Board can be reached at (800) 321-CSLB.

Homeowners can do the work themselves but must follow certain regulations. If you will be hiring anyone, you may have to purchase Workers' Compensation Insurance. If you won't be hiring anyone, you'll be asked to sign an "Owner-Builder Statement." The City can't issue a permit without either insurance or the completed Owner-Builder Statement. You must build to the approved plans; if you decide to make changes, check with the city's plan review staff or your field inspector.

Plans for simple projects such as small single story houses, room additions and garages can usually be drawn by qualified individuals such as draftspersons. Other projects may require plans prepared and signed by a licensed architect or engineer.

## Inspections

It is the property owner's responsibility to call the City of La Mesa for inspections at specific times during construction. You may have your contractor make the call, but it is still the owner's responsibility to make sure the inspections are made. For example, retaining walls require inspections of the footing, after block is laid and steel placed but before grouting, after backfill has been placed, and when all work is finished.

For legal purposes, a construction project is not complete until it has passed the final inspection.

## Building Permit Expiration

A building permit shall expire if the construction work is not commenced or the first City progress inspection is not received within one calendar year from the date of the permit. It also expires if no inspections are received for a period of 6 months after the work is commenced, or if the work exceeds 3 calendar years from the issuance date of the permit.

Once a permit is expired, you may be required to begin the permit process from the beginning and pay all of the applicable fees.

## Park Permits — Reservations

Park permits are available for reservation at City of La Mesa park spaces and should be made online. Online permits are available for groups of up to 50 people, including children. For gatherings larger than 50, visit the Parks and Recreation Department at 4975 Memorial Drive, La Mesa, CA, in person to complete your reservation. All park permits must be booked at least 7 days in advance of your scheduled event.

If your event includes vendors, they must be pre-approved by the Parks and Recreation Department. For new vendor inquiries, contact recreation@cityoflamesa.gov. A processing fee up to $3 will be charged for all online transactions by the software provider. This fee is non-refundable.

For more information, contact the Parks and Recreation Department at 619.667.1300, or email recreation@cityoflamesa.gov.

## Park Rules Requiring Special Permits

A Commercial Use of Public Recreational Property Permit is required for all commercial operators conducting business in City parks. No alcoholic beverages are allowed without a special permit, which is available at the Community Center. There is no overnight parking or camping in any park (Municipal Code sections 9.08.140 and 9.08.153).

Parks are open from 6 a.m. to 10 p.m., except for Harry Griffen Park and MacArthur Park, which open at 7 a.m. and close one hour after sunset. All parks are smoke-free; no smoking or vaping is allowed. Dogs must remain on leash except at the off-leash dog runs located in Harry Griffen Park and MacArthur Park.
