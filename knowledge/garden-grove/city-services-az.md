## Dog and Cat Licensing — Requirements and Fees

All dogs over four months of age, kept in Garden Grove, must be licensed and have a current rabies vaccination. All dogs are required to wear a license tag at all times, attached to a collar or harness. Dog licenses are non-transferable and non-refundable. Licenses are issued on an annual basis, and it is the dog owner's responsibility to keep rabies vaccines and dog licenses current. Service dogs registered pursuant to California Food and Agriculture Code Sections 30850-30854, as well as retired and active military/police dogs, must conform with local rabies control and licensing laws.

## Rabies Vaccination Exemption

Under Health and Safety Code 121690, an exemption may be granted for the rabies vaccine on an annual basis if "a rabies vaccination would endanger the dog's life." The condition must be verified and documented by a veterinarian. To apply, complete the legally required forms, include up to 5 pages of medical records indicating a rabies vaccine would endanger the life of the dog, and submit by mail or fax to Orange County Rabies Control Department (714) 259-1089. There is a $65 annual non-refundable application fee; make the check payable to OC Animal Care.

For more information, contact Orange County Rabies Control Department at (714) 796-6421 or visit https://ocpetinfo.com. Garden Grove Animal Care Services does not provide the exemption. All paperwork must be turned into Orange County Animal Care.

## Limits on Number of Animals and Animal Permits

Garden Grove Municipal Code section 6.04.020 A.6. limits the total number of any animals kept on residential property to four. An animal permit may be filed with Animal Care Services to request additional cats or dogs. Additional cats and dogs must be vaccinated and licensed prior to requesting an Animal Permit.

Per GGMC 6.04.020 A.6., a maximum of four of the following, or four of any combination, is permitted: dogs four months of age or older; roaming cats four months of age or older; and fowl, rabbits, birds, or household pets. Fowl, rabbits, birds, or household pets (excluding dogs and cats) must be kept at all times in a fully enclosed pen, coop, cage, or similar appropriate enclosure with a minimum setback of 25 feet from all adjacent dwelling units and all property lines.

Other limits: five or more cats are permitted if kept at all times in enclosed catteries and in compliance with City Council regulations. A maximum of 10 parakeets and a maximum of 10 racing pigeons are permitted, each in a fully enclosed enclosure with a minimum 25-foot setback from adjacent dwelling units and property lines. A maximum of 100 pigeons may be permitted subject to a conditional use permit under Title 9 of the Garden Grove Municipal Code, in a fully enclosed enclosure with a minimum 25-foot setback.

To obtain an Animal Permit application or learn more about the process, contact Garden Grove Animal Care Services at (714) 741-5565.

## Barking Dog Complaints

Garden Grove Animal Care Services recommends that neighbors first try to resolve the problem with the dog owner or other responsible person before filing a formal complaint. Under Garden Grove Municipal Code section 6.05.020, a barking dog is one that barks incessantly for 30 minutes or more in any 24-hour period, or intermittently for 60 minutes or more during any 24-hour period. A dog is not in violation if barking at a person trespassing or threatening to trespass on the private property where the dog is situated, or when the dog is being teased or provoked.

Civil citation process: A complaint must be submitted to Garden Grove Animal Care Services. An Animal Control Officer will try to contact the responsible person, who is given ten (10) days to resolve the situation. If the situation still exists after ten (10) days, you may file a second complaint. If Animal Care Services is not contacted within a reasonable time of the initial contact of the responsible person, the complaint will expire. Issuing a civil citation is solely at the City's discretion. Before a citation is issued, the complaining party must sign an affidavit under penalty of perjury that the barking dog has disturbed them and is a nuisance as legally defined.

The responsible person must pay the fine within 15 days and contest the citation at the administrative hearing. The requestor must attend the administrative hearing if the responsible person contests the citation; if the requestor does not attend, the citation may be dismissed in favor of the responsible person. Both parties must bring a copy of all evidence to the hearing; electronic media must be compatible with City equipment (media that plays on a standard CD or DVD player will most likely work). Payment of the fine does not excuse the violator from correcting the violation.

The City cannot guarantee anonymity to the complainant or the dog owner. All information obtained is considered public information and may be disclosed to either party. If the dog stops barking or no longer meets the criteria after a citation is issued, the complainant may file a Waiver of Civil Citation, calling up to four (4) business days prior to the hearing to withdraw the complaint.

## Wild Animals and Trapped Wildlife

Under the City's city-wide wild animal policy, the City will not respond to remove healthy trapped wildlife. Trapping and relocating wildlife without a permit issued by the California Department of Fish and Game could be considered a violation of law and abandonment. If you have trapped a wild animal, you must release it or contact a state-licensed trapping or pest control service. Animal Care Services does not remove or relocate wildlife, and will only attempt to capture wild animals that are injured, sick, or pose a direct threat to public health and safety.

To discourage wildlife: keep trash cans covered at all times, remove fallen fruit from trees, remove pet food dishes after feeding, keep yards free from excessive brush and overgrown vegetation, don't feed wildlife, make changes to fencing to keep wildlife out, and consider animal repellant products. For questions, call (714) 741-5565 and press 1.

## Coyote Sightings and Safety

Report coyote sightings to the City's coyote hotline at (714) 741-5286, indicating the date, time, and location. Sightings can also be reported, and a map of coyote encounters viewed, on the Coyote Cacher website at ucanr.edu. If the coyote is acting aggressively, call Garden Grove Animal Care Services at (714) 741-5565.

The California Department of Fish and Wildlife (CDFW) says coyotes are very active March through August. CDFW recommends: keeping small pets inside, particularly at dawn and dusk; keeping pet food and water dishes inside; securing food and trash at all times and removing all sources of water; picking up fallen fruit and keeping compost piles tightly sealed; sweeping up fallen birdseed; removing brush, wood piles, and debris; and installing motion-activated lighting or sprinklers. If a coyote approaches or acts aggressively, throw rocks, make noise, look big, and pick up small children and pets. Do not turn your back to the animal. The Keep Me Wild Brochure is available in English, Vietnamese and Spanish.
