## Reporting Potholes and Immediate Street Repairs

The City Public Works Department addresses issues of immediate concern, such as filling potholes or other situations that need attention right away. The City often depends on residents to alert it to these conditions. To report a pothole, provide the location in the Report It app. The City organizes and prioritizes submitted requests and schedules work to efficiently make repairs.

## Annual Street Maintenance Program

Every street in the City was surveyed and a Pavement Management Program was developed. The program established eight geographical zones. Each year the City advertises and receives bids for a Street Rehabilitation and Maintenance Project that focuses on one of the eight residential maintenance zones. The highest priority major streets are also treated, based on pavement condition.

Phase 1 removes and replaces trees that present a risk to public safety or are damaging streets and sidewalks, replaces damaged or uneven sidewalk areas, and adds pedestrian ramps to meet ADA guidelines. Phase 2 replaces structurally compromised pavement with asphalt concrete, treats weeds and fills cracks, resurfaces streets (chip seal surface coating, seal coat, or an asphalt concrete overlay), and updates street signing and striping, including adding bike lanes in accordance with the City's Bicycle Master Plan.

Source: https://www.escondido.gov/351/Street-Maintenance-Program

## Better Streets – Traffic & School Safety Program

Each year, the Transportation and Community Safety Commission prioritizes and funds traffic safety projects. Projects are nominated by the public and staff, prioritized using adopted scoring criteria, and selected by the Commission for funding. The annual Traffic Management Program List (TMPL) budget is $200,000. Funding has generally gone toward pedestrian safety, school safety, and traffic-calming projects. Typical improvements include changes to striping and signage, pedestrian countdown timers, accessible pedestrian signals (APS), rectangular rapid flashing beacons (RRFB), and radar speed feedback signs.

## Escondido Creek Bikeway Missing Link Project

The completed project (July 2019) constructed a separated Class IV Bikeway (Cycle Track) and a Class I Bike Path through the core of the City. It fills a gap between the Escondido Creek Trail on North Broadway at Woodward Avenue and the Inland Rail Trail on Quince Street just north of the Escondido Transit Center. Key features include a bollard-separated two-way Class IV Bikeway; a new bike bridge over Escondido Creek adjacent to the existing North Broadway bridge; a new traffic signal at North Broadway and Woodward Avenue; and a new signalized crosswalk on North Broadway next to Grape Day Park, in front of the San Diego Children's Discovery Museum. Solar-powered, push-button-activated Rectangular Rapid-Flashing Beacons were installed at the Quince Street crossing just north of the Escondido Transit Center.

A Class IV bikeway is for the exclusive use of bicycles and is physically separated from motored traffic with a vertical feature, which may include flexible posts, inflexible barriers, or on-street parking. The project was partly funded with a TransNet Active Transportation Program Grant. Questions or concerns can go to John Houston, City of Escondido Field Engineering Inspector.

Source: https://www.escondido.gov/274/Escondido-Creek-Bikeway-Missing-Link-Pro

## Escondido Transit Center Active Transportation Connections (ETC ATC) Project

The ETC ATC Project installs a pedestrian bridge on the north shoulder of West Valley Parkway (over the Spruce channel) to improve pedestrian access on the western side of the Escondido Transit Center, near the North County Transit District (NCTD) Sprinter Station. A bridge at this location was in NCTD's original design but was never constructed, leaving an asphalt berm extending into the traffic lanes on West Valley Parkway. Removing the berm will provide space for bike lanes along West Valley Parkway. The project also provides bike lanes on Quince Street and North Tulip Street, connecting cyclists to the Inland Rail Trail bike path (along Escondido Creek flood control channel) and the Escondido Transit Center.
