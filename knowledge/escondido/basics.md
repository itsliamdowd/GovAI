## City Council — Meetings and Agendas

The Escondido City Council meets at 5 pm on the first four Wednesdays of each month. The City Clerk's office prepares the agenda the preceding week and also prepares the minutes. Any item placed on the City Council agenda must be received by the City Clerk three weeks before the scheduled meeting.

The agenda is posted at least 72 hours before the meeting as required by law. Copies of the agenda and accompanying reports are available on Thursday (after 3:30 pm) prior to the upcoming meeting. They can be reviewed in the public viewing area of the Escondido City Clerk's office at City Hall, 201 North Broadway, at the Main Library at 239 South Kalmia during normal business hours, or online. Agendas are available prior to meetings, and minutes are available following approval.

Council meetings are broadcast live on Cox Cable Channel 19 and are replayed the following Sunday and Monday evenings at 6 pm. City Council contact: 201 North Broadway, Escondido, CA 92025; Phone: 760-839-4638.

## Public Comment at City Council Meetings (Speaker's Form)

The public may address the City Council on any agenda item. Complete a Speaker's Form, available on the side walls of the Chambers, and give it to the City Clerk. Comments are generally limited to 3 minutes. To speak on an item not on the agenda, use "Oral Communications" and complete a Speaker's Form the same way.

## How to Provide Public Comments (City Clerk)

Comments on agenda and non-agenda items can be submitted online or in person for all public meetings. You can also participate virtually by telephone during City Council meetings only.

1. Written comments online: submit them using the Written Public Comment Form.
2. Speak in person: comments are generally limited to three minutes. Submit your request to speak before the discussion of your agenda item. Option A: pick up a yellow speaker slip in the racks on both sides of the Council Chambers, complete it, and return it to the City Clerk before your item is considered. Your name will be called when it is your turn. Option B: register in advance by completing the online Request to Speak Form.
3. Speak by telephone (Council meetings only, audio only): register in advance at publiccomment.escondido.gov for the specific meeting date. Select "Call my phone when the meeting starts" and include at minimum your phone number. You will receive a call from 760-546-9959 when the meeting begins. Once you join, listen for the prompts, and someone will unmute you for your allotted time when it is your turn.

## Elections — Offices and Districts

Escondido is a general law city governed by a five-member City Council. The Mayor and the City Treasurer are directly elected at large. The City is divided into four districts, which are used for all regular elections of Council members. The term of office for each member is four years with staggered terms.

Seats up for election in 2026: District 1 (Consuelo Martinez), District 2 (Joe Garcia), and Mayor (Dane White). Seats up for election in 2028: District 3 (Christian Garcia), District 4 (Judy Fitzgerald), and City Treasurer (Douglas Shultz).

## City Budget — Fiscal Year and Process

The City of Escondido adopts its budget each June for the following fiscal year, which runs from July 1 to June 30. Preliminary budget information is brought to a City Council meeting for discussion in April/May, and the final budget is presented at a Public Hearing in June for adoption. The Operating Budget projects short-term revenue and expenditures for day-to-day services. A separate Capital Improvement Project Budget projects long-term revenue and expenditures for major projects such as roads, park improvements, and pipelines.

A Public Hearing for adoption of the FY2026/27 Operating Budget was held at the City Council Meeting on June 17, 2026. The FY2025/26 operating budget was adopted at a public hearing on June 18, 2025. The FY 2024/25 Operating Budget was adopted on June 19, 2024. Final and preliminary budget PDFs are posted on the page.

The City spends 76% of its General Fund departmental budget on public safety, and 85% of the City's costs are personnel costs. Sales tax, property tax, and other taxes (including transient occupancy tax, business license tax, and franchise fees) combined account for 81% of the General Fund's revenue.

## Roundabouts — How to Navigate

A roundabout is an intersection where traffic travels around a central island in a counterclockwise direction. Vehicles entering or exiting must yield to vehicles, bicyclists, and pedestrians.

When you approach a roundabout:
- Slow down as you approach the intersection.
- Yield to pedestrians and bicyclists crossing the roadway.
- Watch for signs and pavement markings that guide you or prohibit certain movements.
- Enter the roundabout when there is a big enough gap in traffic.
- Drive in a counterclockwise direction. Do not stop or pass other vehicles.
- Use your turn signals when you exit.
- If you miss your exit, continue around until you return to your exit.

Source: https://www.escondido.gov/341/Roundabouts
