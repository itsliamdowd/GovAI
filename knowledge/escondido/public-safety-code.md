## Fireworks — Prohibited in Escondido

All fireworks are illegal anywhere in Escondido. Other than at professional shows put on by licensed pyrotechnicians, no fireworks can be possessed, stored or discharged in the city at any time during the year. Prohibited fireworks include sparklers, poppers, snappers, fountains, bottle rockets, and any other explosive devices.

To dispose of fireworks in your possession, contact the Escondido Fire Department at 760-839-5400 to make arrangements. To report anyone selling, providing, or discharging fireworks, contact the Escondido Fire Department immediately at 760-839-5400.

Professionals who will put on a fireworks show must complete an Application for Permit (PDF). A Current Fee Schedule (PDF) is available on the page.

## Fireworks — Municipal Code Penalties

The fireworks ordinances are part of the Escondido Municipal Code, Chapter 17 Offenses-Miscellaneous Provisions, Article 4. Weapons and Explosives. Sec. 17-76 prohibits the sale at retail, exchange, barter, trade, possession or exhibition of fireworks within the city. Sec. 17-77 prohibits the discharge, firing or use of firecrackers, bombs, rockets, torpedoes, Roman candles or other fireworks. The council may permit public display of fireworks by properly qualified individuals or organized bodies under the direct supervision of experts. Sec. 17-75 prohibits the storage or sale of fireworks at wholesale unless authorized by a permit from the council, issued for a period of not exceeding one year.

Under Sec. 17-80, a violation is an infraction punishable by a fine not exceeding $100 for a first violation, a fine not exceeding $200 for a second violation of the same section within one year, and a fine not exceeding $500 for each additional violation of the same section within one year.

## Fire Hazard Severity Zone Map

On Monday March 24, 2025, Cal Fire released the updated Fire Hazard Severity Zone map for Southern California. It classifies areas of Escondido that are most susceptible to wildfire as moderate, high and very high wildfire hazard severity zones. An Escondido Fire Hazard Severity Map (PDF) is available on the page.

In certain areas, the map will guide city development standards and increase requirements for new buildings to have fire-resistant features and defensible space (buffers) between them and the surrounding wildland area. New construction in high and very high zones must adhere to ignition-resistant building standards. Property sellers in the high zone, in addition to the very high zone, must disclose the designation in real estate transactions, and an inspection is required by the fire department. According to Cal Fire, the map is not expected to impact insurance coverage.

Per state law, the City Council must adopt the new maps through an ordinance within 120 days. The city will then apply the ignition-resistant building standards in areas required by the maps no sooner than 30 days after they are adopted. Feedback on the map can be submitted through the form on the page (name, email address, comment).

## Fire Code Requirements for Businesses

Any time you make physical changes to any part of your business, those changes must comply with all local codes. The Fire Department recommends consulting a professional who knows these codes before making plans. Even changes in facility layout, including installation of storage racks or new equipment, can impact the building's safety features and will need to meet Fire Department requirements.

Building and tenant improvement plans must be submitted to the City of Escondido Building Department, which routes them to the Fire Department for review and approval. County plans must be submitted to the County of San Diego Building Department (Rincon del Diablo area) for approval. If plans are not approved, remarks will indicate how they must be altered before work can begin. For questions, call the Escondido Fire Department at 760-839-5400.

The page also links to Construction Permits, Operational Permits, Fire Prevention Checklist, and Fire Extinguishers.

## Fire Code — Overview

The purpose of the Fire Code is to establish the minimum requirements consistent with nationally recognized good practices to safeguard the public health, safety and general welfare from the hazards of fire, explosion or dangerous conditions in new and existing buildings, structures and premises. It also aims to provide safety and assistance to fire fighters and emergency responders during emergency operations. The page is titled California Fire Code (2025).

The Escondido 2026 Fire Code, 2026 Rincon Fire Protection District Fire Code, 2026 Rincon Wildland Urban Interface Code and 2026 San Diego Consolidated Fire Code are more restrictive because of modifications that were reasonable and necessary because of local climatic, geological and topographical conditions.

Source: https://www.escondido.gov/518/Fire-Code

## Escondido Fire Code

The Escondido Fire Code can be found in the Escondido Municipal Code in Chapter 11, Article 2, Division 1. It was adopted as Ordinance 2025-10 on December 17, 2025, and applies to the Local Responsibility Area.

It regulates minimum fire safety requirements for new and existing buildings, facilities, storage and processes. It addresses fire prevention, fire protection, life safety and safe storage and use of hazardous materials in new and existing buildings, facilities and processes. It controls hazards in all buildings and sites, whether indoors or outdoors.

## Community Emergency Response Team (CERT)

CERT is a joint effort between the Escondido Fire Department and community members to become better prepared for emergencies. Escondido Fire Department instructors provide emergency preparedness and response training. Graduates have an opportunity to become disaster volunteers with the city. The page notes that when disasters happen, it can take 72 hours for emergency services to help those affected.

CERT training teaches how to prepare yourself and your family for natural disasters and acts of terrorism, put out small fires, assess damage to buildings, provide basic first aid, and perform simple search and rescue operations. The CERT Training Academy is usually held in late February and early March.

For more information, contact Jeff Murdock with the Escondido Fire Department by email or by phone at 760-839-5400. A 2023 CERT Member Meeting Schedule (PDF) is available on the page.

Source: https://www.escondido.gov/494/Community-Emergency-Response-Team-CERT

## Fire Explorers Youth Program

The Escondido Fire Department Explorer Post is a career-oriented youth program chartered through Learning for Life, active since 1981. Its purpose is to expose young people to the various aspects of the fire service while developing character, morals, values and a sense of responsibility. The Post has up to 25 Fire Explorers, divided into two platoons, with three Fire Explorer Officers.

The Post meets once a month, usually on a Saturday. Fire Explorers must be available between the hours of 0830 and 1700 hours. Initial cost, including uniform, may be approximately $200. Scholarships or sponsorships may be available. Minimum age is 15 years and eighth grade graduate. Maximum age is 21 years.

Applicants may have to pass a Physical Agility / Ability Test, a Written Exam, and an Oral Interview. An application must be fully completed and submitted to be considered. Applications are kept on file until the next recruitment. Email the completed application. The Explorer Application (PDF) and program flyer (PDF) are available on the page.

Source: https://www.escondido.gov/495/Fire-Explorers
