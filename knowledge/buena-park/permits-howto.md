## Building Permits — When Required and Types

A building permit is a license that grants legal permission to construct or alter a structure. In general, a permit is required for all activities regulated by the City Building Code or its referenced codes. Permits are required for all buildings or structures erected, constructed, enlarged, altered, repaired, moved, improved, removed, converted or demolished unless a separate permit for each building or structure has first been obtained from the building official. All block walls 4 feet or higher require a building permit.

Types of permits: Building Permit (construct or modify a building or structure); Electrical Permit (install or modify an electrical system); Plumbing Permit (install or modify a plumbing system); Mechanical Permit (install or modify heating, ventilation, refrigeration, air conditioning and other related systems); Combination Building Permit (for a single-family home or duplex, covers all building, electrical, plumbing and mechanical work for the job; also issued for commercial construction projects); Demolition Permit (demolish any structure). A Building Permit is required for any block wall and retaining wall four feet high from the bottom of the footing to the top of the wall. The Planning Division must review and approve all fence/wall plans and design to ensure compliance with zoning regulations.

## Building Permits — General Process and Conditions

Steps: an application must be prepared, plans submitted, and plan review fees paid. Plans are reviewed for compliance with applicable codes and State Law and are either approved or a set of corrections prepared. If corrections are required, corrected plans are submitted, reviewed, and approved. The owner or contractor pays the permit fee (and any other applicable fees) and obtains the permit, a set of approved plans, and an Inspection Record Card for posting on the site. Inspections are made at key times during construction and before work is concealed. When the work is totally complete, a final inspection is made, the work approved, the connection of utilities is authorized, and a Certificate of Occupancy is issued.

Professionally prepared plans must have a registered design professional's wet stamp and wet signature on the cover or title sheet containing the sheet index and on the cover sheet or first page of calculations, specifications, and reports for all plan submittals. Initial submittal of plans should not be clouded; clouds indicate a permit was already issued and is now being revised, so remove clouds prior to upload for submittal.

General conditions: a permit is required prior to starting any work. Permits become null and void if work authorized is not commenced within 180 days of the issuance date or if such work is suspended or abandoned at any time after the work is commenced for a period of 180 days. No work shall be covered or concealed without first having been inspected and approved.

## Building Permits — Who Can Obtain a Permit

Permits are issued to duly licensed contractors. A homeowner can obtain a permit for the construction, alteration or repair of a one or two-family dwelling and accessory buildings or facilities if: the owner resides or intends to reside in the dwelling, showing proof of residence; the construction is performed by the owner; and the owner signs a statement that no person will be employed in a manner as to become subject to the workers' compensation laws of the State of California.

When a permit is taken out, the signature and identity of the applicant must be verified. A California Driver's License, State of California Identification Card, or other positive identification will meet this requirement. Contractors working in the City of Buena Park are required to have a Buena Park business license. If a contractor has employees, proof of Workers' Compensation Insurance must be shown at the time of permit issuance. Contractors working alone may waive this requirement, but must show a pocket copy of their State Contractor's License.

## Room Additions — Lot Coverage and Setbacks

Lot coverage restrictions apply only to lots in the RS-6 and RS-8 zones. The maximum area of a lot that may be covered by buildings is 40 percent of the total lot area. Swimming pools are not considered lot coverage. Not more than 30 percent of the required rear yard (an area 25 feet deep across the width of the lot) may be covered by structures. Example: on a lot 60 feet wide by 100 feet deep, total allowable coverage by all structures is 2,400 sq. ft., of which no more than 450 sq. ft. may be in the required 25-foot rear yard area.

All buildings, roofed patios, swimming pools or other structures on a residential lot must be setback a minimum of 20 feet from the front property line, five feet from an interior side property line, ten feet from a side property line adjoining a street and 5 feet from a rear property line. Detached structures must be a minimum of six feet from any other structure on the lot. For lot coverage restrictions and setbacks in all other residential zones, check with the Planning Division, which has an On-line Inquiry Portal.

## Sign Permits

A zoning compliance sign permit is required prior to the erection, alteration, or relocation of any sign subject to the sign guidelines in Section 19.904, and Building Code provisions must also be complied with. Signs in conformance with an approved sign program, or business name signs within the maximum allowable sign area for the site, may receive approval by the Planning Division. Applications for individual signs and sign programs that require Planning Commission approval must be on forms provided by the Community Development Department.

Permit-exempt signs (subject to restrictions in Section 19.904.080): public signs, warnings and notices; directional signs; memorial signs and cornerstones; residential directory signs; window signs; construction signs; real estate signs. Prohibited signs: any moving, oscillating, or rotating sign; inflatable signs (other than forced cold-air inflatables); portable signs, not including otherwise permitted temporary signs; off-premise signs (billboards), not including otherwise permitted temporary signs; and roof signs. Properties in the Entertainment Corridor Specific Plan (ECSP) or Auto Center Specific Plan (ACSP) zones are subject to special sign criteria.

Submit the completed application, filing fee, and other required information to Planning staff; the applicant will be notified within 30 days after filing whether the application is complete or what additional information is required. A complete set of plans for each sign includes: a colored scaled graphic drawing with dimensions and area (including letter dimensions); a site plan; building elevation(s) with existing and proposed signage; color chips and/or color brochure samples of all sign materials on an 8-1/2x11 sheet (material samples, poster board, cardboard or foam core will not be accepted); lighting specifications including lumens; and colored photographs of the site and existing signs. A complete building permit application is required for any wall sign, pylon sign, monument (ground) sign or new awning sign.
