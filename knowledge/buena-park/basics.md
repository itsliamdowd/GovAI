## Buena Park History

Local settlers called the area "Plaza Buena," which means "good park" in Spanish. James A. Whitaker founded Buena Park in 1887 when he bought 690 acres of land from Abel Stearns. In 1889 Whitaker got the Pacific Creamery Company, which canned condensed milk under the Lily Cream label, to build a factory in Buena Park. This was the first industry established in Orange County.

The City of Buena Park was incorporated in 1953. On January 27, 1953, Orange County clerk B.J Smith swore in the first city council. The council elected Grady Travis as the first mayor. The city now has a population of over 80,000 residents.

## Buena Park Historical Society and House Museums

The Buena Park Historical Society is a non-profit (501 c3) organization working to preserve the community's heritage. It operates the Whitaker-Jaynes Estate (Est. 1887) and the Bacon House (Est. 1884) museums. The City bought the Whitaker-Jaynes house in 1965 and restored it. It was moved in 1994 to become the cornerstone of the Historical Society. The Bacon family gave the Bacon House to the City in 1976. It was moved to the Whitaker-Jaynes Estate Park and restored to commemorate the United States Bicentennial.

The Historical Society provides tours of both house museums. Tour hours are Monday-Thursday, 12-4 pm. Saturday & Sunday tours are by appointment. Call (714) 521-1887 to make an appointment.

## City Government Structure

Buena Park was incorporated as a General Law City on January 27, 1953. In November 2008, the voters adopted a City Charter. The Charter acts as a "constitution" for the city, and amendments, revisions and repeals of it are subject to the vote of the people.

Buena Park operates as a council-manager type of government. The City Council is the policy-making body and the City Manager is responsible for carrying out Council policy.

## Council Actions — Ordinances, Resolutions and Minute Orders

The City Council takes action in three forms:
- **Ordinance:** the most binding form of action and the legislative enactment of City law. In Buena Park, an ordinance requires a four-fifths vote for adoption. If it does not receive four-fifths, it must return to the next regularly scheduled Council Meeting for adoption. Ordinances become effective 30 days after adoption.
- **Resolution:** establishes Council policy or directs certain types of administrative action. Resolutions of commendation honor deserving individuals or organizations. A resolution is usually effective after a majority vote at the meeting.
- **Minute order:** the most informal action. It requires majority approval of procedural actions such as filing a report or instructing administrative staff to take certain actions.

Source: https://www.buenapark.com/city_departments/city_council/council_actions_and_public_comment.php

## Speaking at Council Meetings — Public Comment and Public Hearings

All meetings are open to the public, and agendas are available for review 72 hours before the meeting. At each meeting residents may speak on any agenda item or during oral communications. To speak, add your name to the digital public kiosk outside the Council Chamber. Speakers approach the lectern and state their name and address clearly. Remarks are limited to 3 minutes.

Public hearings are legal, advertised hearings where the public may address the Council in support of or opposition to a specific proposal. They are required when certain sections of the Municipal Code are amended, changed, created or deleted, or when the zoning or major use of a parcel of land is changed. Public hearings are held at 6pm and are restricted to the issues set on the agenda.

## Municipal Code and Zoning Ordinance

The Buena Park City Code is a compilation of the City's ordinances, which are local laws adopted by the City Council. Violation of an ordinance may be a misdemeanor, unless the ordinance makes it an infraction. The Code has two parts. Part I is the "Buena Park Municipal Code" (Titles 1 through 13). Part II is the "Buena Park Development Code" (Titles 14 through 20). The Zoning Ordinance is Title 19 and pertains to zoning and land use. The City Charter can also be viewed from this page.

## Passport Services — First-Time Adult Applicants (Age 18 and Older)

1. Complete the DS-11 Passport Application Form.
2. Proof of U.S. Citizenship: original U.S. birth certificate, Certificate of Naturalization, or full validity, undamaged U.S passport (valid for 10 years for adults and 5 years for children under 16).
3. Proof of Identification: fully valid/non-expired California driver's license, fully valid out-of-state driver's license, valid or expired and non-damaged U.S. passport book or card, or Certificate of Naturalization.
4. Recent passport photo. Bring your own (taken within the last 6 months) or take one during your appointment.
5. Fees: Application Fee by check or money order only, payable to the "U.S. Department of State." Execution Fee by cash, card, or check payable to the "City of Buena Park."

## Passport Services — Adult Renewals

Buena Park's Passport Services does not process DS-82 adult renewal applications. Eligible renewals may be done by mail or online.

To renew by mail, submit:
1. A completed DS-82 form.
2. Your full validity, undamaged U.S. passport.
3. A recent passport photo (taken within the last 6 months, or taken during an appointment).
4. The Application Fee by check or money order only, payable to the "U.S. Department of State."
5. Proof of name change (e.g., court order, marriage certificate) if your name has changed.

To renew online, use the U.S. Department of State - Bureau of Consular Affairs link on the page.

## Passport Services — Minor Applicants

**Ages 16 or 17:** Follow the requirements for ages 0-15. Only one parent/legal guardian needs to be present with the applicant.
