## Housing Division — Overview and Contacts

The City of Fountain Valley says it is committed to preserving, improving, and expanding housing opportunities for residents across all income levels, especially low- and moderate-income households, seniors, and individuals with disabilities. The City works to preserve the quality and safety of existing housing stock, expand access to affordable housing opportunities, support residents in maintaining long-term housing stability, and promote fair and equitable housing practice.

## Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) Rental Assistance

The Orange County Housing Authority (OCHA) administers federally funded programs that provide rental assistance to qualified tenants in privately owned rental housing. The largest is the Housing Choice Voucher Program. Participants can use the assistance in a variety of rental dwellings and locations with almost any property owner who is willing to participate.

## Affordable Housing — Prado Family Homes

Prado Family Homes is a 50-unit affordable development for primarily extremely low and very low-income families. It offers one, two, and three-bedroom apartment homes. There are 50 tuck-under or carport parking spaces plus 44 uncovered spaces. LifeSTEPS, an independent, non-profit, social service agency, administers the on-site social services.

Prado Family Homes, located at 16790 S. Harbor Blvd., is open and accepting applications for its waiting list. Call the leasing office at 714-274-7011 / TTY 800-735-2929.

## Affordable Housing — Liberty at Founder's Village (Ownership)

This 55 and older senior project was built in 2004 and is affordable to low and moderate income households for 55 years. Income restrictions and eligibility requirements are in the Liberty Application Package (PDF). Potential buyers must complete all of the forms in the package and provide all applicable back up documents to the city for verification of eligibility to purchase.

Units occasionally come up for sale. Subsequent buyers must meet income eligibility and affordability criteria for the term of the affordability. Applications are only accepted when a unit is for sale. The City does not pre-qualify and does not keep a list of eligible applicants.

## Short Term Rentals — Prohibited

Short term rentals are not allowed in Fountain Valley. The City has never permitted them, and the City Council adopted Ordinance No. 1591 explicitly prohibiting short term rentals throughout the City on October 18, 2022. A short term rental (STR) is the rental of a residential dwelling or a portion thereof for a period of 30 consecutive days or less. There are no exemptions to the ordinance.

Once a violation has been verified, the City may issue fines up to $1,500 for the first violation, $3,000 for the second violation and $5,000 for the third and subsequent violations.

Source: https://www.fountainvalley.gov/1443/Short-Term-Rentals-STRs

## Housing Element and Regional Housing Needs (RHNA)

The Housing Element of the Fountain Valley General Plan sets the City's strategy for the maintenance and development of housing for existing and future residents. It covers the state-designated eight-year planning period of October 15, 2021, through October 15, 2029. The 2021-2029 Fountain Valley Housing Element is dated October 2022. Rules for Housing Elements are in California Government Code Sections 65580-65589.

Under the RHNA adopted by SCAG, Fountain Valley must plan to accommodate 4,839 housing units, including 2,093 lower income units, during 2021-2029: 1,307 very low (<50% AMI), 786 low (50-80% AMI), 834 moderate (80-120% AMI), and 1,912 above moderate (>120% AMI). The OC area median income (AMI) is listed as $103K. The City is required to allow for this growth and report to the State annually on progress, but is not required to construct the units itself. The City's appeal of its RHNA allocation was denied on January 19, 2021, and SCAG adopted the final RHNA allocation on March 4, 2021.

The page describes affordability as spending less than 33% of gross household income on housing costs (including utilities, principal and interest).

Source: https://www.fountainvalley.gov/1409/Housing-Element

## Homelessness Services — Central Cities Navigation Center

In June 2024, the City partnered with the cities of Westminster and Garden Grove to open the Central Cities Navigation Center (CCNC). It is a low-barrier transitional emergency housing facility that provides wrap-around services to unhoused individuals in Orange County's Central Service Planning Area. The CCNC offers thirteen beds for unhoused individuals that have a nexus to Fountain Valley.

The Police Department has two Community Resource Officers (CROs) who connect unhoused individuals with shelters, veterans' services, substance abuse programs, and mental health assistance. The City also partners with City Net Orange County for outreach and engagement services.

Source: https://www.fountainvalley.gov/1194/HomelessTransient-Concerns
