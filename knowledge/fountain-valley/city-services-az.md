## Beverage Container Recycling (CRV)

California's Beverage Container and Litter Reduction Act (1987) requires a deposit, called California Redemption Value (CRV), on certain recyclable bottles and cans at the point of purchase. The deposit is 5 cents on containers less than 24 ounces and 10 cents on containers 24 ounces or larger.

## Business Recycling Requirements (AB 341 and AB 1826)

California Assembly Bill 341 requires all commercial businesses and multi-family properties to recycle. It became effective July 1, 2012. Assembly Bill 1826 requires all businesses that generate a certain amount of organic waste (food waste) to develop a program to recycle it. Organic waste includes food scraps, vegetable trimmings, plate scrapings, and spoiled food.

Businesses can comply with any combination of these options: recycle food scraps, donate edible food to a food bank, or self-haul food scraps. The city's waste hauler, Republic, has a Food Recycling Program to help businesses meet AB 1826. In partnership with its waste hauler, the city provides complimentary technical assistance to help businesses comply.

## Curbside Recycling — Accepted and Prohibited Items

Republic Services provides recycling to single-family, multi-family, and business customers under its agreement with the city. Partial list of accepted materials: anything that tears; flattened cardboard; cereal boxes (liners removed) and other food boxes; computer, white, and mixed paper; empty aerosol cans; glass jars and bottles; metal food and soup cans; pie tins; plastic containers labeled #1-7; plastic shopping bags (place bags inside each other to create one large bundle); plastic water and soda bottles; soda cans; telephone books; tissue and soda boxes; wine and liquor bottles. Lightly rinse food residue from containers.

Do NOT place these in recycling bins (put them in the trash bin): carpeting, ceramic items, clothing (donate it), disposable diapers, drinking glasses, empty motor oil or containers, empty paint cans, mirrors, window glass, auto glass, pet waste, rubber latex items, and soiled paper towels or plates.

## Organic Waste Recycling (SB 1383)

California Senate Bill 1383 requires all residents and businesses to recycle organic materials. It applies to all residential homes, all multi-family properties (apartments, townhomes, homeowners associations, etc.), all commercial businesses (public and private), schools, and government entities. Certain businesses must also donate excess edible food to food recovery organizations.

Organic waste includes all food scraps (cooked meat, fish, poultry, bones, fruits, vegetables, bread, pasta, rice, and grains), food-soiled paper (pizza boxes, coffee filters, wet paper towels, soiled napkins), and yard waste (grass, leaves, small branches, plants, weeds, and garden trimmings). Recyclables (paper, cardboard, metal/aluminum cans, plastic, and glass) go in the recycle bin. Organic waste will be composted, and the city will host an annual compost giveaway for Fountain Valley residents.

Landscape companies may no longer legally dispose of green waste in trash containers or landfills and must comply with SB 1383.

Source: https://www.fountainvalley.gov/1523/Organic-Recycling

## Recycling Contamination Monitoring

Under state law, Republic Services staff conduct annual contamination monitoring reviews. Representatives in clearly identified safety vests review the contents of organic, recycle, and trash carts and dumpsters. If contamination is found, a tag is placed on the cart or dumpster identifying the incorrect items. The reviews are mandated by state law and occur annually. ## Reporting Water Pollution

To report water pollution, contact the city's Public Works Field Service Division at 714-593-4600, or the city's water pollution prevention hotline at 714-593-4433 during normal business hours. During non-business hours, call the Police Department at 714-593-4485. For additional information, call the Public Works Department at 714-593-4433.

## Stormwater Requirements for Businesses

All commercial and industrial businesses operating within Fountain Valley must implement pollution control measures and comply with Fountain Valley Title 14, Chapter 14.40, Stormwater Management and Discard Control. The city inspects commercial and industrial facilities and provides stormwater best management practices (BMP) education and enforcement, if necessary.

Facilities subject to the State General Industrial Storm Water Permit must file either a Notice of Intent or a No Exposure Certification. Contact the Public Works Department at 714-593-4433 for more information.

Source: https://www.fountainvalley.gov/181/Stormwater-Pollution
