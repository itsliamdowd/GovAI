## Fire Department Emergency Operations

Fountain Valley Fire Department is an all-risk emergency response agency. It is staffed, trained, and equipped to handle fires, hazardous conditions, hazardous materials spills, medical emergencies, rescue of entrapped persons, swift water rescues, traffic collisions, and wildland firefighting.

A total of 39 sworn personnel are assigned to field operations, with 13 firefighting and emergency medical personnel on duty 24 hours per day. Each engine and the ladder truck are staffed with a captain, an engineer and 2 firefighter-paramedics. A battalion chief serves as the on-duty command officer.

The department operates 2 front-line engines and 1 ladder truck with a 100 ft. aerial ladder, along with a battalion command vehicle. It also has 1 reserve engine and a California Office of Emergency Services engine, which is also used as a reserve engine.

Source: https://www.fountainvalley.gov/241/Emergency-Operations

## Emergency Medical Services and Ambulance

Fountain Valley Fire Department provides advanced life-support (ALS) paramedic and ambulance services. It has provided emergency medical care since the Department's formation in 1964 and began ALS paramedic service in 1975.

85% of the department's emergency responses are for medical aid. Of the four firefighters assigned to each fire company, two are California licensed paramedics and two are certified emergency medical technicians. Ambulance billing rates are posted on the City's Emergency Medical Services page as "City of Fountain Valley: AMBULANCE BILLING RATES."

## Fire Inspection Fraud Prevention — What Real Inspections Look Like

The Fountain Valley Fire Department conducts annual Fire Life Safety and business license inspections at every business address in Fountain Valley. Inspections are done by Fire Suppression personnel from the fire stations, fire prevention personnel from Fire Administration, or Fire Inspection Reserve Seniors Taskforce (FIRST) participants.

Personnel always wear an official fire department uniform with Fountain Valley Fire Department badges and a photo I.D. card. Inspectors wear an official blue uniform with a FVFD badge on the left chest, a FVFD patch on the left sleeve, and proper City identification. Personnel do NOT service fire protection equipment such as fire extinguishers, and do NOT ask for money or leave an invoice for payment during the inspection. They always leave a copy of the Fire Life Safety inspection form. The department does not use private companies to inspect businesses. If billed, you will be billed through the City at a later date.

To confirm that an inspector is a City employee, call 714-593-4436. You can always refuse service and request a Fountain Valley Fire Department inspector visit your business.

Source: https://www.fountainvalley.gov/1204/Fire-Inspection-Fraud-Prevention

## Fire Inspection Checklist for Businesses

During inspections the Fire Department checks that:
- Every business has access to a fire extinguisher within 75' from any point in the building.
- Fire extinguishers are inspected annually and serviced every 6 years.
- Kitchen hood systems are serviced semi-annually (every 6 months).
- Electrical panels are clear of storage, accessible, and have no void spaces.
- Exit doors are operable and free of storage.

Fire protection equipment must be serviced by a private, state licensed company. The company will place a tag on the equipment with the date of service noted if the system is found to be in compliance.

Source: https://www.fountainvalley.gov/1204/Fire-Inspection-Fraud-Prevention

## Protecting Your Business When Hiring Fire Protection Companies

Business owners should not let anyone inspect their fire extinguishers, sprinkler system, hood system, or any other protection device unless they have an agreement with a specific fire protection service. Never sign a blank invoice on the spot. Be cautious of service companies that show up without being contacted.

Ask to see proper identification or state licensing credentials, and refuse fire protection services if these cannot be provided:
- C-10 or C-16 License (based on the type of work) from the California Contractors State Licensing Board
- Business license for the City of Fountain Valley
- Picture I.D. from the California State Fire Marshal's office

Know exactly what work is to be performed and receive a detailed written estimate before consenting to services. Request that the contractor leave all of the old parts that were replaced and billed to you. Receive and retain an invoice, and do not pay cash. Contact Fire or Police for assistance. Call 714-593-4436 for any assistance or questions.

Source: https://www.fountainvalley.gov/1204/Fire-Inspection-Fraud-Prevention

## Fire Development Impact Fee — Overview

Effective October 19, 2026, the City of Fountain Valley will implement a new Fire Development Impact Fee. The City Council approved the program at its meeting held on August 18, 2026. Revenue from the fee goes into a dedicated capital facilities account and is used exclusively to fund the construction and related capital costs of replacing Fire Station No. 1. Fire Station No. 2 will remain at its current location. Fee revenue is tracked, reported annually to the city council, and subject to five-year findings requirements under Government Code Section 66001(d).

Contact: City of Fountain Valley, Fire Department, Fire Marshal Thang Nguyen, 1200 Slater Avenue, Fountain Valley, CA 92708, (714) 593-4436.

## Fire Development Impact Fee — Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs)

ADUs under 750 Sq. Ft. are fully exempt from the fire development impact fee. For ADUs of 750 Sq. Ft. or larger, the fee is calculated proportionally on a case-by-case basis: ADU Impact Fee = Primary Unit Impact Fee × (ADU Square Feet / Primary Unit Square Feet).

Sample: a primary unit base fee of $1,107.58 and an 800 sq. ft. ADU with a 2,000 sq. ft. primary unit gives a ratio of 0.40, so $1,107.58 × 0.40 = $443.03 total ADU impact fee.

## Emergency Preparedness — Kit and Family Plan

The American Red Cross recommends at minimum an emergency kit with water (one gallon per person, per day), non-perishable food, a first aid kit, and medications. A three-day supply of water and food is recommended for evacuation, and a two-week supply if you stay in your home. Provide for pets as well. Complete checklists are at http://www.readyoc.org or http://preparesocal.org/.

Create a family disaster plan including an evacuation plan and a communication plan. Pick two places to meet: right outside your home, and outside your neighborhood in case you can't return home. Ask an out-of-state friend to be your "family contact." Make sure at least one household member is trained in first aid and CPR and knows how to use an automated external defibrillator (AED).

Source: https://www.fountainvalley.gov/246/Emergency-Preparedness
