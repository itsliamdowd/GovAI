# Housing & Rent Control

## Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers

The Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers program extends rental subsidies to very-low-income households and seniors who spend more than 30% of their income on rent. The subsidy equals the difference between the excess of 30% of monthly income and actual rent. The program is administered by the Orange County Housing Authority (OCHA), http://www.ochousing.org/. The city's page links to a landlord video and a tenant video about the program.

Source: https://avcity.org/209/Housing-Information-Programs

## OCHA Special Needs Groups Rental Assistance Programs

The Orange County Housing Authority also administers Special Housing Programs (http://www.ochousing.org/special). These provide rental assistance to special needs groups, including families whose children are at risk of being placed in out-of-home care, disabled persons, homeless persons, and seniors. The programs are:
- Family Unification: Section 8 assistance to families whose children are at risk of being placed in out-of-home care or delayed in returning from care because of the families' inadequate housing.
- Aftercare for Disabled: Section 8 assistance specifically for disabled applicants.
- Shelter Plus Care: Section 8 assistance to homeless persons with disabilities.
- HOPE IV-Elderly Independence: Section 8 assistance to frail seniors.
- Veterans Affairs Supportive Housing (VASH): Section 8 assistance to veterans.

Source: https://avcity.org/209/Housing-Information-Programs

## Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) Program

CDBG is a federal program that funds local community development needs, including housing improvements, safe and livable neighborhoods, and expanded economic opportunities, primarily for low- and moderate-income residents. HUD defines low- and moderate-income households as those earning 80% or less of the county's median income. CDBG-funded programs must meet at least one national objective: benefit low- and moderate-income residents; prevent or eliminate slums or blight; or address an urgent need that threatens the health or safety of the community and cannot be met with other resources.

Source: https://avcity.org/388/Community-Development-Block-Grant-CDBG-P

## What Counts as "Affordable" Housing (Housing Element FAQ)

Per the city's 2021 Housing Element FAQ (dated July 2020), housing is considered "affordable" when total housing cost, including utilities, is no more than 30% of a family's gross income. State law uses five income categories based on county median income: Extremely low (up to 30%), Very low (31-50%), Low (51-80%), Moderate (81-120%), and Above moderate (over 120%). The Housing Element is part of the General Plan and must be updated every eight years. The 2021-2029 cycle is the sixth.

Source: https://avcity.org/DocumentCenter/View/1626/Frequently-Asked-Questions-PDF

## Lead Hazards in Older Rental and Owned Homes

If a home or apartment building was built before 1978, the original paint probably contains lead. Homeowners concerned about lead in paint and soil may hire a California Dept of Public Health-certified inspector, or collect samples and send them to an EPA-accredited lab. In pre-1978 buildings, prevent lead dust by controlling moisture and immediately cleaning up paint chips. When remodeling, wear a NIOSH-certified respirator, lay down plastic sheeting, and keep children and pregnant women away from the work area.

The EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting Rule requires contractors, property managers, maintenance workers, painters, and other specialty trades who disturb lead-based paint in pre-1978 homes, child care facilities, and schools to be certified and follow specific work practices.
