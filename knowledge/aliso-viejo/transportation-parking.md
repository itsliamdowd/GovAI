## Senior Mobility Program — Service Details

The City of Aliso Viejo and the Orange County Transportation Authority (OCTA) partner to provide a Senior Mobility Program (SMP) for Aliso Viejo residents age 60 and older. The City contracts with Orange County Yellow Cooperative for scheduled and on-demand, curb-to-curb transportation. Trips go to and from the Sea Country Senior and Community Center in Laguna Niguel and the Florence Sylvester Senior Center in Laguna Hills. They also go to Aliso Viejo Ranch, Aliso Viejo City Hall, Iglesia Park Community Center, and Aliso Viejo Library.

The service is available from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m, Monday through Friday, excluding holidays. Vehicles are wheelchair accessible. There is no cost for Aliso Viejo residents. The service is only for transportation to and from designated senior centers. It is not for medical appointments, shopping, errands, or social engagements. For more information, call 949-425-2589.

## Senior Mobility Program — Eligibility and How to Register

The program is open to Aliso Viejo residents, ages 60 and older. Applicants must show proof of residency and age. Residents aged 60 and older or their designated caregiver (family member/Power of Attorney-POA) may apply. A fully completed registration packet is required. After the application is processed, OC Yellow Cooperative contacts the applicant to schedule transportation. On-demand services will arrive within 60 minutes of requested time. For scheduling questions on a Standing Order, call Melissa Gomez at 714-427-2555 x307, mobile 714-559-8300. For On-Demand service, call 1-877-538-7959.

## OCTA Access Transportation Program (Paratransit)

This is a shared-ride, curb-to-curb paratransit service for qualified applicants. It is for people whose physical or cognitive limitations prevent them from using OCTA's fixed route bus service. Access service is provided within a ¾ mile of, and during similar hours as OCTA's regular fixed route service. Trips must be scheduled no less than 24 hours before the requested time. Riders are picked up within a quoted 30-minute pick-up window, and ride times generally do not exceed 90 minutes for each trip.

For eligibility criteria or to apply, contact the Access Eligibility Section at 714-560-5956, ex. 2, or visit the OCTA website. TTD (hearing impaired phone) information access: 714-560-5474. OCTA reservations: 877-628-2232. TTD reservation access: 800-564-4232.

## Age Well Senior Services Non-Emergency Medical Transportation

This program provides transportation for people who cannot drive or who are cutting down on long-distance or night driving. It also serves people with temporary and permanent disabilities. ## Recreational and Oversized Vehicle Parking on Public Streets

Under Municipal Code Section 9.02.030, an oversized vehicle is any vehicle with a length in excess of 24 feet, a width in excess of 84 inches, or a weight in excess of 10,000 pounds. Under Municipal Code Section 9.04.090, the maximum parking time limit for any vehicle upon a public street is 72 hours. Recreational/oversized vehicles may not be parked and left standing on any public street or highway in the city between 2 and 6 a.m. on two consecutive days. They also may not be left standing between 6 and 2 a.m. for more than 12 consecutive hours on two consecutive days. Exceptions apply under certain temporary circumstances (loading/unloading or when performing emergency repairs).

The storage or long-term parking of recreational vehicles (RV) or motor homes is not allowed on any public street or highway within the city limits. For parking on a private street, contact your homeowner association (HOA).

## Oversized Vehicle Parking — Ordinance No. 2025-247

The City Council adopted Ordinance No. 2025-247 at its September 17, 2025 Regular Meeting. It amends Sections 9.02.030 and 9.04.090 of the Aliso Viejo Municipal Code. The definition of "oversized vehicle" now specifically includes recreational vehicles of a width in excess of 84 inches, measured at the widest portion of the body not including mirrors or other extensions. "Park" or "leave standing" includes failing to move a vehicle at least one-tenth of a mile as measured on the odometer during the time period specified. Without an odometer reading, it means failing to move the vehicle at least 100 feet, measured from axle to axle, from its last marked location.

Under Section 9.04.090, oversized vehicles may be parked on city streets only under these conditions:
- While actively loading or unloading persons and/or property, including preparing the vehicle for travel or storage, and for a period not to exceed 12 hours during which the vehicle cannot reasonably be taken on the road or moved to a place of otherwise lawful storage.
- When parked in connection with, or in aid of, a service to or on a property in the block where the vehicle is parked.
- While performing repairs of an emergency nature on the vehicle itself.

The ordinance does not apply to authorized emergency vehicles. It takes effect 30 days after adoption and only once signs or markings giving adequate notice have been placed. It is available for review in the office of the City Clerk, 12 Journey, Aliso Viejo, California 92656, Monday through Thursday, 7:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. and every other Friday, 7:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.

## Neighborhood Traffic Calming — Request Process

Only roadways identified as local streets per the City of Aliso Viejo General Plan or the Caltrans California Road System—Functional Classification Map are eligible for traffic calming. Residents must document a concern or request (such as speed humps) on the resident survey form in Appendix A of the guidelines. Residents in a neighborhood with a Homeowner's Association (HOA) send surveys and complaints to the HOA, which reports them to the Public Works Department. Neighborhoods without an HOA need a neighborhood traffic committee of one to three residents to communicate with Public Works.

Public Works then does a preliminary review and, if the street is eligible, collects data such as speed surveys, collision history, and traffic volumes. Phase I improvements are non-physical (signing and striping). Phase II improvements are physical (such as speed humps) and are considered only after Phase I improvements have been in place for six (6) months. Phase II requires a petition with majority support (67%) of all affected households in the project area and 100% written support from homeowners directly affected by the device. Removing a device is considered only after it has been in place a minimum of twelve (12) months and requires a petition with majority support (67%). Stop signs are treated as traffic control devices, not traffic calming measures.
