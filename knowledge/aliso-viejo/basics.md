## About Aliso Viejo — Incorporation and Form of Government

Aliso Viejo became Orange County's 34th city when it incorporated on July 1, 2001. Voters passed the incorporation initiative with 93.3% in favor. The About page also says that on March 6, 2001, more than 90% of voters decided to make Aliso Viejo a city. The City's tagline is "Live Life Inspired."

Aliso Viejo is a general law city. It develops policies and procedures in accordance with California State law.

Source: https://avcity.org/303/About-Aliso-Viejo

## Contracted City Services and Other Providers

Aliso Viejo contracts for many of its services. These include Building and Safety, Code Enforcement, Engineering, Fire, Legal, Police, Public Works, Street Maintenance and Street Sweeping. City-contracted services also include animal care and trash pick up.

Library services are provided by the Orange County Library System. Aliso Viejo Community Association (AVCA) administers landscape maintenance of most slopes, medians and parks. The exception is Iglesia Park, which the City owns.

## Selection of Mayor and Mayor Pro Tem

The Mayor and Mayor Pro Tem are selected at the first meeting of each December. They serve a one-year term beginning January 1. Sitting Council Members fill these positions by a vote of the majority of members. Voters do not directly elect them.

## City Council Elections and Council Districts

Aliso Viejo is governed by a five-member City Council elected by district. Council Members serve staggered four-year terms and must reside within the district they represent. Regular municipal elections are held concurrently with statewide elections on the first Tuesday in November of even-numbered years. Voters may only vote for candidates running within their Council District.

The page links a current district map (https://avcity.org/DocumentCenter/View/4844/District-Map-2023). It also links an interactive "Find Your District" map where residents can enter their residential address.

## Voter Registration and Voting Options

Register to vote or update voter information online at https://registertovote.ca.gov/. Voter registration forms are also available at public libraries, post offices and City Hall. To receive a registration form by mail, call the Orange County Registrar of Voters office at 714-567-7600. Completed forms can be hand-delivered to the Registrar's office at 1300 S. Grand Ave. Santa Ana, or dropped off in any U.S. mailbox. No postage is necessary. You must be registered at least 15 days prior to the election in which you intend to vote, or complete the Same-Day Voter Registration process.

Under the Orange County Registrar of Voters election model, voting begins the month before any election day and every voter receives a vote-by-mail ballot. Voters can mail the ballot, vote in person at a vote center, or deliver the ballot to a ballot drop box, vote center or the Registrar of Voters office. More information is at http://www.ocvote.com.

## Political Campaign Signs — Rules and Reporting

Political signs may be displayed on private property with the permission of the property owner or occupant. They are prohibited on public property and in the public right-of-way. This includes streets, medians, sidewalks, parkways, public buildings, traffic signs and signals, utility poles, utility boxes, streetlight poles, fire hydrants, public trees and landscaping, and bridges. The City may remove signs placed on public property without notice.

Signs on AVCA-owned property need prior approval from the AVCA Architectural Review Committee, which meets on the fourth Tuesday of each month. Candidates submit the AVCA Political Sign Request Form (https://avcity.org/DocumentCenter/View/5062).

To retrieve signs removed by the City, call Public Works at (949) 425-2530. For signs removed by AVCA, call (949) 243-7750. To report a political sign on public property or one that creates a safety concern, call Code Enforcement at (949) 425-2525. For signs along state highways, call Caltrans District 12 at (657) 328-6000.

Source: https://avcity.org/156/Elections

## City Clerk's Office — Functions and Contact

Government Services/City Clerk includes the City Clerk's Office, Risk Management, Public Information Office (PIO), social media and Emergency Support services. The City Clerk is the City's Elections Official, Political Reform Act Filing Officer, Custodian of Records, and Compliance Officer. The Clerk also serves as Clerk of the Council and keeps records of all City Council proceedings. The Clerk ensures that meetings, agendas, minutes, reports, public notices, resolutions and ordinances are prepared, posted and published in compliance with legal statutes, including the Brown Act.

Risk Management coordinates the receipt, investigation and resolution of liability claims filed against the City. The Local Appointments List (Maddy Act posting) is available as a PDF on the page.

Contact: 12 Journey, Suite 100, Aliso Viejo, CA 92656. Phone: 949-425-2505. Fax: 949-425-3899. The Director of Government Services is Maria Kendig.

## Public Records Requests

Submit a Public Records Act request online at https://portal.laserfiche.com/s0923/forms/RecordsRequest, by email to the City Clerk, or by mail or in person at City of Aliso Viejo, City Clerk's Office, 12 Journey, Aliso Viejo, CA 92656. For questions, call the City Clerk's Office at 949-425-2505.

Members of the public may inspect disclosable City records at no charge. When copies are requested, applicable duplication and, when necessary, mailing costs may be charged to the requestor. Requests that need a broader search or coordination among departments are coordinated by the City Clerk's Office.

The City does not maintain marriage licenses, birth and death certificates, fictitious business name filings, passports, or documents recorded with the County. Contact the Orange County Clerk-Recorder (https://www.ocrecorder.com/) for these.
