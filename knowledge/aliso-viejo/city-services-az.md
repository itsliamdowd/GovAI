## Recreation & Community Services — Department Overview

The Recreation & Community Services Department offers Aliso Viejo residents programs, special events, and cultural and educational experiences. It also manages the City's Community Connection and Enews. The department oversees operations at the Aliso Viejo Ranch, Iglesia Community Center and Park, the Aliso Viejo Aquatic Center, and the Aliso Viejo Center. The City owns and manages Iglesia Park and Vista Park. All others are maintained by Aliso Viejo Community Association (AVCA).

Contact: 100 Park Avenue, Aliso Viejo, CA 92656. Phone: 949-425-2550. Registration is available online, and a Registration Form & Information (PDF) is posted on the page.

## Senior Mobility Program (Transportation)

The City and the Orange County Transportation Authority (OCTA) provide a Senior Mobility Program (SMP) for Aliso Viejo residents age 60 and older. The City contracts with Orange County Yellow Cooperative for scheduled and on-demand, curb-to-curb transportation. Destinations are the Sea Country Senior and Community Center in Laguna Niguel, the Florence Sylvester Senior Center in Laguna Hills, Aliso Viejo Ranch, Aliso Viejo City Hall, Iglesia Park Community Center, and Aliso Viejo Library. Service is available from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m, Monday through Friday, excluding holidays.

Vehicles are wheelchair accessible. There is no cost for Aliso Viejo residents. The service is only for transportation to and from designated senior centers, not for medical appointments, shopping, errands, or social engagements. For more information, call 949-425-2589. An Information and Registration Packet (PDF) is available on the page.

## Stormwater Program

Stormwater and urban runoff flow untreated through the storm drain system into local waterways such as creeks, rivers and beaches. Anything thrown, swept or poured into the street, gutter or catch basin can flow directly into waterways. This includes trash, pet waste, cigarette butts, motor oil, anti-freeze, pesticide and fertilizer runoff, paint rinsed in the gutter, and toxic household chemicals.

The City has a pollution prevention program under its local implementation plan (LIP). The page links to the Irrigation Run-off Prohibition (PDF), How to Prevent and Report Sewage Spills, Pet Care Tips, RV Dumping Tips, guidance for new development and redevelopment projects (including the BMP Design Manual and Construction Best Management Practices), and best management practices for mobile businesses such as automobile detailers, carpet cleaners, and pressure washing.

## Animal Services and Pet Licensing

Since January 2009, the Mission Viejo Animal Services Center has handled animal services for the City of Aliso Viejo. The facility is at 28095 Hillcrest, Mission Viejo, CA 92692. It provides pet licensing, picks up injured wildlife, impounds stray dogs/cats, issues citations, and shelters homeless animals. Dogs, cats, and rabbits are available for adoption. Phone: 949-470-3045.

All dogs over the age of four (4) months in Aliso Viejo are required to have and wear a current license tag. Cat licenses are recommended but not required by law. A pet's license is proof of a valid rabies vaccination. Pet licensing is handled by Mission Viejo Animal Services: call 949-470-3045 or email petlicensing@cityofmissionviejo.org.

Source: https://www.avcity.org/153/Animal-Services

## Nuisance Wildlife and Coyote Sightings

For nuisance wildlife, see the California Department of Fish and Wildlife's website or call the enforcement line at 562-598-1032.

Immediately contact Mission Viejo Animal Services at 949-470-3045 if:
- Your pet is attacked or killed.
- A coyote is on or living close to your property.
- The coyote is acting unusually aggressive toward a person.
- You have been bitten by a coyote.
- The coyote is injured or appears sick.

Mission Viejo Animal Services evaluates each report to determine whether the coyote is behaving unusually or is a threat to human safety. If you encounter a coyote, keep your distance.

## Financial Reports

The City's Financial Reports page covers three report types:
- The Annual Comprehensive Financial Report contains the City's financial statements, including the Independent Auditors' Report and the Management's Discussion and Analysis (MD&A). It also includes a citywide valuation of infrastructure assets.
- The Annual Report of Financial Transactions is prepared for the State Controller's Office. Under Government Code section 40804, the City must publish a summary of it.
- The Single Audit Report covers financial statements and federal award expenditures. It is legally required in any fiscal year the City meets or exceeds the federal expenditure threshold.
