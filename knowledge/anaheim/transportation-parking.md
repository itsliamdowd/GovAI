## Parking Enforcement — Common Violations

The Anaheim Police Department provides parking enforcement. Vehicles may not park in red curb zones (AMC 14.32.370.0104), in designated fire lanes (CVC 22500.1), or within 7.5 feet of a fire hydrant (AMC 14.32.035). They may not park in tow-away or "No Stopping Anytime" zones (AMC 14.32.189). Vehicles may not block public or private driveways (CVC 22500(e)) or sidewalks (CVC 22500(f)). They may not double park (CVC 22500(h)), obstruct traffic (CVC 22500(g)), or stop or park in alleys (AMC 14.32.030.020).

Other common violations are parking in permit-only areas (AMC 14.32.450.010), parking without a required permit (AMC 14.32.178), and parking in excess of 72 hours (AMC 14.32.191). Violating street sweeping restrictions (AMC 14.32.500) is also a common violation. Additional restrictions apply to oversized vehicles (AMC 14.32.206) and overnight commercial vehicle parking (AMC 14.32.200). Current vehicle registration is required (CVC 4000(a)(1)). Violations may result in citations and/or vehicle impound.

Parking in designated accessible parking spaces without proper authorization is prohibited (CVC 22507.8(c)). Disabled parking permit misuse is covered by AMC 14.32.430.

## Pay or Contest a Parking Citation

Parking citations issued in Anaheim are processed through pticket.com. The Anaheim Police Department does not process parking citation payments or appeals directly. To pay or contest a citation, you need either the citation number or the vehicle license plate number.

California Vehicle Code Section 40215 allows parking citations to be contested if the citation was issued in error. The review process has three levels: Initial Review, Administrative Hearing, and Civil Appeal. Initial Reviews must be requested within 21 calendar days of the citation issue date. Administrative Hearings must be requested within 21 calendar days of the Initial Review decision. Civil Appeals must be filed within 30 calendar days of the Administrative Hearing decision. Forms, payment options, and contesting instructions are available through pticket.com.

Source: https://pd.anaheim.net/310/Pay-or-Contest-a-Parking-Citation

## ARTIC Overnight Parking Authorization

Overnight parking at ARTIC (Anaheim Regional Transportation Intermodal Center) is only for ARTIC users. It is limited to a maximum of 14 days, and requests of more than 14 days are not accepted. Overnight hotel parking is not permitted, and violators are subject to tow. The online form authorizes one vehicle to park overnight in a designated area at the owner's sole risk. Vehicles left over the requested days may be impounded at the vehicle owner's expense.

The form asks you to check one option: "Frequent Overnight Parker and Monthly Transit Pass Holder" or "One Time Only Overnight Transit Parker". It requires your name, phone, email, mailing address, and the dates the vehicle will be left. It also requires the vehicle make, model, license plate and color, plus your transit provider and ticket number. Frequent overnight parkers not displaying the permit may be towed at the vehicle owner's expense. The permit does not exempt you from any other parking restrictions.

## E-Bike Classes and Where to Ride

California law divides e-bikes into three classes. Anaheim Municipal Code 13.08.120 requires riders to use designated bike paths where available.

- **Class 1:** The motor assists only while pedaling, up to 20 mph. There is no minimum age and no driver's license or license plate is required. Riders under 18 must wear a helmet.
- **Class 2:** The motor can propel the bike without pedaling (for example, a throttle), up to 20 mph. There is no minimum age and no driver's license or license plate is required. Riders under 18 must wear a helmet.
- **Class 3:** The motor assists only while pedaling, up to 28 mph. The bike must have a speedometer. The minimum age is 16 and a helmet is required for riders and passengers. A driver's license and license plate are not required.

Class 1 and Class 2 e-bikes may use bike lanes and sharrows/roadways, but not freeways. They may be ridden on sidewalks in Anaheim with restrictions. Sidewalk riding is allowed except in business districts or where posted, and riders must yield to pedestrians. E-bikes may not be operated on sidewalks using throttle-only power. Class 3 e-bikes are not allowed on sidewalks in Anaheim, and restrictions often apply on parks and trails. Check posted signs before riding on parks and trails.

## E-Bike Rules of the Road and Electric Motorcycles

E-bike riders must travel in the same direction as vehicle traffic and ride on the right side of the roadway when required. They must use designated bike lanes when required. Exceptions may apply when passing, preparing for a left turn, avoiding hazardous conditions, or approaching a location where a right turn is permitted. Riders must obey traffic signs and signals. They must keep at least one hand free to control the handlebars when carrying items. They may not carry a passenger unless the bike has a passenger seat. It is illegal to operate a bicycle or e-bike under the influence of alcohol or drugs. Modifying an e-bike in a way that changes its speed capability or classification may affect whether it still legally qualifies as an e-bike.

Electric two-wheeled vehicles that do not meet the Class 1, 2 or 3 definition may be classified as motorized bicycles, motor-driven cycles or motorcycles. They may require a driver's license or motorcycle endorsement, registration and license plates, a DOT-compliant motorcycle helmet, and required lighting, mirrors and brakes.
