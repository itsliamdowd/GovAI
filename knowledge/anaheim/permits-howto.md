## Building Permit Plan Review — How to Submit

The Building Division is currently under the provisions of the 2025 Edition of the California Building Standards Codes Cycle. To submit a project for plan review, use the "Plans Required for Submittal" handout to find the number of plan sets required for your scope of work. You also need a completed Plan Check Application form.

A second cycle of plan review before permit issuance is a resubmittal, not a revision. Resubmittals require four things:
1. Old plan check sets.
2. New corrected construction sets.
3. A plan check comment response letter.
4. No clouding of corrections. Instead, provide a narrative identifying the changes.

If plans are approved and the permit is issued, a later change is a revision. All changes to the plans MUST be clouded for a revision.

## Construction Plan Requirements and Over-the-Counter Review

Construction plans must clearly indicate the location, nature and extent of the project. They must also show in detail how the project complies with the Building Codes. To be complete, submittal documents must contain all information required by California Building Code Section 107. Architectural plans include these items:
- Site plan.
- Floor and roof plans.
- Elevations and cross-sections.
- Means of egress analysis.
- Green Building Code requirements.
- Title 24 energy calculations.

Structural plans include these items:
- Foundation plan.
- Framing plans.
- Roof framing plans.
- Structural details.
- Structural calculations.

Plans must be prepared by a qualified person. Building & Safety staff does not prepare plans. For most project types, the plans must be prepared by a California Licensed Architect or Engineer. Submitting incomplete, inaccurate or non-compliant plans could delay the permit process and incur additional plan review fees.

Smaller projects needing no more than 30 minutes of plan review can typically be reviewed over-the-counter. These project types are normally reviewed over-the-counter:
- Residential patio covers.
- Swimming pools.
- Wall signs.
- Re-roofing.

## Accessory Dwelling Units (ADU/JADU) — Three Approval Paths

An ADU is a residential dwelling unit with complete independent living facilities for one or more persons. It may be detached or attached. It must be on a lot with a proposed or existing primary residence. A JADU must be built within an existing single-family residence and requires a deed restriction for owner-occupancy. The provisions for both are in Anaheim Municipal Code Section 18.38.015.

There are three ways to get an ADU approved:
1. **Standard ADU/JADU submittal and review.** You work with a design professional to prepare plans, then go through the standard building review process.
2. **ADU Pre-Approved Plan Catalogue.** These plans were already reviewed and approved by the City. Some may still need a design professional to finalize items such as the site plan, Title 24, structural details and a soils report.
3. **ADU Express.** This program lets qualified single-family properties complete the ADU review process within 1-3 days. Homeowners choose a pre-approved detached ADU from the catalogue. Use the ADU Express Eligibility Tool and enter your address to check eligibility.

## Business License — Fees, Penalties and Changes

A minimum charge of $37 is required for processing any initial or renewed license application. It is in addition to the business license tax, home occupation permit fee, or regulatory permit fee, and will not be prorated. The processing fee is non-refundable.

A business that operates without first obtaining a license, or that fails to pay a renewal, pays a penalty of 10% or $10, whichever is greater, for each month delinquent. It also pays an additional amount equal to 1-1/2% thereof per month, calculated from the 1st day of delinquency to the date of payment.

A license is valid for 1 year. A renewal is automatically forwarded 30 days before the expiration date. A processing fee of $35 is required for a change of address or a change of business name if the license is current. To start either change, call 714-765-5194 or visit the Business License Counter.

A business license is not transferable, and it is terminated when ownership changes. The new owner must get their own license. All licenses must be displayed on the premises. A business with no fixed address must carry the license on the person and present it on request.

## Business License — Who Needs One and Before You Apply

Any individual, corporation, sole proprietor, partnership or other entity that wishes to conduct a business within the City must secure a business license. A separate license is required for each branch or location of business. Exemptions are limited to businesses specifically exempted by State or Federal statute (Anaheim Municipal Code Section 3.04.130).

Before applying, contact the Business License Division and ask the Planning Department about zoning requirements for your business type and location. You must also comply with all City, County and State regulations for zoning, building, health and safety. You can download an application online. You can also call 714-765-5194 to have one mailed, faxed or emailed. To confirm your business is an allowable use at your desired location, contact the Planning Department at 714-765-5139.

## Home-Based Business and Home Occupation Permit

Home-based businesses apply by filling out and submitting the business license application. The standard business license fee applies. There is no additional cost for the home occupation permit.

Standards include these (the full list is in Anaheim Municipal Code Section 18.38.130):
- Only the residents may be employed in the home occupation.
- The business shall not cause a significant increase of pedestrian or vehicular traffic.
- The business shall not create excess noise or odors nor adversely affect the property of another person.
- No exterior modifications may be made that change the look of the residence to non-residential.

Per the business FAQ, every business conducted from a home must have both a city business license and a home occupation permit. The home occupation permit is obtained from the Planning Department. It must be submitted with your business license application. Planning reviews the business type to ensure it is allowed in a residential area.
