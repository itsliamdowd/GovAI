## California Building Standards Code — 2025 Edition

Beginning on January 1, 2026, the El Cajon Community Development Department will follow State law and enforce the 2025 Edition of California Building Standards Codes (also known as Title 24 of the California Codes of Regulations). The State adopts new codes every three years. California Health and Safety Code Sections 17922, 17958 and 18941.5 require that the latest edition apply to local construction 180 days after publication.

Architects, engineers, designers, developers, owners and contractors must be familiar with the codes in effect at the time of plan submittal. As required by State law, the Community Development Department cannot approve projects that do not comply with the codes in effect at the time of plan submittal.

## Communications Division — 911 and Dispatch

The El Cajon Police Communications Division is the only Public Safety Answering Point (PSAP) for accessing police, fire and medical services within the City of El Cajon. It is staffed 24 hours a day, 365 days a year. The 911 system uses the Automated Location Index (ALI) to cross-reference the phone number of incoming 911 calls to determine the caller's location. It can track mobile phones as well as landline phones.

Public Safety Communications Operators answer 911 and business line calls. Police Dispatchers dispatch calls for service. The page also links to 911 Information, 911 for Kids, and Who Should I Call?

## Police Investigations — Case Status and Contacting a Detective

The Investigations Division telephone number is (619) 579-3320. Call it to find out the investigative status of a crime report, including whether the case was assigned to a detective and whether any leads have been developed. To give a detective additional information, call the Investigation Division, and get the crime case number from the Records Division before calling.

Crime cases take one to fourteen days to process, depending on severity and workload, and the entire process may take several weeks. Detective sergeants decide which cases are investigated, based on witness and evidentiary solvability factors. All crime reports are reviewed by detectives to link series related crimes. A crime victim who no longer wants to prosecute can generally file a "No Prosecution" form, which cancels the investigative and prosecution process.

## Police Investigations — Units

- District Investigations Unit (DIU): property crimes including general theft, auto theft, fraud/identity theft, forgery, arson, explosive devices and vandalism.
- Major Crimes Unit (MCU): homicides, missing persons and all other crimes of violence.
- Special Investigations Unit (SIU): gang, narcotic, vice, and fugitive investigations.
- Family Protection Unit (FPU): domestic violence cases and crimes against children. It also handles sex related crimes, registration of narcotic and sex offenders, diversion programs for juvenile offenders, and resources for domestic violence victims.
- Gang Street Team (GST): one Sergeant and four Officers working to prevent and combat criminal gang activity.

## Police Patrol Division

Approximately 60 uniformed officers and 10 supervisors work around the clock, seven days a week, and a lieutenant watch commander heads each shift. The city is divided into five beats, and officers are responsible for handling all crimes within their beat. Patrol officers take crime reports, conduct traffic enforcement, respond to calls for service, make arrests, resolve disputes, and work to prevent crime.

## Metropolitan Division — Community Programs and Trespasser Form

The Metropolitan Division includes the Special Operations Unit (SOU), the Traffic Unit, and the Special Enforcement Unit (SEU). SOU uses uniformed bicycle officers to patrol the downtown area and city parks. It provides a School Resource Officer to each of the four major high school campuses in the city. The Crime Free Multi-Housing Program (CFMH) was implemented in 1997 and has over one hundred participating properties. The Crime Prevention Specialist organizes Neighborhood Watch programs.

For assistance with removal of trespassers, download the Illegal Lodging Enforcement Letter, print it, and submit the signed copy to the Community Policing Division.

## Traffic Division

The Police Traffic Division works to reduce vehicle and pedestrian accidents, educate the public through enforcement, and investigate collisions. Its duties include DUI Checkpoints, Car Seat Inspections, and assistance with public events such as the El Cajon Grand Prix Bike Race and the Mother Goose Parade. Motorcycle Officers, Accident Investigators and Parking Enforcement Specialists address traffic issues throughout the city.

## Reserve Officer Program

Reserve Officers may work as an additional officer in a patrol car or as a single person car, depending on training and certification. Duties include patrol by car, bicycle, and on foot, directing traffic, investigating complaints, guarding and transporting prisoners, writing reports, and issuing traffic and parking tickets.

Applicants must be 21 years of age, reside in San Diego County, possess a high school diploma or GED, pass a physical agility test, be in good health, and possess a California Driver's License. They must also pass a psychological exam, a polygraph test, a medical exam, and a background investigation. For more information, call Lieutenant Joe Crawford at 619-579-3319.

Source: https://www.elcajon.gov/your-government/departments/police/your-police-department/reserve-officers

## Retired Senior Volunteer Patrol (RSVP)

RSVP functions include:
- Enforcing handicapped parking regulations
- Conducting vacation home security checks
- Performing traffic control
- Patrolling business districts, parking lots, and school zones
- Calling and/or visiting homebound seniors (YANA Program)

Applicants must be 50 years of age or older, be a US citizen, successfully complete a background investigation, possess medical and automobile insurance, be physically able to perform all patrol-related duties, and possess a valid California Driver's License.

Participation requires a minimum of one six-hour shift per week, a minimum commitment of one year, attendance at monthly training meetings, periodic refresher training, and a one-week academy. To join, contact the RSVP Coordinator.
