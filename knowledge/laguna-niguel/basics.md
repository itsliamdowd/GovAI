## About Laguna Niguel

Laguna Niguel is a 14.72 square mile planned community in South Orange County, California, halfway between Los Angeles and San Diego. It is surrounded by the cities of Aliso Viejo, Dana Point, Laguna Beach, Laguna Hills, Mission Viejo and San Juan Capistrano. It is a General Law city with municipal elections in November of even numbered years.

Existing and planned use of the city's 9,456 acres includes 3,549 acres residential, 276 acres commercial, 223 acres mixed uses, 222 acres public/institutional facilities and 3,650 acres designated to parks and open space. 72% of Laguna Niguel's 24,891 housing units are owner occupied.

## City Council — Structure and Responsibilities

The City Council has five (5) members who serve four year staggered terms. Each year the Council appoints a Mayor and a Mayor Pro Tem from its own membership to serve a one-year term. The Mayor presides over City Council meetings, represents the Council at business and ceremonial events, and executes all City ordinances, resolutions, and contracts. The Mayor Pro Tem performs these duties in the absence of the Mayor.

The Council enacts local laws, adopts the annual budget and Capital Improvement Program, and reviews and adopts policies, agreements, and other City business items. It appoints the City Manager and City Attorney, as well as the members of the citizen advisory boards, commissions and ad-hoc committees. City Council contact phone: (949) 362-4373.

## City Council Meetings — How to Speak or Submit Comments

Any person wishing to address the City Council may do so in person at the meeting, remotely during the meeting, or in writing before the meeting. Comments are generally limited to three (3) minutes per person unless otherwise announced by the Mayor. No action will be taken on any items not on the agenda.

In person: complete a "Request to Speak" form and submit it to the City Clerk. Speakers are called in order by the Mayor.

Remotely: join the meeting using the dial-in number provided on the agenda. When announced by the Mayor, request to speak by using the "Raise Hand" feature by dialing *5. Speakers are called in the order requests are received. When called upon, state your name for the record and provide your comments.

In writing: email written comments to cityclerk@cityoflagunaniguel.org no later than 4:00 p.m. on the day of the meeting. Written comments are not read aloud during the meeting.

## Council-Manager Form of Government

Laguna Niguel's page describes the council-manager form of government as combining the political leadership of elected officials with the managerial experience of an appointed local government manager. The council is the legislative body and focuses on approving budgets, community goals and strategies, major projects, and long-term considerations such as growth, land use development, capital improvement plans, capital financing, and strategic planning. The council hires a professional manager to carry out administrative responsibilities and supervises the manager's performance.

The city manager prepares a budget for the council's consideration; recruits, hires, and supervises staff; serves as the council's chief advisor; and carries out the council's policies. The manager makes policy recommendations, but the council may or may not adopt them, and the manager is bound by whatever action the council takes. The Mayor is selected by a majority vote of the City Council and holds office for one year.

## City Manager's Office

The City Manager is the Chief Administrative Officer responsible for the proper administration of all City business and reports directly to the City Council. The City Manager is expected to: enforce all City laws and ordinances; appoint and remove Department Heads and City employees; offer professional advice on all aspects of the City's operations; review all City Council agenda material; prepare and submit the annual City Budget and Capital Improvement Program; and monitor the City's financial position and needs.

## City Clerk's Office

The City Clerk's Office coordinates and prepares City Council agendas and minutes; manages records; maintains the Municipal Code; administers elections; administers local reporting requirements under the Political Reform Act; and reports to the Fair Political Practices Commission and/or the Secretary of State. The City Clerk attends all City Council meetings to record actions taken. Minutes are presented to the City Council for adoption at the next regularly scheduled meeting.

Following Council meetings, the office coordinates the execution of ordinances, resolutions, contracts, agreements, bond releases, and other official documents approved by the Council. The Maddy List provides information regarding City commission and committee appointments.

Contacts: Marissa Asistin, City Clerk, (949) 362-4301; Stacey T. Chavez, Deputy City Clerk, (949) 362-4302.

## Municipal Elections

General Municipal Elections are held on the first Tuesday, following the first Monday, in November of even numbered years. The City Clerk's Office administers the City's Municipal Elections, which are consolidated with the Statewide Election. All election services are provided according to the California Elections Code.

## Voter Registration and Voting Options

Voter registration forms are available at public libraries, post offices and City Hall. To receive a form by mail, call the Orange County Registrar of Voters office at 714-567-7600. Completed forms can be hand-delivered to the Registrar's office at 1300 S. Grand Ave. Santa Ana or dropped off in any U.S. mailbox; no postage is necessary. You must be registered at least 15 days prior to the election in which you intend to vote or complete the Same-Day Voter Registration process.

Voters can mail their ballot, vote in person at a vote center, or deliver their ballot to a ballot drop box, vote center, or the Registrar of Voters office.

## Campaign Contribution Limit

No person or committee may make to any city candidate or their controlled committee, and no such candidate or committee may accept, contributions totaling more than $1,000 for any city council election at which the candidate is on the ballot or a write-in candidate. This does not apply to a candidate's contribution of their personal funds to their own campaign committee, but it does apply to contributions from a spouse's separate property.

Candidates must file the appropriate Fair Political Practices Commission forms with the City Clerk prior to soliciting or receiving any contributions.

Source: https://www.cityoflagunaniguel.org/62/Elections

## District Elections for City Council

On January 16, 2024, the City Council adopted Ordinance No. 2023-226, establishing by-district elections, defining district boundaries, and scheduling elections within the districts. All five City Council members are elected by district. Voters vote for one City Council Member who lives in their district. This replaced at-large citywide elections.

Districts 1, 3, and 5 are elected starting with the November 2024 General Municipal election and every four years thereafter. Districts 2 and 4 are elected in November 2026 and every four years thereafter. Districting does not impact city services to the public. The next U.S. Census collection is 2030, and the City will conduct redistricting to balance district populations after each decennial census.

The City held public hearings on September 5, 2023, September 19, 2023, November 7, 2023, and December 5, 2023, each at 7 p.m. at Laguna Niguel Council Chamber, 30111 Crown Valley Parkway Laguna Niguel, CA 92677. The final vote was January 16, 2024 at 7 p.m.
