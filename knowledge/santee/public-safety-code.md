## Fire Department Overview

The Santee Fire Department is a full-service department covering 16.5 square miles and approximately 60,000 residents, with a large Wildland–Urban interface. It has an ISO Rating 1. Services include fire suppression, rescue, emergency medical services, fire prevention services, vegetation management, public education, emergency preparedness and trauma support.

Daily emergency resources are 3 fire stations, 3 paramedic assessment engine companies, 1 paramedic assessment truck company, 2 paramedic transport ambulances (24-hour units) and 1 shift Battalion Chief. The department also operates basic life support (BLS) units staffed by emergency medical technicians for lower-intensity medical calls.

The department has three divisions: Administrative, Emergency Services, and Fire Prevention.

## San Diego River Hazard Mitigation Grant Program

The City of Santee has been awarded Hazard Mitigation Grant Program (HMGP) funding to reduce wildfire risk along the San Diego River corridor. The project area is approximately 300 acres. The total proposed project cost is $7,284,240, which includes a non-federal funds cost sharing requirement. The San Diego River Conservancy is granting the City $907,568 toward cost sharing assistance and other wildfire risk reduction efforts.

The project has two phases. Phase 1 (Environmental Review and Planning) is the current phase. It covers a Fuels Treatment Plan, CEQA and NEPA environmental review, resource assessments and mapping of invasive plants. Phase 1 does not authorize widespread vegetation removal. Phase 2 (Implementation) is pending funding approval and regulatory permits, and no Phase 2 work will occur unless all environmental approvals, permits and funding are secured.

Timeline: 2022 applied for the grant; 2024 received the grant award notice; 2026 the Phase 1 package was submitted to Cal OES and is under review. Next steps as of 9/3/2026: Cal OES preliminary review (estimated 4-6 months), then FEMA review (estimated 1-2 years). Overall grant close out is currently scheduled for 2/20/27, and Cal OES is working with FEMA on a timeline extension.

Contact: Dustin DeMatteo, 619-258-4100 ext. 207, City of Santee Fire Department Hazard Mitigation Grant Program, 10601 Magnolia Avenue, Santee, CA 92071.

## Wildfire — Fire Hazard Severity Zones

CAL FIRE maps Fire Hazard Severity Zones (FHSZs). Requirements depend on whether a property is in a Very High, High, or Moderate zone.

Very High zone: new buildings must have at least 100 feet of defensible space. New construction must use ignition-resistant materials and follow Chapter 5 of the California Wildland-Urban Interface Code. Sellers must provide a Natural Hazard Disclosure Report and have a vegetation management inspection by the fire department.

High zone: defensible space is not currently required by code but is strongly recommended. The same new-construction standards (Chapter 5 of the California Wildland-Urban Interface Code) and the same seller disclosure and vegetation management inspection apply. Moderate zone: there are currently no additional wildland-urban interface regulations or requirements.

The Red Cross offers free home wildfire preparedness visits.

## Fire Watch

A fire watch is required when a fire protection system is out of service, during construction or demolition as required, or when directed by the Fire Marshal. The property owner, owner's designee, or occupant must notify the Fire Marshal when a fire protection system is out of service. Fire watch is in effect 24 hours per day, 7 days per week, unless the Fire Marshal specifies otherwise.

Personnel must be employed by a state-bonded and insured security service or approved by the Fire Marshal, and have no other duties. They must patrol all required areas at least once per hour and maintain a written log of patrols.

The signed log must be submitted to the Fire Marshal daily in person at 10601 Magnolia Ave. Santee, CA 92071 or by email at fireinfo@cityofsanteeca.gov. Fire watch continues until the Fire Marshal authorizes termination in writing or by electronic communication; verbal termination is not permitted.

## Fire Plan Review & New Construction

The City of Santee currently only accepts digital submittals of plans and permits, through the Permitting and Licensing Portal. Fire plan review covers emergency vehicle access, water supply, new construction, change of occupancy use, renovations, and fire and life safety system changes, plus acceptance tests and field inspections.

The City has adopted the 2024 edition of the California Fire Code (including Appendix Chapters B, BB, C, CC, F, and H) and the 2024 California Wildland-Urban Interface Code, with local amendments.

Fire protection system submittals: no submittal is required for sprinkler work of ≤6 heads, no hydraulic changes and same hazard classification, or alarm work of ≤5 devices, no new circuits and no change to sequence of operations. An abbreviated submittal is required for sprinkler work of 7–20 heads with minor layout changes, or alarm work of 6–10 devices with minor reprogramming. Anything beyond that, or in high-risk occupancies, requires a full submittal.

A construction permit (FIRE permit) is required for private fire service mains and for installing or modifying automatic fire sprinkler systems. Hydraulic calculations must include a 10% reduction from the source (CFC Section 903.3.5 City of Santee Amendment). Piping need only be hydrostatically tested at working pressure for 2 hours.

Fire flow reports are provided by the Padre Dam Municipal Water District for a nominal fee via its website. For Emergency Responder Communication Enhancement Systems (ERCES) radio frequencies, submit inquiries to rcsengineering@sdsheriff.gov.

## Fire Insurance — ISO Class 1 and Fire Risk Reduction Community List

The Santee Fire Department holds an ISO Class 1 (01/1X) rating, the top rating nationwide, which may help lower insurance premiums. The City of Santee has also been added to the State of California Board of Forestry and Fire Protection's Fire Risk Reduction Community List (FRRCL). Since 2022, California Department of Insurance regulations (Title 10, Section 2644.9(d)) require insurers to factor in the FRRCL designation when offering rates. Residents are encouraged to contact their homeowner's insurance provider to ask about a discount.

If an insurer is not applying approved discounts, the policyholder can submit a Request for Assistance (RFA) to the Department of Insurance through the CDI website.

After a nonrenewal: ask your insurer about risk mitigation steps, shop around with local agents or brokers, and explore the California FAIR Plan for basic fire insurance. The FAIR Plan can be paired with a Difference in Conditions (DIC) policy for comprehensive coverage. The CDI Consumer Hotline is 1-800-927-4357.
