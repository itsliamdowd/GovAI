## Fair Housing Program

Under direction of the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), the City of Santee has a responsibility to affirmatively further fair housing. The program aims to reduce housing discrimination in the housing rental, sales, lending, and insurance markets. It covers discrimination on the basis of race, sex, color, religion, national origin, familial status (presence of children), or handicap, in compliance with the Fair Housing Act.

The City contracts with CSA San Diego County (CSA) as its Fair Housing Program Administrator. CSA sponsors public fair housing educational activities, outreach activities, and referral activities. It also promotes the San Diego Regional Analysis of Impediments to Fair Housing Choice, as accepted by the Santee City Council on June 10, 2020.

## Essential Housing Program

The Essential Housing Program was adopted on August 25, 2021 by Ordinance 592 to boost housing production and improve housing affordability. It expedites and incentivizes the construction of new housing over a five-year period. Certain residential projects that meet specific criteria can be determined "Essential Housing Projects." Such a project is deemed in compliance with the City's General Plan and is not subject to General Plan and Zone Base District Amendments to move forward with entitlements. It remains subject to all other discretionary permit requirements.

It applies to a housing development project of residential units only, or mixed-use development (including live/work spaces) with at least two-thirds of the square footage designated for residential use. A project must achieve at least 50 credits across eight categories, with 10 required credits in the Housing category. The categories are Land Use, Housing, Mobility, Open Space and Conservation, Sustainability, Trails and Sidewalks, Safety, and Parks and Recreation.

A project is not an Essential Housing Project if any of these apply:
- The development footprint is within a floodway as defined in Santee Municipal Code Chapter 11.36.
- The development footprint is in an area permanently protected by one or more conservation easements.
- The General Plan land use designation or zoning does not currently allow residential uses and the site is not in the Residential Sites Inventory of the Housing Element.
- The site is not on an existing or planned (identified in the General Plan) prime arterial, major arterial, parkway or collector.
- The site is within Safety Zone 1, 2, or 5 of the Gillespie Field Airport Land Use Compatibility Plan.

## Essential Housing Program — Review Timeline and Certification

The Director has up to 30 days to review an Essential Housing Application and render a determination. If a project achieves 50 credits, the Director certifies it as an Essential Housing Project. This determination is final and cannot be appealed to the City Council.

After receiving the signed certification, the applicant has 120 days to submit a formal development application and all required information. If the applicant does not, the certification expires, unless extended by the mutual written consent of the Director and applicant.

The determination is a ministerial action under CEQA and is not subject to CEQA. Certification does not authorize, entitle, or permit a development project, and the project itself remains subject to applicable CEQA requirements. A Notice of Exemption is filed with the San Diego County Recorder's Office. Certified Essential Housing Projects listed: Fanita Ranch, Santee Town Center, and Mission Gorge Road.

## Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) and Junior ADUs

An ADU is an attached or detached residential unit accessory to a single-family or multi-family unit. A JADU is a type of ADU with a maximum of 500 square feet. ADUs must provide complete, independent living facilities (living, sleeping, eating, cooking, and sanitation) and be a permanent housing solution, not a short-term rental. JADUs must be contained entirely within an existing or proposed single-family residence, provide an efficiency kitchen, and provide or share a bathroom and sanitation facilities with the primary residence.

The City's ADU Fee Waiver Program ended on September 27, 2024. ADUs are subject to all fees typically collected at permit issuance. ADUs in Santee must comply with the 2022 California Green Building Standards Tier II Voluntary Measures and Santee Municipal Code Sections 13.10.040 J and K.

Under AB 1332 (2023), applicants may apply for a permit using preapproved plans. The City accepts County of San Diego Standard ADU Plans, City of Chula Vista Standard ADU Plans, and City of Encinitas Permit-Ready ADU Plans. The County plans are provided at no cost to residents.

Under AB 2533 (2024), qualified unpermitted ADUs or JADUs built before January 1, 2020 may be eligible for a building permit. Homeowners may obtain a confidential third-party code inspection from a licensed contractor, provided health, safety, and building standards in the Substandard Structure Checklist are met.

## SB 9 Two-Unit Projects and Urban Lot Splits

Senate Bill (SB) 9 (2021) allows the creation of up to four units on land zoned for single-family housing through a Two-unit Project, an Urban Lot Split, or both. A Two-unit Project allows up to two residential units, attached or detached, on a single lot. An Urban Lot Split allows a lot to be split into two lots with up to two residential units on each lot. The City's SB 9 Guide covers eligibility, standards, development scenarios and the approval process.

## Preliminary Applications (SB 330 / Housing Crisis Act)

SB 330 (2019), the Housing Crisis Act (HCA), allows submittal of a complete preliminary application for certain housing development projects to lock in ordinances, policies, and project fees. Eligible projects:
- Residential housing projects of two or more units (excluding hotels, assisted living or other commercial dwelling units).
- Mixed-use projects with at least two-thirds of the square footage designated for residential use.
- Transitional, Supportive, or Emergency Housing projects.
- Farmworker housing, as defined in subdivision (h) of Section 50199.7 of the Health and Safety Code.

The HCA does not apply to housing development projects located within a very high fire hazard severity zone. An SB 330 Preliminary Application Form is available on the page.

## Density Bonus and Streamlined (By-Right) Housing Development

Density Bonus is a State law that allows a property owner to increase the site's established density and receive incentives and concessions related to zoning development standards for the development of affordable housing.

Eligible housing development applications can use a streamlined, ministerial approval process when the proposal meets all objective code standards. The City has a Streamlined Housing Application Guide. Under Santee Municipal Code (SMC) 13.11, eligible projects must:
- Be proposed on a by-right site identified in the Housing Element Sites Inventory.
- Provide at least 20% affordability to low-income households.
- Comply with all applicable objective design, performance, improvement and development standards.

Related state laws listed:
- AB 1397 (2017) requires a by-right approval process for relisted housing development sites.
- AB 803 (2021), the Starter Home Revitalization Act of 2021, creates a ministerial approval process for the subdivision of certain multi-family lots up to the lot's density.
- SB 423 (2023) builds on SB 35 (2017), creating a ministerial approval process for certain multifamily projects. SB 423 projects must include affordable housing and pay prevailing wages.
