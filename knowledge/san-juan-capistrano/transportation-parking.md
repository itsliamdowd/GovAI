## Residential Parking Permits — New Areas and Renewal

Parking permit programs are implemented at the request of residents. A petition (PDF) process and approval by City Council are necessary to implement the program in a new area. Residents in a neighborhood next to a permit area cannot get a permit unless the program is extended there.

It is the permit holder's responsibility to renew by the expiration date printed on the permit. No renewal notice is mailed. Notices go out through the City's electronic "e-news" (sign up on the home page and check the press release box). Valid picture I.D., proof of residence and current vehicle registration are required annually.

Source: https://www.sanjuancapistrano.org/FAQ.aspx?QID=108

## Downtown Employee Parking Permits

If your business is generally within the downtown area or the Los Rios Historic District, employee parking permits are available at City Hall. The permit lets an employee park in marked, time-restricted public parking spaces. The cost of each employee parking permit is $8 per year. Replacement permits are issued for a fee of $11.

## Commuter Parking Permits — Downtown Parking Structure

The City issues commuter parking permits for the spaces on the basement level of the downtown parking structure next to the Franciscan Plaza off of Verdugo Street. Permits are valid for a calendar year, and the fee is pro-rated based on the date of purchase. Forms on the Parking page are the Parking Permit Application (PDF), the Liability Contract (PDF) and the Commuter Parking Fee Schedule (PDF).

## Commuter Parking Permits — How to Apply

Complete the Parking Permit Application (PDF) and the Liability Contract (PDF), and bring or mail two signed copies of each. You must show proof of registration and a driver license. Submit the forms to Customer Service at City Hall during regular business hours.

An annual commuter parking permit is not required to park in spaces 1 through 73. Without a permit, parking in those spaces costs $2 per day.

## Parking Structure Daily Fee and Overnight Parking

## Parking Citations — How to Pay

Parking citations may be paid by mail, phone or online at www.CitationProcessingCenter.com. To pay by phone, call (800) 989-2058.

To pay by mail, use a money order, cashier's check or personal check. Do not send cash. Confirm the check is written for the proper penalty amount. Make checks payable to City of San Juan Capistrano and include your citation number in the memo. Enclose the notice of parking violation with your payment and/or proof of correction(s). Mail to: City of San Juan Capistrano c/o Citation Processing Center, PO BOX 10479, Newport Beach, CA 92658-0479.

For questions about parking citations, contact Jennifer Ashe at JAshe@sanjuancapistrano.org or (949)443-6369.

Source: https://sanjuancapistrano.org/302/Parking

## Encroachment Permits — Work in the Public Right-of-Way

All work in the public right-of-way (ROW) requires an encroachment permit from the City's Engineering Division. This includes:
- construction or utility work
- excavation, trenching, or boring
- sidewalk replacement
- driveway widening or replacement
- curb and gutter work
- parkway culverts
- placement of dumpsters or storage units

The public ROW includes streets, curbs, median areas, public sidewalks, City-owned property and easements, and parkways (the landscaped or grass area between the curb and sidewalk, or between the sidewalk and private property). If you are unsure whether your project encroaches into the ROW, email ENGPermits@sanjuancapistrano.org or visit the One-Stop-Shop at City Hall.

Source: https://sanjuancapistrano.org/301/Permits---Encroachment-Transportation

## Electric Vehicle Charging Station Permits

There are two types of EV chargers, Level 1 and Level 2. An electrical permit may be required to install an Electric Vehicle Charging Station (EVCS).

Level 1 chargers plug directly into a standard 120-volt receptacle outlet. They do not require a permit from the Building Division as long as there are no changes to the electrical system to provide the 120-volt receptacle. Any change to an electrical system requires a permit before work begins. Level 2 systems require a 240-volt electrical circuit and typically require an electrical permit and inspections.
