## Accessory & Junior Accessory Dwelling Units (ADU/JADU) — Rules

ADUs are allowed on all single-family or multi-family parcels. JADUs are no more than 500 square feet in size. A JADU must be contained entirely within an existing accessory structure or within an existing or proposed single-family residence. A property with a Single Dwelling Unit may have both an ADU and a JADU. A property with Multiple Dwelling Units may have two detached ADUs and may convert habitable and non-habitable spaces to ADUs.

There is expedited permit processing for ADUs up to 800 square feet in size, less than 16 feet tall, and at least 4 feet from a side and rear yard property line. ADUs up to 1,000 square feet are permitted with additional review. ADUs are permitted to encroach into side and rear yard setback areas. No covered parking is required for ADUs. No replacement covered parking is required for conversions of garages to ADUs.

There are no City Development Impact Fees for ADUs less than 750 square feet in habitable floor area. The Development Impact Fees for ADUs 750 square feet or larger are charged proportionately in relation to the habitable area of the primary unit. No ADU or grading related to construction of an ADU is permitted within two hundred feet (horizontal) of a General Plan designated ridgeline. The City's regulations are in ADU/JADU Ordinance 1101.

## ADU/JADU — Review Processes and Application Steps

The City has two review processes for proposed ADUs and JADUs: Building Permit Review and Zoning Compliance Review. A proposed ADU or JADU that meets all the applicable requirements in the City's table qualifies for Building Permit Review, which is a streamlined review. If it does not qualify, it is subject to Zoning Compliance Review, which has more restrictive development standards. ADUs must comply with all applicable building and fire code requirements. The Orange County Fire Authority (OCFA) Submittal Form is a checklist for when submittal to OCFA is required.

To apply, first contact a planner with any questions about the standards. Next, submit conceptual plans for a free preliminary plan review. This confirms whether your ADU qualifies for Building Permit Review or Zoning Compliance Review. In approximately one week you will receive an email from a Planner with instructions for the next step.

If your ADU qualifies for Building Permit Review, you submit a permit application to the City's Building Division. The application must include a completed and signed Accessory Dwelling Units Checklist.

If your ADU does not qualify, the Planner will give you comments on how to amend it. If you choose to amend, repeat the preliminary review. If you choose not to amend, a Planner will provide a Building Permit number and start a formal Zoning Compliance Review. Corrections must be addressed and resubmitted until all code requirements are met. You are then asked to schedule an appointment with the Building Division, referencing that Building Permit number, to submit your Building Permit application.

## ADU/JADU — Addressing and Deed Restriction

ADUs require their own address. The primary unit and the ADU must share the same numerical address. Unit Number #1 is assigned to the main residence and Unit Number #2 is assigned to the ADU. ADU applicants will be asked to submit an Address Assignment Request Form as part of the Building Division's plan check process.

A Deed Restriction must be recorded against the title of the property before a building permit for construction of an ADU or JADU is issued.
