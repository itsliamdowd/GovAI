## Recycling — Resources and Free Mattress Recycling

The City's Recycling page points residents to outside resources for recycling beverage containers, electronic waste, motor oil and more: CalRecycle, Keep America Beautiful, RecycleSmart, and a BetterBin App guide (PDF) for sorting recyclables.

Orange County Waste and Recycling (OCWR) and the Mattress Recycling Council offer free commercial and residential mattress recycling at OC landfills through the Bye Bye Mattress program. Residents may contact CR&R for a free bulky item pick up, or take items directly to the Olinda Alpha or Prima Deshecha landfills. Most standard mattresses and box springs are accepted and must be separated from trash. Prima Deshecha and Olinda Alpha accept both commercial and public drop-off. Frank R. Bowerman landfill is commercial only.

## Residential Organics Recycling (Green Cart Food Scraps and Yard Waste)

The City and CR&R Environmental Services began the Residential Organics Recycling Program on July 1, 2020. Food scraps and yard waste are co-collected in the green cart. All residents' monthly solid waste services include three carts: refuse, dry recyclables, and organics/green waste. Residents who do not have an organics green cart can order one from CR&R Customer Service at 877-728-0446.

Free kitchen food waste containers are being distributed to residents. Each comes with a promotional envelope containing 10 Bio-Bag compostable bags (plus a $1.00 off coupon) and an instructional flyer. Empty the kitchen container into the curbside green cart on your regular garbage collection day. Do not place the kitchen container itself at the curb.

Accepted food includes bread, pasta, rice, beans, dairy, nuts, coffee, tea, fruits, vegetables, processed foods, meat and fish. Frozen, spoiled or moldy food is also accepted. Not accepted: liquids, glass, plastic, foam, metal or foil, single-use coffee pods, dryer or vacuum lint, ashes, pet food, pet waste or cat litter, dead animals, diseased plants, diapers, clothing or textiles, rocks, sand, dirt, lumber or logs, pills or medications, chemical or cleaning wipes, compostable to-go containers, cups and utensils, and chewing gum. Items that don't belong go in the black refuse cart.

Compostable bags must be Biodegradable Products Institute (BPI) or ASTM D6400 approved, and plastic bags must never be used. Compostable bags are sold at:
- Denault's Ace Hardware, 31862 Del Obispo Street
- Marbella Farmer's Market, 31109 Rancho Viejo Road
- Costco Wholesale, 33961 Doheny Park Road
- Smart and Final Extra, 34091 Doheny Park Road
- Gelson's Market, 30731 Gateway Place

For questions, contact Noreen Swiontek, Environmental Programs Analyst, (949) 487-4307.

## Medication and Sharps Disposal

The San Juan Capistrano Household Hazardous Waste Collection Center is at 32250 Avenida La Pata, phone 714-834-4000. Hours are Tuesday-Saturday, 9am-3pm. Residents can also call 714-834-4000 for additional disposal options. The City asks residents to sign up for its newsblasts for information on medication take-back events.

Residents are asked not to flush medications down the toilet or drain. To dispose of medications in the trash:
1. Take the medications out of their original containers.
2. Mix them with an undesirable substance such as cat litter or used coffee grounds.
3. Put the mixture into a disposable container with a lid, or into a sealable bag.
4. Cover or scratch off personal information, including the Rx number, on the empty containers.
5. Place the sealed container and the empty medication container in the trash.

Disposing of sharps in the household trash is against California law. Sharps must be in a heavy-duty, puncture-resistant container with a tight-fitting lid, such as an empty detergent, liquid fabric softener or bleach bottle. Home-generated sharps can be taken to the Household Hazardous Waste Collection Center at 32250 Avenida La Pata. The California Sharps Program also offers free sharps container kits.
