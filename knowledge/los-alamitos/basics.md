## Speaking at a City Council Meeting (In Person)

Anyone may attend and speak during designated public comment periods. To speak, fill out a blue speaker card before the meeting begins and give it to the City Clerk, or wait until the Mayor opens Oral Communications. Speakers who filled out a card are called first, then the Mayor opens Oral Communications to anyone else. Come to the microphone at the front of the room and share your name, address, and topic, and mention any group or organization you represent. Providing your name and contact information is optional, but it helps City staff follow up.

Comments are generally limited to 3 minutes for items on the agenda and 5 minutes during Oral Communications (general public comment). Direct all comments to the entire City Council, not individual members. Speakers and audience members must remain respectful. Shouting, clapping, stomping, profanity, or ignoring the Mayor's instructions are not permitted, and the Mayor may stop a disorderly speaker. Anyone who refuses to follow the rules may be removed and could face legal consequences.

## City Council Members and Terms

The City Council is the legislative body of the City. It has five members who serve by District and are elected by the registered voters of their District. Terms are four years, staggered, and members can serve up to three consecutive terms. A member who has served three consecutive terms can serve again after 365 consecutive days out of office (Los Alamitos Charter Section 501).

Current members:

- Shelley Hasselbrink, District 4, term ends November 2026
- Emily Hibard, District 5, term ends November 2026
- Gary Loe, District 2, term ends November 2028

Each year at the first regularly scheduled City Council meeting in December, the Council elects one of its members as Mayor. The new Mayor then conducts the election of a Mayor Pro Tempore (Los Alamitos Municipal Code Chapter 2.04.100).

## General Municipal Elections

The City Clerk is the election official. The General Municipal Election is held on the first Tuesday in November of even-numbered years to elect City Council members and consider local ballot measures. Three positions are open at one election and two positions at the next election two years later. Council Members serve four-year terms, must be registered voters, and must reside within their district boundary. The City does not have a directly elected Mayor. Each year the five council members select one of their own as Mayor.

The next General Municipal Election is November 3, 2026. Residents vote for candidates who run in the district where they live. The website has an interactive web map where you can enter an address to find your district, plus the approved boundary map and demographic data.

## Voter Registration

To be eligible to vote in an election, you must register at least 15 days before the election date. Your registration stays active whether or not you voted in the last election. You can register online at the Orange County Registrar of Voters website, or mail a completed form to the County Registrar. Voter registrations are maintained by the County Registrar.

To qualify, you must:
- Be a U.S. citizen at the time you register
- Be at least 18 years of age before the date of the next election
- Not be in prison or on parole for a felony conviction

You must re-register if you move, change your name, or change your political party affiliation.

## Campaign Contribution Limits

The City of Los Alamitos does not impose local limits on campaign contributions, so the state limit applies by default. No candidate may accept more than the state limit from a single source. The limit does not apply to a candidate's contributions of their own personal funds to their own campaign. Information is at https://www.fppc.ca.gov/learn/campaign-rules/state-contribution-limits.html.

## City Clerk's Office

The City Clerk is appointed by and serves at the pleasure of the City Council. The office:
- Prepares City Council agendas
- Records and maintains Council minutes, ordinances, and resolutions
- Preserves official City records
- Conducts City elections
- Records deeds
- Administers oaths
- Receives and processes liability claims against the City
- Responds to Public Records Act requests
- Provides voter registration applications
- Handles State Political Reform Act filings, including serving as filing officer for campaign disclosure statements
- Maintains the Los Alamitos Municipal Code and Zoning Ordinance
- Coordinates City boards, commissions, and committees

## Development Services Department

Development Services has two divisions, Community Development and Public Works, and operates under the City Manager. Its areas are:
- Planning: maintains and implements the General Plan.
- Economic Development: leads business attraction and retention.
- Code Enforcement: enforces the Municipal Code as it relates to neighborhood and community issues.
- Building and Safety: provides services for building and development projects.
- Public Works: maintains public infrastructure, City vehicles, and construction equipment.
- Engineering: manages the Capital Improvement Program and seeks funding for projects.
