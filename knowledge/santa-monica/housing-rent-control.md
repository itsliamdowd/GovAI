# Housing & Rent Control

## Rent Control — Properties Covered

Santa Monica's Rent Control Charter Amendment (adopted 1979) covers most multi-unit residential buildings constructed before April 10, 1979. Single-family homes are generally not covered, though some exceptions exist. The Rent Control Board maintains a "Look Up a Rent" tool on its website where you can search by property address to check if a unit is controlled and view the Maximum Allowable Rent (MAR).

Source: https://www.santamonica.gov/topic-explainers/rent-control-in-santa-monica

## Rent Control — Maximum Lawful Rent Calculation

The total lawful rent consists of three parts: (1) Maximum Allowable Rent (MAR), the rent ceiling excluding pass-throughs; (2) Registration Fee Pass-Through, 2026/2027 = $240/year per unit, with owners able to pass through $120 ($10/month) to tenants; (3) Property Tax Surcharge, owners may pass through a pro rata share of up to five ballot-measure tax line items, capped at the lowest of the actual amount, 4% of 2026/2027 MAR, or $35/month. Surcharge applies only to tenancies starting before March 1, 2018, if the property was not reassessed on or after that date due to a sale, and only if properly noticed to the tenant with a copy of the tax bill.

Source: https://www.santamonica.gov/maximum-lawful-rent

## Rent Control — General Adjustment 2026

The 2026 General Adjustment (GA) is 2.6%, effective September 1, 2026, with a maximum increase of $70 for units with a current maximum allowable rent of $2,674 or more. The GA is calculated as 75% of the percentage change in the Los Angeles-area Consumer Price Index for the 12 months ending in March. A unit must have a tenancy in place for at least one year to be eligible for the GA. All registration fees and penalties must be paid in full by August 1 for a unit to be eligible. Landlords must give tenants at least 30 days' notice of any increase, using the "Notice of Change in Terms of Tenancy 2026" form.

Source: https://www.santamonica.gov/maximum-lawful-rent

## Rent Control — General Adjustment History

2025 GA: 2.3%, effective Sept 1, 2025, with a $60 cap for MARs of $2,587 or above. The GA range is set by law: not less than zero percent nor greater than three percent. Historical adjustments are available in the Rent Control Board's General Adjustment Summary; check the Board's website or contact the office for older years.

Source: https://www.santamonica.gov/maximum-lawful-rent

## Rent Control — Tenant Registration & Initial Rent

Since January 1, 1999, property owners have generally been able to set the initial rent for new tenancies at market rate. The MAR is then based on that registered rent plus allowed subsequent adjustments. Owners must register each new tenancy with the Rent Control Agency. Tenants and owners can look up a registered property's MAR using the Look Up a Rent tool.

Source: https://www.santamonica.gov/maximum-lawful-rent

## Rent Control — Petition & Dispute Process

Landlords and tenants can file rent adjustment petitions with the Rent Control Board. Tenants can petition for below-maximum-rent determinations if they believe they are paying above the lawful maximum. Landlords can petition for above-guideline increases based on capital improvements, increased operating expenses, or other grounds specified in the ordinance. The Board holds hearings and makes determinations. Contact the Rent Control Office at (310) 458-8751 or rentcontrol@santamonica.gov for details on the petition process and forms.

Source: https://www.santamonica.gov/rent-control-charter-amendment-regulations

## Rent Control — CIVICS Portal & Online Filing

Landlords and tenants access the Rent Control Board's online CIVICS portal for filing documents, paying registration fees, and managing accounts. A CIVICS account registration is available for both landlords and tenants. The Rent Control Document Portal allows users to search scanned property documents. Rent Control Office: Room 202 at City Hall, 1685 Main St, (310) 458-8751, rentcontrol@santamonica.gov. Staff respond to emails the same or next business day, Monday–Friday, 8 a.m.–12:30 p.m. & 1:30–4:30 p.m.

Source: https://www.santamonica.gov/topic-explainers/rent-control-in-santa-monica

## Tenant Protections — Just Cause Eviction

The Residential Landlord and Tenant Ordinance (RLTO) and Just Cause Eviction protections apply citywide, both in rent-controlled and market-rate units. Tenancies can be terminated only for limited reasons specified in state law and the RLTO. The city's Evictions page details the grounds for termination and tenant rights. Contact the Rent Control Office or Santa Monica's Housing Office for tenant resources.

Source: https://www.santamonica.gov/topic-explainers/rent-control-in-santa-monica

## Tenant Protections — Tenant Harassment Ordinance

Santa Monica prohibits tenant harassment in all residential units, rent-controlled and otherwise. Prohibited acts include retaliation for asserting tenant rights, harassment intended to induce a tenant to leave, and other coercive conduct. Violations can be reported to the city's Code Enforcement Division at (310) 458-4984 or code.enforcement@santamonica.gov.

Source: https://www.santamonica.gov/topic-explainers/rent-control-in-santa-monica

## Tenant Protections — Relocation Assistance

Tenants displaced due to owner move-in, Ellis Act removal, or other covered reasons may be eligible for relocation assistance from the city. Amounts vary by household size and income. Contact the Housing Office at SMHousing@santamonica.gov or (310) 458-8740 for eligibility and application details.

Source: https://www.santamonica.gov/housing-displaced

## Below Market Housing (BMH) Waitlist

Santa Monica's Below Market Housing program offers apartments in the private sector at below-market rent. Rather than a subsidy, applicants are matched to vacancies based on household size and income. The city maintains a single BMH waitlist. Priority is given to households that live or work in Santa Monica. Applicants must opt-in within 3 days to stay on the list, and five strikes (declined housing offers) result in removal. Income limits apply (check santamonica.gov/housing-bmh for current limits as of May 4, 2026). Applying is a two-step registration process; both steps must be completed to join the waitlist. The former Community Corp SM waitlist is no longer active; reapply with the City.

Source: https://www.santamonica.gov/housing-bmh

## Below Market Housing — Displaced Preference

Households displaced in Santa Monica within the past 12 months due to Ellis Act removal, natural disaster, or other reasons get priority on both the BMH and Section 8 voucher waitlists. To claim displaced status, apply online and email displaced@santamonica.gov with proof of displacement. Domestic violence qualifies for Section 8 priority but not BMH priority.

Source: https://www.santamonica.gov/housing-displaced

## Section 8 Voucher Program

Santa Monica's Section 8 program is administered by the Santa Monica Housing Authority. It differs from the Below Market Housing program: Section 8 provides rental subsidies (a voucher paying the difference between the tenant's contribution and market rent), not below-market apartments. Section 8 has its own waitlist with separate income limits and eligibility criteria. Displaced households receive priority. For details and application, contact the Housing Authority or the city's Housing Office at SMHousing@santamonica.gov or (310) 458-8740.

Source: https://www.santamonica.gov/housing

## Homelessness Services & At-Risk Housing

Santa Monica's Housing Office provides emergency assistance and navigation for residents at risk of losing housing or experiencing homelessness. Services include emergency rent assistance, temporary shelter support, and connections to permanent supportive housing. Contact the Housing Office at SMHousing@santamonica.gov or (310) 458-8740.

Source: https://www.santamonica.gov/housing-at-risk

## Renter Aid & Community Resources

The city maintains a Community Resource Directory in multiple languages listing local legal aid, housing, senior, disability, and consumer services available to renters. The directory is accessible through the city website.

Source: https://www.santamonica.gov/topic-explainers/rent-control-in-santa-monica

## Affordable Housing Development

The city also develops and preserves affordable housing through its Housing and Human Services Department. Projects include deed-restricted affordable apartments, inclusionary housing requirements in new developments, and supportive housing for homeless residents. Check santamonica.gov/housing for current projects and programs.

Source: https://www.santamonica.gov/housing
