## Code Enforcement — What It Handles and How to File a Complaint

The Code Enforcement Division is at City Hall, 2nd Floor, 303 W. Commonwealth Ave., Fullerton, CA 92832 (Commonwealth Ave. and Highland Ave.). It enforces regulations on:
- conditions of an existing structure that constitute a clear and present danger to the public
- construction without appropriate building permits
- property maintenance complaints, such as outside storage, inoperable vehicles, dilapidated structures, and chipping or peeling paint
- violations of the Fullerton Municipal Code
- land use conditions on private property
- improperly placed or illegal signage

Common problems can be reported online at any time through the Citizen Support Center (24 hours). You need the address or cross streets of the problem property, and new users must register. Reportable problems include:
- weeds over 12 inches tall on private property
- inoperable vehicles on private property
- trash cans stored in public view
- dumping or discards on private property
- lawn/yard parking
- property in need of repairs or clean up

Other ways to reach the division:
- Email: CodeEnforcement@cityoffullerton.com
- Main line: (714) 738-6553
- Complaint line: (714) 773-5704 (messages can be left anytime and are retrieved the next business day)
- U.S. Mail: Code Enforcement Division, City of Fullerton, 303 W. Commonwealth Ave., Fullerton CA 92832

The City responds to a complaint with an inspection within ten working days of the date the online complaint is retrieved from its server. Indicate on the complaint form if you want a call back. Some case information must be kept confidential. For vendor food-related complaints, contact the Orange County Health Care Agency at (714) 433-6000.

## Code Enforcement — Matters Not Enforced and Sign Rules

The City acts on behalf of the public at large and does not take action on behalf of one citizen against another. It does not handle neighbor disputes or civil problems that do not violate public law or pose a public nuisance. Code Enforcement does not enforce these civil issues:
- property line disputes
- drainage between private properties (unless governed by a drainage easement, or erosion, subsidence, or surface water runoff problems are created by grading or modifications)
- repair or modification disputes between private property owners and the HOA (in HOA enforced communities), unless the home is tenant occupied or unpermitted work was performed
- tree roots or trees on private property that overhang, encroach upon, or damage a neighboring private property
- blocking of private views

Signs are not allowed in all areas and zones, so see the City's Sign Ordinance before placing signage. Allowable signs must be on private property with the owner's consent. They may not be placed on public property (utility poles, street lights, parks, parkways, etc.). Signs for a function sponsored by a public agency or non-profit organization may be displayed if installed no earlier than 30 days before the event and removed within three days after the event.

## Noise Complaints

Under FMC Chapter 15.90, a noise disturbance is any sound that exceeds the specified decibel levels set for different zones (residential, commercial, industrial) and times of day. Sounds that continuously exceed those levels are considered disturbances.

Call the Police non-emergency line, (714)-738-6700, if the noise is happening outside normal business hours (especially late at night or early in the morning) and is causing immediate disturbance, such as loud parties, ongoing loud music, or disturbances from bars and clubs. Police are also advised when the noise comes with suspicious or potentially illegal activities.

Contact Code Enforcement at (714) 773-5704 if the noise is a recurring problem, or is related to a specific business, industrial operation, or construction activity that violates the noise standards. Code Enforcement handles ongoing and consistent violations from a particular source during regular business hours.

Exemptions from the noise standards include:
- school bands, school athletic and school entertainment events
- outdoor gatherings with a city permit and/or license, public dances, shows, and sporting and entertainment events
- activities on public parks, public playgrounds, and public or private school grounds
- emergency work
- vehicular traffic noise on public streets

For the drilling of water wells, the Director of Development Services may approve an exception or limited exemption. If you are cited for noise nuisance, you may file an appeal to City Council within 15 days following receipt of notice.

## Parking Rules (Overnight, Lawn, RV)

Parking on streets or the public right-of-way is regulated by the Police Department Traffic Division, (714) 738-5313. Code Enforcement only enforces parking violations on private property.

The Municipal Code prohibits parking any vehicle on any street or highway from 2-5 a.m. every day unless approved for an exemption as indicated by signing. Vehicles left parked or standing on any highway, street or alley for more than 72 hours can be towed. The City Council may exempt a neighborhood from the overnight parking requirement if the area has inadequate off-street parking. To request an exemption, call the Office of Traffic Engineering at City Hall at 714-738-6857.

A Resident Only Parking Permit does NOT grant permission to park overnight on the street. These permits are valid from 7:00 am to 7:00pm.

Parking any vehicle on lawns, sidewalks or in parkway areas is prohibited by the Municipal Code. This is also generally true of storing abandoned, wrecked, dismantled, unregistered or inoperative vehicles on private or public property.

Recreational vehicles may be parked in your driveway if:
- there is no direct vehicular access to your side or rear yards
- the RV does not protrude into the sidewalk or street
- the vehicle is not occupied for living purposes
- the number of vehicles is limited to those owned by the occupant of the dwelling

## Garage Sale Regulations

The Fullerton Municipal Code permits one garage sale every six months at a residence, and no sale may last longer than three consecutive days. Items may not be displayed or sold in the public right-of-way (such as a sidewalk), and no sales or displays may take place after sundown. Everything sold must have been owned or used by you or someone in your household. Commercial merchandise may not be sold in addition to or in place of your own items.

No more than three signs are allowed per sale, and each sign may be no larger than 2 square feet. Other sign requirements:
- Signs may be posted on other people's property only with their written permission.
- Signs may not be posted in the public right-of-way or on public property, including telephone poles, power poles, street lights, street signs, city trees, sidewalks, crosswalks or curbs.
- The City removes illegally posted signs and may assess the persons responsible for the cleanup and removal costs.
- Signs may be displayed during daylight hours only.
- Signs must be removed at the conclusion of the sale.

For questions, call (714) 738-6553 or visit the Community Preservation Division of the Community Development Department at Fullerton City Hall.

## Animal Regulations and OC Animal Care

Fullerton has adopted Orange County Animal Control regulations by reference and contracts with OC Animal Care for enforcement. OC Animal Care is at 1630 Victory Road, Tustin, CA 92782, (714) 935-6848; after hours (5:30pm - 7:00am): (714) 935-7158; website www.OCPetInfo.com. For barking dog complaints or general Orange County animal regulation questions, call (714) 796-6442. To report a rooster, call 714-773-5704 with the address where the rooster is located. Roosters are not allowed in Fullerton.

Leash laws: Dogs may not run loose in City parks and playgrounds and must be on leashes no longer than 10 feet. Dogs are permitted only in park parking lots or on the adjacent walkways unless an area is posted allowing it. Owners must curb their pets in public areas and clean up after them. Pooch Park is the off-leash dog area.

Dogs and cats:
- Single-family homes on lots of 20,000 square feet or less: maximum of four pets per household, no more than three of which can be dogs, plus a pet's offspring under 4 months. Four dogs are allowed with a noncommercial kennel license from the Orange County Animal Shelter. One additional dog or cat may be kept on lots over 20,000 square feet.
- Apartments, condominiums, duplexes or mobile homes: maximum of two adult dogs or cats, or one of each, plus offspring under 4 months. A stricter management or homeowners' association rule still applies.
- Dog licensing: all dogs in Orange County over 4 months must be licensed and vaccinated against rabies. License within fifteen (15) days of acquisition or within fifteen (15) days after the license becomes due. Proof of a California approved rabies vaccination is required (OCCO 4-1-70).
- Cat licensing is optional and available through OC Animal Care.

Livestock: Small livestock such as poultry (except crowing roosters) and rabbits are allowed if no coops or pens are within 30 feet of a house on an adjacent property. Equine and bovine animals are generally permitted when two adult animals are kept on a lot of not less than 32,670 square feet, with one additional animal for each additional 15,000 square feet. Offspring of bovine animals are considered adults at 6 months, and of equine animals at 1 year.

Wild animals: Keeping wild animals in residential areas is prohibited, with exceptions including birds (except as otherwise provided by law), hamsters, guinea pigs, white rats and/or mice, nonpoisonous snakes not exceeding 6 feet, turtles, nonpoisonous toads, lizards, salamanders, newts, chameleons, kangaroo rats, and fish (except piranha and walking catfish). Report wildlife crime 24 hours a day to fws_tips@fws.gov or 1-844-FWS-TIPS (397-8477).

For other questions, email CodeEnforcement@cityoffullerton.com.

## Fireworks — When, Where, and Who Can Use and Buy

Only Safe and Sane fireworks are permitted in Fullerton. Voters approved Measure X on November 6, 2012, adopting Ordinance No. 3183. City Council adopted Resolution No. 2016-11 on February 2, 2016.

- **Use:** only on July 4, from 10 a.m. to 10 p.m.
- **Purchase:** July 1, 2 and 3 from noon to 10 p.m.; July 4 from 10 a.m. to 9 p.m. Individual organizations may set their own hours within these limits.
- **Minimum age** to buy and use: 16 years.
- **Where allowed:** private property 10 feet or more away from houses, garages, buildings or structures, with the property owner's or occupant's permission.
- **Where prohibited:**
  - public properties, including parks, sidewalks, streets, alleys, and public schools
  - the Very High and High Fire Hazard Severity Zones designated by the California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection, generally known as the Brea Dam Recreational areas and the Coyote Hills areas
  - underneath any tree or combustible structure
- A map of prohibited areas is given to customers at each fireworks stand and is on the City's website.
- The City does not permit fireworks of any kind at a city-wide Fireworks show on July 4th.

## Fireworks Safety Tips

- Never allow young children to play with or ignite fireworks. Adults should always supervise.
- Never have any portion of your body directly over a firework when lighting the fuse.
- Light one item at a time and move back to a safe distance immediately after lighting.
- Never try to re-light or pick up fireworks that have not fully functioned.
- If an item does not light easily, or lights but fizzles, stand back and do not handle it for 30 minutes.
- Never throw fireworks or point them at another person.
- Keep a bucket of water or a garden hose handy.
- Never carry fireworks in a pocket or shoot them off in metal or glass containers.
- After fireworks fully function, douse the spent device with plenty of water before discarding.
- Fireworks are prohibited to be sold or used in the High Fire Severity Zones.

## Reporting Illegal Fireworks

For emergencies, call 9-1-1. Do not call 911 unless there is an actual police, fire or medical emergency. For non-emergencies, or to report illegal possession and/or discharge of fireworks in unauthorized areas, or the exact location of illegal fireworks use, sales, or suspicious activity, call (714)-738-3165. For tips on illegal firework sales, email ReportFireworks@fullertonPD.org.

On the 4th of July, a team of six police officers is designated to respond specifically to fireworks calls, and uniformed and plainclothes officers patrol the City. Officers are dispatched based on the priority or nature of the call, and emergency "in progress" calls get a faster response. In general, officers can only enforce illegal fireworks violations they personally observe. CERT (Community Emergency Response Team) patrols the hill areas, which are also high fire areas.
