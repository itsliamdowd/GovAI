## Code Enforcement: What Code Conformance Officers Do

The City employs Code Conformance Officers on a seven day workweek who enforce the City's Municipal Code. Most common violations include:
- Accumulation of junk, trash & debris
- Abandoned buildings
- Overgrown weeds and vegetation (FPS)
- Non-permitted vendors
- Business Licenses (Finance)
- Persons/business operating outside of intended use or prohibited use
- Abandoned residential properties
- Nonconforming signs/Excessive signage

The City's jurisdictional authority does not allow it to involve itself in civil complaints, such as a neighbor's tree overhanging another property or property line disputes.

## Trash Container Regulations

A City ordinance makes it unlawful for a property owner or other person in control of the property to place trash or recycle containers at curbside, alley or public right of way prior to 4 p.m. on the day prior to the scheduled collection. Containers must be removed prior to noon on the day following collection day.

All trash and recycle containers must be kept securely closed to prevent the contents from scattering and to keep the contents inaccessible to insects, rodents and other animals. Persons are responsible for cleaning up any spillage or debris resulting from such placement or from collection activities.

## Push Cart Vendors & Sale of Merchandise

It is unlawful to conduct business in the city without a valid business tax license (6.04.260). Except under a permit issued pursuant to Chapter 15.60 or as provided in Section 10.22.040(B), it is unlawful to sell or display any merchandise upon or within a public sidewalk or right-of-way, any public park or any other public property (10.22.040).

It is also unlawful to sell merchandise from a pushcart except pursuant to a permit issued by the city council. An identifying decal issued by the finance director must be affixed to each licensed pushcart (10.22.050). Section 10.22.070 of the MuniCode covers consequences and fines. To obtain a National City Business License, see the Finance Department.

## Disaster Preparedness

The National City Fire Department's Emergency Management Program offers a six-step preparedness journey:
1. Know your risks. National City's most significant hazards include earthquakes, flooding, hazardous materials incidents, extreme heat and power outages.
2. Make a written family emergency plan. The County of San Diego's Personal Disaster Plan can be downloaded in English, Español and Tagalog. A version for individuals with Access and Functional Needs is also available in the same three languages.
3. Build an emergency kit with water, non-perishable food, flashlights, batteries, first aid supplies, prescription medications, important documents, cell phone chargers, cash and pet supplies.
4. Stay informed by signing up for AlertSanDiego, learning about earthquake early warning notifications, following official City communication channels and monitoring trusted local news sources.
5. Learn lifesaving skills: CPR/AED & First Aid Classes, Hands-Only CPR, Vial of Life, PulsePoint, and Spanish-language courses (hemorrhage control, LISTOS, CERT, first aid).
6. Get involved with the Community Emergency Response Team (CERT), LISTOS California, or the Annual Fire Department Open House.

Source: https://www.nationalcityca.gov/community/disaster-preparedness

## Fire Plan Reviews & Permits

The Fire Department reviews fire protection plans and issues permits so that new construction, tenant improvements, fire protection systems, and other regulated projects comply with the California Fire Code and applicable local regulations. The process is:
1. Schedule a pre-development meeting (recommended for Tenant Improvements, New Construction, and Accessory Dwelling Units) by calling (619) 336-4553.
2. Review the Contractor Requirement handouts.
3. Complete the Permit Application appropriate for your project.
4. Submit the completed application by email at fire@nationalcityca.gov.
5. Provide three sets of construction plans, a California Contractor License, a National City Business License, and the completed Credit Card Payment form.
6. Pay fees according to the City's adopted Permit Fee Schedule.
7. Fire Prevention personnel review the plans. If revisions are required, comments are provided for resubmittal.
8. Once plans are approved and fees paid, the permit is issued and fire safety inspections are scheduled as the project progresses.

Do not begin work before obtaining approved plans and the required permits. Most plan reviews are completed within 10 to 30 business days, depending on the complexity of the project. Questions: (619) 336-4550 or fire@nationalcityca.gov.

Source: https://www.nationalcityca.gov/government/fire/fire-prevention/get-plan-reviews-permits

## Scheduling Fire Business Inspections

The Fire Prevention Division conducts inspections for new businesses, tenant improvements, fire protection systems, operational permits, and other projects requiring Fire Department approval. Contact the Fire Prevention Division at least 48 hours before your requested inspection date.

Provide the project or business address, the type of inspection requested (pre-inspection, final inspection, etc.), the fire protection system or project being inspected, and a contact name and phone number. Before the inspection, all work should be completed, approved plans available if required, fire protection systems operational, and a responsible representative present. Do not request a final inspection until all work is complete, because incomplete projects may require additional inspections and applicable reinspection fees.

Phone: (619) 336-4550. Email: fire@nationalcityca.gov.

Source: https://www.nationalcityca.gov/government/fire/fire-prevention/schedule-business-inspections

## Business Self-Inspection Program

The program allows eligible low-risk commercial businesses, as determined by the National City Fire Department, to complete an annual fire and life safety inspection using a self-inspection checklist. Businesses that require annual fire inspections under the California Fire Code continue to receive scheduled on-site inspections.

Participating businesses review the checklist, inspect their business, correct any fire and life safety deficiencies, and submit the completed form by the requested deadline. The Business Self-Inspection Form is available in English, Spanish and Tagalog. The Fire Prevention Division reviews all submitted forms and may conduct follow-up inspections when necessary.

Source: https://www.nationalcityca.gov/government/fire/fire-prevention/business-self-inspection-program

## Request a Fire Report

Fire report requests are processed through the City's NextRequest Public Records Portal, where you can submit a request online, communicate with City staff, and track the status of your request. Include as much information as possible: incident date, approximate incident time (if known), incident address or location, your name, your contact information, and any additional information that may help locate the report.

Pursuant to the California Public Records Act, copy fees are $1.00 for the first page and $0.15 for each additional page. Applicable fees are communicated during the request process. If the incident is under investigation, the final fire report may not be available until the investigation is completed.

Source: https://www.nationalcityca.gov/government/fire/fire-administration/get-fire-reports

## Emergency Medical Services

The Fire Department's EMS Program provides 24-hour emergency medical response throughout National City, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year. The Department partners with American Medical Response (AMR) for ambulance transport. Firefighters often arrive first and begin patient assessment and treatment while AMR crews respond.

Since the Paramedic Program launched in 2007, National City firefighters have provided Advanced Life Support (ALS) on frontline emergency apparatus whenever staffing permits. Firefighter/paramedics may provide advanced patient assessment, cardiac monitoring, airway management, medication administration, trauma care, CPR and defibrillation, and other advanced lifesaving interventions.

## Fire Operations Division

The Fire Operations Division provides 24-hour emergency response to fires, medical emergencies, rescues, hazardous materials incidents, traffic collisions, and other emergency and non-emergency requests for service, operating from three fire stations. It is supervised by the on-duty Battalion Chief. Staffing consists of (4) Battalion Chiefs, (12) Captains, (9) Engineers, (18) Firefighters, and (1) EMS Coordinator.

Community programs include Become a Firefighter, Emergency Medical Services, Vial of Life Program, Pulse Point AED Program, Safe Needle Disposal, Schedule Fire Station Tours, Schedule Ride-Along Program, and CPR/AED & First Aid Classes.

## Become a Firefighter

Minimum qualifications are: at least 18 years of age; high school diploma or equivalent; valid California Driver's License; and Emergency Medical Technician (EMT1A) certification at the time of hire. Applicants must also successfully complete the Candidate Physical Ability Test (CPAT) or another approved physical ability examination. Highly desirable qualifications include a California Paramedic License, Fire Academy Certificate, career or volunteer firefighting experience, and Fire Science education.

To get started, complete the required education and certifications, obtain your EMT1A certification, complete a Fire Academy, complete the CPAT, and apply when recruitments are announced by the City. Current recruitments are administered through the Human Resources Department. More information is at JoinNCFD.org.

Source: https://www.nationalcityca.gov/government/fire/fire-operations/become-a-firefighter

## Trauma Intervention Program (TIP)

The Trauma Intervention Program (TIP) is a nonprofit volunteer organization that works with public safety agencies. Trained volunteers, requested by police and fire personnel, respond to provide emotional first aid and practical support to people affected by traumatic events. They may assist families after a sudden death, survivors of suicide loss, victims of fire, traffic collisions or violent crime, children, older adults in crisis, and witnesses.

To become a volunteer, applicants must successfully complete the TIP Training Academy and pass a background check. Information on upcoming academies is available through the Trauma Intervention Program of San Diego. Website: https://tipsandiego.org. Email: info@tipsandiego.org. Phone: (855) 847-7343.

Source: https://www.nationalcityca.gov/government/fire/fire-administration/join-trauma-intervention-program
