## Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) Program — Overview and Waiting List

The National City Housing Authority (NCHA) administers the Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) Program for eligible low-income households and seniors who live or work in National City. It is federally funded through the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD).

Due to insufficient federal funding and no additional funding anticipated, NCHA is not able to select new families from the Section 8 HCV waiting list, cannot provide an estimated waiting period, and does not anticipate selecting new families in the near future. With current funding, NCHA can only support families already participating in the program. Residents are asked to continue to monitor the website for updates.

## HCV Eligibility and Income Limits

To be eligible, the household must be either a family or a single person whose income does not exceed the area limits set by HUD. Only applicants meeting Extremely Low and Very Low-Income standards are eligible. Low-Income standards are used only when, under certain circumstances, owners convert from a project-based to a tenant-based program. Congress requires that 75% of new admissions to the tenant-based program and 40% of new admissions to the project-based programs be Extremely Low-Income. The remaining new admissions may not exceed the Very Low-Income limit.

HUD income limit definitions: Extremely Low-Income is income not exceeding the higher of the federal poverty level or 30% of area median income. Very Low-Income is 50% of area median income. Low-Income is 80% of area median income.

## HCV Participant Responsibilities

Participants in the NCHA Housing Choice Voucher Program have the following responsibilities:
- Pay their portion of the rent in accordance with the rental agreement and family obligations
- Notify your National City Housing Specialist and property owner/agent of any changes in the household composition
- Pay for utilities and services as outlined in the rental agreement
- Keep the unit clean and undamaged
- Not engage in any illegal activities
- Be responsible for the actions of their guest(s)
- Permit access to the unit for repairs
- Refrain from disturbing others
- Allow only occupants on the rental agreement to reside in the unit
- Abide by the terms of the Housing Choice Voucher, the rental agreement, federal regulations and applicable NCHA Administrative policies

## Emergency Housing Vouchers (EHV)

## HCV Port In / Port Out Procedures

Port In (moving your voucher to National City Housing): Paperwork for an incoming transfer can take up to 30 days to process after NCHA receives the initial paperwork from your current housing agency. Have a temporary place to stay and money to support your family until the transfer process is done. NCHA cannot begin the transfer until it receives a Portability Packet from your current housing agency. NCHA is not absorbing at this time, only billing. NCHA only services the National City 91950 area (excluding Lincoln Acres). Ask your housing agency to mail or fax the portability packet to: National City Voucher Program, Housing Choice Voucher Program, 140 E 12th Street, Suite B, National City CA 91950. Email: Section8@nationalcityca.gov.

Port Out (moving out of NCHA's jurisdiction): Contact your housing specialist for additional information and to receive a Request to Utilize Portability Form. Due to a funding shortage, participants can only port out to a housing agency that will absorb the voucher. HUD's website is www.hud.gov, or call HUD toll-free at 1-800-955-2232 for a list of Housing Agencies.

## Fair Housing — Tenant and Landlord Services (CSA San Diego County)

CSA San Diego County is National City's contracted fair housing service provider. Open 8:00 AM - 4:30 PM, Monday - Friday. Toll free: (800) 954-0441. Office: (619) 444-5700. If you are deaf, hard of hearing, or require telephone relay services, dial 711. Email: outreach@c4sa.org.

CSA is a HUD Approved Housing Counseling nonprofit agency that will investigate report, monitor, and provide counseling to tenants and training to landlords on fair housing law. Everyone in National City is protected by State and Federal Fair Housing laws. The City operates under the U.S. Fair Housing Law (Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1968) and other federal and State of California housing laws which prohibit discrimination on the basis of sex, sexual orientation, race, color, religion, national origin, marital status, familial status (presence of children) or physical disability.

## CDBG & HOME Grant Programs

The National City Housing Authority administers the Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) and Home Investment Partnerships Act (HOME) program funds, received by the City as a direct entitlement from HUD. The programs are primarily designed to assist low- to moderate-income households by providing affordable housing, revitalizing neighborhoods, and creating employment opportunities through economic development.

Required plans and reports include the Five-Year Consolidated Plan (2025-2029 Five-Year Consolidated Plan, PDF), the Annual Action Plan (Annual Action Plan FY 26-27; the AAP was submitted to HUD annually prior to May 15th), the Consolidated Annual Performance and Evaluation Report (CAPER, which the City must submit to HUD by September 28 at the close of each fiscal year; FY 24-25 CAPER posted), and the Citizen and Community Participation Plan (Fourth Amendment approved per Resolution 2025-75). The City has been allocated HOME-American Rescue Plan Act (HOME-ARP) funding for affordable rental housing; HOME-ARP Project: Union Tower (Awarded $1.5M). The City participates in the San Diego Regional Alliance for Fair Housing and contracts with CSA San Diego County.

Source: https://www.nationalcityca.gov/government/national-city-housing-authority/cdbg-home-programs
