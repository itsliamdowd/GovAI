## Temporary Use Permits (TUPs) — What Requires One

Temporary Use Permits (TUPs) are used for special activities, events, or structures that are beneficial to the public for limited periods of time, even though they would not comply with building, fire, zoning, or other local codes. Chapter 15.60 of the National City Municipal Code regulates these permits. In general, any organized activity that uses public property, facilities, parks, sidewalks, streets, or any public right-of-ways requires this permit. In some cases, activities or events on private property also require a TUP. There are 3 types: Class A, Class B, and Class C.

## TUP Classes and Examples

Class A requires City Council approval. Examples include outdoor auctions by charitable or philanthropic organizations (one per year), balloon rides/races, block or holiday parties, carnivals/circuses/traveling shows, fairs/bazaars/trade shows, temporary farmer's markets, musical concerts/festivals, parades/bicycle tours/walks/runs on city streets, political and religious activities outdoors or in temporary structures, and photography/filming that requires street closures and/or traffic control in excess of three minutes or involves stunts or special effects. Uses/activities exceeding the duration in Section 15.60.040 of the NCMC require City Council approval.

Class B is issued by the Neighborhood Services Department and is subject to conditions and City Codes, as applicable. Examples include photography/filming without street closures/traffic control in excess of three minutes and without stunts or special effects; construction-office mobile homes/trailers on active construction sites (duration of construction); temporary offices or classrooms after a building permit is issued for a permanent building on-site (duration of one year); sales office/model home in condominiums or subdivisions (duration of one year); and temporary sales office at condominiums or subdivisions (duration of six months).

Class C is subject to Business License Regulations. Examples include Christmas tree sales lots; garage sales (maximum four consecutive days and not more than two per calendar year); outdoor fire sales (15 days at the site where the fire occurred); rummage sales by local charitable or philanthropic organizations; and special promotion/outdoor sales/displays (three days, not more than two per calendar year, with a minimum of thirty days between activities). Uses/activities exceeding the duration specified on the business license require City Council approval.

## TUP Fees

Class A Use: the City Council has established the fee for processing Class A TUP applications at 100 percent of all direct and indirect costs incurred by City departments reviewing the application, with a maximum fee of $356 unless the City Council approves a higher fee. The T.U.P. application fee equals the total costs of all departments reviewing the application. If a department's costs for a specific application exceed the average cost, the actual cost incurred is used.

Contact the Building & Safety Department for the T.U.P. application fee for: Special Event Permit: 350.00; Class A Use: $487.00; Class B Use: $359.00; Class C Use: $205.00.

All fees paid to "City of National City" for temporary use permit activities that are withdrawn or not exercised, after the application has been processed, are non-refundable. ## Building Permits — Types and When Required

Most building construction projects or improvements to buildings require a building permit of some kind, with some exceptions. A permit is required for all construction-related activities involving the construction or erection of new buildings and structures, or improvements, alterations, or additions to existing buildings regulated by the building codes and ordinances of the City of National City and the State of California or as determined by the Building Official.

Permit types: a Building Permit is required to construct or modify a building or structure, including pools, signs, or towers. An Electrical Permit is required to install or modify an electrical system. A Plumbing Permit is required to install or modify a plumbing system. A Mechanical Permit is required to install or modify heating, ventilation, refrigeration, air conditioning and other related systems. A Combination Building Permit is required where the construction involves multiple trades and covers all the building, electrical, plumbing, and mechanical work necessary for the job; it is issued for both residential and commercial projects. A Demolition Permit is required to demolish any building, structure, or improvement to such.

## Building Permits — Who May Pull a Permit and Permit Validity

Building permits are available to properly licensed contractors working on behalf of and as authorized by the tenant or property owner. They are also available to owners of one- and two-family dwellings, provided the work is done by the owner, a member of their immediate family, or individuals hired by them, and the owner resides or intends to reside in the dwelling.

A building permit becomes null and void if the authorized work is not commenced within one year of the issuance date, or if work is suspended or abandoned for a period of one year after it has commenced. A permit may be extended for an additional period of up to 180 days or as determined by the Building Official, if the applicant submits a written request with reasons to the Building Division before the original permit expires. Extensions are also regulated by Section 15.24.045 of the National City Municipal Code. An Info Guide (PDF) on how to obtain a Building Permit is available on the page.

## Building Inspections — How to Schedule

Inspections can be scheduled online through National City's web-based inspection tracking system, which also lets you track real-time results of a completed inspection from a cell phone, tablet, or computer. You must first create a user account, which can be used for future permits. The inspection request requires: Name; Job Address; Contact Information (Email and/or Phone Number); Date of Inspection; Inspection Type; Permit Number; whether the property is Commercial or Residential; and a preferred time frame of AM or PM. A Today's Inspection Schedule page shows the approximate time the inspector will arrive.

Inspections must be requested 24 hours in advance. Inspections are conducted during the approximate hours of 9:00am – 12:00pm and 1:00pm – 4:00pm, Monday – Thursday; inspections are not conducted on Fridays. Field inspectors are available to answer questions about projects under construction from 7:00 a.m. – 8:30 a.m. and 5:00 p.m. - 6:00 p.m., Monday through Thursday.

## Building Inspections — At the Time of Inspection

With a building permit you receive a Building Inspection Record card and a set of approved plans. Both must be kept on the job site and made available to the inspector at the time of inspection or as requested. The person doing the work must notify the Building Division that work is ready for inspection and must provide proper access and means for the inspector to inspect the work, as inspectors do not carry ladders or special tools.

The inspector notes the results on the inspection record card. If the work is satisfactory, work may continue to the next phase. If the work deviates from the approved plans or specifications, or violates adopted building codes and standards, a correction notice is posted on the job site, and re-inspection of the corrected work is required before moving to the next phase or as allowed by the inspector.

## Building Inspections — Inspection Types

General building inspections: Footings/Reinforcing/Setbacks (before any concrete is poured); Framing; Concrete Slab or Under-Floor; Diaphragm/Shear; Insulation (an insulation certificate is accepted in lieu of this inspection for single-family dwellings or duplexes); Lath or Drywall/Gypsum Board (before joints and fasteners are taped and finished, or stucco or veneer is applied); and Final Inspection. ## Fire Department Plan Review and Permits

The National City Fire Department reviews fire protection plans and issues permits for new construction, tenant improvements, fire protection systems, and other regulated projects to ensure compliance with the California Fire Code and applicable local regulations. Before submitting, review the Contractor Requirements for your project, gather all required plans and supporting documentation, and complete the appropriate Permit Application.

Process: (1) A pre-development meeting is recommended for Tenant Improvements, New Construction, and Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs); to schedule, call (619) 336-4553. (2) Review the applicable Contractor Requirement handouts. (3) Complete the Permit Application appropriate for your project. (4) Submit the completed application electronically via email at fire@nationalcityca.gov. (5) Provide three sets of construction plans, a California Contractor License, a National City Business License, and the completed Credit Card Payment form. (6) Pay fees according to the City of National City's adopted Permit Fee Schedule. (7) Fire Prevention personnel review the plans; if revisions are required, comments are provided for resubmittal. (8) Once plans are approved and all applicable fees are paid, the permit is issued and fire safety inspections are scheduled as the project progresses.

Do not begin work before obtaining approved plans and the required permits. Most plan reviews are completed within 10 to 30 business days, depending on the complexity of the project. Questions: Fire Prevention Division, (619) 336-4550, fire@nationalcityca.gov.

Source: https://www.nationalcityca.gov/government/fire/fire-prevention/get-plan-reviews-permits

## Fire Prevention — Schedule a Business Inspection

The Fire Prevention Division conducts inspections for new businesses, tenant improvements, fire protection systems, operational permits, and other projects requiring Fire Department approval. Contact the Fire Prevention Division at least 48 hours before your requested inspection date. Provide the project or business address; the type of inspection requested (pre-inspection, final inspection, etc.); the fire protection system or project being inspected; and a contact name and phone number.

Before the inspection, make sure all work has been completed, approved plans are available if required, fire protection systems are operational, and a responsible representative is present. Do not request a final inspection until all work has been completed; incomplete projects may require additional inspections and applicable reinspection fees. Phone: (619) 336-4550. Email: fire@nationalcityca.gov.

Source: https://www.nationalcityca.gov/government/fire/fire-prevention/schedule-business-inspections
