## Fire Station Tours

The San Marcos Fire Department provides services from four fire stations located throughout the City. Fire station tours let the public learn about the department, its facilities and equipment, and pick up fire safety tips.

To schedule a tour, contact the station nearest you or call SMFD at (760) 744-1050, ext. 3400. Administrative business hours are Monday through Thursday from 7:30 am to 5:30 pm, and alternating Fridays. The department's service area is 33 square miles.

## Regional Emergency Services Training Center

The San Marcos Regional Emergency Services Training Center is at 184 Santar Place in San Marcos, with easy access from highway 78. It serves fire, law enforcement, emergency medical and other related emergency service organizations.

The center has a fire/rescue drill tower and an instructional building. Floors one through three have re-configurable floor plans and an artificial smoke environment. The fourth floor has a ventilation prop for a flat or pitched roof. The tower also has an environmental burn room for live fire training and a working sprinkler and standpipe system. The drill ground has a fire pump test facility and an all-weather prop area for vehicle extrication and controlled propane/natural gas exercises.

For more information, contact Maria Conklin, (760) 744-1050 ext. 3400.

## Calling 9-1-1 and Non-Emergency Numbers

Do not call 9-1-1 unless the emergency threatens human life or property and demands immediate attention. For non-emergency information or assistance, call the fire department at 760-744-1050, ext. 3400 (during business hours) or the sheriff's department at 858-868-3200.

Within San Marcos, 9-1-1 calls are first routed through the San Diego County Sheriff's Communication Center, which transfers the call to the appropriate agency. After a transfer, be ready to repeat the address of the emergency, your phone number and the type of emergency. A landline shows your number and address to the dispatcher. A cell phone shows only the general vicinity. From a cell phone, give cross streets or a landmark if you are unsure of your location, and call from a landline if you can.

TDD callers (Baudot Mode only): dial 9-1-1 and tap the space bar until someone answers.

## Prepare for Emergencies

In a disaster you may be without immediate help for up to 72 hours, so maintain an emergency supply kit. Make a family plan and know your hazards. The page recommends these apps: Genasys Protect, Alert San Diego App, SDGE Alerts and the San Marcos City App. It also recommends joining CERT.

If an evacuation is ordered, follow local officials' instructions on routes and shelters. Do not return to an evacuated area until officials say it is safe. Official information sources are the City website, San Marcos Fire Department, San Diego County Sheriff, Alert San Diego, SDGE, Genasys Protect and 2-1-1.

Key terms:
- **Advisory:** Be prepared to evacuate at any time. This is the first level of warning.
- **Evacuation WARNING:** A potential threat to life and property. An evacuation order may be issued if conditions worsen.
- **Evacuation ORDER:** Leave the designated area immediately because of an immediate threat.
- **Shelter in place:** Take refuge in a designated safe location within a building instead of evacuating.

Contact: San Marcos Fire Department, 1 Civic Center Drive, San Marcos, CA 92069, (760)744-1050, ext. 3400.

## Heavy Rain Safety and Sandbags

Free, fill-your-own sandbags are available at Hollandia Park (12 Mission Hills Ct.). Supplies are replenished when storms are approaching, and park hours are extended as needed.

Other tips: avoid flooded roads and never drive, ride or walk through moving water. Secure outdoor items, keep storm drains near your property clear, and keep an emergency kit ready. Sign up for alerts at ReadySanDiego.org. Emergency radio stations are KOGO 600 AM, KLSD 1360 AM, KPBS 89.5 FM and NOAA Weather Radio 162.40 MHz.

## Power Outages

During an outage, use flashlights or battery lamps and avoid candles or open flames. Unplug large appliances and wait a few minutes after power returns before turning everything back on. If you see downed power lines, don't touch them. Call 911 and report to SDG&E at 1-800-411-7343.

Never connect a generator to the grid. During wildfire season, SDG&E may preemptively turn off power (PSPS) and offers generator and battery rebates for qualifying residents.

## El Niño and Flood Preparedness

El Niño can bring heavy rain, strong storms and localized flooding. Before the rainy season, residents are advised to:
- Clear gutters, storm drains and drainage areas.
- Inspect roofs.
- Secure loose outdoor items.
- Keep drainage channels and culverts clear.
- Learn how to fill and place sandbags (a PDF guide is linked on the page).

During a flood, avoid walking or driving through flood waters. Six inches of moving water can knock you down, and two feet can sweep a vehicle away. Move to higher ground if flash flooding is possible, and wait for officials to say it is safe before returning to an evacuated area.

For alerts, register mobile phones with Alert San Diego, download the SD Emergency app (iPhone and Android), or use Genasys Protect. The page also suggests tuning to radio station AM 1610.

## CERT (Community Emergency Response Team)

The San Marcos CERT program has 100 members available to assist first responders and the City in a disaster. The team has quarterly training and a drill once a year. Training covers disaster preparation, extinguishing small fires and controlling utilities, basic first aid and triage, search and rescue, incident command, disaster psychology, elements of terrorism, and wildland fires and evacuations.

The City offers a CERT Academy once a year with Escondido Fire Department, held in San Marcos. There is no cost to attend. Classes are 4 to 8 hour commitments, usually one or two days a week in the evening or on the weekend, for three weeks. To join the CERT team you must be over 18, a resident of San Marcos, and pass a background check.

## Defensible Space Inspections (AB38)

To schedule a local vegetation management defensible space inspection with the fire department, call 760-744-1050 ext. 2409 or fill out the San Marcos Fire Defensible Space Inspection Request Form linked on the page.

Under AB38, a seller of real property in a high or very high fire severity zone, on or after January 1, 2021, must give the buyer a standardized disclosure notice on how to fire harden a home and which features may be vulnerable to wildfire and flying embers.

A 2% Transaction Fee applies to all credit and debit card payments made to the City of San Marcos. The City still accepts check and cash payments with no fees.

## Weed Abatement and Wildfire Mitigation

The City of San Marcos has an ordinance that prohibits the accumulation of weeds, grass and vegetation. The fire department runs an annual vegetation management program in which properties are inspected. To report hazardous weed conditions, or with questions about abating weeds on your own property, call 760-744-1050, ext. 3409.

Defensible space guidance:
- Keep the area 30 to 50 feet immediately around a home or structure treated, cleared or reduced.
- Within the remaining 70 to 150 feet of defensible space, space plants horizontally and vertically, thin native vegetation, and remove lower tree branches at least six feet from the ground. The minimum is 100 feet from structures built prior to January 1, 2004, and 150 feet for those built after.
- Clear flammable vegetation to at least 10 feet from roads.
- Keep turnarounds on dead-end roads and long driveways clear.
- Post your address on a highly contrasting background, visible from the street.

If your property may contain rare, threatened or endangered species or sensitive habitat, contact the City's Planning Division at 760-744-1050, ext. 3237. Register cell phones with Alert San Diego (www.AlertSanDiego.org) for Reverse 9-1-1 calls.
