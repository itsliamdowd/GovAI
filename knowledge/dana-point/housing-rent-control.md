## Short-Term Rental (STR) Permits — Caps and Priority

An STR Permit is a City-issued authorization that allows property owners in Dana Point to legally operate a short-term rental. The City adopted STR regulations in 2023 to allow the issuance of new STRs. The regulations set a permit cap of 115 in the Coastal Zone, and a cap of 115 outside the Coastal Zone with a sub-cap of 60 for Non-Primary STRs. Once the cap is met, applications that did not receive a permit are automatically placed on a waitlist.

In the Coastal Zone, the maximum of 115 permits applies to Non-Primary, Multi-Family, and Mixed Use Parcel STRs. Home Stay and Primary STRs are not associated with the cap, but each time a Home Stay or Primary STR Permit is issued, the 115 cap is reduced by one. Outside the Coastal Zone, the total cap of 115 applies to all STR types, with a maximum of 60 Non-Primary STRs.

Priority for permit issuance, in and out of the Coastal Zone, is: (1) Primary STRs and Home Stay STRs, (2) Multi-Family Homestays, (3) Mixed-Use Parcel STRs, (4) Non-Primary STRs. For application questions or to be added to the interested parties list, contact Antoinette Kulick, (949) 248-3565, stroperations@danapoint.org. The page also links an STR Inspection Checklist, Coastal Zone STR Regulations, Outside Coastal Zone STR Regulations, an STR Owner List, and an interactive map of permitted STRs.

## Short-Term Rentals in HOAs in the Coastal Zone

STR permit applications must include evidence that the proposed STR is not legally prohibited by the applicable homeowner's association conditions, covenants, and restrictions (CC+Rs). Without HOA approval, the City will not issue the STR permit. No action is required for HOAs that allow STRs.

The California Coastal Commission (CCC) took the position that HOAs may only deny an STR if the rule for doing so is "legal." An HOA's prohibition may be legal if it predates 1976 or if the HOA has a coastal development permit (CDP) authorizing the ban on STRs. The City's program does not explicitly require a CDP to prohibit STRs. For HOAs seeking to make their regulations legally defensible, the City is offering a streamlined CDP process with a reduced fee (subject to City Council approval). ## Short-Term Rental Good Neighbor Rules

Short-term rental operators are expected to have their visitors follow these rules:
- **Noise:** No outside noise from the short-term rental may be heard during the quiet hours of 10:00 P.M to 7:00 A.M.
- **Parking:** A maximum of two parking spaces are allowed per unit unless additional parking is approved by the City. Use the driveway and/or garage before spots on the street.
- **Occupancy:** Maximum overnight occupancy is two persons per bedroom plus two additional persons within the unit. Daytime maximum occupancy is 2.5 times the overnight occupancy but not to exceed 20 people. The property owner may impose more restrictive limits.
- **Trash:** Trash may not be stored where it is visible from the street and sidewalk. The owner must provide sufficient trash containers and take the trash and recycling to the curb.

## Short-Term Rental Complaints — How to Report

If visitors at a permitted short-term rental are not following the rules, the City encourages these steps:
1. Contact the owner of the short-term rental and let them know their tenants are violating the Good Neighbor Policy (owner/management info is on the STR Owner/Manager Contact List).
2. Contact Code Enforcement at the City of Dana Point: (949) 248-3564.
3. Contact the Short-Term Rental Hotline: (949) 234-7549.
4. Contact the OC Sheriff's Department (OCSD): (949) 770-6011.

Provide a name and return phone number. All calls made to the City and OCSD are kept confidential and treated as an anonymous complaint, but code enforcement needs a contact person to verify that a violation is occurring.

## Housing Element (2021-2029) Update

The Housing Element is a chapter of the General Plan that sets the City's strategy to meet the housing needs of current and future residents. State law requires it to include the City's housing strategy for existing and projected housing demand, identify adequate sites for the City's allocated share of regional housing needs, and analyze local policies, regulations and requirements that could constrain housing development for all income levels. The Public Safety Element is updated alongside it to meet Senate Bill 1035 (2017).

The City's 2021-2029 Housing Element had to be adopted by February 11, 2022 to avoid any penalty. Adoption hearings were held before the Planning Commission on December 12, 2021 and the City Council on February 1, 2022.
