## Online Building Permit Applications (eTRAKiT)

All building permit applications are submitted online through the eTRAKit Permit Portal. Solar permits are submitted separately through Symbium.

To submit an application, log in, select "Apply" and choose your permit type. Enter the project address and scope of work, upload your plans and required documents, then submit. You'll receive a confirmation email. Upload clear, complete PDFs and name each file by what it is (for example, "Site Plan" or "Structural Calcs"). The submission checklist lists what each permit type requires.

## eTRAKiT Account Creation and Login Help

Contractors register or reset their password using their State License Number. Homeowners and everyone else use Public Registration. Full step-by-step instructions with screenshots are in the Create Your Account Guide.

If you are locked out, go to the eTRAKiT portal and click "Forgot Password" (top right). Contractors choose "Contractor," enter their email, and click "Reset Password." A contractor's State License Number is both the username and the entity number. If you didn't get a reset email, or your email isn't recognized, see the Create Your Account Guide or call Eve Cuddihy at (949) 248-3564.

## Permit Application Status and Review Times

After you submit, your application moves through several statuses in eTRAKit:
- Under Review: the city is reviewing your application; uploads are paused during this time.
- Plan Check Fees Due: pay your plan check fees so review can continue.
- Pending Resubmittal: corrections are ready; view them in the portal and resubmit.
- Approved with Conditions: approved with conditions; the city will email you directly.
- Ready to Issue: pay your issuance fees; you'll be notified automatically.
- Permit Issued: your permit is issued, and documents are available on the portal.
- Declined: your application wasn't accepted; the city will email you directly.

Standard plan checks are reviewed within 10 business days. Larger commercial, mixed-use, or multifamily projects take 15 to 20 business days for the first review.

## Corrections and Resubmittals

If your permit comes back for corrections, you'll see the status "Pending Resubmittal" and receive an email. Open your permit in the portal to view the correction comments and any marked-up plans. Upload your revised documents and resubmit through the portal. If you can't find your corrections, call Eve at (949) 248-3564.

For help with permitting, call Eve Cuddihy at (949) 248-3564. The Help for Homeowners page explains what needs a permit, who to contact first, and what to expect. How-to videos are available for Create an Account and Submit an Application; Respond to Corrections is listed as "Coming Soon!"

## Subdivisions — Tentative Maps and Approval Process

Subdividing a single, legal parcel into four (4) or fewer new parcels or units requires a Tentative Parcel Map. Subdividing a single, legal parcel into five (5) or more parcels or units requires a Tentative Tract Map. Subdivisions include condominium projects as defined in Section 1350 of the California Civil Code and community apartment projects as defined in Section 11004 of the Business and Professions Code.

All maps must be approved by the City Planning Commission ("Subdivision Committee") at a public hearing and subsequently recorded with the County of Orange. Issues of concern may include conformance with minimum lot size or lot area per unit requirements; dedication and/or improvement of adjacent public rights-of-way to ultimate standards in conformance with the General Plan Circulation Element; installation of, or commitment to install, all site infrastructure (streets, water and sewer lines, etc.); conformance with the City's Subdivision Code and the California Subdivision Map Act as to form and content; and conformance with the standards for Vesting Tentative Tract Maps (if applicable).

For questions, contact the Planning Division and/or the Building/Safety Division at 949-248-3564. Staff are available to discuss proposals Monday – Thursday, 7:30AM to 5:00PM and 7:30AM – 4:00PM on Fridays.

## Wildfire Preparedness — Fire Hazard Severity Zones and Building Standards

Dana Point is a Fire Risk Reduction Community, a statewide designation adopted by Resolution No. 2026-01. Property owners may be eligible for insurance discounts through California's Safer from Wildfire program. The City will be prioritized for wildfire prevention and mitigation grants.

An interactive map lets you enter your address to find out whether your property is in a designated Fire Hazard Severity Zone. Depending on your property's designation, State law may require:
- Defensible Space: Properties in Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zones must maintain 100 feet of defensible space around structures, with the most intensive vegetation management within the first 30 feet.
- Home Sales: Sellers must disclose a property's Fire Hazard Severity Zone designation and provide documentation showing compliance with defensible space requirements.
- Building Standards: New construction and qualifying remodels must comply with California's Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI) building standards, which include fire-resistant roofing, exterior walls, windows, vents, and other wildfire-resistant features.
- Zone 0 (Coming Soon): The State is developing new regulations that will require an ember-resistant area within five feet of structures by limiting combustible vegetation and materials.

Questions: State Fire Marshal, FHSZinformation@fire.ca.gov or (916) 633-7655; City of Dana Point, jmccloskey@danapoint.org; California Department of Insurance, (800) 927-4357.
