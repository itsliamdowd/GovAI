## Street Sweeping — Schedule and Parking Rules

The City of Dana Point manages a citywide contract for street sweeping. Street sweeping occurs once per week on all public streets in Dana Point. Residents must move their cars out of the street on street sweeping day so the sweeper can sweep the entire street without weaving in between parked cars.

Parking is restricted on certain streets during street sweeping hours. Generally, parking restrictions are not considered if the street sweeper can reach at least fifty (50) percent of the curb on any given street or in any given neighborhood. Streets that did not meet this criterion were designated for parking restrictions via City Council Resolution. In unrestricted areas where cars are not being moved, staff use neighborhood mailers, website information, temporary no parking signs in challenging areas, and Police Services citations for stored vehicles exceeding the 72 hour limit on public streets.

To find when your street is swept, use the search feature on the Street Sweeping page or the Citywide Street Sweeping Maps (PDF, 2MB) at the bottom of the page. For concerns about how street sweeping is operating on your street, contact the Public Works Department at (949)248-3554 or publicworks@danapoint.org.

## Street Sweeping — Holiday Weeks

Trash collection does not occur on these designated holidays: New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving Day, and Christmas Day. On weeks when a holiday occurs, trash service is delayed, which affects street sweeping because trash cans interfere.

During holiday weeks (except Christmas and New Year's Day weeks), the City continues to sweep residential areas and enforce as much as possible, working around trash can conflicts. The City also sweeps arterial streets such as PCH, Golden Lantern, and Crown Valley during all holiday weeks. Direct questions to publicworks@danapoint.org or call Public Works at (949) 248-3554.

## Preferential Parking District (Dana Hills High School)

In September of 1999, the City Council established the first and only Preferential Parking District in Dana Point around Dana Hills High School, per Chapter 12.14 of the Dana Point Municipal Code. Its intent is to prevent student parking on residential streets around the high school. The district has been expanded several times to include additional streets. The City has also added parking on Golden Lantern and Stonehill Drive.

To obtain a parking permit, a resident must prove to the satisfaction of the City that he or she resides within the district. The resident must also prove that the vehicle is currently registered in his or her name and registered in the State of California. Residents may also request guest permits. On any given day, temporary guest permits may be issued for larger groups, and each is only valid on the chosen day. The City asks for at least 72 hours notice for temporary parking permits.

Any vehicle parked in the district during the specified hours without a valid residential or guest permit will receive a parking ticket from the Dana Point Police Department. To obtain a residential or guest permit, contact the Public Works & Engineering Services Department at (949) 248-3554.

## Traffic Engineering Program

The Traffic Engineering Program is responsible for safe and efficient day to day traffic operations on the City's street system. It also coordinates long-range transportation planning within the City and other regional transportation programs. The City has a contracted Traffic Engineer. The page links the Traffic Management and Calming Policy (PDF, 13MB) and the OCTA District 5 Bikeways Feasibility Study.

The division writes and processes work orders for traffic related work citywide. This includes new signs, modifications to existing signage and striping, traffic calming, congestion relief, and curb marking modifications. It also oversees the City's traffic signal system, evaluating signal timing and responding to complaints.

## Traffic Signal Timing and Coordination

Signal synchronization is used on a number of the busiest arterial highways. The major arterial streets coordinated in the City include Pacific Coast Highway, Stonehill Drive, Golden Lantern, and Del Obispo. To coordinate signals, each signal in a group uses a common cycle length, which is usually determined by the largest intersection in the group and may be rather long.

On coordinated streets, the side street must wait until the main traffic movement on the arterial has gone through the intersection. The City's coordination goal is to get the greatest number of vehicles through the system with the fewest stops while maintaining safety, so the busiest traffic movements are given precedence over smaller ones.

## Street Maintenance — Who to Contact

Public Works, at publicworks@danapoint.org or 949-248-3554, handles these items:

- asphalt or pothole repairs
- damaged sidewalk, curb, gutter, or other general issues on City owned streets

## Salt Creek Bike Path

The Salt Creek Bike Path parallels the Monarch Beach Golf Course and empties out into Salt Creek Park. It is approximately one mile from the trailhead off of Camino Del Avion to the tunnel at Pacific Coast Highway. The trail type is concrete. It is open Sunday through Saturday, 06:00 AM–10:00 PM.
