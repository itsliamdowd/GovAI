## Fire & Rescue Department — Divisions and Services

The Fire & Rescue Department protects life, property and the environment through its mitigation, prevention, response, and recovery services. The Fire/Rescue/Emergency Medical Services Division staffs 6 fire stations, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. The Fire Prevention & Community Risk Reduction Division provides public education, emergency management, life safety planning and inspections, and code enforcement services. Both divisions are supported by the Fire Administration division.

Fire Prevention/Community Risk Reduction develops and enforces local fire, life safety, property, and environmental protection standards. It enforces State adopted fire & life safety codes, reviews building construction plans, conducts building construction and business inspections, and investigates citizen complaints. It also manages the city's hazardous materials disclosure program.

## Emergency Medical Services

All firefighters on the Costa Mesa Fire Department are trained and equipped to provide emergency medical care. Firefighter-emergency medical technicians provide basic life support level care. Firefighter-mobile intensive care paramedics provide advanced life support level care. There are six fire stations strategically located throughout Costa Mesa.

Firefighters are the first responders for all medical emergencies.

Source: https://www.costamesaca.gov/government/departments-and-divisions/fire-rescue/emergency-medical-services

## Reporting a 9-1-1 Emergency

When reporting a 9-1-1 emergency, be prepared to provide the exact location of the emergency, what is involved (auto, home, vegetation, etc.), the number of persons involved, your location, and the phone number you are calling from so that you can be re-contacted if you become disconnected.

If possible, remain at the scene and provide emergency responders with your eyewitness account of what occurred or what occurred at the time of your arrival at the scene.

## Fire Engine Response Time and Emergency Response

It takes between 3-5 minutes for engines to arrive on scene after a 9-1-1 call has been placed.

The state vehicle code requires that while responding Code Three, an emergency response vehicle must have all emergency lights on and the siren sounding, even at night. A Code Three response is initiated when life, property or the environment is in immediate danger. Examples are chest pain, difficulty breathing, fire, hazardous materials spill, and auto accidents.

## Fire Department Operations Overview

Costa Mesa has over 113,000 residents in an area of 16.8 square miles, and Costa Mesa Fire & Rescue responds to over 12,500 calls annually. The closest emergency response unit is dispatched to the emergency, regardless of city boundary.

Responses come from six fire stations within the City limits. Every firefighter is cross-trained and certified as an EMT and can provide BLS (Basic Life Support) care. The Department has 30 Mobile Intensive Care Paramedics licensed to provide ALS (Advanced Life Support) care. Fire Suppression personnel work 24-hour shifts. The Department also staffs its own Arson Investigation Unit.
