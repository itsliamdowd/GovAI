## Street Maintenance and Pothole Repair

The Street Maintenance Section is responsible for 188 miles of streets and 14 miles of alleys. Its work includes pothole patching, dig-out repairs, overlays, crack seals, and slurry seals. The objective is to repair potholes on City streets and alleys within 24 hours of notification or discovery.

## Sidewalk, Curb and Gutter Trip Hazards

Displaced or damaged concrete sidewalks, curbs and gutters in the public right-of-way are temporarily repaired to remove immediate trip hazards. Trip hazards are repaired by grinding the lifted area. If a sidewalk or apron has too high a vertical displacement (usually above 1.5 inches), it is temporarily ramped with asphalt and/or concrete. The location is then prioritized for future sidewalk removal and replacement, which is handled by the Engineering Division of the Public Works Department.

## Storm Drain Maintenance and Standing Water

Costa Mesa has approximately 42 miles of storm drains and 1,165 catch basins. City staff inspect, maintain and repair the system. This includes clearing blocked drains, removing debris from storm drain/catch basin structures, and cleaning and repairing damaged drain pipes. The goal is to keep debris and pollution from reaching the ocean, in compliance with the National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (NPDES).

Standing Water Hotline: (714) 754-4994.

## Traffic Signal Problems and Signal Operations

The Traffic Operations section responds to traffic signal trouble calls: traffic light outages, four way red flash calls, broken pedestrian pushbutton, and failed vehicle detection. It operates the City's Traffic Operations Center (TOC), which houses the Advanced Traffic Management System (ATMS).

City-owned traffic signal infrastructure maintained by the Traffic Maintenance section includes:
- 127 Traffic Signals
- 20 Battery Back-Up Units
- 48 Closed Circuit Television (CCTV) Cameras
- 7 In-roadway Illuminated Crosswalks
- 47 Electronic Radar Speed Feedback Signs
- 9 Flashing School Beacons
- Citywide Communication Network

The City's traffic signal maintenance contractor is on-call 24 hours a day for emergency and safety issues such as traffic accidents, power outages, pole knockdowns, and signal conflicts.

## Street Sweeping

All public arterial and residential streets are swept once a week. Citywide parking prohibitions are in effect on sweeping days. Signs at the entrance of each residential tract display the day of the week and the hourly window period for sweeping.

On July 19, 2022, City Council approved revised regulations to enforce street sweeping parking regulations at any time during the posted hours. The police department also enforces whenever the street sweeper is actively sweeping. Parking enforcement aides follow the street sweeper, and any vehicle found impeding the sweeper will receive a citation.

## Indigent Parking Citation Payment Plan

Beginning July 1, 2018, the City offers Payment Plans to Indigent Registered Owner(s)/Lessee(s) with unpaid parking citations issued on or after July 1, 2018 for amounts of $300.00 or less.

The application must be requested within 60 calendar days of issuance of the citation, or within 10 days of Administrative Hearing determination, whichever is later. The plan allows payment in up to $25.00 monthly increments, paid off within 18 months of the approval date. Upon approval, a non-refundable Payment Plan fee of $5.00 is assessed to the total Payment Plan amount and all late fees and penalties are waived. Waived late fees and penalties may be reinstated if the person falls out of compliance with the plan.

To show indigence, provide one of the following as applicable:
- Proof of income from the 3 most recent pay stubs showing the individual meets the income criteria in subdivision (b) of Section 68632 of the Government Code.
- Proof of receipt of benefits under the programs in subdivision (a) of Section 68632 of the Government Code: a copy of the most recent Verification of Benefits Form or Award Letter for Social Security.
- If there is no income or the person receives public assistance, a copy of their annual earnings from the Social Security Department.

To request a plan, read and sign the Payment Plan Terms and Conditions and submit it with a signed Request for Indigent Payment Plan & Payment Plan Terms and Conditions Application. Both are available from www.citationprocessingcenter.com and/or the front desk of the Costa Mesa Police Department.

## What to Do After a Traffic Collision

First check for injuries. Do not move injured people unless necessary for their immediate safety (e.g. fire, rollover). If there are no injuries and the vehicles can be driven, move them off the roadway immediately. If they cannot be moved, turn on emergency flashers if they work and move yourself and your passengers to safety off the roadway when it is safe.

Call 9-1-1 when it is safe and give the dispatcher your location by intersection or street address. A pay phone call goes directly to Costa Mesa Police Dispatch. A cellular phone call goes to California Highway Patrol Dispatch, which forwards it to Costa Mesa Police Dispatch once you say which city you are calling from.

Be prepared to tell the dispatcher:
- That you are reporting a traffic collision
- The location, nearest intersection, or address
- Whether there are injuries and how many
- Whether you are a witness or a party involved
- A description and direction of travel of any vehicles leaving the scene (hit and run)

Stay on the line until the dispatcher says it's OK to hang up.

If there is any injury or death, the State of California requires a collision report to be filed with the police department. The collision report is forwarded to the Department of Motor Vehicles and appears on your driving record regardless of fault. The driver found at fault may receive a traffic citation at the scene or by mail. Ask witnesses to stay until police arrive, or to leave a name and contact phone number.

## DUI Checkpoints and Education

The Costa Mesa Police Department maintains a full-time DUI team. Annually it conducts a minimum of twelve DUI checkpoints and saturation patrols funded by the California Office of Traffic Safety, often with the California Highway Patrol and the Santa Ana and Newport Beach Police Departments. Notification of the date, time, and location of checkpoints is posted in the local news and on the City website. Costa Mesa PD has a zero tolerance policy for driving under the influence.

The "Every 15 Minutes" program educates first-time drivers and their parents about the dangers of DUI. It is presented biannually at Estancia and Costa Mesa High Schools.

## Child Car Seat Law
