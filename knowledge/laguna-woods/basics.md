## About the City of Laguna Woods

The City of Laguna Woods occupies approximately three square miles of land that was once part of South Orange County's Moulton Ranch. On March 24, 1999, it was incorporated as Orange County's 32nd city. The median age of its 17,644 residents is approximately 75.

The city is located just over one mile from Laguna Canyon and approximately five miles from the Pacific Ocean. It is bordered by the cities of Aliso Viejo, Laguna Beach, and Laguna Hills, as well as the Laguna Coast Wilderness Park and other protected open space areas. Laguna Woods Village is the largest and most populous of the five private communities that residents call home.

Source: https://www.cityoflagunawoods.org/about-us/

## Form of Government and Contract City Staffing

Laguna Woods is a general law city with a council-manager form of government. It operates within the parameters of California municipal law, with an elected City Council as its legislative body. The City Council appoints a City Manager, who serves as chief executive officer, and a City Attorney, who serves as primary legal counsel.

The city has 18.25 full-time equivalent employees. It operates as a "contract city," meaning its small staff leverage the resources of contract, franchise, and joint powers agencies to provide services.

Source: https://www.cityoflagunawoods.org/about-us/

## City Council — Members and Terms

The five members of the City Council are residents of Laguna Woods who are elected by registered voters to four-year terms. Elections are "at-large" (citywide) in even-numbered years, with two and then three members elected at subsequent elections. All five offices are nonpartisan.

## Mayor and Mayor Pro Tem

The City Council appoints a Mayor and Mayor Pro Tem from among its membership. They preside over City Council meetings, execute certain legal instruments and authorizations, and attend to various ceremonial and representative matters. The Mayor Pro Tem serves as the Mayor in their absence.

## Running for City Council — Eligibility

Candidates for City Council must be: 18 years of age or older by election day; a citizen of California; a registered voter in Laguna Woods at the time nomination papers are issued; qualified to vote for the office at the time nomination papers are issued; and eligible to take the oath of office.

California Government Code Section 1021 prohibits individuals convicted of designated crimes specified in the State Constitution and California law from holding a City Council office. All City Council offices are nonpartisan, so candidates' political party preference is not included on the ballot.

Source: https://www.cityoflagunawoods.org/elections/

## Running for City Council — Nomination Period

Nomination papers are available from the City Clerk's Office during normal office hours, beginning not earlier than the 113th day nor later than the 88th day before each municipal election. If one or more incumbents eligible to be elected do not file nomination papers by the 88th day before the election, the nomination period extends through the 83rd day before the election for all persons except the incumbent(s) who failed to file.

## Incompatible Public Offices

California Government Code Section 1099 prohibits holding two public offices simultaneously if either office may audit, overrule, remove members of, dismiss employees of, or exercise supervisory powers over the other. It also applies if there is a possibility of a significant clash of duties or loyalties, or if public policy considerations make it improper. Positions of employment and positions on governmental bodies that have only advisory powers are not subject to the prohibition.

Questions about the compatibility of offices can go to the California Attorney General's Office at (916) 322-3360.

Source: https://www.cityoflagunawoods.org/elections/

## Campaign Contribution Limits

On December 20, 2000, the City Council unanimously adopted a $250 contribution limit from any individual to any candidate for office in Laguna Woods, or to any candidate-controlled committee, in any single election. The limit does not apply to a candidate's contribution of their personal funds, community property funds, or domestic partnership funds to their own campaign committee. It does apply to separate property contributions from the candidate's spouse or domestic partner. The limit is codified as Laguna Woods Municipal Code Chapter 1.14.

Source: https://www.cityoflagunawoods.org/elections/
