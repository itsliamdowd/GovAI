## About the County of Orange

The County of Orange is a regional service provider and planning agency. Its core businesses include public safety, public health, environmental protection, regional planning, public assistance, social services and aviation.

Mission statement: "Making Orange County a safe, healthy, and fulfilling place to live, work, and play, today and for generations to come, by providing outstanding, cost-effective regional public services." The County's core values are Respect, Integrity, Caring, Trust and Excellence.

## AlertOC — Emergency Notification System

AlertOC is the County's mass notification system for keeping residents and businesses informed of emergencies. By registering, time-sensitive messages may be sent to your home, cell or business phone. Text messages may also be sent to cell phones, e-mail accounts and hearing impaired receiving devices. For more information, visit AlertOC.com.

Source: https://www.ocgov.com/about-county/emergency/prevention-preparation

## Emergency Preparedness — Make a Kit and Create a Plan

County employees, city officials and emergency professionals will be on the scene after an emergency, but they cannot reach everyone right away. The page advises making an emergency supply kit of the most important items you may need. Visit ReadyOC.com for more information.

The page also advises making a family communications plan that includes an evacuation plan and coordinates with your school, work and community, and practicing it with your entire family. Visit ReadyOC.com/make-a-plan for the basics.

The ReadyOC campaign has launched a "Promise to Prepare" challenge for people who live, work or go to school in Orange County.

Source: https://www.ocgov.com/about-county/emergency/prevention-preparation

## Getting Immediate Assistance — 9-1-1 and 2-1-1 Orange County

Always call 9-1-1 for life-threatening emergencies.

2-1-1 Orange County is an information and referral system that links residents to community health and human services. Dial toll free 2-1-1 at any time day or night to reach certified, multi-lingual information and referral specialists. Services covered include:
- Food
- Shelter/housing resources
- Prenatal care
- Workforce development
- Government assistance programs
- Equipment resources for older adults
- Access to health insurance programs
- Urgent care, medical, dental and vision services
- Prevention and safety services
- Substance abuse assessment and treatment programs
- Child development assessment services
- Early detection and treatment programs
- Disaster response, including volunteer screening and charitable contributions options
- Support group connections
- Parks, recreation & other municipal departments

More information: www.211oc.org.

Source: https://www.ocgov.com/about-county/emergency/getting-immediate-assistance

## Disaster Recovery — Water Well Disinfection and Testing

Individual water wells may be contaminated by debris flow or flood damage to well system equipment and piping. Once all repair work is completed to the well and water lines, the well should be disinfected and water samples taken to a State Certified Laboratory for testing. The OC Health Care Agency's Water Quality Laboratory (WGL) website has more information.

For general recovery recommendations visit Ready.gov/recovering-disaster. For guidelines on what to do if asked to evacuate, visit Ready.gov/evacuation.

Source: https://www.ocgov.com/about-county/emergency/disaster-recovery-relief

## Household Chemical Disposal

You can dispose of motor oil, car batteries, leftover paint or unused pesticides at a Household Hazardous Waste Collection Center. There are four in Orange County, operating Tuesday through Saturday 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. with the exception of certain holidays. For locations and other information, call (714) 834-6752.

Source: https://www.ocgov.com/about-county/emergency/handling-hazardous-wastes-materials

## Treatment of Hazardous Waste (Businesses)

Businesses planning to treat hazardous waste on-site must notify the Certified Unified Program Agency (CUPA) by completing a notification form and obtaining authorization from the CUPA. Specialists will conduct inspections of treatment facilities. More information is on the OC Health Care Agency's CUPA website.

Source: https://www.ocgov.com/about-county/emergency/handling-hazardous-wastes-materials
