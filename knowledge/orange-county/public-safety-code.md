## Immediate Assistance — 9-1-1 and 2-1-1 Orange County

Always call 9-1-1 for life-threatening emergencies. 2-1-1 Orange County is an information and referral system that links Orange County residents to community health and human services. Callers dial toll free 2-1-1 at any time day or night and are connected with certified, multi-lingual information and referral specialists.

Services the 2-1-1 database covers include food, shelter/housing resources, prenatal care, workforce development, government assistance programs, equipment resources for older adults, access to health insurance programs, urgent care, medical, dental and vision services, prevention and safety services, substance abuse assessment and treatment programs, child development assessment services, early detection and treatment programs, disaster response (including volunteer screening and charitable contributions options), support group connections, and parks, recreation & other municipal departments. More information: www.211oc.org.

Source: https://www.ocgov.com/about-county/emergency/getting-immediate-assistance

## AlertOC — Emergency Mass Notification

AlertOC is the County's mass notification system, used to keep residents and businesses informed of emergencies. By registering with AlertOC, time-sensitive messages may be sent to your home, cell or business phone. Text messages may also be sent to cell phones, e-mail accounts and hearing impaired receiving devices. More information: AlertOC.com.

Source: https://www.ocgov.com/about-county/emergency/prevention-preparation

## Emergency Preparedness — Make a Kit and Create a Plan (ReadyOC)

Make an emergency supply kit of the most important items you may need in an emergency. County employees, city officials and emergency professionals will be on the scene after an emergency but cannot reach everyone right away. More information: ReadyOC.com.

Make a family communications plan that includes an evacuation plan and coordinates with your school, work and community, and practice it with your entire family. Basics for creating a plan for before, during, and after an emergency: ReadyOC.com/make-a-plan.

The ReadyOC campaign has also launched a "Promise to Prepare" challenge for people who live, work or go to school in Orange County.

Source: https://www.ocgov.com/about-county/emergency/prevention-preparation

## Household Hazardous Waste Collection Centers

You can dispose of motor oil, car batteries, leftover paint or unused pesticides at a Household Hazardous Waste Collection Center. There are four in Orange County that operate Tuesday through Saturday 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. with the exception of certain holidays. For locations and other information, call (714) 834-6752.

Source: https://www.ocgov.com/about-county/emergency/handling-hazardous-wastes-materials

## Treatment of Hazardous Waste by Businesses

Businesses planning to treat hazardous waste on-site must notify the Certified Unified Program Agency (CUPA) by completing a notification form and obtaining authorization from the CUPA. Specialists will conduct inspections of treatment facilities. Information is available on the OC Health Care Agency's CUPA website.

Source: https://www.ocgov.com/about-county/emergency/handling-hazardous-wastes-materials

## Disaster Recovery — Evacuation and Well Water Testing

Individual water wells may be contaminated as a result of debris flow or flood damage to well system equipment and piping. Once all repair work is completed to the well and water lines, the well should be disinfected and water samples taken to a State Certified Laboratory for testing. The OC Health Care Agency's Water Quality Laboratory (WGL) has a website with more information.

For recovery recommendations after a disaster, visit Ready.gov/recovering-disaster. For guidelines on what to do if asked to evacuate, visit Ready.gov/evacuation.

Source: https://www.ocgov.com/about-county/emergency/disaster-recovery-relief

## El Niño Emergency Response Website

The County of Orange launched an El Niño emergency-response website on October 9, 2026, with news, links to County resources and city sites, and information on preparing for storm-related weather impacts: www.ocgov.com/el-nino. OC Public Works also has a webpage on preparing homes and businesses for potential floods and traveling safely in wet weather: pwom.oc.gov/el-nino.

Residents and businesses are encouraged to create an emergency plan and portable emergency kit and sign up for emergency alerts at AlertOC. To stay informed, monitor @OCGovCA on X (formerly Twitter), @OCGov on Facebook, @OCGov on Instagram, local media, and #ocelnino on social media.

Source: https://www.ocgov.com/press/orange-county-launches-dedicated-el-nino-emergency-response-website

## Local Emergency Proclamation — Hurricane Marie

On September 17, 2026, the County of Orange proclaimed a local emergency in response to ongoing coastal impacts related to Hurricane Marie. The proclamation was approved and was to be considered for ratification at a special meeting to be scheduled the following week. The County/OA Emergency Operations Center continues to be activated to a Low Level, staffed by OC Sheriff's Department Emergency Management Division personnel. Governor Newsom proclaimed an El Niño-specific state of emergency on September 21.

Source: https://www.ocgov.com/press/orange-county-proclaims-state-local-emergency-related-hurricane-marie
