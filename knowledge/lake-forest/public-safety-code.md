## Fire Hazard Severity Zone Maps

On March 24, 2025, CAL FIRE transmitted new Local Responsibility Area Fire Hazard Severity Zone (FHSZ) Maps to Orange County cities and the County. The maps show areas the State Fire Marshal has identified as Moderate, High, and Very High Hazard Severity Zones. The zones are used to designate areas where California's defensible space standards and wildland urban interface building codes apply. They can be a factor in real estate disclosure, and the City references them in its Local Hazard Mitigation Plan.

State law requires local jurisdictions to: make the map available for public review and comment within 30 days of its transmittal; adopt a map by ordinance within 120 days of its transmittal; and file the adopted ordinance and map with the Board of Forestry and Fire Protection within 30 days of adoption. The new maps expand the boundaries of the FHSZs relative to the 2007 CAL FIRE map. Local jurisdictions may expand FHSZ designations beyond those recommended by the State Fire Marshal, with a finding supported by substantial evidence, but may not contract the areas or decrease the FHSZ levels.

## Wildfire Evacuation Orders and Terms

Lake Forest has an Evacuation Map where you can type in your address to see the status of an evacuation order in your neighborhood. The AM 1690 broadcast can also be heard on the web at http://bit.ly/LakeForestAM1690.

- **Evacuation Warning:** OCSD urges persons and their animals in the evacuation area(s) to relocate to safer locations. Those with access and functional needs and those with large animals should leave now. Loss of life and property is a risk but is not imminent. Personal discretion on whether to evacuate is allowed but not advised. If a person feels threatened at any time, evacuate immediately; do not wait for an official notification.
- **Immediate Evacuation Order:** OCSD orders all persons and their animals in the designated area(s) to immediately relocate. The risk of loss of life and property is extreme and imminent. Evacuation is mandatory. Persons refusing to comply will not be forcibly removed from their homes but cannot expect rescue or other lifesaving assistance once the incident is underway.
- **Shelter-in-Place:** OCSD orders all persons and their animals to remain indoors during or following an emergency event. It is recommended when an incident offers little or no time to evacuate or when it is safer to remain indoors.
- **Repopulation:** Community members may return to a previously evacuated area once the threat has been mitigated, infrastructure and utilities are secured, and OCSD has approved the return.

For wildfire readiness, the page refers residents to the Orange County Fire Authority's Ready, Set, Go! safety program.

## Public Safety Power Shutoffs

During high winds, Southern California Edison (SCE) may de-energize transmission lines to minimize wildfire risk. In Lake Forest, the power shutoffs would most likely impact residents in the foothills. The City will warn residents of potential Public Safety Power Shutoffs. Residents are encouraged to visit www.sce.com/psps for more information and to sign up for alerts from SCE.

## Homeless Liaison Officer Program

Homeless Liaison Officers (HLO) are Deputies who receive additional training on mental illness and on connecting the homeless population with available services and resources. They work with service providers and partner agencies including South County Outreach, Orange County Mental Health, Veterans Services, Families Forward, Coast to Coast and Ashland House. Support includes short term and long term housing, employment opportunities, mental health services, food gift cards, gas cards, socks, clothing, and hygiene kits. HLO's have also transported homeless individuals to the Laguna Hills Department of Motor Vehicles, the Social Security Office, and various courts and government offices.

Since 2015, the Homeless Liaison Officer has helped 85 homeless individuals find temporary and permanent housing. ## Megan's Law — Sex Offender Information

California's Megan's Law requires a sex offender, upon release from custody, to register at the local law enforcement agency in their city of residence. Offenders must provide residency, employment, and vehicle information. Each must register annually within 5 days of their birthday and within 5 days of changing their address or employment, for the rest of their lives.

Megan's Law authorizes, but does not mandate, law enforcement to release information to communities. A tiered classification system governs how much information is released: Other (single misdemeanor sex offense), Serious (single felony offense or misdemeanor child molestation), High Risk (at least 1 violent sex offense), and Violent Offender (at least 1 violent sex crime against 1 or more victims). Residents can access the Megan's Law website to view information about sexual offenders.
