## Rent Control and Evictions

The City of Laguna Beach does not have a local rent control ordinance. Landlords are subject to California Assembly Bill (AB) 1482, passed in 2019 and effective January 1, 2020. AB 1482 imposes statewide rent stabilization: rent increases are limited to no more than 10%, with no limit between tenants. It also restricts evictions for tenants who have lived in a unit for one year or more, allowing "at fault" and "no fault" evictions.

The City does not enforce AB 1482. It can only be enforced in court, and tenants may want to consider contacting an attorney or a local tenant advocacy organization. More information: https://tenantprotections.org/ and https://oag.ca.gov/consumers/general/landlord-tenant-issues.

## Fair Housing Laws and Source-of-Income Protections

All states must comply with the federal Fair Housing Act. California's Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA) and the Unruh Act, along with other state laws, prohibit housing discrimination because of: race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, disability, age, ancestry, citizenship, gender identity and gender expression, genetic information, immigration status, marital status, primary language, sexual orientation, source of income, and any other arbitrary basis.

FEHA protects people from housing discrimination based on source of income. Senate Bill (SB) 329 redefined source of income.

## Rent Subsidy Program (Housing Choice Vouchers)

The Orange County Housing Authority (OCHA) administers the Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) Program in Laguna Beach. HCVs help cover the difference between the rent a lower-income household can afford and the rent a participating landlord is charging. Call OCHA at (714) 480-2700 to find out more.

OCHA accepted applications for the HCV waiting list September 18, 2023, 8:00 a.m. PDT through September 29, 2023, 11:59 p.m. PDT (ochousing.org/2023-waiting-list-opening).

Property owners can visit the Orange County Housing Authority's Landlord/Owner webpage to participate and earn a signing bonus. Orange County United Way leads the Welcome Home OC Program, which provides owners double security deposits, daily holding fees, protection from damage and loss, and a signing bonus with the first 12-month lease.

## Senior Housing Rehab and Repair Program (LBS HOME)

LBS HOME is a partnership between the City of Laguna Beach and Habitat for Humanity Orange County. The City funds up to $5,000 per project (no strings attached) while Habitat for Humanity OC makes the repairs. Laguna Beach Seniors is the Program Administrator and manages the confidential intake and referral into the program. The program helps older homeowners on limited incomes make essential repairs that promote health, safety, and accessibility so they can "age in place."

## Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) — Applying

An ADU is a residential unit added to a lot with a single-family or multi-family residence, either converted from existing space or new construction attached to or detached from the primary residence. ADUs have their own kitchen, bathroom, living, and entry areas. A Junior ADU (JADU) is limited to no more than 500 square feet.

Applications are accepted online through the Public Permit Portal: (1) complete the Planning Application; (2) register an account for the Public Permit Portal; (3) submit project plans and the completed application through the portal. ADUs and JADUs are regulated by state law, the Laguna Beach Municipal Code, and the City's certified Local Coastal Program. On March 11, 2025, the City Council adopted an urgency ordinance, effective immediately, repealing and replacing Chapter 25.17 of the Municipal Code to comply with changes to state law.

## Short-Term Lodging — Licensing and Taxes

Chapter 5.84 of the Municipal Code took effect July 1, 2025, with enforcement beginning October 1, 2025. Operators must have a Short-Term Lodging Unit License plus a City business license, an Administrative or Conditional Use Permit, and Transient Occupancy Tax (TOT) registration. The license is typically valid for up to three years (matching the use permit term). A valid City business license is required every year; failure to maintain it can result in revocation. All advertisements and listings must show the license number.

Operators must collect a total of 14% on all rental revenue: 12% City TOT and 2% Laguna Beach Tourism Marketing District (LBTMD) assessment. Quarterly TOT reports must be filed even if no rentals occurred. Hosting platforms (e.g., Airbnb, VRBO, Booking.com) must show the license number, collect and remit the 12% TOT and 2% LBTMD if they collect payments, and remove unlicensed listings when directed by the City.

Source: https://www.lagunabeachcity.net/government/departments/community-development/planning-zoning/current-projects/short-term-lodging

## Short-Term Lodging — Operating Requirements and Penalties

Operators must provide a contact available 24/7 who can respond to complaints within 60 minutes. For new applications, notify all residences and businesses within 200 feet at least 30 days before license issuance. A copy of the approved nuisance response plan must be posted in a visible location inside the rental. Operators must comply with occupancy limits, parking requirements, and noise regulations, collect basic guest information and a signed rental rules agreement, place the City's "Good Neighbor Brochure" and trash and recycling instructions in the unit, and maintain a complaint log and response records.

Violations may lead to fines, suspension, or revocation of the license. If revoked, a license cannot be reissued for at least three years.

## Short-Term Lodging — Where Allowed and Limits

Short-term lodging (STL) is no longer allowed in the residential districts (R-1, R-2, and R-3). Existing permitted STL units in those districts are legal nonconforming and may continue to operate; the use runs with the land as opposed to the owner. STL is allowed in the LB/P, C-N, C-1, and CH-M zoning districts and within the CBD-1, CBD-2, CBD Central Bluffs, CBD Office, and CBD Visitor Commercial Districts of the Downtown Specific Plan subject to an Administrative Use Permit, and in the SLV zoning district subject to a Conditional Use Permit. Applications are filed through the online public permit portal.

No more than 20% of the total units in commercial and mixed-use districts may be converted to STLs, with a maximum of 300 total units city-wide, including STLs approved prior to October 2020. An additional 165 home-share units are permitted in single-family, duplex, and triplex dwelling units in the permitted commercial and mixed-use districts; the use permit fee for home-share units is waived. Properties with five or fewer units may convert a maximum of one unit; properties with more than five units may convert a maximum of 20% of units. New STL permits are valid for three years and must be renewed every three years.

## Laguna Beach Community Land Trust

On March 26, 2024, the City Council directed staff to facilitate the establishment of the Laguna Beach Community Land Trust, stemming from the effort to address artist work/live opportunities. A Community Land Trust is a non-profit that acquires and holds land in trust for the community so that land remains permanently affordable. The Trust typically owns the land while individuals or families purchase or lease the homes or buildings on it; homeowners typically sign ground leases of 99 years with resale restrictions.
