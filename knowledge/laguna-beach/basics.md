## About Laguna Beach

Laguna Beach is a small town in southwest Orange County. It occupies 8.84 square miles, has a population of 23,000 and welcomes six million visitors each year. It was founded on June 29, 1927. The City tree is the Corymbia citriodora (lemon-scented gum) and the City flower is the Pelargonium (geranium).

The City provides recreational activities, cultural arts events, public works maintenance of streets and parks, transit operations, community development, administrative services, and police, fire, and marine safety protection. It operates under a council-manager form of government. Policy-making and legislative authority are vested in a five-person City Council. The City Manager carries out the City Council's policies and ordinances, oversees day-to-day operations, and appoints department heads.

## City Clerk's Office

The City Clerk's office prepares and maintains the City's official municipal records and works with City Council and Council-appointed Commissions, Boards, and Committees. It prepares the agenda before and the minutes after each City Council meeting. It administers the City's record management system, maintains the Municipal Code, and administers certain tort claims. ## Commenting on a City Council Meeting Item

Emailing the City Council does not guarantee that a comment will be submitted as a public record at a future meeting. To ensure an email or comment on an upcoming meeting is entered into the public record, submit it through the City Clerk. For a comment on an item that isn't on the agenda, indicate whether you'd like it included in the official meeting record.

City records, including emails, are subject to the California Public Records Act. Unless exemptions apply, emails sent to City staff or Council members, any attachments, and any replies are subject to disclosure upon request. Neither the sender nor any recipient should expect privacy regarding the contents.

## Rules of Decorum

The Rules of Decorum and Civility Policy promotes mutual respect, civility, and orderly conduct among elected and appointed City officials, City staff, and members of the public. It is not intended to deprive any person of the right to freedom of expression. The City expects elected and appointed officials and employees to comply and seeks cooperation from members of the public. Standards for City officials at public meetings include treating everyone courteously, listening to others respectfully, exercising self-control, giving open-minded consideration to all viewpoints, and focusing on the issues and avoiding personalizing debate.

## Elections and Voter Information

The City Clerk is the local elections administrator and coordinates with the County of Orange in the conduct of Municipal elections. Regular elections are consolidated with statewide elections, scheduled on the first Tuesday of November of even-numbered years. City Council Members, the City Treasurer, and the City Clerk are elected for four-year terms. Council Members serve "staggered" terms, so either two (2) or three (3) seats are open for election every two years.

To run for office, a candidate must:
- Be 18 years old by Election Day
- Be a resident of Laguna Beach and a citizen of the United States
- Be a registered voter at their current address at the time nomination papers are issued, and, unless otherwise specifically provided, qualified to vote for the office
- Be eligible to take the oath of office and be able to be bonded in the amounts provided for by statute
- Not have been convicted of designated crimes specified in the Constitution and laws of the State of California

## Fair Political Practices Reporting

California campaign finance disclosure law requires local elected officeholders, candidates for local elected offices, and committees supporting or opposing local candidates to file their campaign disclosure reports with the City Clerk at specified intervals. The City Clerk's page offers an eFile Portal (electronic filing system) for Form 700, Campaign Disclosures, Lobbyist Reporting, and more, plus archives of campaign financial disclosures and Form 700 economic interest statements.

Under AB 2151, paper-filed campaign statements must be posted on the City website within 72 hours of each applicable filing deadline. If the final day of that period is a Saturday, Sunday, or holiday, the period extends to the next day that is not one. Posted data omits street names, building numbers, and bank account numbers, but the local filing officer makes a complete, unredacted copy available to any person upon request. More information: www.FPPC.ca.gov. Contact the City Clerk's Office at (949) 497-0705.

## Homeless Services — Alternative Sleeping Location (ASL)

Since November 2009, the City has owned, funded, and contracted for the operation of a low barrier emergency shelter, the Alternative Sleeping Location (ASL), at 20652 Laguna Canyon Road. It operates under a service provider agreement with Friendship Shelter. The ASL provides up to 20 overnight beds as a housing focused shelter, with time limited stays structured around individualized housing plans. Enrollment periods occur in renewable increments for individuals who actively engage in case management and housing placement efforts.

A structured daytime program provides access to support staff, County mental health resources, hygiene facilities, laundry services, computer and internet access, and referrals to community based services. The ASL has assisted more than 200 individuals in transitioning to more stable housing situations. Access is limited to enrolled participants during program operating hours. It is not a drop in shelter, and unregistered guests are not permitted on site.

## Homeless Services — 2019 Settlement and ASL Reports

On September 23, 2019, United States District Judge David O. Carter approved a settlement agreement between the City and Orange County Catholic Worker. Under it, the City works with trained personnel to offer available shelter placement at the ASL if possible. It also ensures trained outreach personnel help assess the needs of individuals with disabilities to determine an appropriate placement with a reasonable accommodation. It ensures the number of ASL beds does not fall below 60% of the unsheltered individuals in Laguna Beach as described in the 2019 countywide Point-in-Time Count. The City can continue to enforce applicable laws under appropriate circumstances. No monetary damages or attorney fees were exchanged.

Under Section 3.B(2)(k) of the Class Claims Settlement Agreement, the City makes available biannual ASL reports. They cover the number of denials of reasonable accommodation requests, complaints to the ADA Coordinator about ASL accessibility, appeals to the ADA Coordinator, appeals to the City Manager, and the outcomes of each. Reports are linked on the page, from 05-05-2020 to 12-31-2022.
