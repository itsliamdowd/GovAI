## Fire and Life Safety Inspections and Fees

Annual fire and life safety inspections of local businesses, schools, and multi-family residential occupancies are led by Fire Prevention Inspectors and supported by the Fire Department's four engine companies. Approximately 1,665 inspections are completed each year, including follow-up inspections. State-mandated inspections of schools and multi-family occupancies, both transient and non-transient residential buildings, are prioritized.

The City Council adopted the Fire Department fee schedule on April 12, 2022, which includes inspection and operational permit fees. The 2024–25 Inspection Cycle is the first cycle with full fee implementation. A FAQ on Fire Inspection Billing is attached to the inspection report and provided at each inspection.

## Vegetation Management — Fuel Breaks and Weed Abatement

The Fuel Break Program uses targeted goat grazing and hand crews to maintain fuel breaks along much of the City's perimeter and within select interior canyon areas. It was established more than 30 years ago.

The Annual Weed Abatement Program addresses invasive weeds and grasses across approximately 400 properties throughout the City. All identified properties are inspected and verified for compliance by Fire Prevention Division personnel.

## Coyote Sightings and Prevention

If you see a coyote in a residential area, call Animal Services at (949) 497-0701 or report the sighting to coyotes@lagunabeachcity.net.

Recommended steps:
- Keep pets indoors in the evening and at night.
- Secure the lid to your garbage cans and dumpsters.
- Clean up thoroughly after you grill outside, at home or in a City park.
- Do not leave food or water outside.
- Harvest your garden and fruit trees as soon as the vegetables and fruit are ready.

Animal Services recommends that pets not be let outside during evening hours. Cats are at risk at all times and should be kept indoors completely.

## Alarm Permits

Anyone with a burglary or alarm system for a home or business in Laguna Beach must obtain an alarm permit through the City's alarm program administrator, Alarm Program Systems (APS). Each permit is valid for two years. The fee is $65 for a new residential or business alarm system, and renewals are $45. Register online at the Alarm Program Website. For help or questions about permits or fees, call (888) 865-9770.

False alarm penalties:
- Three false burglary alarms in 12 months with permit (per false alarm): $175
- More than six false burglary alarms in 12 months with permit (per false alarm): $350
- Burglary false alarm without a permit: $175
- Robbery false alarm regardless of permit: $265
- There is a 10% charge per month for all unpaid balances up to a maximum of 100%.

## Other Alert Systems — AlertOC, Wireless Emergency Alerts, Outdoor Warning System, MyShake

AlertOC is Orange County's regional mass notification system, geo-targeted to affected areas. Registration is available on the page. Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA) are text warnings delivered directly to mobile devices within the notification zone. No registration is required, and the alerts reach visitors as well.

The Outdoor Warning System, installed with Genasys Inc., uses speakers and sirens throughout town for emergency alerts and instructions. The County of Orange can interrupt radio and television broadcasts through the Emergency Alert System. This system can only narrow the alert area to the entire County of Orange.

The MyShake app, managed by CalOES, provides earthquake early warnings. It sends audio and visual warnings for magnitude 4.5 or higher and Modified Mercalli Intensity III (weak) shaking.
