## Film and Photography Permit

Submit a permit request at least two business days before the date you want to conduct the activity. If the filming interferes with traffic or involves potential public safety hazards, an application may be required at least 5-7 business days in advance. Download and complete the Film and Photography Permit Application. Return it with the other required information and fees to Linh Doan at ldoan@westminster-ca.gov. For more information, call Linh Doan at (714) 548-3178.

Required insurance documents: General Liability - $1 Million; Worker's Compensation Verification; Additional Insured Endorsement; Hold Harmless; Indemnification.

The applicant must distribute written notification to adjacent property owners at least 48 hours before filming if there is an impact to on-street parking, filming on public right-of-way such as street or sidewalk, use of any equipment that will generate noise, or as determined by the City of Westminster. For private property, the Production Company must obtain written permission from the property owner. For public (City owned) property such as a park, sidewalk or street, the Production Company must notify the Police Department if pedestrian or vehicular traffic will be affected, so that traffic control officers can be assigned.

## Building Permit Fees

The City collects a plan check fee, a permit fee, and fees for other agencies such as Fire Authority. Plan check fees cover the cost of plan review. Permit fees cover the cost of providing inspections during construction. Fee schedules are adopted by resolution of the City Council and are based on the value of the project as established by codes and valuation tables. Plan check fees are collected at the time of submittal. Permit fees are collected at the time of permit issuance.

## Which Projects Require a Building Permit

No building, structure or service equipment may be erected, constructed, enlarged, altered, repaired, moved, improved, removed, converted or demolished unless an appropriate permit has been obtained from the Building Official. Exceptions: painting, papering, stucco repair, counter and partitions not over five feet high, replacement of plumbing fixtures where no modification is necessary to the water or sewer piping, and portable electrical appliances energized by a cord or cable having an approved factory attached plug end. A permit is required for replacement of windows and water heaters. Removal and re-installation of dishwashers, garbage disposals, and water closets is exempt.

A new swimming pool requires a permit. For pool permits, remodeling permits, or construction permit information, contact the Building Division at (714) 548-3254.

## Construction Plan Requirements

Once the Planning Division approves design and zoning requirements, prepare Construction Plans to submit to the Building Division. Plans must clearly indicate the location, the existing condition and the proposed scope of work, and show in detail how the project will comply with the Building Codes. For a permit application to be deemed complete, the submittal documents must contain all information required per California Building Code Section 107.

Architectural plans: Site Plan; Roof/Floor Plans; Elevations and Sections; Means of Egress Analysis; Green Building Code Requirements; Title 24 Energy Calculations. Structural plans: Foundation Plan; Framing Plans; Structural Details; Structural Calculations. Not all items may be necessary for every scope of work. Some projects must be prepared by a licensed Architect/Engineer or other licensed professionals. Speak with a Plan Checker in the Building Division to clarify. Incomplete, inaccurate, or non-compliant plans could delay the permit process and incur additional plan review fees.

## Plan Check Timelines

Depending on the project type, review may take a few hours to a few months. Most accessory projects with plans based on City Standards (patio covers, pools and spas, signs, reroofing, masonry walls, security fencing and lights) can be checked over the counter and permits issued the same day. The City recommends calling for an appointment to make sure a plan checker is available.

Projects that do not qualify for over-the-counter plan check, or that require review by Fire Authority, Health Agency, Planning or Engineering, go through standard plan check. Standard plan check takes 15 to 30 business days, depending on how many plans are ahead of yours. It may take more than one or two plan check rounds. Once corrections are made, subsequent plan checks are completed within 7 to 14 working days.

Source: https://www.westminster-ca.gov/departments/community-development/building-division/faqs

## Who Can Prepare Plans and Pull Permits

Except for one-story conventional residential buildings and minor additions or improvements as defined by code, plans must be prepared and signed by an architect or an engineer. Once plans are approved, Building Division staff will notify the applicant.

A licensed contractor can pull permits. The property owner, or an authorized agent such as a tenant or an employee with written authorization, may pull most permits. For the owner's protection from possible liabilities, the City recommends that contractors pull permits. Contractors are required to have a valid State License, City business license and worker's insurance. To obtain or renew a contractor's license, contact the State of California Contractors Licensing Board at (800) 321-2752.

## Building Inspections — Scheduling

Once construction plans are approved by all reviewing agencies, a building permit can be issued. At permit issuance you receive approved plans and an inspection card listing the required inspections. An inspection is required at the end of all major phases of the project.

Schedule inspections through the online portal Westminster Build. Have the work completed before calling for an inspection. Time frames might not be schedulable online. To request a time frame, contact the inspector between 7:30 a.m. and 8:30 a.m. on the day of the inspection.

Required to schedule: the permit number or numbers; property address and unit numbers; type of inspection or inspections; your name and telephone number; and a gate access code if in a gated community. The approved set of plans, inspection card, permits and subcontractors' proof of license must be available at the job site during inspections. Have the contractor available to meet the inspector. Once the project is completed, the inspector signs off the permit and it is placed in the permanent file for the property.

Source: https://www.westminster-ca.gov/departments/community-development/building-division/building-inspections

## Final Approvals and Certificate of Occupancy

Before the release of new utilities, or occupancy of new construction or major remodels, a departmental checklist must be completed for each building. It is routed to Planning, Engineering (Public Works), and Fire Authority when applicable. Once it has all required signatures, it goes to the Building Official for final approval.

A Certificate of Occupancy is issued prior to use or occupancy of new buildings and when there is a change in the occupancy classification of an existing building. It is produced by the Building Official after completion of most new construction, or when there is a change in occupancy type within an existing structure. New single family homes, residential garages, and storage buildings are exempt.

## Business License — Leasing Property and Transfers

Under Municipal Code Sections 5.12.150 & 5.12.162, leasing property is a business and requires a license. If you lease a commercial property (retail, office, industrial) to even a single tenant, you need a business license. If you lease 3 or more residential units, even if they are not on the same property, you need a business license.

You don't need a "landlord" business license if you operate the only business on your property. If you receive a letter saying otherwise, contact HdL at (657) 622-0222. City-issued business licenses are not transferable. To transfer a business, close your account; the new owner must purchase their own license.

Source: https://www.westminster-ca.gov/departments/community-development/building-division/business-license

## Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) — How to Apply

For projects involving only an accessory dwelling unit and/or junior accessory dwelling unit, you may use the combined Planning Division Zoning Clearance and Building Division Permit Plan Check.

- Step 1, Consult and Design: Determine feasibility, hire a professional architect or designer familiar with residential construction, and consult the Planning, Building and Engineering Divisions on submittal requirements. Design in compliance with Zoning Code and flood zone requirements (if applicable). Check no-build easement areas on the property; easement information comes from a full title report from a title company.
- Step 2, Prepare Construction Plans/Submittal Documents: The architect or designer prepares plans per the California Building Code and Fire Code (framing, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, Title 24, energy calculations, etc.).
- Step 3, Review: Formally submit a complete set of plans and supplemental documents to the Building Division, in person or online through the Westminster Build Online Portal. Provide all application forms and documents for all three Divisions.
- Step 4, Construction: Once approved by all applicable agencies, departments, and divisions, a building permit is issued. Inspections occur during construction.

New residential projects with four or more new units require a Development Review. The Planning Division must review and process them first, before the Building Division plan check.

Source: https://www.westminster-ca.gov/departments/community-development/planning-division/accessory-dwelling-units
