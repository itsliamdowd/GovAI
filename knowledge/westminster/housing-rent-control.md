## Tenant-Based Rental Assistance (TBRA) Program

On June 11, 2025 the Westminster City Council approved the use of federal HOME Investment Partnerships Program funds to implement the Westminster Tenant-Based Rental Assistance (TBRA) Program. The goal is to help households experiencing or at risk of experiencing homelessness achieve self-sustainability through rental assistance and supportive services. Interval House, a qualified service provider, carries out the housing and supportive services components. Referrals may come from, but are not limited to, the Westminster School District, the Westminster Family Resource Center, and the Westminster Police Department.

You may be eligible if you need housing support, earn at or below 50% AMI income limits, reside in the City of Westminster, and are currently experiencing homelessness or at risk of homelessness. Possible help includes Financial Support, Case Management, and Counseling.

2026 Income Limits (50% AMI) by household size: 1 – $65,150; 2 – $74,450; 3 – $83,750; 4 – $93,050; 5 – $100,500; 6 – $107,950; 7 – $115,400; 8 – $122,850.

For more information and help with eligibility determination, contact Interval House at (562) 594-9492 or rentalassistance@intervalhouse.org.

## Westminster Housing Authority and Affordable Housing Lists

The City Council activated the Westminster Housing Authority (WHA) on February 9, 2011, with findings that unsafe or unsanitary housing conditions exist and that there is a shortage of safe and sanitary housing. In 2012, after the dissolution of the former Westminster Redevelopment Agency (RDA), the WHA was designated as the Housing Successor for redevelopment housing functions. The WHA is responsible for increasing, improving, and preserving the community's supply of low and moderate income housing available at affordable housing costs.

The page lists projects to which the WHA has provided financial assistance to develop rent-restricted housing in the City ("List of Affordable Units in Westminster"). Under Health and Safety Code Section 33418 and AB 987, the WHA must maintain a database of units developed or assisted with funds from the former RDA's Low and Moderate Income Housing Fund. That database is linked as the "AB 987 Affordable Housing Database."

Source: https://www.westminster-ca.gov/departments/city-manager/housing-division/affordable-housing

## Fair Housing

The City does not provide legal assistance and does not mediate in tenant and landlord disputes. You may wish to contact a lawyer or a legal aid office to discuss any rights you may have.

Federal and State laws prohibit discrimination in the sale, rental, leasing, negotiation, advertising, and financing of housing based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, handicap, familial status, marital status, age, disability, ancestry, sexual orientation, source of income or any other arbitrary reason.

Resources: Self-Help Guide to California Courts (https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/) to search for free or low-cost legal help; the "Housing is Key" website for information on eviction protections related to COVID-19; A Guide to Residential Tenants' and Landlords' Rights and Responsibilities; and a Fair Housing Brochure.

## Homeless Prevention — Donations and Neighborhood Safety

The Police Department asks residents to donate instead of giving money to the homeless. Donate to reputable organizations working in the community with the homeless population to provide necessities such as food, hygiene items, clothing, blankets, and other various services.

Crime Prevention through Environmental Design (CPTED) ideas include securing trash enclosures, reducing foliage that may be used as hiding spots, ensuring adequate lighting, limiting access to problem areas, creating a Neighborhood Watch or social groups, and creating neighborhood programs for graffiti removal, litter clean ups, and area beautification. No single strategy will reduce all crime.

## Federal Housing Grants (CDBG and HOME), Plans and Public Review

The City receives annual funding through the Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) and HOME Investment Partnerships (HOME) programs to support housing and community development activities that primarily benefit low- and moderate-income residents.

The FY 2025/2026 Draft CAPER is available for public review September 15 - September 30, 2026. Every five years the City develops a Five-Year Consolidated Plan, as required by HUD, setting its strategy for investing CDBG and HOME funds. Each Annual Action Plan covers a single program year beginning July 1 and ending June 30. Posted documents include the FY 2025-2029 Consolidated Plan & FY 2025/2026 Annual Action Plan, the FY 2026/2027 Annual Action Plan, the FY 2025-2029 Citizen Participation Plan, and the Orange County Regional Assessment of Fair Housing (AFH), in which Westminster participates as a HUD Entitlement City.
