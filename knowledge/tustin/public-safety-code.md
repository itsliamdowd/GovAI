## Code Enforcement — Services and How to Report a Concern

The Code Enforcement Division investigates and enforces the Tustin City Code, which includes business license requirements, noise regulations, property maintenance requirements, the Sign Code, water quality laws, the Zoning Code, the Uniform Housing Code, and construction without appropriate permits. The Division also works with local, state, county and law enforcement agencies. Administrative Citations can be paid online.

For Code Enforcement concerns call 714-573-3298, email tustincodeenforcement@tustinca.org, or report a concern online at www.tustinca.org/reportconcern. Hours: Monday through Thursday 7:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., Friday 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m.

Forms for protection of private property include a Trespassing/Disturbing the Peace Letter of Authorization and Request for Enforcement, and a Sample No Trespassing Sign. If using Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge, download and save the file before completing and submitting it.

## Graffiti Removal Service

The City of Tustin has a FREE graffiti removal service available to all property owners within the City. It is limited to graffiti visible from the public right-of-way or visible from a commercial parking lot open to the public. Due to liability reasons, graffiti on a roof, parapet, or higher on a building must be removed by the property owner.

To participate, fill out, sign and return an Entry Permit and Release of Liability form for each property you own in the City. Removals from painted surfaces are color matched. Removals from non-painted surfaces are done using chemical removal and power washing.

To report graffiti, call the Graffiti Removal Hotline at 714-573-3111 or report it online. If you have information on a graffiti incident, also contact the Tustin Police Department Non-Emergency number at 714-573-3225.

## Fire Hazard Severity Zone Maps

On March 24, 2025, CAL FIRE and the Office of the State Fire Marshall released the 2025 Local Responsibility Area (LRA) Fire Hazard Severity Zone (FHSZ) Maps for Southern California. The CAL FIRE City of Tustin map designates portions of the city within Very High, High, and Moderate Fire Hazard Severity Zones. To see if your property is in a zone, use CAL FIRE's Fire Hazard Severity Zone Viewer.

"Hazard" is based on physical conditions that create a likelihood and expected fire behavior over a 30 to 50-year period, without considering mitigation such as home hardening, recent wildfires, or fuel reduction. The map does not evaluate "risk."

## Animal Bites and Nuisance Issues

If you need immediate assistance or have been bitten by an animal, dial 911. If a bite breaks the skin, Animal Control may have to quarantine the animal or have it tested for rabies. Dogs and cats are routinely subject to quarantine if they have caused a skin break to a human with their teeth. Dog owners who know their dog or cat has bitten another person must report the incident to Animal Control immediately. Animal Control does not routinely examine low risk animals such as rabbits, rodents, hamsters, or guinea pigs.

To report an animal bite, call Orange County Animal Care at 714-796-6421 or fill out their online form. For information on barking dogs, visit Orange County Animal Care.

## Computer Scam Alert

There have been reports of Orange County residents receiving phone calls offering computer support. In one case, a victim allowed a caller remote access to his computer after paying a $200 fee. The caller then locked him out and promised to unlock it only when an additional $200 was received.

The caller may direct the victim to a website that confirms a supposed virus, offer to remotely access and fix the computer for a fee, and lock the device until further money is paid.

To protect yourself:
- Continually check for software updates from a trusted source such as Microsoft or Apple.
- Only open email from trusted sources and do not click suspicious links.
- Do not download files from websites.
- Keep firewall protections running at all times.

## Police Officer Employment Requirements

Requirements include:
- **Age:** at least 21 years of age at the time of appointment.
- **Background:** good moral character, no convictions for felony or crimes of moral turpitude, and passing an extensive background investigation.
- **Citizenship:** a U.S. citizen, or a permanent resident alien eligible for and having applied for U.S. citizenship. Under California law, citizenship must be conferred within three years of applying.
- **Communication:** fluent English, oral and written. Bilingual skills are desirable.
- **Education:** high school graduation or equivalent.
- **License:** a valid California driver's license and satisfactory driving record at appointment.
- **Physical:** pass a physical agility test, medical examination and psychological evaluation. Requirements include normal hearing and color vision, vision 20/100 in each eye and correctable to 20/20, and weight proportional to height.

## Police Officer Categories

There are four categories:
- **Police Recruit (Entry Level):** for candidates with no sworn experience or academy training. Recruits are sponsored to a POST-approved academy. Full-time academies take approximately six months, followed by the field training program.
- **Police Officer (Lateral Entry):** must currently be a sworn police officer with a law enforcement agency, in or outside California. Candidates from outside California may be required to complete a brief POST-approved re-qualification course.
- **Police Officer (Academy Graduate):** has completed, or is enrolled in, a POST approved police academy.
- **Police Reserve Officer (Reserve I / II):** part-time, "at will" positions. Reserve Officers must work a minimum of 16 hours per month and complete a basic POST academy (Level I) or a POST certified Level II Reserve academy before appointment.

Appointment as a Police Officer begins an eighteen-month probationary employment status.

## Police Officer Selection Process

Steps: submit a City of Tustin employment application; application review; written exam; physical agility test; oral interview; eligibility list; background process; polygraph exam; medical exam; psychological exam.

The POST written exam is required of entry-level recruits only, and allows up to three hours. Academy graduates, current academy enrollees and police officers do not take it. Lateral candidates from other states must obtain a waiver or other certification approval from POST.

The physical agility test (PAT) requires photo identification, and late candidates will not be admitted. Events are a body drag (32 feet), a 6' chain link and solid fence climb, a 1.5 mile run, 30 push-ups in under 2 minutes, and 30 sit-ups in under 2 minutes. Candidates get up to 2 opportunities per event, and times and scores are not provided.

The oral interview lasts approximately 30 minutes. Successful applicants are placed on an eligibility list for up to one year. The background investigation takes approximately two months.

Source: https://www.tustinca.org/547/Selection-Process-for-Police-Officers

## Police Cadet Program

The Police Cadet Program introduces young men and women ages 18 to 22 to the field of law enforcement. Cadets work part-time while enrolled in college, in assignments including Communications, Community Policing Unit, Fleet Maintenance, Front Desk, General Investigations, Property, Records, and Traffic.

Hours are typically assigned around the student's class schedule: approximately 20 hours per week during school months, and up to 40 hours per week during summer and/or school breaks.
