## Street Sweeping and "No Parking" Enforcement

Police Service Aides enforce the City's parking laws. One of their main duties is making sure vehicles are off residential streets ("NO PARKING") during street sweeping. All streets in La Palma are swept once a week, usually the weekday following your normal trash collection day, between noon and 3 p.m. A color coded street sweeping map shows the sweeping day for your area.

If a unique situation prevents you from removing a vehicle from the street sweeping area, contact the Police Dispatcher at 714-690-3370 prior to noon on the day of sweeping. Street sweeping is provided by a third party contractor as part of residents' sanitation/trash fees. For comments or concerns about sweeping services, contact the Public Works Department at 714-690-3310. For comments or concerns about an enforcement action, contact the Police Department at 714-690-3370.

Street sweeping enforcement is generally cancelled during weeks where a City recognized holiday causes conflicts with the trash/waste collection schedule (for example, when a Monday holiday pushes trash collection back to the same day as street sweeping). The City recognized holidays are: New Year's Day, Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Day, President's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Veterans Day, Thanksgiving Day, and Christmas Day.

## Recreational Vehicle and Trailer Overnight Parking

The La Palma Municipal Code prohibits oversized vehicles (recreational vehicles) and non-motorized vehicles (trailers) from parking on any public street, privately owned street, or any driveway in a residential zoning district between 2:00 a.m. and 5:00 a.m., except with an exemption (permit). Each residence is granted fifty (50) exemptions in a calendar year for each affected vehicle they own. To accommodate guests, each household is granted thirty (30) exemptions for non-resident vehicles parked on public streets, private streets, or residential driveways.

La Palma residents must contact the La Palma Police Department, in advance and before midnight, to obtain an overnight parking permit (exemption). There is no charge for this permit. Residents must provide all of the following: resident's name, address and phone number; vehicle make and model; location where the vehicle will be parked; and the vehicle license plate number. Permits can be obtained by calling 714-690-3328 or by sending an email.

The overnight parking exemptions do not override other parking laws, such as parking on a street in excess of 72 hours, parking oversized vehicles within 100 feet of an intersection, or parking on the street during street sweeping. For comments or concerns about an enforcement action, contact the Police Department at 714-690-3370.

## Permit Parking Zones (A and B)

La Palma has two preferential Permit Parking zones. Zone A: Windermere Way, Sommerset Circle and Coventry Circle. Zone B: Bransford Drive (Walker Street to Tiara Lane), Walker Street (frontage road), Bon Villa Circle, and Caprice Circle.

The Permit Parking Ordinance prohibits the parking of all vehicles between 2 a.m. and 5 a.m., except with a valid permit. The ordinance is designed to limit overnight transient parking on streets within the restricted zones.

Those living in the restricted area can contact the La Palma Police Department to obtain permits for vehicles registered at their home as well as guest permits. There is no fee for these parking permits. For more information, contact the Police Department at 714-690-3370.

## E-Bike Ordinance

The La Palma City Council adopted Ordinance No. 2025-01 on September 2, 2025, to regulate the use of e-bikes and other electric mobility devices. Article IX of the La Palma Municipal Code prohibits riding and operating e-bikes, motorized scooters and other similar electric mobility devices in certain public areas: sidewalks, public facilities, outside of any roadway or other paved public area, on any public athletic field, or over any recreational trails. New signs have been installed throughout La Palma, including the Edison Right-of-Way and public facilities. The La Palma Police Department is actively enforcing the ordinance.

An e-bike is a bicycle equipped with fully operable pedals and an electric motor of less than 750 watts. California divides electric bicycles into three classes:
- Class 1: motor assists only when you pedal; assistance stops at 20 miles per hour. Operators and passengers under 18 must wear a helmet.
- Class 2: pedal-assist plus a hand throttle that can propel the bike without pedaling; assistance stops at 20 miles per hour. Operators and passengers under 18 must wear a helmet.
- Class 3: motor assists only when you pedal, up to 28 miles per hour, and equipped with a speedometer. Operators must be 16 years of age or older. Helmets are required for operators and passengers regardless of age.

## Street Light Outages

The majority of street light poles in La Palma are owned and maintained by Southern California Edison (SCE). To report an inoperable street light, call SCE at (800) 611-1911 or report the outage at the SCE website. Provide the address where the pole is located as well as the cross street; the pole number painted on the pole or engraved on a metal tag placed at eye-level; and a description of the problem (example: burned out, flickering, working intermittently).

If SCE says the pole is not one of theirs, contact the Public Works Department at (714) 690-3310 or email Public Works.

## Street Rehabilitation Projects — What Residents Should Know

In 2014, La Palma completed a Seven-Year Residential Street Rehabilitation Program. The next cycle will include Rubberized Emulsion Aggregate Slurry (REAS) in residential areas. Before any pavement rehabilitation project, the City conducts concrete sidewalk, curb, and gutter rehabilitation and crack-sealing on the scheduled streets. The City currently uses two methods: REAS slurry seal and Asphalt Rubber Hot Mix (ARHM) overlays.

Contractors typically barricade the construction area until the pavement is ready for vehicles. If residents ignore the barricades, cleaning costs for material tracked onto driveways and vehicles are the responsibility of the person failing to obey the barricades. On freshly slurry sealed streets, motorists should avoid abrupt starts, stops, or sharp turns, especially on hot days, which can lift the slurry and leave tire tracks.

For ARHM overlays, La Palma typically uses 1½ to 2 inches of rubberized overlayment. Overlay work starts with edge grinding and pothole repairs. Some small rocks may become dislodged in the days after application, and approximately one week after installation the City conducts street sweeping in the area to remove loose aggregate. No vehicles should be driven over the road surface until the contractor removes the barricades.
