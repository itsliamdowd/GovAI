## 2021-2029 Housing Element Update

The Housing Element is one of the seven elements required by the State to be included in the City's General Plan. It addresses adequate housing opportunities for present and future residents and provides the primary policy guidance for housing for the City's decision-makers. The City kicked off the Housing Element Update in February 2020.

The draft Housing Element was submitted to the California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) for review on June 21, 2021. On August 20, 2021, HCD issued a review letter finding that revisions were necessary to comply with State Housing Element Law. On October 5, 2021, the City Council conducted a public hearing and adopted the revised Housing Element. It was submitted to HCD for certification on October 12, 2021. On January 10, 2022, HCD issued a letter finding that additional revisions were required before it can be certified.

On December 22, 2023, City staff resubmitted the adopted Housing Element to HCD for review. Under State law, any subsequent revisions of the draft must be posted on the City's website, and a link emailed to all individuals and organizations that have previously requested notices relating to the Housing Element, at least seven days before the draft revision is submitted to HCD. The March 2024 Revised Draft Housing Element and Technical Report is available in clean and tracked-changes versions on the page. Questions: email commdev@cityoflapalma.org or call (714) 690-3340.

## Affordable Housing Programs and Finding Affordable Rentals

The City is required to have a Housing Plan under the California Government Code and must update it every seven years. The City's 13 housing programs for the planning period January 1, 2021, to October 1, 2029, are: Section 8 Rental Assistance; Funding for Preservation and Rehabilitation of Affordable Housing; Mortgage Assistance Certificate Program; Mortgage Credit Certificate Program; Reverse Mortgage Program; Section 202 Elderly or Handicapped Housing; Home Spotlight Award Program; Emergency Shelters, Low Barrier Navigation Centers, Transitional and Supportive Housing, and Agricultural Employee Housing; Density Bonus; Residential Land Use Designations and Controls; Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing; Reasonable Accommodation for Persons with Disabilities; and Facilitate Housing Development to Accommodate Regional Needs.

Orange County Housing & Community Development maintains the Affordable Rental Housing List, a list of rental housing units by city that are deed restricted to provide affordable units. It is not a listing of vacancies. If you are homeless and need assistance, contact the OC Housing & Community Development to see what assistance may be available.

## Section 8 Rental Assistance

The Section 8 program provides rental assistance to low and moderate income families, elderly and disabled persons who spend more than 30% of their monthly income on rent. The subsidy represents the difference between 30% of the recipient's monthly income and the federally approved fair market rent (FMR). Further information is available through the Orange County Housing Authority and the Department of Housing and Urban Development.

The Orange County Housing Authority (OCHA) from time to time opens and closes the waiting list to accept pre-applications for rental assistance. Information is on the OCHA website at www.ochousing.org, or call OCHA at (714) 480-2700.

## Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing

The Fair Housing Council of Orange County provides fair housing services, including housing discrimination response, landlord tenant relations, housing information and counseling, and community education programs. The City will support the Fair Housing Council by promoting these services. In addition, the City will:
- Ensure that all development applications are considered, reviewed, and approved without prejudice to the proposed residents, contingent on the application's compliance with all entitlement requirements.
- Accommodate persons with disabilities who seek reasonable waiver or modification of land use controls and/or development standards pursuant to procedures and criteria set forth in the applicable development regulations.
- Work with the County to implement the regional Analysis of Impediments to Fair Housing Choice and HUD Consolidated Plan.
- Facilitate public education and outreach by posting informational flyers on fair housing at public counters and libraries, and on the City's website.
- Conduct public meetings at suitable times, accessible to persons with disabilities, and near public transit, with interpretation and translation services provided when requested at public meetings when feasible.
- Encourage community and stakeholder engagement during development decisions.
