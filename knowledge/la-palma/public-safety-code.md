## Code Enforcement — Overview

The City of La Palma's Code Enforcement staff works to ensure that the City's municipal codes and property maintenance standards are met, through proactive enforcement and notifications received from the community. The intent is not to punish individual property owners but to work with them to gain compliance with adopted codes, regulations and restrictions. Failure to comply with the Municipal Code can result in fines and legal action.

Responsibilities include City code compliance, commercial, Fats, Oils and Grease (FOG) permits, graffiti abatement, inoperable vehicles, industrial, property maintenance, and residential.

Contact: Code Enforcement, City Hall, 7822 Walker St., La Palma, CA 90623. Phone: 714-690-3342.

## Residential Code Enforcement — Conditions Cited

The Code Enforcement Division surveys all properties in residential neighborhood areas and issues corrective notices for unsightly exterior conditions, such as:
- Poor landscape maintenance, including mowing, watering and weed removal
- Lack of adequate landscaping in the front yard
- Overgrown yards
- Structures in need of painting, poorly maintained house paint, trim
- Unsightly, deteriorated fencing
- Broken windows
- Storage in the front yard (e.g. storage of trash cans in the front yard)
- Inoperable vehicles
- Debris and trash visible from the street

## Trash, Vehicle, Yard and Basketball Hoop Rules

Trash, garbage, boxes, rubbish, trash containers, etc. may not be stored within public view. They must be stored behind a fence or in the garage. Trash ready for collection may be placed at curb after 3 p.m. on the day before collection is scheduled, and the empty cans must be removed by midnight on the day of collection.

All vehicles parked or stored on the driveway are to be road operational (including currently registered) and should be kept clean and free of debris.

All yards visible from the public right-of-way are to be properly landscaped and maintained in a neat and orderly condition. Trees and bushes near streets and sidewalks should be trimmed regularly to prevent obstructions into the sidewalk.

Sidewalks and public streets should only be used for vehicular and pedestrian use. Basketball hoops and other recreational devices left on the street or sidewalk can create a hazard. Store them on your property, preferably behind a fence or wall, and out of public view.

## Pool, Spa and Pond Requirements

All bodies of water more than 12 inches deep must be enclosed by a fence at least five feet high with self-closing and self-latching gates or doors. All bodies of water must be kept clean and clear, so that the bottom can be clearly seen. Only clear, non-chlorinated water, which contains no filter media, is permitted to be discharged to the street gutter.

## Urban Runoff Pollution and Fats, Oils and Grease

The discharge of water into street gutters or storm drains which is not composed entirely of storm water and/or which contains pollutants is prohibited. Prohibited pollutants include, but are not limited to: household waste, trash paints, concrete, plastics, yard waste, excessive pesticides or fertilizers, petroleum products, fuels, oils, coolants, excessive soil sediment, animal waste, construction waste, glues, paint flakes, and stucco fragments. Storm drains flow directly to the Ocean.

Dumping fats, oils and grease down the garbage disposal could cause problems in your plumbing, sewers and the ocean.

## Residential Code Enforcement Relief Program

The program was designed to assist lower income La Palma homeowners who are charged with a Code Enforcement violation but cannot afford to make the necessary repairs. It provided a grant of up to $10,000 to allow low-income homeowners to bring their homes up to the City's minimum standards.

Funding for the program was lost when the "Dissolution Act" (AB1X 26) dissolved redevelopment agencies, which had been the program's funding source. At this time alternative funding has not been identified. Responsible agency: Code Enforcement Division.

## Emergency Management and Preparedness

During a state of emergency, the La Palma City Manager acts as the City's Director of Emergency Services, while City department heads assume Emergency Operations Center roles in operations, planning, logistics, and finance. The La Palma Police Department drafts emergency plans, conducts disaster training, and coordinates with county, state, federal, and non-governmental response agencies. The Police Department manages the Police Explorer Program, Volunteers in Policing (VIP) Program, and the Community Emergency Response Team (CERT).

## AlertOC Emergency Notifications

AlertOC is a joint County and City mass notification system that keeps residents and businesses informed of emergencies and certain community events. Residents and businesses are strongly encouraged to register their contacts at www.alertoc.org. Time-sensitive voice messages may be sent to home, cell, or business phones, and text messages may be sent to cell phones, e-mail accounts and hearing impaired receiving devices.

The system is pre-loaded with all landline phone numbers. Phone service provided through cable or cell companies is not included, and these customers must self-register. Residents and businesses must also self-register additional contact points, such as cell phone numbers or email addresses.

Example uses include fires or floods, bomb threat, hostage situation, chemical spill or gas leak, other emergency incidents, drinking water contamination, utility outage, evacuation notice, and missing person. When a message has been issued, do not call the Police Department unless you have an emergency or other important matter.

Source: https://www.cityoflapalma.org/155/AlertOC

## Community Emergency Response Team (CERT)

CERT trains residents to care for themselves, their family, their neighbors, and then to assist the overall neighborhood during an emergency. La Palma participates in the regional West County CERT program with Buena Park, Cypress, Los Alamitos, Seal Beach and Westminster.

To become a member you must attend a 20-hour FEMA approved course covering Disaster Preparedness, Medical Ops I & II, Light Search and Rescue, Fire Suppression, Team Organization and Disaster Psychology. The basic CERT training course is in general offered twice a year. Details: www.westcountycert.com.

Source: https://www.cityoflapalma.org/181/Community-Emergency-Response-Team-CERT

## Police Explorers (Explorer Post 437)

Explorer Post 437 is for youth between the ages of 14 and 20 who are interested in community service and a career in law enforcement. Activities include a four day Explorer Academy, crowd and traffic control for special events throughout Orange County, ride-alongs, and assisting officers on patrol.

To join, apply online. For questions, email police@lapalmaca.gov or call 714-690-3381.
