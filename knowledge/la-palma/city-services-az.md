## Household Recycling Program (Trash, Green Waste and Recycling Carts)

In July 2017, Park Disposal (EDCO) expanded its trash collection program to include green waste recycling. All single family homes were issued a 95-gallon grey trash cart and a 95-gallon green waste cart for the automated curbside recycling program at no additional cost. The blue recycling cart (max 65-gallon) is still used.

GREEN cart: food and organic waste, grass clippings, brush, weeds, hedge trimmings, branches, leaves, palm fronds, and wood (including untreated and non-lead based painted).

BLUE cart: recyclable plastics labeled 1 through 7, aluminum and steel cans (including empty aerosol cans), glass bottles and jars, mixed paper (junk mail, magazines, cereal boxes, shredded paper, etc.), cardboard, plastic water and soda bottles, and telephone books. Additional blue recycling carts are available at no additional cost, and a smaller cart option is available upon request.

GRAY containers: all other waste, including plastics not labeled with a number 1 through 7 and the recycle symbol, trash bags and plastic grocery bags, and pet or diaper waste. For more information, visit www.LaPalmaRecycles.com or call Park Disposal at 714-522-3577.

## Bottles and Cans (CRV Redeeming)

The primary place to recycle and redeem CRV bottles and cans is a certified recycling center. There are approximately 2,000 certified recycling centers throughout the state. To find the nearest one, visit CalRecycle or call CalRecycle's toll-free hotline at 1-800-RECYCLE. Residents who do not want to redeem their bottles and cans for cash can place them in their blue recycling cart, and the City's waste hauler will remove them.

## Universal Waste (Batteries, Fluorescent Bulbs, Electronics)

These universal wastes may NOT be placed out for curbside collection or disposed of in a bin or container meant for solid waste. They MUST be disposed of at a Recycling & Transfer Center:
- Common batteries: AA, AAA, D cells, C cells and button batteries (e.g. hearing aid batteries).
- Fluorescent tubes and bulbs, including mercury containing lamps.
- Electronic devices: computer and television monitors, laptop computers, miscellaneous computer components, printers / scanners, fax machines, VCR's, radios, cell phones, telephones and microwave ovens.
- Pilot light sensors: mercury containing switches are found in some gas appliances such as stoves, ovens, clothes dryers, water heaters, furnaces and space heaters.
- Items containing mercury: gauges, thermometers, thermostats and switches.
- Non-empty aerosol cans that contain hazardous materials: cans labeled TOXIC or FLAMMABLE that are not completely empty.

Residents can also dispose of other household hazardous waste at the Orange County Collection Center, 1071 N. Blue Gum Street, Anaheim, near the junction of the 91 and 57 freeways. For more information, visit www.oclandfills.com or call 714-834-6752.

## Sharps (Syringe/Needle) Disposal

State law prohibits disposing of medical sharp waste (syringes, needles, and lancets) in the normal trash system. Residents can dispose of up to two gallons of sharps waste sealed in any hardy plastic container through a free on-call curbside program. To schedule a pickup, call Park Disposal directly at 714-522-3577. Residents do not need to be home during the collection.

Residents can also take sharps to the Orange County Collection Center at 1071 N. Blue Gum Street, Anaheim. Place the sharps in a sturdy plastic container, preferably a designated bio-hazard medical waste container. For more information, visit www.oclandfills.com or call 714-834-6752.

## Mattress Recycling (Bye Bye Mattress Program)

OC Waste & Recycling and the Mattress Recycling Council offer these options:
- Free residential mattress recycling at OC landfills: OC residents may take unwanted mattresses and box springs to the Olinda Alpha or Prima Deshecha landfills.
- Residential bulky item pick up: OC residents may contact their local waste hauler about a free bulky item pick up of mattresses.
- Commercial mattress recycling at OC landfills: businesses may take recyclable mattresses and box springs ONLY (no mixed waste or trash) to the Frank R. Bowerman, Olinda Alpha or Prima Deshecha landfills.
- Retailer take back: California retailers are required by law to pick up old mattresses at no cost when they deliver a new one (exceptions apply). Ask at the time of purchase.

ACCEPTED: standard mattresses, standard box springs, and futon mattresses (no bases or other furniture parts). NOT ACCEPTED: items that are wet, heavily soiled or infested with bedbugs; air mattresses that contain no upholstery material (such as camping beds); car beds; cushions.

For more information, visit oclandfills.com/mattress or call OC Waste & Recycling during regular business hours at 714-834-4000.

## Mandatory Organic Waste Reduction (SB 1383)

On October 5, 2021, the City of La Palma adopted an Ordinance amending Chapter 18 of the La Palma Municipal Code to incorporate SB 1383. The Ordinance became effective on November 4, 2021. SB 1383 affects residents as well as businesses.

Residents can put both food scraps and yard trimmings in the GREEN organics cart ("If It Grows, It Goes!"), including food-soiled paper. If you use a bag, it must be compostable, not plastic or biodegradable (look for "compostable: BPI meets ASTM 6400 Standard" on the packaging).

Put in the green cart:
- Yard trimmings: brush, flower cuttings, garden trimmings, hay, house plants, lawn clippings, leaves, prunings, shrubbery, straw, tree twigs (4 feet or less in length and 6 inches in diameter), weeds, wood chips.
- Food scraps: compostable plastic bags only; raw or cooked food scraps; veggies; meats; seafood; dairy products; breads, grains, pastas; fruits; bones, shells.
- Food-soiled paper: pizza boxes, paper cups, paper plates, napkins, paper towels.

Do NOT put in the green cart: ashes, large tree trunks and stumps, pet waste or manure, plastic bags (regular or biodegradable), plastic, glass or metal, poison oak or poison ivy, rocks, concrete, sod or dirt, treated or painted wood.

Landscape companies may no longer legally dispose of green waste in trash containers or landfills. Local education agencies (K-12 public schools, charter schools, and school districts) must maintain mandatory commercial recycling and organic recycling programs.

## Mandatory Commercial Recycling (AB 341)

AB 341 requires businesses, including public entities, that generate four cubic yards or more of commercial solid waste per week, or a multifamily residential dwelling of five units or more, to arrange for recycling services (starting July 1, 2012). All businesses and commercial customers in La Palma are in compliance due to mixed waste processing provided by the hauler, EDCO. Contact EDCO to sign up for recycling or to learn about your recycling options for compliance.

## Mandatory Organics Recycling for Businesses (AB 1826)

AB 1826 (effective January 1, 2016) requires businesses and multi-family complexes with 5 or more units that generate specified amounts of organic waste to arrange for organics collection services. This includes schools, hospitals, stores, restaurants, for-profit or nonprofit organizations, and residential dwellings with 5+ units. Multifamily dwellings are not required to have a food waste diversion program.

Start dates: April 1, 2016, businesses generating 8 cubic yards of organic waste per week; January 1, 2017, businesses generating 4 cubic yards of organic waste per week; January 1, 2019, businesses generating 4 cubic yards or more of commercial solid waste per week. The requirement may expand to businesses generating 2 cubic yards or more of commercial solid waste per week if the statewide 2020 target was not met.

To discuss organics collection at your business, contact the City of La Palma at (714) 690-3310.

## Water Quality and Brown Water

Brown water is usually caused by sediment in the water mains disturbed by events such as fire hydrant use or water main line flushing. Brown water is rarely a health threat; the discoloration is usually naturally-occurring mineral sediments (mainly iron and manganese). Weekly water samples are taken from various locations in the City, as required by the State Water Board, and the City publishes an annual Water Quality Report.

To clear brown water, keep the water flowing from your hose bib until it runs clear. Alternatively, run the fixture furthest from your meter (i.e. upstairs shower), run your sprinklers, turn on faucets and fixtures, or flush toilets a few times. Monitor for discolored water before starting laundry to avoid stains. If brown water continues, notify the Public Works Division at (714) 690-3310 or publicworks@lapalmaca.gov.

## Stormwater Program and Reporting Illegal Dumping

La Palma has two drainage systems: sanitary sewers and storm drains.
