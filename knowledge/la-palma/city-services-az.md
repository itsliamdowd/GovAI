## Animal Control Services (SEAACA)

The Southeast Area Animal Control Authority (SEAACA) began providing animal control services for La Palma on January 1, 2010. The shelter is at 9777 Seaaca St., Downey, CA 90241, phone 562-803-3301. It has a low cost wellness clinic that is open to the public, Tuesday through Saturday.

Shelter viewing hours: Tuesday - Friday 11 a.m. - 6 p.m.; Saturday 10 a.m. - 5 p.m.; closed Sunday, Monday & holidays. Licensing hours: Tuesday - Friday 9 a.m. - 6 p.m.; Saturday 9 a.m. - 6 p.m.; closed Sunday, Monday & holidays.

## Animal Licensing

All dogs are required to have a valid pet license within 30 days of becoming 4 months of age or moving into the City. Dog owners pay $20 for altered animals and $40 for unaltered animals. Seniors (55+) receive a 50% discount for altered pets. The SEAACA licensing period is from July 1 to June 30.

## Carpet Recycling

The California Carpet Stewardship Program is administered by CARE under AB 2398 (modified in 2017 by AB 1158) and managed by CalRecycle. It is funded by an assessment on carpet sold in California. The funds support markets for products made from recycled carpet, underwrite the collection and transport of carpet to and from drop-off sites, and educate stakeholders about keeping carpet out of landfill.

To find the nearest CARE drop-off location, visit www.carpetrecovery.org.

## Household Recycling Carts (Green, Blue, Gray)

Park Disposal (EDCO) issued a 95-gallon grey trash cart and a 95-gallon green waste cart to all single family homes at no additional cost. The blue recycling cart (max 65-gallon) is still used. Additional blue recycling carts are available at no additional cost, and a smaller cart option is available upon request.

GREEN cart: food and organic waste, grass clippings, brush, weeds, hedge trimmings, branches, leaves, palm fronds, and wood (including untreated and non-lead based painted).

BLUE cart: recyclable plastics labeled 1 through 7, aluminum and steel cans (including empty aerosol cans), glass bottles and jars, mixed paper, cardboard, plastic water and soda bottles, and telephone books.

GRAY cart: all other waste, including plastics not labeled 1 through 7 with the recycle symbol, trash bags and plastic grocery bags, and pet or diaper waste.

For more information visit www.LaPalmaRecycles.com or call Park Disposal at 714-522-3577.

## Mandatory Commercial Recycling (AB 341)

Under AB 341, businesses, including public entities, that generate four cubic yards or more of commercial solid waste per week, or a multifamily residential dwelling of five units or more, must arrange for recycling services (starting July 1, 2012).

All businesses and commercial customers in La Palma are in compliance due to mixed waste processing provided by the hauler, EDCO. Contact EDCO to sign up for recycling or to find out about your recycling options for compliance.

Source: https://www.cityoflapalma.org/705/Mandatory-Commercial-Recycling-AB-341

## Mandatory Organics Recycling for Businesses (AB 1826)

AB 1826 became effective on January 1, 2016. It requires businesses and multi-family complexes (with 5 or more units) that generate specified amounts of organic waste to arrange for organics collection services. This includes schools, hospitals, stores, restaurants, for-profit or nonprofit organizations, and residential dwellings with 5+ units. Multifamily dwellings are not required to have a food waste diversion program.

Start dates: April 1, 2016, for businesses generating 8 cubic yards of organic waste per week; January 1, 2017, for 4 cubic yards per week; January 1, 2019, for businesses generating 4 cubic yards or more of commercial solid waste per week. If statewide organic waste disposal in 2020 was not reduced by 50 percent of 2014 levels, the requirements expand to businesses generating 2 cubic yards or more of commercial solid waste per week.

To discuss organics collection at your business, contact the City of La Palma at (714) 690-3310.

Source: https://www.cityoflapalma.org/556/Mandatory-Organics-Recycling---AB-1826

## Mandatory Organic Waste Reduction (SB 1383)

SB 1383 sets targets to reduce organic waste disposal 50% by 2020 and 75% by 2025, and to rescue for people to eat at least 20% of currently disposed surplus food by 2025. It affects residents as well as businesses, requires some businesses to donate excess edible food, and carries significant penalties for non-compliance. On October 5, 2021, the City of La Palma adopted an Ordinance amending Chapter 18 of the La Palma Municipal Code to incorporate SB 1383. The Ordinance became effective on November 4, 2021.

Put food scraps, yard trimmings and food-soiled paper (pizza boxes, paper cups, paper plates, napkins, paper towels) in the GREEN organics cart. Yard trimmings include tree twigs 4 feet or less in length and 6 inches in diameter. If you use a bag, it must be compostable, not plastic or biodegradable (look for "compostable: BPI meets ASTM 6400 Standard" on the packaging).

Do NOT put in the green cart: ashes, large tree trunks and stumps, pet waste or manure, plastic bags (regular or biodegradable), plastic, glass or metal, poison oak or poison ivy, rocks, concrete, sod or dirt, and treated or painted wood.

Landscape companies may no longer legally dispose of green waste in trash containers or landfills. Local education agencies (K-12 public schools, charter schools, and school districts) must maintain mandatory commercial recycling and organic recycling programs.

Source: https://www.cityoflapalma.org/734/Mandatory-Organic-Waste-Reduction

## Mattress Recycling

Through the Bye Bye Mattress Program, OC Waste & Recycling and the Mattress Recycling Council offer these options:
- Free residential mattress recycling at the Olinda Alpha or Prima Deshecha landfills (mattresses and box springs).
- Residential bulky item pick up: contact your local waste hauler about a free pick up of mattresses.
- Commercial mattress recycling at the Frank R. Bowerman, Olinda Alpha or Prima Deshecha landfills (recyclable mattresses and box springs ONLY, no mixed waste or trash).
- Retailer take back: California retailers are required by law to pick up old mattresses at no cost when they deliver a new one (exceptions apply).

ACCEPTED: standard mattresses, standard box springs, and futon mattresses (no bases or other furniture parts). NOT ACCEPTED: items that are wet, heavily soiled or infested with bedbugs; air mattresses that contain no upholstery material (such as camping beds); car beds; and cushions.

For more information visit oclandfills.com/mattress or call OC Waste & Recycling during regular business hours at 714-834-4000.

## Sharps (Syringe/Needle) Disposal

State law prohibits the disposal of medical sharp waste (syringes, needles, and lancets) in the normal trash system. A free on-call curbside collection is available. Residents can dispose of up to two gallons of sharps waste sealed in any hardy plastic container. Schedule a pickup by calling Park Disposal directly at 714-522-3577. Residents do not need to be home during the collection.

Sharps can also be taken to the Orange County Collection Center at 1071 N. Blue Gum Street, Anaheim, in a sturdy plastic container, preferably a designated bio-hazard medical waste container. For more information visit www.oclandfills.com or call 714-834-6752.

Source: https://www.cityoflapalma.org/384/Recycling

## Trash Collection and Containers

The City contracts with EDCO Disposal for solid waste sanitation and street sweeping. Residential trash is collected once a week, usually the day before street sweeping. EDCO provides three containers: trash and green waste containers up to 95-gallon, and blue recycling containers up to 65-gallon. To change container sizes, contact EDCO Disposal at 714-522-3577.

Trash containers may be placed near the curb after 3 p.m. on the day before your scheduled collection day. They must be stored out of public view and behind your side gate before midnight after your trash is collected and on non-collection days.

Trash collection does not occur on New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving Day and Christmas Day, which delays the weekly trash collection schedule by one day.
