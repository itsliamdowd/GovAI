## Animal Control — Services and Contacts

Animal control and shelter services in Yorba Linda are provided by Orange County Animal Care (OCAC). OCAC handles patrol and impound of stray and owner-released animals, rabies control, enforcement of leash and nuisance laws, the barking dog ordinance, animal cruelty investigations, citations, and impound fee collection. ## Animal Regulations — Allowable Animals and Animal Care

Three adult dogs or cats are allowed per household. Anyone owning or having custody of four or more dogs or cats over the age of 4 months must receive approval of a Conditional Use Permit from the Planning Commission. Dogs over 4 months old that have been in the City more than 30 days must have a dog license and wear it at all times. Property owners or occupants are responsible for any animal cry or sound that may disturb the peace and comfort of neighbors.

Horses are permitted by right on properties in the RA, RLD, and RE zones with a lot size of 15,000 square feet. They are permitted in the RS and RU zones under certain restrictions subject to a Conditional Use Permit.

Animal waste must be removed and disposed of in a sanitary manner by the person having custody of the animal. Manure must be removed from the ground and stalls at least once per day and placed in covered containers. The containers must be removed from the premises or composted at least once per week. Touch or float-activated watering devices with automatic shut-off must be used for animal drinking instead of standing water troughs, to minimize mosquitoes.

## Dog Leash Requirements

Under OCCO 4-1-45, a dog on any public property must be restrained by a substantial chain or leash not exceeding six (6) feet in length, and be under the charge of a person competent to exercise care, custody, and control over the dog. This applies to all public spaces, including streets, sidewalks, parks, and trails. Dogs in Yorba Linda are only permitted to be off-leash at Checker's Dog Park.

Report off-leash dogs to Orange County Animal Control, which can be contacted 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. Regular hours (Monday–Friday, 8 a.m.–5 p.m.): 714-935-6848. After-hours emergencies (5 p.m.–8 a.m.)/weekend: 714-259-1122. If your call is not answered immediately, leave a voicemail. After-hours response times may vary.

## Pet Licensing

All dogs in Orange County over the age of four months are required by law to be licensed and vaccinated against rabies. Residents owning or having custody of any dog must license the dog within 15 days of acquisition or within 15 days after the license becomes due. ## Barking Dogs, Wildlife Sightings, and Animal Bites

A barking dog nuisance is a violation of the City's Municipal Code. Orange County Animal Control handles all enforcement for barking dogs and animal nuisances. For the complaint process and form, visit OCAC's website or call OCAC's Operations Department at 714-796-6442.

Coyotes: OC Animal Care is not licensed to trap or relocate healthy wildlife and does not provide coyote management services. Only dead or dying animals should be reported for removal. Rattlesnake and common snake sightings can be reported to Orange County Animal Control at 714-935-6848, or at 714-259-1122 after business hours.

Residents are required by law to report any bite or scratch from a domestic or wild animal. To report one, call OCAC at 714- 796-6421 or complete the online form on OCAC's website.

## Construction & Remodeling — Storm Water Best Practices

To control erosion and sediment, schedule grading and excavation projects during dry weather. Cover excavated material and stockpiles of asphalt and sand with plastic tarps. Prevent erosion by planting fast-growing annual and perennial grasses. Use a crushing company to recycle cement, asphalt, and porcelain rather than taking them to a landfill.

Never clean brushes or rinse paint containers into a street, gutter, or storm drain. Clean brushes and tools used with oil-based paints with thinner, then filter and reuse the thinner or contain it and dispose of it at a Household Hazardous Waste facility. Clean latex-based paint brushes and tools in a sink that drains into the sewer. When dry, used brushes, empty paint cans (lids off), rags, and drop cloths may go in the trash. Chemical paint stripping residue should be taken to a household hazardous waste collection site, and leftover paint can be reused or recycled at one.

Do not mix up more fresh concrete or cement than you need. Store bags of cement and plaster under a cover, protected from rainfall, runoff, and wind, away from gutters and storm drains. Never dispose of cement washout or concrete dust onto driveways, streets, gutters, or storm drains. A Best Management Practices Guide for preventing erosion (PDF) is available on the page.

## Household Hazardous Waste Disposal

Leftover household products with corrosive, toxic, ignitable, or reactive ingredients, such as paints, cleaners, oils, batteries, and pesticides, are Household Hazardous Waste and require special handling. Yorba Linda residents can dispose of these items for free at any of the County's four Household Hazardous Waste Collection Centers.

The closest center is at 1071 N. Blue Gum Street, Anaheim, CA 92806. Hours: 9 a.m.- 3 p.m. Tuesday-Saturday (closed on major holidays and during rainy weather). For regulations and information, call the Orange County collection center at 714-834-6752.
