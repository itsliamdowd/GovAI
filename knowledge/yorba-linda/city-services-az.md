## Water Safety and Drowning Prevention

The City is partnering with Orange County Fire Authority (OCFA) to provide drowning prevention information. Children can drown within 20 seconds and in as little as two inches of water. Adults ages 50 and older account for more than half of drownings each year, and OCFA advises adults to never swim alone, even at home.

The "ABCs of Water Safety" are:
- **A, Active Adult Supervision:** Children stay within the eyesight and reach of the responsible person, and an adult acts as designated "Water Watcher."
- **B, Barriers:** Use self-closing, self-latching gates that open outward away from the pool, and multiple-level barriers.
- **C, Classes:** Everyone should learn to swim, and adults should learn CPR, first aid, and rescue techniques. The City offers summer swim lessons and CPR and First Aid courses.

For more information, contact OCFA at 714-573-6200.

## Pool Safety Act (Pool and Spa Requirements)

The Pool Safety Act went into effect in January 2018. It applies to any newly constructed or remodeled pool or spa at a private, single-family home. Before final building permits are issued, the local building code official must confirm that the pool or spa has at least two of seven drowning prevention safety features.

The seven features are:
- An enclosure that isolates the pool or spa from the home.
- Removable mesh fencing with a self-latching, self-closing gate that can accommodate a key-lockable device.
- An approved safety cover.
- Exit alarms on the home's door that provides direct access to the pool or spa.
- A self-closing, self-latching device with a release mechanism placed at least 54 inches above the floor on the home's door providing direct access to the pool or spa.
- An authorized alarm placed in the pool or spa that sounds upon detection of accidental or unauthorized entrance into the water.
- Any other means of protection determined to be equal or greater than the previous features.

## Code Enforcement — How to Report Violations

File a complaint with Community Preservation through the online Report a Code Violation Form (highly encouraged). You can also call the hotline at 714-961-7138, where you may leave a voicemail 24 hours a day. Provide the address of the property, a description of the nature of the activity, and any other relevant information.

Complaints may be filed anonymously, but providing contact information lets code enforcement staff discuss the complaint in detail. Complainant names are kept confidential and not released except as required by law.

After a complaint, a Code Enforcement Officer inspects to determine whether a violation is occurring. Violations are prioritized as high (immediate or readily apparent threat to health, safety, or the environment, such as prohibited discharge into the stormwater system), medium (potential threat if left uncorrected), or low (all other violations, such as signs, shopping carts, basketball hoops, trash cans). The officer continues the process until compliance with the Municipal Code is achieved.
