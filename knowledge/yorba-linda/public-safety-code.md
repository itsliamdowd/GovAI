## Emergency Management Division

The Emergency Management Division, in coordination with all City Departments, works to strengthen the City's ability to prepare for, mitigate against, respond to, and recover from any disaster. ## Emergency Evacuation Plan Overview

Wildfires are one of the most significant threats to life and property in Yorba Linda. Residents should know their evacuation zone, learn multiple ways out of their neighborhood, and review local exit routes before an emergency. A household evacuation plan should include multiple evacuation routes; a meeting place outside your neighborhood; an out-of-area emergency contact; plans for children, older adults, people with disabilities, pets, and livestock; and a list of essential items to take. A Go Bag should include important documents, medications and prescriptions, phone chargers, cash and credit cards, water and snacks, clothing and personal items, pet supplies, and irreplaceable items if time allows. Plan to be away from home for an extended period of time.

An evacuation warning means there is a potential threat to life or property. Residents who need extra time to leave, including those with pets, livestock, young children, older adults, or access and functional needs, should consider leaving immediately. An evacuation order means there is an immediate threat to life. Residents must leave the area immediately, and the area will be closed to public access. Leave immediately when officials issue an order and do not wait for someone to come to your door. Do not return to an evacuated area until officials say it is safe.

When an evacuation is anticipated or ordered, and only if time allows: review your plan, place your Go Bag in your vehicle, gather everyone in your household including pets, wear protective clothing (long pants, a long-sleeved shirt, sturdy shoes, eye protection, and a face covering), follow official evacuation routes and instructions, and leave as early as possible.

Emergency information may come through AlertOC (phone, text, or email), High-Low sirens from law enforcement vehicles, door-to-door notifications when time allows, the City website, social media (Yorba Linda, the Orange County Sheriff's Department, and the Orange County Fire Authority), and the free Watch Duty app. The Orange County Fire Authority responds to wildfires; the Orange County Sheriff's Department issues and enforces evacuation warnings and orders and manages traffic control; Yorba Linda Emergency Management supports public information, evacuation resources, shelter coordination, vulnerable populations, and recovery efforts.

## Emergency Preparedness Guide

Residents should be prepared to be self-reliant for three days without utilities, electricity, water service, access to a supermarket or local services, or possibly without response from police, fire, or rescue. The guide lists four steps:

1. Be Informed: register with AlertOC and use Ready.gov.
2. Make a Plan: consider a Family Communications Plan, a Shelter-in-Place plan, a Get Away plan, and Emergency Plans at School and Work.
3. Build a Kit: water, non-perishable food, first aid, prescriptions, flashlight, and a battery-powered radio, enough for at least three days, maybe longer.
4. Get Involved: learn about the City's Community Emergency Response Team (CERT).

The page links to a sample Family Communications Plan (PDF), the Get Ready brochure (PDF), a Basic Home Emergency Kit List (PDF), FEMA's list of recommended supplies (PDF), and a Visual Checklist Disaster Supply Kit (PDF).

## Community Emergency Response Team (CERT) Training

The City, in partnership with the Orange County Sheriff's Department and Orange County Fire Authority, runs a CERT training program that teaches residents disaster preparedness and basic disaster response skills. Training uses a nationally recognized curriculum approved by FEMA. All courses must be completed for a trainee to be considered a graduate. Both CERT training options are provided free of charge, but pre-registration is required.

The in-person course requires 20 hours of hands-on, in-person instruction. Topics: CERT Team Organization, Disaster Medical Operations, Disaster Psychology, Fire Safety, Light Search and Rescue, and Terrorism Awareness.

To register or get information, email Chris Peña, Emergency Management Coordinator, or call 714-961-7118. Emergency Management Division, 4845 Casa Loma Avenue, Yorba Linda, CA 92886.

## Code Enforcement — How to Report Violations

File a complaint with Community Preservation by completing the online Report a Code Violation Form (highly encouraged), or call the hotline at 714-961-7138. You may leave a voicemail 24 hours a day. Provide the address of the property, a description of the nature of the activity, and any other relevant information.

Complaints may be filed anonymously, though providing contact information lets code enforcement staff discuss the complaint in detail. Complainant names are kept in confidence and not released except as required by law.

After a complaint is received, a Code Enforcement Officer inspects to determine whether a violation is occurring. Violations are prioritized as high (immediate or readily apparent threat to health, safety, or the environment, such as prohibited discharge into the stormwater system), medium (no immediate threat but potential if left uncorrected), or low (all other violations, such as signs, shopping carts, basketball hoops, trash cans, etc.). The officer continues the process until compliance with the Municipal Code is achieved.

## 2025 CalFIRE Fire Hazard Severity Zone Maps

On March 24, 2025, CalFIRE released updated Fire Hazard Severity Zone (FHSZ) maps for Local Responsibility Areas, the first comprehensive revision since 2007. Yorba Linda has Moderate, High, and Very High zones. More than 6,500 acres are classified within Moderate, High, or Very High zones, of which over 4,700 acres are in the Very High category. The maps do not mandate land-use changes.

What residents should know: properties in Very High zones must maintain defensible space by clearing flammable materials around structures; sellers must disclose if a property is in a High or Very High zone; new buildings in these areas must meet stricter, fire-resistant building standards. The California Department of Insurance has noted that most insurers already use independent wildfire risk models, and the updated maps are not expected to affect premiums directly. The City, Orange County Fire Authority, and Yorba Linda Water District issued a joint letter (PDF) for residents to share with their insurance provider.
