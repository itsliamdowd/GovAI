## City Council — Members, Role and Contact

The City Council is the legislative body for the City of Yorba Linda. It has five Yorba Linda residents elected to four-year terms. As a general law City, the Council establishes laws and policies that guide Yorba Linda's development and provide guidance on issues related to the overall quality of life within the City. The Council also serves as the Successor Agency to the Redevelopment Agency.

Current members: Carlos Rodriguez, Mayor; Peggy Huang, Mayor Pro Tem; Tara Campbell, Councilmember; Janice Lim, Councilmember; Shivinder Singh, Councilmember. Each year the Councilmembers elect one member to serve as Mayor. The Mayor presides over meetings and represents the City at various functions. A Mayor Pro Tem is also selected to serve in the absence of the Mayor. Each Councilmember may serve a maximum of three four-year terms.

Contact the City Council at 4845 Casa Loma Avenue, Yorba Linda, CA 92886, phone 714-961-7110. Under the California Public Records Act, any email sent to the City or any Councilmembers using the links on the website is a public record and may be viewed by any member of the public. City staff may also access Council emails for technical and administrative purposes. Per Resolution No. 2011-5049, the public may access Council-related documents, including Candidate Disclosure Statements, Ethic Certificates, and compensation, in the Transparency in Government section of the website.

## City Council Meetings — Schedule and How to Watch

Regular meetings are held at 6:30 p.m. on the first and third Tuesdays of each month in the Yorba Linda City Council Chambers, 4845 Casa Loma Avenue, Yorba Linda, CA 92886.

Ways to watch: attend in person; join using the posted phone number and Zoom meeting ID; or watch the live broadcast on Spectrum Cable Channel 3, AT&T U-Verse Channel 99, and live video streaming. To watch a past meeting, visit the City's video streaming page and select the meeting date from the video archive. Meetings are rebroadcast Tuesdays and Thursdays at 6:30 p.m. and Sundays at 2 p.m. on Channel 3 and AT&T U-Verse Channel 99.

You can sign up for the agenda notification mailing list to learn when the latest Council meeting agenda is available. City Council Policy C-14 (PDF) has guidelines for giving an audio-visual presentation at a Council meeting.

## Council Meeting Agendas, Minutes and Recaps

The City Clerk's Office publishes a Council Agenda package consisting of the agenda, staff reports, and associated attachments. It posts the agenda the week prior to the City Council Meeting in compliance with the Brown Act. The agenda describes each item and the recommended action by City staff.

To find meetings, use the search bar on the page. Upcoming published meetings appear at the top, in the "Upcoming Meetings" section. For past meetings, go to the "Past Meetings" section, select the year, then select the meeting type. The Agenda option lets you watch the meeting and view meeting documents. The Video option shows the meeting without documents. The Minutes option shows documents only. For technical problems, email the City's Help Desk. On a mobile device, rotate to landscape mode, or use the mobile viewing page. The application is not compatible with Internet Explorer; use Firefox, Chrome, or Safari.

City Council Recaps are posted to the City website after each meeting to briefly summarize topics from the agenda. They are provided by City staff as a public service and are not an official record of Council action. The official minutes are approved at the following meeting and are available afterward. The latest recap is posted in the Latest News section, and past recaps are in the Archive Center.

## Public Comment at Council Meetings — Speaking In Person

Public comments are taken both in person and from remote participants. You may address the City Council on any item on the agenda, or on any matter within the subject matter jurisdiction of the City Council. Comments are limited to five minutes per speaker.

To speak in person, complete a Public Comment Card and submit it to the City Clerk. The Mayor will call your name when it is your turn. At the podium, speak directly into the microphone, clearly state your name for the public record, and state the city where you live.

## Public Comment at Council Meetings — Remote Participation

Remote participants comment through the Zoom application using the phone number or Zoom link listed on the meeting agenda. Community members who wish to address the Council remotely are encouraged to submit a "Remote Public Comment Request Form" before joining the meeting. This helps the City Clerk identify speakers and organize comments.

By phone: join using the posted phone number and meeting ID. When the appropriate public comment period begins, press *9 to raise your hand, and the City Clerk will call on you. To keep your phone number private, dial *67 before joining.

By computer, tablet, or mobile device: join using the Zoom link on the agenda. When public comment begins, select "Raise Hand." Wait until the Mayor or City Clerk recognizes you. Only one remote participant may speak at a time. When it is your turn, select "Unmute" in the lower-left corner of the Zoom window, state your name, and begin. You have up to five minutes, and a beep sounds when time has ended. If you do not unmute after two attempts, staff may lower your hand and proceed to the next speaker, and you may raise your hand again once the technical issue is resolved. You may change your Zoom display name for privacy; adding the agenda item number after your name may help staff identify the item you wish to address. Under the Brown Act (Section 54953.3), providing your address and phone number is voluntary.

Beginning July 1, 2026, pursuant to California Government Code section 54953.4 (Senate Bill 707), City Council meetings will include a two-way telephonic service to allow remote attendance and public comment. If a disruption of telephonic service prevents the public from attending or observing remotely, the City Council will recess the open session for at least one hour and make a good-faith effort to restore service. The meeting will not reconvene in open session until service is restored or the City Council makes the findings required by law to continue without remote access.

## Submitting Written Public Comments

You may submit written comments by email on an item listed on the agenda, or on a matter that is not listed on the agenda but is within the City Council's subject matter jurisdiction. Use "Written Public Comments" in the email subject line. For comments about a specific agenda item, include the agenda item number and title in your email. Comments that do not identify an agenda item are treated as general public comments and included with the general public comment portion of the meeting.

## Accessibility Accommodations for Council Meetings

If you need an accommodation under the Americans with Disabilities Act to attend or participate in a meeting, contact the City Clerk's Office at least 48 hours before the meeting. Staff will review your request and make every reasonable effort to provide the requested accommodation.

By phone, call 714-961-7150. By email, include "ADA Accommodation Request" in the subject line, provide your name, address, and telephone number, and describe the accommodation you are requesting.

## Conduct at Council Meetings

A person physically present at a meeting who engages in disorderly conduct that disrupts the meeting may be directed by the presiding officer, or a majority of the City Council, to leave. If the person refuses, law enforcement may escort them from the meeting. A person required to leave may continue attending remotely by following the instructions on the posted agenda but will remain muted. A person attending remotely who engages in disorderly conduct that disrupts the meeting may be muted at the direction of the presiding officer or a majority of the City Council.

## City Clerk's Office

The City Clerk's Office upholds federal, state, and local statutes, including the Political Reform Act, the Brown Act, and the Public Records Act. Its functions include:

- Preparing and posting agendas and meeting minutes.
- Publishing and posting notices of meetings, ordinances, and other City business as required by law.
- Caring for and keeping custody of all City records, researching and releasing information in response to public requests for records under the California Public Records Act, and coordinating information requested by Subpoenas for Records.
- Serving as Filing Officer for the Political Reform Act of 1974.
- Conducting municipal elections.
- Managing and coordinating Council-appointed Boards and Commissions.
- Maintaining the Yorba Linda Municipal Code and the online content management system.
