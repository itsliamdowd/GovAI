## Permit Center — What It Processes

The Permit Center's primary function is to process permits for the construction and alteration of buildings and structures within the City. It processes and issues building, combination, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, swimming pool & spa, and solar permits, as well as certificates of occupancy. It also provides project coordination with other city departments and building code information services to the public. It manages and archives permit records and construction documents for use by the general public.

Through the HB ACA Portal you can apply for permits and plan check, check the status of your project, research historical permit records, and see a list of recent Permits Issued.

## When a Building Permit Is Required

Building permits are required to erect, enlarge, alter, remove, demolish, or repair a building or structure in the City of Huntington Beach. Examples include patio covers, fences, block walls, re-roofs, stairway & guardrail replacements, tenant improvements, signs, and paving and restriping a parking lot. Some instances do not require a building permit, per the list of Building Permit Exemptions. Call (714) 536-5241 with questions.

## Building Permit Process — Steps

The general permit process consists of:
- Preparation of construction documents or plans and specifications.
- Getting approval from the Planning Division pertaining to the scope of work.
- Submission of construction documents to the Building Division for review.
- Coordinating with other departments such as Fire and Public Works and obtaining clearances/approvals as required.
- Payment of development fees (once all applicable departments have given approvals).
- Permit issuance.

Source: https://www.huntingtonbeachca.gov/departments/community_development/building_inspection/index.php

## Building Permit Fees and Payment

There is no single fee for the various permits. Fees are based on the valuation of the project and the number of plumbing, mechanical and electrical systems. The building, planning, and fire (if applicable) plan check fee is collected when the plans and documents are submitted for plan check. The permit fees are collected when the permits are issued. Permits may be paid for by cash, check or credit cards between 8:00 a.m.-3:00 p.m. A Fee Schedule and Fee Calculator are linked on the page.

Per the California Building Code (Section 109.4), work commencing before obtaining the necessary permit(s) is subject to an additional fee, as approved by the adopted fee schedule.

Permits are valid for 1 year from the date of issuance. Permits also extend 180 days from the day of an approved inspection (partial approval does not count).

## Construction Hours Under a City Permit

Under HBMC 8.40.090 (D): Special Provisions - residents with a valid City permit are allowed to commence construction, repair, remodeling, or grading of real property between the hours of 7:00 A.M. to 7:00 P.M. from Monday thru Friday, including Saturday. On Sunday or a Federal holiday, construction, repair, remodeling, or grading of real property is not allowed.

## 2025 California Building Standards Code and Application Deadline

The new 2025 California Building Standards Code went into effect on January 1, 2026. The 2022 California Building Standards Code remains in effect and is applicable to all plans submitted prior to January 1, 2026.

Applications that remain incomplete after March 31, 2026, will be voided. To move forward with the project, a new application and plans stating the 2025 California Building Standards Code must be submitted.

If you receive an unexpected payment request or one that differs from prior instructions, do not respond or submit payment. ## Certificate of Occupancy — Who Needs One and How to Apply

A Certificate of Occupancy (CofO) is issued by the Building Division and allows occupancy or use of a building or structure or portion thereof. All businesses operating within Huntington Beach city limits need one, regardless of whether they plan to occupy an existing building or build new construction. If you are occupying a Commercial or Industrial space, you must apply and be issued a Certificate of Occupancy prior to receiving a Business License.

To initiate your application, create an account on the HB ACA Portal. Detailed instructions are linked on the page.

Cost: see the Building Department Fee Schedule for Certificate of Occupancy fees. There is no additional cost for a Certificate of Occupancy that is part of currently permitted construction at the same location. If a temporary Certificate of Occupancy is needed prior to full completion of construction, approval must be obtained from the Inspection Manager and a fee must be paid prior to issuance.

Source: https://www.huntingtonbeachca.gov/departments/community_development/building_inspection/permit_center/new_business_certificate_of_occupancy.php

## Pre-Certificate of Occupancy Inspection (Building Code Compliance Report)

The Building Code Compliance Report is a proactive way to identify and correct possible deficiencies before a new owner or tenant is involved. Its cost coincides with the Certificate of Occupancy fees (based on the type of occupancy) in the Fee Schedule. The City researches the approved plans and permit history for a specific address, performs an inspection, and gives the applicant a detailed report on the findings. It can determine whether significant alterations have occurred without permits.

Common problems include non permitted mezzanines in manufacturing or warehouses, non permitted tenant improvement work, expired building and/or associated permits, mechanical, electrical and plumbing work that requires permits and listings, exiting out of buildings per occupancy classification, non-permitted electrical changes to the service or sub-panels and distribution, and changes in occupancy classifications.

The report does not allow, permit, design, or imply approval for the future use of a building. Before purchasing or leasing, verify with your licensed professional and all of the city departments (Community Development, Public Works, and Fire) whether the intended use will be compatible with the building.

## Planning Division — Applications and Permits Processed

Current Planning processes applications for: Coastal Development Permits, Conditional Use Permits, Entitlement Plan Amendments, Preliminary Plan Reviews, Tentative Tract Maps, Variances (Including the Flood Plain Variance), Annexation Requests, Precise Plan of Street Alignments, Lot Line Adjustments, Sign Code Exceptions, Temporary Use Permits, Tentative Parcel Maps, Address Changes, CC&R Reviews, Certificate of Compliances, Final Maps, Limited Sign Permits, Temporary Sign Permits, Temporary Sales/Event Permits, and Animal Permits.

Minor projects such as conforming room additions, patio covers and fences are reviewed administratively by Planning staff at the Planning Counter, provided they comply with the Zoning and Subdivision Ordinance. More complex projects are typically processed through the Zoning Administrator or the Planning Commission, with few projects heard by the City Council unless appealed.

Source: https://www.huntingtonbeachca.gov/departments/community_development/planning_zoning/services.php

## Zoning Administrator — Projects and Hearings

The Zoning Administrator reviews projects that meet certain criteria in the Zoning & Subdivision Ordinance. These typically include new single-family dwellings in the Coastal Zone, small multi-family residential developments, commercial and industrial land uses and developments, minor zoning code variances, and land subdivisions involving four or fewer parcels. Actions taken by the Zoning Administrator are subject to appeal to the Planning Commission.

Open public hearings are held on the first and third Wednesday afternoon of the month at 1:30 p.m. Agendas are available to the public on the Thursday prior to the meeting. Regularly scheduled meetings are subject to cancellation due to a lack of agenda items. Notice is mailed to all property owners of record and tenants within a 500 radius of the project site, and published in the Huntington Beach Independent newspaper, a minimum of ten days prior to the hearing date.

Staff contact: Jason Kelley, Senior Planner/Liaison to Zoning Administrator, (714) 374-1553.

Source: https://www.huntingtonbeachca.gov/government/boards___commissions/zoning_administrator.php

## Planning Commission — Projects, Review Time and Meetings

The Planning Commission typically acts on major development proposals, tentative tract maps, zoning map amendments, zoning text amendments, general plan amendments, and similar applications. The review process for a Planning Commission item normally requires four to five months.

The Planning Commission meets on the second and fourth Tuesday of each month at 6:00 p.m. in the Council Chambers. Study sessions are frequently held at 5:15 p.m. prior to the meeting in Room B-8; they are open to the public but are not a public hearing. Agendas are available to the public the Thursday prior to the meeting. Meetings are televised live on HBTV Channel 3. The Community Development Department is at 2000 Main Street, Huntington Beach, CA 92648.
