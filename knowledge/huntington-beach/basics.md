## About Huntington Beach

Huntington Beach is a seaside city in Orange County, Southern California, bordered by the Pacific Ocean on the west. It has 8.5 miles of beach and is nicknamed "Surf City." The city is 35 miles south of Los Angeles and 90 miles north of San Diego, and has a population near 200,000 residents. It incorporated in 1909 under its first mayor, Ed Manning, and is named for railroad magnate Henry Huntington.

The climate is generally sunny, dry and cool, with temperatures ranging mostly from 65° to 80° Fahrenheit. In summer, temperatures rarely exceed 85 degrees. In winter, they rarely fall below 40 degrees. Annual rainfall is less than 12 inches, with most falling between December and March. Ocean temperatures average 55 to 65 degrees.

## Economy and Tourism

Tourism and technology are the leading industries in Huntington Beach. The city is home to more than 650 industrial businesses, including Boeing, Quicksilver, Cambro Manufacturing, and C & D Aerospace. Boeing is one of the state's largest employers. Quicksilver, the second largest employer in Huntington Beach, has its worldwide corporate headquarters in the city.

The city hosts an annual visitor population of more than 11 million people, with the highest attendance during summer, special events, and weekends. The downtown district includes an art center, a shopping district, and the International Surfing Museum. The Surfing Hall of Fame and the Surfing Walk of Fame are also downtown. Huntington Beach is near three airports: John Wayne/Orange County (SNA), Long Beach (LGB), and Los Angeles International Airport (LAX).

## Education

More than 50 public and private schools offer elementary, middle, high school, and adult education to Huntington Beach residents. Golden West College is located within the city limits. Further educational opportunities are offered nearby at the University of Irvine, the California State Universities of Long Beach and Fullerton, and a number of private colleges.

## City Council

Huntington Beach residents are represented by a seven-member, part-time City Council. The Council is the city's legislative authority. Its duties include establishing goals and policies, enacting legislation, adopting the city's operating budget, and appropriating funds for city services. The mayor is selected annually from among the Council Members and runs the City Council meetings.

Current members and term end dates: Pat Burns (2026), Casey McKeon (2026), Don Kennedy (2028), Gracey Larrea-Van Der Mark (2026), Butch Twining (2028), Chad Williams (2028), Andrew Gruel (2026). Casey McKeon is listed as Mayor and Butch Twining as Mayor Pro Tem.

## City Clerk's Office

The City Clerk's Office is at City Hall, 2nd Floor, 2000 Main Street, Huntington Beach, CA 92648. Phone: (714) 536-5227. Fax: (714) 374-1557.

The City Clerk is the City's official Custodian of Records. Records date back to the City's incorporation on February 17, 1909. They include original City Council minutes, resolutions, ordinances, deeds, and agreements. The office maintains and updates the Municipal Code and the Zoning and Subdivision Code. It also conducts municipal elections, offers voter registration forms, and processes candidates for elective office. It is the Filing Officer for election campaign reports and Statements of Economic Interests. It is a designated U.S. Department of State Passport Acceptance Facility and keeps a collection of historical photographs and books about the city.

## City Council Meetings — Decorum and Public Comment

Members of the public address the City Council from the podium or designated area. Comments are addressed to the Council as a whole or to the Presiding Officer. Signs, placards, banners, props or similar items may not be larger than 18x24", illuminated, or attached to a pole, stick or other device. The Chief of Police assigns a Sergeant-at-Arms to all City Council meetings.

To speak in person in the City Council Chambers, complete a Request to Speak form delivered to the City Clerk. Sign-ups begin in person 30 minutes prior to the start of Study Session, Closed Session, or Regular City Council Meeting, whichever comes first. They are accepted until the public comment period begins. Each person may have up to 3 minutes to speak, but the Mayor may reduce the time if there are many speakers.

Written supplemental communication on agenda items can be emailed to the City Clerk's Office. Information received by 9:00 AM the day of the meeting is distributed to Council before consideration of agenda items. Information received after 9:00 AM becomes part of the official public record after the meeting. Presentation materials must be submitted to the City Clerk's Office no later than 9:00 AM the day of the meeting. No digital presentations are accepted at the City Council meeting.

## Elections, Voter Registration & Campaign Contributions

Voter registration applications are available online at the Orange County Registrar of Voters website, www.ocvote.com. To register in California you must be a U.S. citizen at registration, a California resident, and at least 18 years of age on or before the next election. You must not be in prison or on parole for a felony conviction, and must not have been judged by a court to be mentally incompetent. The deadline to register is 15 days before each local and statewide Election Day. You must re-register when you move, change your name, or change your political party choice. Vote centers can be found at https://ocvote.gov/elections/vote-center-locations. Vote-By-Mail information is at www.ocvote.com.

## Passport Services

The City Clerk's Office is at 2000 Main Street, Huntington Beach, CA. Phone: (714) 536-5227. Services include processing of DS-11 passport applications, expedited processing, and passport photos. Passport services are by appointment only, Monday through Thursday from 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Appointments are made through the City's Qless Kiosk. For emergency travel needs, contact the National Passport Center at 1-877-487-2778 or visit travel.state.gov.

First-time applicants must bring:
- Proof of U.S. citizenship
- Government-issued photo I.D.
- Check or money order. Cash, credit or debit cards are not accepted for payments to the U.S. Department of State.

All first-time applicants, regardless of age, must appear in person. Children under 16 must have the consent of both parents or legal guardians. Applicants 16 to 17 must appear in person with one parent or legal guardian. Photocopies of citizenship documents are not acceptable. Applicants born in California must submit a state-certified Certificate of Live Birth, and California birth abstracts and hospital birth records are not acceptable.

Fees:
- DS-11 (Age 16 and Over): $130.00
- DS-11 (Age 15 and Under): $100.00
- Execution Fee Assessed on DS-11: $35.00
- DS-82 (Self Renewal by Mail): $130.00
- Passport Card (Adult): $30.00
- Passport Card (Minor): $15.00
- Expedited Service: $60.00
- File Search Fee (if record was issued before 1994): $150.00
- 1-3 Day Return Delivery: $26.87
- Passport Photo: $16.00

Fees payable to the U.S. Department of State must be made by check, cashier's check or money order only. The $35.00 execution fee is payable to the City of Huntington Beach and can be paid by cash, check or money order.

To renew by mail (DS-82), all four conditions must be true:
- You can submit your most recent passport, which is not mutilated or damaged.
- You were at least 16 years old when it was issued.
- It was issued less than 15 years ago.
- You use the same name as on that passport, or your name was changed by marriage or court order and you can submit supporting documentation.

If you do not meet these conditions, you are not eligible to renew by mail.

## Fire Department Divisions

The Huntington Beach Fire Department has four divisions.
- **Fire Administration:** provides management, research, clerical, financial, and records support. It administers the FireMed Membership Program.
- **Emergency Response:** provides emergency force for fire, medical, rescue, and hazardous materials incidents. Eight fire stations have paramedic engine companies staffed by four personnel each. The Division also coordinates the Community Emergency Response Team (CERT) Program and the Radio Amateur Civil Emergency Services (RACES) Program.
- **Community Risk Reduction** (formerly Fire Prevention): enforces local, state, and federal codes through inspection and code enforcement, plan review, public education and fire investigative services. It conducts oil inspections, including enforcement of the Huntington Beach Oil Code. It also operates the Hazardous Materials Business Plan Program (HMBP).
- **Marine Safety:** provides year-round lifeguard services on the City's beach, including medical aid and code enforcement services. It manages the summer Junior Lifeguard Program and also provides lifeguard services to Sunset Beach. The Division has 14 permanent Marine Safety Officers and management staff, supported by more than 150 recurrent ocean lifeguards.
