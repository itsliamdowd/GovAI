# Permits & How-To

## Building Permit — Projects That Need a Permit

Businesses must obtain building permits for construction and interior improvements. Other projects that require building permits are: additions, demolitions, new interior walls, drop ceilings, new plumbing lines and fixtures, new electrical wiring or lighting fixtures, and replacing or installing HVAC equipment.

Finish work that does not require a permit includes carpeting, non-structural flooring (tile, laminate, and hardwood), painting, and wallpapering. The Building and Safety Division can help determine what work requires a building permit.

## Building Permit — Application and Plan Check

Before applying for a building permit, check with the Planning Division to make sure your proposed business is permitted at your desired location and whether a Conditional Use Permit is required. The Planning Division can be contacted at 562-383-4100.

You, your contractor, or your designer submit plans to the Building and Safety Division through the online portal. Non-structural changes (e.g., door/window replacements) can often be drawn by your contractor, and in some cases a licensed contractor or lessee might qualify for an over-the-counter permit. For structural work, detailed plans and calculations are mandatory. A plan check fee is required upon submission. The initial plan review takes about two weeks, and you receive a list of any needed corrections. After revising your plans, resubmit them through the online portal.

Building & Safety is at 110 East La Habra Boulevard, La Habra, CA 90631, phone 562-383-4116. Community and Economic Development Counter Hours are Monday-Thursday, 7:30 a.m.-1:00 p.m.

## Permits From Other Agencies

Orange County Health Department (OCHD) permits are required for businesses preparing or serving food or drinks. Call OCHD at 714-433-6000.

South Coast Air Quality Management District (AQMD) requires a Notice of Demolition and an asbestos survey for the demolition of structures larger than 100 square feet. Call AQMD at 909-396-2000.

A preliminary Water Quality Management Plan (WQMP) must be submitted for review and approval before a building permit is issued if your project went through the discretionary review process and received Planning Commission approval. Contact the Engineering Division at 562-383-4151 to learn which type of WQMP to prepare. CalGOLD (a State website) can help you learn which permits your business requires.

## Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) — Types, Locations and Limits

An ADU is a dwelling unit with complete independent living facilities on the same lot as a single-family or multifamily dwelling. A Junior Accessory Dwelling Unit (JADU) is located entirely within the walls of an existing or proposed single-family dwelling and is no more than 500 square feet in size. A JADU only requires an efficiency kitchen and need not have a private bathroom if it has full access to a bathroom in the primary dwelling.

ADUs are permitted on lots with an existing or proposed single-family or multi-family dwelling (including duplexes). JADUs are only permitted within a single-family dwelling. No more than one ADU and one JADU are permitted on a lot with an existing or proposed single-family dwelling. On multi-family lots, attached ADUs may only be proposed within existing non-living areas (e.g. storage, garage) and are limited to 25% of the existing units, or one ADU, whichever is greater. Up to two detached ADUs are permitted on multi-family lots, subject to a 16-foot height limit and 4-foot rear and side yard setbacks.

On July 21, 2025 the City Council approved amendments to LHMC Section 18.12.150 Accessory Dwelling Units. The City has adopted Ordinance CC2025-05, which reflects the changes.

## Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) — How to Get a Permit

The Building & Safety Division handles all building permit intake. Your submittal package must include a completed Building Permit Application, Project Plans, and an ADU Survey. Once all required documentation is received, Building & Safety routes the package to the Building Division, Planning Division, Engineering Division and Public Works. If your submittal requires Fire Department review, you must contact Los Angeles County Fire Department for their requirements.

Southern California Edison (SCE) review is required for ADUs within or near an SCE easement, before submitting to the City. Include a copy of the Preliminary Title Report with your submittal documents. If an SCE easement exists on the property, your application is deemed incomplete and placed on hold until an approved SCE Encroachment Agreement is received.

A plan check fee is due at time of submittal. The plan check is not approved until all departments/divisions have approved your plans. Once approved, the applicant receives an email notification with an invoice. After the invoice is paid, you receive your approved plans and your inspection job card. You may then begin construction and request inspections as needed. For fee questions, contact Building and Safety at 562-383-4116.

Source: https://lahabraca.gov/1487/Accessory-Dwelling-Units

## Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) — Pre-Approved Plans

The County of San Diego offers pre-approved building plans that are acceptable to the City of La Habra for plan and permitting. The property owner or applicant works with a design professional to complete the plan set for a full submittal. Supplemental materials such as Title 24, Cal-Green, and roof truss calculations are also required, along with additional La Habra-specific information (such as climate zone, BMPs, architectural compatibility).

The pre-approved plans cannot be modified. Custom designed plans can be submitted through the standard building permit review process. Specify on the permit application which standard plan you are using.

## Film Permits

All motion pictures, television and still photography must apply for a film permit to film in the City. Students with a letter from their school, signed by the department chair confirming it is a school project, are not required to meet commercial requirements. Filming involving fewer than four people also does not have to meet the commercial requirements.

Film permits must be submitted 30 days in advance. Once the City receives the information, it reviews it within 24 hours to determine what conditions may need to be met. You also need to request a Los Angeles County Fire Permit. For more information, contact the Community Center at 562-383-4200.

Fees: Application fee: $87. Administrative fee: $124.56. Per car fee: $15.00. Police: $110.00 per hour. Traffic Administrative fee: $135.00.

Source: https://lahabraca.gov/700/Filming-in-La-Habra

## Special Event Permit — Business Outdoor Sales and Events

Special Events are permitted for businesses located within the City of La Habra with the approval of a Special Event Permit. 12 special events are permitted per calendar year, with a minimum of 30 days between events. Special events with outdoor sales are limited to four events per calendar year with 30 days in between events. Generally, small Special Events include grand openings, holiday sales, or other promotional events.

The Special Event Permit for small events must be submitted to staff at least 45 days prior to the event. For large Special Events, such as car shows, street fairs, carnivals, and food festivals, a Site Plan Application must be submitted at least 90 days prior to the event. Applications for either a small or large event must be submitted through the City's online portal.

Source: https://lahabraca.gov/368/Outdoor-Sales-or-Events

## Encroachment / Excavation Permit

Encroachment permits are required for most types of work within the public right-of-way. Right-of-way typically includes streets, sidewalks, and parkways behind the curb. Even when there is no sidewalk, the street right-of-way usually extends behind the curb. When in doubt, check with the Engineering Division for the exact extent of the City's right of way. Whoever applies for the permit is ultimately responsible for guaranteeing completion of any work started.

Submittal for all Engineering permits: email the completed application for plan check to Engineering@lahabraca.gov, with the project Address in the subject line. Provide a scaled plan sketch with dimensions or site map (if applicable); Traffic Control Plans (TCP) or Watch Manual; a valid copy of the contractor's license; a valid copy of the contractor's city business license; and a certificate of liability insurance listing City of La Habra as additionally insured.

Permit fees are based on the type and amount of work involved. A cash deposit may be required to guarantee completion of the work, protection and/or repair of existing facilities, or to enforce compliance with restricted work hours. The cash deposit is fully refundable upon successful compliance with the terms of the permit.

Source: https://www.lahabraca.gov/1310/Permits-Fees

## Block Party Street Closure Permit

A typical "Block Party" consists of several families extending social activities into a residential (public) street. The event is non-profit in nature and is closed to the outside public. A block party street closure permit may be obtained for the temporary closure of select residential streets after review and approval of the City's Engineering Division.
