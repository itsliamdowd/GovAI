## Affordable Housing Resale Program

The City of Carlsbad's Affordable Housing Resale Program is closed until the current list of applicants has been exhausted. In 2021, the City Council authorized the program as part of an overall effort to reduce homelessness and provide more affordable housing in Carlsbad.

The city requires housing developers to set aside a percentage of units to be made available to low-income buyers. When an original buyer is ready to sell their affordable unit, the city has the first right of refusal to purchase it, which keeps the unit within the city's affordable housing inventory.

## Housing Choice Voucher (Rental Assistance) — Wait List

The city, as a Public Housing Agency, administers the Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) program, a federally funded rental assistance program that helps pay rent for qualified low income residents. The program is currently full and the wait list is closed. There is no anticipated date when the wait list will reopen.

It is approximately a 15 year wait from time of application to be considered for rental assistance. Notice of the list reopening will be advertised on the city's website, on social media and in the local media/news. Current waitlist applicants should contact the office directly by email or phone. Residents can also sign up on the page for email notification when the waitlist opens.

## Housing Choice Voucher — Landlord Guide

Advantages to an owner/property manager are timely rental payments via Direct Deposit from the City of Carlsbad; a biennial Housing Quality Inspection of the unit; staff assistance in finding tenants for vacant units; and pre-screening by the agency for sex offender registration (Megan's Law) and drug-related or violent criminal activity.

Getting started: notify the agency of vacancies by emailing housing@carlsbadca.gov or submit the Notice of Vacancy Form. Monthly rent must be comparable to other unassisted rentals in the area. Landlords may charge HCV participants an application fee if it matches the fee of non-HCV tenants. Upon approval, the tenant provides the landlord a Request for Tenancy Approval (RFTA) to complete, after which a Housing Quality Standards (HQS) inspection will be scheduled. The landlord signs a lease/rental agreement with the tenant and a Housing Assistance Payments (HAP) Contract with the Housing Agency; both take effect the day the unit passes the HQS inspection and the tenant takes possession. Security deposits must not exceed those for comparable private market or other unassisted units and are paid by the renter. Landlords must also complete the PaymentWorks registration for direct rental payments and register for the RentCafe vendor portal.

Responsibilities: maintain the property in good condition and complete repairs promptly; do not enter the unit without proper notice and the tenant's permission except in an emergency; comply with Equal Opportunity and Fair Housing Requirements; enforce and comply with the rental agreement and HAP contract; collect the security deposit, tenant's share of rent, and charges for tenant-caused damages as specified in the rental agreement; pay for utilities and services as obligated; do not collect excess rent from the tenant except their share/predetermined portion; make reasonable modifications for persons with disabilities; and report suspected fraud to the Housing Agency (confidential & anonymous).

Source: https://www.carlsbadca.gov/departments/housing-homeless-services/affordable-housing/rental-assistance

## Smoke-Free Multiunit Housing

Smoking and vaping (including cannabis) is banned inside and outside Carlsbad apartments, condos, townhomes and other multiunit homes under a city law that took effect Jan. 1, 2025, except for certain designated outdoor spaces. It applies to properties with three or more residential units, including apartment buildings, condos and townhomes, senior and assisted living facilities, long-term health care facilities, and single family homes licensed as care facilities. It does not apply to hotels, motels and certain timeshares, mobile home parks, campgrounds, or single family homes (including those with accessory dwelling units). The ban covers units, private balconies, porches, decks, patios, and common areas (except designated smoking areas). All new or renewed multiunit leases or rental agreements must include the smoke-free provisions.

Enforcement is private: landlords and other tenants may take legal action against violators. The city's Police Department and Code Enforcement Division will not be responsible for enforcement. Landlords may issue written warnings, and violators may be subject to fines. Leases entered into, renewed, or continued after Jan. 1, 2025 may be terminated at the landlord or property manager's discretion if a tenant violates the smoking provisions.

Landlord obligations: notify all tenants; post "no smoking" signage in common areas; include non-smoking provisions in all new or renewed leases initiated after Jan. 1, 2025; include a disclosure about the ordinance in all condominium sales contracts after Jan. 1, 2025; and investigate and enforce violations, including a written notice to the resident. A designated outdoor smoking area must be at least 25 feet from any enclosed areas, play area, swimming pool or other recreation area, have a clearly marked perimeter with signs, and not overlap with any other non-smoking areas.

Tenant steps: (1) speak with your neighbor; (2) if the issue persists, report it to your landlord or property manager and document your communication in writing; (3) if no action is taken, residents, as third-party beneficiaries, may seek a court order to enforce the smoking provisions of a lease or rental agreement entered into, renewed, or continued after Jan. 1, 2025 (a last resort).

## Short-Term Vacation Rental (STVR) Permit

A short-term vacation rental is a rental of a home or accessory dwelling unit for under 30 consecutive days, regulated by Carlsbad Municipal Code Chapter 5.60. STVRs are permissible only on property zoned for residential use and located within the California Coastal Zone or in the La Costa Resort and Spa Master Plan area, and are strictly prohibited outside those areas. ADUs and JADUs issued a building permit after Jan. 1, 2020 are prohibited from being an STVR. Occupancy is limited to two people per bedroom, plus one person per unit.

The owner must first obtain an STVR permit from the Community Development Department (Form P-98, plus Impact Response Plan Form P-98A), then a Business License (Home-Based Business) from the Finance Department. A local contact person must be available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week to respond within 45 minutes to complaints. Noise is prohibited between 10 p.m. and 10 a.m. Apply online through the Customer Self Service Portal. Questions go to STVR@CarlsbadCA.gov or 442-339-5978. The Finance Department can be reached at Business.License@CarlsbadCA.gov or 442-339-2495.

A new annual fee of $231 is required for all STVR permits applied for after Sept. 1, 2026. Existing operators pay the new fee when their permit or business license is up for renewal. STVRs are subject to a 10% Transient Occupancy Tax and a 2% Carlsbad Tourism Business Improvement District assessment. Three or more administrative citations and/or verifiable municipal code violations within a 24-month period result in permit revocation, and a new application for the property will not be accepted for a minimum of 36 months.

## Housing Commission

The Housing Commission advises the Community Development Commission and/or City Council on affordable housing programs, policies and regulations, the General Plan Housing Element, financial assistance for affordable housing projects, and programs to prevent, reduce and manage homelessness. It has five members appointed by the mayor and confirmed by the City Council; members must be Carlsbad residents and registered voters and serve four year terms.

Regular meetings are held on the second Thursday of the month at 4 p.m. in the City Council Chamber, 1200 Carlsbad Village Drive, Carlsbad, CA 92008. Staff contact: Christian Gutierrez, Housing Services Manager, 442-339-2299, christian.gutierrez@carlsbadca.gov.

To comment in person, fill out a speaker request form in the foyer and submit it to the Clerk before the item begins. Speakers have three minutes; group representatives have 10 minutes if three other group members are present. To comment in writing, email christian.gutierrez@carlsbadca.gov with the agenda item number in the subject line. Emails received by 1 p.m. the day prior to the meeting will be provided to the Commission before the meeting starts.

## Housing Plan Update (Housing Element)

The state approved Carlsbad's housing plan (housing element) on July 13, 2021; it covers 2021-2029 and details how Carlsbad will accommodate projected housing needs. The city was required to designate space for about 3,900 new housing units, of which about 2,100 need to be affordable for people with lower incomes. On Jan. 30, 2024 the City Council approved rezoning 16 properties to meet state requirements. The action did not approve any specific projects, and the city does not build housing; property owners decide whether and when to build. Rezoning of sites in the Coastal Zone also requires California Coastal Commission approval.
