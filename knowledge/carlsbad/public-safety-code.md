## Fire & Life Safety Division — Permits, Plan Checks and Inspections

The Fire & Life Safety Division is part of the Community Risk Reduction Bureau. ## Emergency Alerts and Evacuation Notifications (Genasys Protect and AlertSanDiego)

In 2024, regional police, fire and emergency management leaders moved to the Genasys system for evacuations. First responders can create evacuation zones with detailed geographic boundaries and send targeted notifications to people in those zones. Evacuations were previously planned using a grid system.

Residents can download the Genasys Protect app or visit its website to look up their zone, subscribe for real-time evacuation alerts, and track the status of specific neighborhoods. The app can also monitor zones outside Carlsbad.

AlertSanDiego is still the official countywide notification system. Residents should register to receive critical alerts through landlines, cellphones, email and text.

Source: https://www.carlsbadca.gov/residents/safety/emergency-preparedness

## Emergency Preparedness — Family Plan and Where Updates Are Posted

The city's Make a Plan guidance has four steps:
1. Discuss how you will receive emergency alerts and warnings, your evacuation route, your family/household communication plan, and whether your emergency preparedness kit needs updating.
2. Consider specific household needs, such as ages, medical needs including prescriptions and equipment, disabilities or access and functional needs, languages spoken, pets or service animals, and school-aged children.
3. Fill out a Family Emergency Plan (a Family Emergency Communication Plan can be downloaded).
4. Practice the plan with your family or household.

During an emergency affecting Carlsbad, the city posts updates on the city website, city social media, Genasys Protect, AlertSanDiego, and 2-1-1 San Diego (call or visit website). The page has guidance for earthquakes, wildfires, tsunami, terrorism, power outage and extreme weather.

The Office of Emergency Management and Resilience coordinates emergency preparedness planning and training for city staff and the Carlsbad community. ## Fire Incident Reports

Fire incident reports are public records, with a processing time of up to 14 days. You will be notified when the report is ready. Reports can be picked up or mailed only; they are not faxed or sent electronically. The fee is $10, cash or check only (payable to the City of Carlsbad).

To request by mail, submit a completed incident request form with payment to: City of Carlsbad, Fire Administration, Attn: Records, 2560 Orion Way, Carlsbad, CA 92010. An online fire incident report request with electronic payment is also available.

## Fire Extinguisher Guidance

Every home should have at least one fire extinguisher, and it is advisable to have one in every room that poses a fire danger (such as the kitchen or garage). The National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) recommends at least one fire extinguisher for every 600 square feet of living area. Mount it on a wall, 36 to 44 inches off the floor, away from fire hazards and near an exit door or escape route.

A typical general purpose residential extinguisher has a rating of "1-A: 10-BC." A multiple-purpose extinguisher rated for A, B and C class fires is best for the home. Call 9-1-1 for a fire that cannot be controlled with a fire extinguisher.

Source: https://www.carlsbadca.gov/departments/fire/q-a

## After a Fire — Assistance Contacts and Roofing Rules

- Emotional support: the Trauma Intervention Program is available to North County residents. To request a counselor, call 760-931-2104.
- Emergency housing: check your insurance policy. If uninsured, contact the American Red Cross at 760-757-5403 or the Salvation Army at 619-231-6000.
- Lost pets: contact North County Animal Control at 760-438-2312.
- Vehicles damaged in a structure fire: contact the homeowner's and vehicle insurance agents. If the automobile is not insured, North County Transit District, 760-722-6283, is available for mass transit.
- Perishables: medications, food and beverages exposed to smoke or heat should not be consumed. The page's rule is "If in doubt, throw it out."

Wood shingle roofs are no longer permitted on new construction or on replacement roofs. Fire retardant roofing materials are required as replacement becomes necessary and on all new structures.

## Emergency Operations, EMS, CPR and Carbon Monoxide Safety

Emergency Operations is responsible for fire suppression, rescue, emergency medical service delivery, lifeguard service and marine safety, and disaster mitigation. Personnel are housed in seven fire stations and respond to an average of 15,000 service calls each year.

A firefighter/paramedic is on every apparatus. Bike medics, who wear red shirts and ride bicycles, patrol the downtown and beach areas. The team consists of two medics.

The free PulsePoint app, available for iPhone and Android, alerts CPR-trained citizens if someone nearby is experiencing sudden cardiac arrest. For a heart attack or suspected heart attack, call 9-1-1 immediately. The Fire Department does not offer CPR certification classes and refers residents to the American Heart Association.

For carbon monoxide, the Consumer Product Safety Commission recommends at least one CO detector with an audible alarm near each bedroom, and at least one on each story of a multi-story home.

Source: https://www.carlsbadca.gov/departments/fire/fire-operations/emergency-medical-services
