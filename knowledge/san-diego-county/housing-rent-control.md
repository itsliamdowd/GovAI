## Rental Assistance Programs

Housing and Community Development Services (HCDS) has several programs to help low-income people pay their rent. The Rental Assistance page lists these programs:
- Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher.
- Veterans Services, which help homeless veterans receive rental assistance and support services through VASH (Veterans Affairs Support Housing) and other programs.
- Housing Opportunities for Persons with AIDS (HOPWA), which provides some housing and support services for people with HIV-AIDS.
- Public Housing.
- Moderate Rehabilitation Program.
- Ending Homelessness programs.
- Resident Advisory Board.

The page also lists "Enhanced Landlord Incentive Program Extended! Available Now!" under What's new at HCDS. Related links include the Voucher Payment Standard, Family Handbook, Housing Directory, Fair Housing, and Report Fraud.

## Extended Foster Care — Housing

Extended Foster Care is available from age 18 until you turn 21. You become part of the program when you turn 17. To get it, your social worker must approve the place where you live. You might be able to stay in your current home or choose a new place.

Placements include:
- An approved home of a relative or family friend.
- A foster family home.
- The home of a non-related legal guardian.
- Transitional housing programs the County approves.
- An apartment by yourself or with roommates.
- Renting a room.
- Dorms or university housing.
- San Pasqual Academy alumni housing.

If you leave, you can re-enter Extended Foster Care any time before your 21st birthday. You must agree to meet the participation requirements, sign a voluntary agreement, and live in an approved setting. If you are 21 or older you cannot get Extended Foster Care. You can visit 211SanDiego.org or call 2-1-1 to find other resources.

For more information, call 619-767-5275 or email cfwbefcinfo.hhsa@sdcounty.ca.gov.

Source: https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/service/help-for-current-and-former-foster-youth-to-live-independently/

## Independent Living Skills Program

The Independent Living Skills program helps current or former foster youth learn skills and get community resources to prepare to live on their own. You become part of the program when you turn 16. Skills taught include how to find safe, affordable housing and how to manage your home.

The program is for two groups:
- Foster youth 16 to 18 years old in placement outside their home, who get help from County staff.
- People 18 to 21 years old who are non-minor dependents or former foster youth, who get help from outside agencies.

For more information, call 866-457-4636 or use the form on the page.
