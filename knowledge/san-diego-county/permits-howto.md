## How to Read Your DEHQ Permit

Fields on a Department of Environmental Health and Quality (DEHQ) permit:
- A = Expiration Month and B = Expiration Year: the month and year the permit will expire.
- C = Owner/Operator Name, D = Facility Name, E = Facility Located at: the current records on file. If any is incorrect, contact the division below.
- F = Mailing Address: the permit contact's name and address where permits get mailed.
- G = Record Number: how DEHQ identifies your business or facility. Reference it when paying online or contacting DEHQ.
- H = Type of permit.
- I = Expiration Date.
- J = Attention: important notes or instructions for this permit.
- K = Notes: notes for all permits.

Contacts:

## California License to Sell Nursery Stock

To sell any nursery product you must have a California License to Sell Nursery Stock. The license is not transferable to another person. The fee for a new license is due at the time any change in ownership occurs. It is issued for a 12-month period. There are four such periods: January through December; April through March; July through June; and October through September. New licenses are issued for the license period commencing nearest to the date of receipt of the application.

The license must be renewed no later than the 10th day of the first month of the licensee's license period. If the fee is not received by that date, the license is forfeited. It can then be renewed only upon payment of a penalty in the form of a registration fee, or upon receipt of a signed statement that no nursery stock was sold from the first day of the license period to the date the regular fee was paid.

The minimum fee for a license is $150. It is required for each branch sales yard, store or sales location owned and operated in the state. A location where nursery stock is grown or assembled for distribution directly to customers requires an additional license. An acreage fee is also required for land in excess of one acre used in the production, storage or sale of nursery stock. The acreage fee may not be less than $25 or more than $900. It does not apply to licensees whose gross income from cut flowers and cut ornamentals is 75 percent or greater of the gross income of their nursery.

## Nursery Stock Certificates, Shipping and Receiving Plants

If your nursery is found to be commercially clean, you may request a California Nursery Stock Certificate. Call the Plant Health and Pest Prevention Program at (760) 752-4700 for a nursery inspection.

If you ship nursery products to other states, counties or countries, you may need one or more of: a Certificate of Quarantine Compliance, a State Phytosanitary Certificate, a Federal Phytosanitary Certificate, or a California Nursery Stock Certificate. 48-hour advance notice is required for certification inspections, and inspection fees apply.

Persons or companies receiving nursery products not labeled with a California Nursery Stock Certificate must call (760) 752-4713 for inspection and release. Incoming plant products must be kept in an isolated holding area away from production areas. Some shipments may need to be held for several days pending lab analysis of samples.

## Food Facility Public Health Permit

Any person operating a retail food facility (including vending vehicles or a catering business) is required by law to apply for and obtain a valid public health permit. San Diego County has 18 cities with more than 13,800 permanent retail food facilities, including over 8,100 restaurants. Environmental Health Specialists conduct more than 32,000 inspections at these food facilities each year.

The Food and Housing Division conducts risk-based inspections, focusing on items that strongly affect food safety and foodborne illness. Handwashing, food temperatures and dishwashing are more important than a missing light bulb or a broken tile. Inspections use a statewide standard "Food Inspection Report" that lists Major Violations, Minor Violations and Good Retail Practices. Mobile food facilities are also inspected and can be inspected at the County Operation Center. More information is at www.sdcountymobilefood.org.

Source: https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/food-safety-permits-inspection-resources/

## Restaurant Inspection Results and Grading System (SD Food Info)

The SD Food Info website lets the public search online for the most recent routine inspection information for all retail food facilities in San Diego County. Inspections are unannounced and performed by a Registered Environmental Health Specialist (REHS), who provides a signed copy of the inspection report at the end. Under state law each food facility must maintain a copy of the most recent inspection report onsite and make it available for review upon request.

Grading:
1. Each violation is assigned points. A Major Risk Factor is worth four points, a Minor Risk Factor two, and a Good Retail Practice one.
2. The points are added up and subtracted from 100 to give the inspection "score".
3. "A" = score of 90 to 100 percent (satisfactory compliance with state law). "B" = 80 to 89 percent (needs improvement). "C" = 79 percent or less (failing grade).
4. The grade card must be displayed near the public entrance during hours of operation.

During an inspection, an Official Notice of Violation (NOV) is issued when the REHS determines the facility is not in compliance with the law. The notice lists the violations along with a specified time to correct each one.

## Land Development — Grading and Improvement Plan Submittals

The Land Development Division provides engineering and review services for construction and development projects in the unincorporated areas of San Diego County. Schedule an appointment with the Land Development Counter for: Grading Permit or Improvement Plan Application Submittal; Grading Permit or Improvement Plan Issuance; and All Mapping Applications (Final Map, Parcel Map, SB9 Map, Remandment, and Modifications).

After scheduling, go to DEHQ before submitting to the Land Development Counter. This must be completed before the appointment time, or you will be sent over at check-in and may have to reschedule. If it is a Code Compliance case, obtain the required stamps on the plan before submittal.

DEHQ signature on the Pre Screen Checklist (PDS-820) form and creation of a DEHQ record are required before submittal of Grading and Improvement Plan projects. If DEHQ review is required per the checklist, payment is required to DEHQ before the first review submittal. As of December 1st, 2025, DEHQ is located at 5500 Overland Ave. Suite 110, 1st Floor. DEHQ fees follow the fee schedule in effect for the fiscal year in which services are performed. Applicants may be re-invoiced at current rates if work occurs in a different fiscal year than when the original fees were assessed.

At any submittal, electronic files are accepted via a file transfer link. If you submit hard copies, you must also provide all documents electronically. This applies to new projects and resubmittals for Major, Minor, and Stockpile Grading Permits; Major, Minor, and Public Improvement Plans; Final Map, Parcel Map and Centerline Applications; and PDP SWQMP Referrals. Electronic files must include individual PDF files of all required documents, plans, and reports. ZIP files, encrypted or password-protected files are not allowed.

Source: https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/land-development-permits/

## Fire Safety Inspections for Businesses — Fees and Payment

The County Fire Marshal inspects businesses and some facilities for compliance with the Consolidated Fire Code in the San Diego County Fire Protection District and Deer Springs Fire Protection District. Facilities are usually inspected once a year. In some cases the inspector will schedule the inspection, and in others the inspector will come without notice. Make sure the inspector can get to any part of the building that needs inspection. If corrections are needed, you receive a report, and a follow-up inspection happens after you correct the issues.

For fiscal year 2026/2027 the rate for an inspection or reinspection is $164.00 per hour, covering inspection time and administrative work. Fees are pro-rated if it takes less than an hour. A third-party vendor emails the invoice to the contact on record, and sends a paper copy if there is no response.

Pay by check or money order payable to the County of San Diego. Mail to: County of San Diego Attn: San Diego County Fire 5580 Overland Avenue Suite 100 San Diego, CA 92123. Tenants are responsible for the part of a commercial building they occupy. Common areas are inspected separately and invoiced to the building contact. Questions: 858-974-5999, Fire@sdcounty.ca.gov. For invoice questions, include a copy or the inspection number and date.

Source: https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/service/fire-safety-inspections-for-businesses-and-other-facilities/

## Submitting Building and Discretionary Plans to the Fire Marshal

San Diego County Fire reviews plans in the San Diego County Fire Protection District and Deer Springs Fire Protection District. If your site is in another district, submit plans to the fire authority for your area.

- Residential or commercial building plans, and a residential minor grading permit that is part of a residential building permit application: submit to the County Planning & Development Services Building Division, which notifies Fire.
- Major and minor grading permits, and major, minor and public improvement plans: submit to the PDS Land Development Division. They will not route the plans to Fire. Contact the Land Development staff handling your application to arrange submission.
- Fire protection system plans (alternative fire suppression, emergency responder radio coverage, fire alarm, commercial fire sprinkler, residential NFPA 13D sprinkler, private underground fire service mains): submit to San Diego County Fire Protection District after the building plans are approved.
- Discretionary project plans: submit to the PDS Project Planning Division, which routes them to Fire.

To request form PDS-399F (project facility availability – fire), complete the Owner's Information and Section 1, then email the form and a Plot Plan to David Sibbet, Fire Services Coordinator (david.sibbet@sdcounty.ca.gov).

Source: https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/service/submit-building-and-discretionary-plans-to-fire-marshal/
