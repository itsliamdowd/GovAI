## Small Business Support Programs

The County's Business Engagement & Strategic Training (BEST) Initiative has three parts. STEP (Support, Training, and Education for Procurement) offers free workshops and one-on-one support for small businesses, non-profits and community-based organizations interested in doing business with the County. CORE (Compliance, Operations, and Readiness Education) is a pilot program offering free bookkeeping assistance and training on administration and labor compliance. BUILD (Bonding and Underwriting Insurance for Local Development) is a pilot program providing free resources and funding for bonding and insurance.

The Small-Local Preference Program gives small and local businesses an advantage when they compete for County contracts. The County does not certify businesses. A small business certified with another public agency or jurisdiction can still qualify through the Reciprocal Small-Local Business Certification Program.

Source: https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/small-business-support/

## Report Child Abuse or Neglect

Call 800-344-6000 to make a report. You can call 24 hours a day, every day. Report suspected child abuse or neglect at 858-560-2191. Within California, call toll free 800-344-6000. Call if you are concerned about a child's safety, a parent afraid you may harm your child, or a child or youth who believes you are being abused.

A social worker evaluates each report to determine if the child is at risk. The County could investigate within 24 hours, investigate within 10 days, or refer to a community agency without investigating.

Mandated Reporters must contact authorities if they suspect or are told about child abuse. Mandated Reporters only can submit non-urgent child abuse reports online and can self register.

Source: https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/service/report-child-abuse-or-neglect/

## How Child Abuse Investigations Work

The Child Abuse Hotline is (800) 344-6000. After a report, the County decides whether the issue is serious enough to investigate, using Structured Decision Making tools to determine whether to investigate, the safety of a child, and the risk of future harm.

A social worker meets with the child's family and people close to the family. If the child is safe in the home, the investigation is closed. It often takes about 30 days to complete an investigation. If the child is not safe, the County opens a voluntary or court-ordered case.

Voluntary services are offered when a family can keep their child safe without the court's involvement. The family must agree to work with the County for at least six months. A juvenile court case is opened if a family cannot keep a child safe without court oversight or a child falls under Welfare and Institutions Codes (WIC) 300a-j. A case may stay open at least six months. A child who cannot live at home may stay with a relative, family friend, or licensed resource (foster) home. The child may also stay for a short time at Polinsky Children's Center.

Source: https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/department/cfwb/how-we-investigate-child-abuse-and-handle-cases/

## Child Sex Trafficking — Get Help or Report

If you or someone you know needs help, call 800-344-6000. Caring, trained people can talk to you 24 hours a day. Youth who suspect human trafficking can also report it at 800-344-6000.

Warning signs a youth is being trafficked include running away from home, skipping school often, a sudden drop in grades, a new cellphone or multiple cellphones, a sudden change in behavior, attitude or clothes, and tattoos. The page offers parent, educator and youth brochures (PDF). Local services listed include San Diego Youth Services and North County Lifeline Project LIFE. The San Diego County District Attorney's Office CSEC information is at ProtectSanDiegoKids.org.

## Fire Safety Inspections for Businesses

The County Fire Marshal inspects businesses and some facilities for the Consolidated Fire Code in the San Diego County Fire Protection District and Deer Springs Fire Protection District. Inspected facilities include hotels, motels, apartment complexes, dormitories, timeshares, schools, institutional facilities, high rises, business and commercial facilities, commercial construction, and commercial fire protection systems. Facilities are usually inspected once a year. Sometimes the inspector will come without telling you first. You must make sure the inspector can get to any part of the building that needs inspection. If corrections are needed, the inspector sends a report and does a follow-up inspection after you correct the issues.

For fiscal year 2026/2027 the rate for an inspection or reinspection is $164.00 per hour, covering inspection time and any administrative work. If the work takes less than an hour, the fee is pro-rated. A third-party vendor emails the invoice to the contact on record, then sends a paper copy if there is no response.

Pay by check or money order payable to the County of San Diego. Mail payment to: County of San Diego Attn: San Diego County Fire 5580 Overland Avenue Suite 100 San Diego, CA 92123.

Tenants are responsible for the part of a commercial building they occupy. Common areas (lobbies, restrooms, mechanical rooms, parking lots, building exteriors) are inspected separately, and the invoice goes to the building contact.

Questions: 858-974-5999, Fire@sdcounty.ca.gov. For invoice questions, include a copy in an email, or provide the inspection number and date.

Source: https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/service/fire-safety-inspections-for-businesses-and-other-facilities/

## Cannabis Business License (Unincorporated San Diego County)

You must be a verified Socially Equitable Cannabis Program applicant to submit a new application. The application window opens on October 5, 2026. Only verified applicants can apply until October 2029. You must get a license from the California Department of Cannabis Control before the County can issue a local license.

Zoning requirements include:
- A location at least 600 feet from sensitive uses (schools, day cares, youth centers).
- Outdoor and mixed light cultivation 100 feet from all lot lines and 300 feet from legally established dwellings.
- No growing on land with slopes of 25% grade or more.
- Temporary Cannabis Events 600 feet from residential zones.

The process has three phases: Phase 1 verifies eligibility, Phase 2 is technical review and permitting, and Phase 3 is final inspection and local license issuance, which requires proof of a valid California Department of Cannabis Control license.

Social Equity Program applications are currently closed. The County expects to accept applications again in late 2026 or early 2027. Tier A is incarceration for a San Diego County cannabis-related arrest or conviction before November 8, 2016. Tier B is deportation, arrest, or conviction for cannabis-related crimes before November 8, 2016, and low income (80% or below of the Area Median Income for San Diego County, as determined by HUD). Required documents are two forms of valid ID (at least one a photo ID), documentation of your arrest, conviction, deportation, or incarceration, and, for Tier B, proof of meeting the low-income criteria.

Application help: Cannabis.Licensing@sdcounty.ca.gov, 858-694-2705. Social Equity Program help: Brandon Lewis, Social Equity Manager, Office of Equity and Racial Justice, brandon.lewis@sdcounty.ca.gov.

Source: https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/service/apply-for-a-cannabis-business-license/
