## 9th Street Enhancement Project — Overview and Status

The 9th Street Safety Improvement Project is a roadway safety project along 9th Street from Calla Avenue to the southern terminus of 9th Street at the Naval Outlying Landing Field (NOLF). It is just over one (1) mile long. Primary improvements include buffered green bike lanes, curb pop-outs, and enhanced crosswalks with Rectangular Rapid Flashing Beacons (RRFBs). This is typically done by reallocating underutilized vehicular lanes.

Construction was set to begin Monday July 22, 2024, and was expected to finish by Summer 2025. Work started at the south end of 9th Street and advanced northward to Calla Avenue. A June 11, 2026 update announces the successful completion of the project within budget. The project earned an Honor Award from the American Public Works Association in the Transportation Projects ($2 Million–$5 Million) category.

The project also repaved the road from Imperial Beach Boulevard to Palm Avenue, added new lane striping, built ADA pedestrian ramps at intersections, and installed new traffic signal poles at 9th Street and Palm Avenue.

## 9th Street Enhancement Project — Approvals, Funding, and Outreach

On November 2, 2022, City Council directed staff to conduct additional community outreach. On March 15, 2023, Council approved Resolutions No's 2023-16 and 2023-17, which authorized an amendment to the Circulation Element, approved a Coastal Development Permit, and provided a CEQA categorical exemption. On June 18, 2024, Council considered a resolution to award the construction contract to Tri-Group Construction and Development, Inc. The design maximizes parking, enhances crosswalks, improves sightlines, and adds decorative landscaping.

The project is funded by a State Active Transportation Program grant, Transnet funds and SB1 funds, for a total project cost of $4,786,500. Outreach included public meetings and workshops, an online survey, and social media.

## 9th Street — Lane Configuration and Intersection Changes

9th Street changed from four lanes to three: one lane in each direction and one center turn lane. The City says this reduces sudden lane changes, helps traffic move more smoothly, lowers the chance of crashes, makes it easier for emergency vehicles to get through, and creates space for bike lanes and safer crosswalks.

The 4-way stop was moved from Donax Avenue to Elm Street, and Donax Avenue is now a two-way stop. Traffic engineers found that the 4-way stop at Donax Avenue caused traffic backups that reached the traffic signal at Palm Avenue. Bollards at 9th & Palm protect the new intersection design and make bicyclists more visible to drivers. The bollards and intersection design will be reviewed again during the Palm Avenue corridor improvements project.

## 9th Street — Bike Lane and Street Markings

- Solid Green: shows where bikes travel. Cars should not drive in these lanes except to enter or exit a driveway or parking area.
- Dashed Green: used where cars and bikes may cross paths, such as near driveways.
- Dashed White Bike Line: warns drivers and cyclists that they are approaching an intersection where they may need to share space. Drivers must yield to bicyclists.
- Double Yellow Centerline: separates traffic traveling in opposite directions. Left turns into driveways are allowed.
- Two-Way left turn lane: the center lane is only for left turns or U-turns.

Drivers must always yield to bicyclists in bike lanes. The green bike lane paint and crosswalk markings (both paint and mosaic) are designed to meet current Caltrans safety standards and are not slippery.

## 10th Streetscape Project

The 10th Streetscape Enhancements project provides a missing accessible sidewalk connection between Donax Avenue and Palm Avenue. It includes curb extensions and enhanced pedestrian crossings. It also includes landscape, trees, pedestrian lights, enhanced decorative concrete public art, benches and seat walls, and shade trees to increase tree canopy and reduce the urban "heat island" effect. The improvements will reduce illegal parking and dumping along the project.

## 13th Street Corridor Improvement Plan

The City is developing an improvement plan for the 13th Street Commercial Corridor, which stretches from the northernmost point of the city to the Naval Outlying Landing Field (NOLF). The plan is supported by a $335,000 Housing Acceleration Program (HAP) Grant. It will explore strategies to encourage infill housing development, improve access to public transit, promote fair housing, and reduce vehicle miles traveled (VMT). It will identify policies and design concepts for future improvements such as pedestrian safety upgrades, multi-modal street designs, and potential redevelopment opportunities.

## Bayshore Bikeway Resiliency Project

Through an Ocean Protection Council Prop 68 grant, the City is developing plans to repurpose a 1.2-mile segment of the Bayshore Bikeway corridor into a multi-benefit coastal resilience corridor. The project will provide enhanced flood protection for the Bayshore Bikeway corridor and adjacent vulnerable communities, improve coastal access, and strengthen ecosystem resilience by adding transitional habitat areas along the bay's edge. Updates are posted on the Imperial Beach Bayshore Bikeway website.

## Street Sweeping Schedule and Parking Enforcement

The City's street sweeping schedule resumed on May 1, 2021. Sweeping is provided through the franchise waste hauler (EDCO) and covers 130 curb miles per month:

- Weekly: commercial areas that include open striped and raised curb medians (Palm Avenue, Highway 75, 13th Street, Imperial Beach Boulevard, Seacoast Drive, and Ocean Lane).
- Twice per month: beachfront areas, with enforcement of posted parking restrictions along the sweeping route.
- Monthly: non-beachfront residential areas, paved alleys, and parking facilities.

A Street Sweeping Schedule (PDF) is available on the page. The County's Sheriff's Department is responsible for enforcing the street sweeper public parking regulations.

## Street Maintenance Division

The Street Maintenance Division repairs and maintains roads, sidewalks, alleys, and easements. It assists the Sewer Division with storm drainage facilities, assists with street and park tree maintenance, and handles streetscape and irrigation, signs, and streetlights. It is also responsible for traffic signal maintenance.
