## Housing Choice Voucher Rental Assistance (Formerly Section 8)

This HUD funded program subsidizes the rents of qualified very low-income tenants to affordable levels. In general, a household's income may not exceed 50% of the area median income (AMI) adjusted for household size. A family issued a voucher is responsible for finding a suitable housing unit of its choice where the owner agrees to rent under the program.

For Imperial Beach residents, the program is administered by the County of San Diego Housing Authority. You may apply in person or online. To learn about portability to or from Imperial Beach, you must contact the County. Contact: Housing Authority of the County of San Diego, 3989 Ruffin Rd, San Diego CA 92123. TOLL FREE (877) 478-5478 | LOCAL (858) 694-4890.

## Mandatory Termination of Tenancy Notice Filing (Landlords)

Under Chapter 9.90.060(A)(1) and Chapter 9.90.060(B)(1)(b) of the Imperial Beach Municipal Code ("Just Cause for Termination of a Residential Tenancy Ordinance"), the owner of a "Residential Rental Unit" who serves a termination of tenancy notice is REQUIRED to submit each termination of tenancy, along with the information in the City's online form or a substantial equivalent, within 3 business days of the notice being served on the tenant. The exception is owners exempted under Imperial Beach Municipal Code 9.90.040. The City uses the information to assess available resources and to track terminations of tenancy.

The form asks for the complex name and address, owner name, phone and email, number of units, number of vacant units, number of tenants served a termination notice, unit number(s), and contracted rent at time of notice (optional). Attach the Termination of Tenancy and all relevant documents for each impacted unit. Maximum size allowed is 10MB, maximum number of files is 10, and allowed types are .gif, .jpg, .jpeg, .png, .doc, .docx, .xls, .xlsx, .rtf, .pdf, .txt.

## Eviction Information & Eviction Assistance

San Diego County eviction prevention provides emergency assistance to low-income tenants and people at risk of becoming homeless. Support is available for households with children, single mothers, seniors, and residents without children if one of the occupants is temporarily or permanently disabled. Legal help is available from Legal Aid Society of San Diego at 877-534-2524, and CSA San Diego at 619-444-5700 provides advocacy against housing discrimination.

The money mostly comes from Emergency Solution Grants (ESG), administered nationally by HUD. Applications are taken locally by non-profits listed through the San Diego County website. Each agency has its own criteria, which may include the following:
- The tenant must be a resident of San Diego County.
- There must be an eviction, pay or quit notice from the landlord, or a foreclosure notice from a mortgage holder.
- Any money for rental arrears must prevent the eviction from occurring.
- The family must prove the ability to pay future rent and bills on their own.
- No other resources, whether from a local charity or Department of Human Services, can be available.
- Income levels based on certain poverty levels must be met.

Funding can run out at any time, but referral services will still try to direct the interested party to another local resource for security deposits or back rent. ESG grants can help bring utility or water arrears current if the eviction notice is due to unpaid utility or water bills. Rapid-Rehousing is available to someone currently homeless and places the family as quickly as possible into a new home with ongoing support services. ESG grants pay for credit repair classes, lease application fees and security deposits, and storage fees can be paid for a very short period of time. Case management is also coordinated.

Some County agencies, such as the Interfaith Shelter Network, may provide motel vouchers as a last resort. These are generally restricted to when all local overnight shelters are full or the waiting list for transitional housing units is backed up. Free motel vouchers may also be available for currently homeless individuals with a serious medical issue that would be made worse without a place to stay, including some people being discharged from a local hospital.

## Fair Housing and Discrimination Complaints

Fair Housing is protected by the federal Fair Housing Act on the basis of race, color, national origin, religion, sex, disability (physical/mental) or familial status (presence of children). The California Fair Employment and Housing Act of 1959 also protects against discrimination based on ancestry, age, sexual orientation, gender identity, marital status, familial status (presence of children) and source of income.

The City has contracted with CSA to serve as its Fair Housing Program Administrator. CSA sponsors public fair housing educational activities, outreach and referral activities, and promotes the San Diego Regional Analysis of Impediments to Fair Housing Choice.

If you believe you were denied housing or the opportunity to apply for housing in Imperial Beach, or were a victim of illegal discrimination or harassment because of a characteristic protected by federal or state law, email CSA or call 800-954-0441. You may also file a complaint online directly with the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD).

Tips for making a complaint:
- Contact CSA within one year of the incident.
- Document as much information as possible.
- Speak to a housing counselor at CSA and provide the facts about the incident.
- Your incident may be referred to a legal representative for further investigation.
- An attempt will be made to assist in resolving the issue.
- If the issue remains unresolved and there is evidence that a violation has occurred, the complaint may be tried in court.

Source: https://www.imperialbeachca.gov/253/Fair-Housing

## Homelessness — Community Outreach Worker and Coordinated Entry

The City has a full time Community Outreach Worker who works one-on-one with clients toward a housing solution. She is available by appointment, or walk-in at city hall depending on her schedule and availability; it is always best to set an appointment. Reach her at 619-679-0350 or by email. The City's Homeless Outreach Team spends one morning a week out in the neighborhood providing street outreach.

The Coordinated Entry System (CES) ensures all people experiencing homelessness in San Diego County have fair and equal access and are quickly identified, assessed, and prioritized for housing resources. The first step is to have an assessment called the VI-SPDAT completed. The Community Outreach Worker can assist with the CES process; to schedule an appointment call 619-679-0350 or email.

City contacts: Tony Rolfe, Housing & Neighborhood Services Manager; Viri Ramirez, Community Outreach Worker.

Source: https://www.imperialbeachca.gov/501/At-Risk-Of-Or-Experiencing-Homelessness
