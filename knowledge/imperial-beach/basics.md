## City Council Meetings

Meetings are at 6 pm on the 1st and 3rd Wednesdays of every month in Council Chambers, 825 Imperial Beach Boulevard, Imperial Beach, CA 91932. The agenda for each meeting is available for public inspection at City Hall prior to the meeting. Persons wishing to address the Council on a subject not on the agenda may complete and submit to the City Clerk a Request to Speak form and speak under Public Comment.

The City Council consists of a Mayor and four Councilmembers, each serving four-year terms. Council and Mayor elections are non-partisan and are held in November of even-numbered years. The City Council acts as the Planning Commission, Imperial Beach Redevelopment Agency Successor Agency, Financing Authority, Housing Authority and Personnel Board.

The Mayor and City Councilmembers file statements of economic interests with the City Clerk's office. Copies may be obtained from the Fair Political Practices Commission (FPPC), 1102 Q Street Suite 3000, Sacramento, CA 95811, or from the City Clerk, 825 Imperial Beach Boulevard, Imperial Beach, CA 91932.

## City Council Redistricting

Following the 2020 census results, which showed minimal changes in the City's population, the voting district map adopted in 2018 remains in full compliance with all applicable law. Ordinance No. 2022-1208, adopting boundaries for City Council Districts following the 2020 Decennial Federal Census and pursuant to the Fair Maps Act, was introduced at the City Council meeting of March 2, 2022 and adopted at the City Council meeting of March 16, 2022.

This will be the City Council district map for the next 10 years, until the next Census. There is no change to election sequencing.

## District-Based Council Elections (2018 Ordinance)

Beginning in 2020, Imperial Beach City Council elections transitioned from at-large voting to district-based voting. The City is divided into four districts, and only the voters residing in a district decide who represents that district. The Mayor continues to be elected by all voters in the city. Districts 2 and 4 were up for election on November 3, 2020, and Districts 1 and 3 in 2022.

On December 5, 2018, the City Council adopted Ordinance Number 2018-1178, which establishes a by-district election process in four council districts while continuing with an elective office of Mayor. On November 7, 2018, the Council selected the "Rose B" map.

Source: https://www.imperialbeachca.gov/634/2018-City-Council-Election-Districts

## About Imperial Beach

Imperial Beach has 3.5 miles of uncrowded, white sand beaches and big waves, with a wooden pier and views of both downtown San Diego and the Coronado Islands. Two beachfront parks, Dunes Park and Pier Portwood Plaza, offer grassy expanses, playgrounds, picnic tables, barbecues and more.

Portwood Pier Plaza has grassy expanses, picnic areas, a tot-lot, an entertainment stage, and access to the pier and beach. The plaza also has surfboard benches with plaques on the city's big-wave surfing history from 1937 to the 1950s, and Surfhenge, a public art monument to surfing.

Source: https://www.imperialbeachca.gov/462/About-Imperial-Beach

## Fire-Rescue Department

Imperial Beach Fire-Rescue is an all-hazard Department established in 1946, with one fire station built in 1979. It provides fire suppression, emergency medical services, prevention, education, inspection, community service, and weed abatement inspections. The city contracts with the City of Chula Vista for ambulance services for the transport of patients to hospitals. The department responds to approximately 3,000 calls for emergency service each year.

Emails and phone calls will only be replied to during regular business hours. The Fire Chief is John French.

## Marine Safety (Lifeguards)

Lifeguards patrol the shoreline from 7 am to dusk, 365 days a year. Daily staffing varies from four lifeguards in the winter months to as many as twenty in the spring and summer months. Lifeguards respond on a four-mile stretch of coast, from the south end of Silver Strand State Beach to the U.S.-Mexico border. The primary jurisdiction area is from Carnation Avenue to the south end of Seacoast Drive.

The pier is located off of Evergreen Avenue and is available for public walking and fishing. Fishing from the pier does not require a license, but catch regulations are enforced.

Marine Safety is at the Dempsey Holder Safety Center, 950 Ocean Lane, Imperial Beach, CA 91932. Phone: 619-423-8328. Fax: 619-628-1421. Emergency Phone: 911.
