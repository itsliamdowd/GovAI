## Storm Water Code Enforcement

The City runs a stormwater code enforcement program to detect and eliminate illicit discharges and illegal connections to the stormwater conveyance system. Illegal connections include discharge of grey water to the City right-of-way and non-permitted connections of drainage pipes or sump pumps to the stormwater conveyance system. Illegal discharges include over-irrigation runoff, trash or debris in the City right-of-way, wash water leaving the property from any residence or business, and discharge of any liquid or material into the stormwater conveyance system.

To report a suspected stormwater violation, call the Storm Water direct line at 619-424-4095 or use the online report form. Non-emergency requests can also be submitted through the MyImperialBeach App (iPhone and Android) or the Report an Issue page on the City website. A Pet Waste Regulations FAQs (PDF) is listed under Regulations.

## Imperial Beach Fire-Rescue — Department Information, Services and Contact

Imperial Beach Fire-Rescue is an all-hazard Department responsible for the protection of life and property of the citizens of Imperial Beach and surrounding communities. It was established in 1946 and is an Insurance Services Office (ISO) Class 4 department with one fire station, built in 1979. The department provides fire suppression, emergency medical services, prevention, education, inspection, community service, and weed abatement inspections. It responds to approximately 3,000 calls for emergency service each year. The city contracts with the City of Chula Vista for ambulance services for the transport of patients to hospitals.

Fire-Rescue is at 865 Imperial Beach Boulevard, Imperial Beach, CA 91932. Phone: 619-423-8223. Emergency Phone: 911. Hours: Monday through Friday (with alternate Friday closures) 7:30 am to 5:30 pm. Emails and phone calls will only be replied to during regular business hours. The Fire Chief is John French.

## Pool Safety and Drowning Prevention

Essential water safety tips from Fire-Rescue:
- **Lock it up:** Keep pools gated and locked. Home pools should be enclosed with a locked fence that is at least 4 feet high. Use multiple layers of protection such as safety covers, gate alarms, door alarms, or motion-detection devices.
- **Safety first:** Wear a U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when out on boats. Children should also wear one when swimming in any body of water. "Water wings" or "floaties," inflatable rings, and other pool toys are not safety devices. Remove toys when a pool is not in use.
- **Keep a watchful eye:** Supervise children in the water at all times, and designate a "Water Watcher."
- **Swimming lessons:** Teach your child to swim or enroll them with qualified instructors.
- **No alcohol:** Don't drink if you are boating, swimming or supervising children in the water.
- **Learn CPR.**

In an emergency: get the child out of the water immediately, call 9-1-1, and begin CPR if you are trained. If you do not know CPR, follow the operator's instructions until help arrives.

## Commercial Fire Code — Common Violations

Imperial Beach enforces the currently adopted California Fire Code. Commonly identified violations include:
- **Address identification:** 8" address numbers are required for all Imperial Beach businesses, in a contrasting color and visible from the street.
- **Exits:** Emergency exits must be free of obstructions and available for use any time the building is occupied. Blocking an emergency exit is a misdemeanor. Rated doors cannot be propped open or altered without approval from the door manufacturer and Fire Department. Lighted exit signs and emergency egress lighting must be tested monthly. Slide bolts or other secondary locks cannot be installed on any exit door without Fire Department review and approval.
- **Fire walls and rated construction:** These cannot be altered (holes, windows, doors, etc.) without review and approval from the Fire Department. Do not block or hold open fire doors.
- **Storage:** Keep storage less than 24 inches from the ceiling in areas without fire sprinklers and less than 18 inches from the ceiling in areas with fire sprinklers.
- **Electrical:** Extension cords are never allowed as a permanent source of power. A rated power strip must be powered directly by an outlet and can never be interconnected with another rated power strip. Broken or missing outlet, switch and junction box covers must be replaced. Never block any electrical panel.

Source: https://www.imperialbeachca.gov/277/Fire-Safety-For-Business-Owners

## Commercial Fire Extinguishers, Sprinklers and Alarms

- **Portable fire extinguishers** are required in all businesses and must be inspected annually by a California State Fire Marshal licensed contractor. Fire-Rescue can provide, on request, a list of service companies with a valid City of Imperial Beach business license. Imperial Beach Fire-Rescue does not inspect or recharge portable fire extinguishers. Extinguishers are for incipient stage fires: ensure you have a way out and call 911 before attempting to use one.
- **Automatic fire sprinkler systems** must be inspected quarterly by a CSLB C-16 licensed contractor. Do not store items within 18 inches of the bottom of any sprinkler head, and never attach anything to or hang anything from one.
- **Automatic fire alarm systems** require annual maintenance by a CSLB C-10 license holder specifically trained in fire alarm systems. A traditional C-10 electrician cannot perform this work.

For free safety training on these topics, contact the Fire Prevention Division at 619-628-1392. Training is currently only available during city business hours.

Source: https://www.imperialbeachca.gov/277/Fire-Safety-For-Business-Owners

## KNOX Rapid Access Box

A business with an automatic fire sprinkler system, an automatic fire alarm system, or an automatic extinguishing system (commercial kitchen fire suppression system, clean gas suppression system) is required to obtain and maintain a KNOX rapid access box. When locks or door access codes are changed, provide the new keys/codes to Imperial Beach Fire-Rescue and staff will lock them into the KNOX box.

Each box is keyed to the jurisdiction, and an Imperial Beach Fire Marshal signed form is required to complete the order with the KNOX Company. Contact the Fire Prevention Division at the Fire Department for a signed order form. Address: Imperial Beach Fire-Rescue Department Fire Prevention Division, 865 Imperial Beach Boulevard, Imperial Beach, CA 91932.

## Developers — Fire Code and Life Safety System Plan Review

Imperial Beach uses the currently adopted edition of the California Code of Regulations, Title 24, and may adopt specific codes in a more restrictive manner than the state. For specific code modifications, refer to the Imperial Beach Municipal Code.

Submittals for life safety systems such as fire sprinklers, fire alarms, and engineered suppression systems go directly to the Fire Prevention Division. Plans are reviewed in-house, typically within 7 to 10 business days. The applicant shall submit (3) copies and one digital copy for state-required record retention. Review fees are listed on the Fire Department Development Review Application (PDF).

## Developers — Fire Construction Inspections

Construction inspections should not be scheduled less than 48 hours in advance. Schedule by emailing Tom Santos. Available times:
- Monday–Thursday: AM 10 and 11; PM 2, 3, and 4
- Friday: AM 10 and 11; PM 2 and 3

Fire Department administrative offices are closed on alternate Fridays (check the City's Homepage for dates). Regular inspections are not available during city-observed holidays or closed Fridays, and additional fees apply to after-hours inspections.

Intermediate and pre-concealment inspections include rough wiring, piping, hydrostatic testing, underground, backflush, thrust blocking, fire wrapping, etc. Project final inspections (for occupancy) are performed once all life safety system finals are completed. The structure should be "move-in ready" with no outstanding punch list items.

After-hours inspections may be possible, subject to staff availability and approval of the Fire Chief. Inspection fees are paid directly to the Imperial Beach Finance Department at the rate in the Imperial Beach Master Fee Schedule.

## Home Smoke Alarms and Carbon Monoxide Alarms

Smoke alarms are required in all homes, in and immediately outside every bedroom and on every level or story. Generally, they are not allowed within 3 feet of any shower room door, heating or air conditioning supply vent, or ceiling fan blade tip, or within 20 feet of a cooking appliance. Test at least monthly, and check with the manufacturer, as testing may be required more often.

Carbon monoxide alarms have been required in California since 2011 when gas appliances, an attached garage, or a fireplace are provided. Test at least monthly, and check with the manufacturer.

Source: https://www.imperialbeachca.gov/279/Fire-Safety-For-Residents

## Home Fire Extinguishers, Sprinklers and Exit Drills

Keep at least one portable fire extinguisher in your home. Buy a multipurpose model suitable for Class A (wood and paper), Class B (liquid fuels) and Class C (energized electrical equipment) hazards. Call 911 before attempting to use one. Imperial Beach Fire-Rescue does not service or recharge portable fire extinguishers.

Automatic fire sprinklers have been required in all new homes since 2011. A residential system should typically be tested annually. Check with Imperial Beach Fire-Rescue, your system installer, or insurance provider.

For exit drills, draw a plan of your home showing two exits from each room, designate a safe outside meeting place, and practice periodically. The drill can be changed to earthquake (duck, cover, and hold) or tsunami.

Source: https://www.imperialbeachca.gov/279/Fire-Safety-For-Residents

## Fireworks

All consumer fireworks are illegal in the City of Imperial Beach and throughout San Diego County. Anyone found possessing or discharging fireworks faces significant fines and or criminal prosecution. Consumer fireworks include sparklers, firecrackers, bottle rockets, roman candles, and aerial shells. A professional fireworks display can be approved by the Fire Department with permitted special events. It requires permitting, insurance, and the correct licenses issued by the Office of the State Fire Marshal. All professional firework displays require an additional Fire Department permit. Email the Fire Prevention Division for more information.

Source: https://www.imperialbeachca.gov/280/Fire-Safety-For-Special-Events

## Reporting Fire and Hazard Complaints

An immediate life safety hazard requires calling 911, and emergency responders will be dispatched. For a suspected hazardous condition, contact Imperial Beach Fire-Rescue in person and complete a complaint form, or complete the form online. Anonymous complaints are not accepted by the city.

Staff process the form, contact the property owner by mail, and conduct a site assessment to determine whether the condition is confirmed or unfounded. If confirmed, the owner has reasonable time to remedy it. If the condition turns out to violate the city municipal code rather than the state fire code, the Imperial Beach Code Compliance Division processes it using a similar process.

Source: https://www.imperialbeachca.gov/279/Fire-Safety-For-Residents
