## Thanksgiving Cooking Fire Safety

Thanksgiving is the peak day for home cooking fires, and the number of home fires doubles on Thanksgiving. If you roast your turkey, set a timer. If you fry your turkey, use a fryer with thermostat controls, thaw the turkey completely (ice on the bird causes the oil to splatter), and don't overfill the pot with oil. Keep children and pets at least three feet away from the fryer, and always use the fryer outdoors.

Stay in the kitchen when you are frying, boiling or broiling, so you can catch spills or hazardous conditions before they become a fire. Keep the area around the stove clear of anything that can burn, such as packaging, paper towels and dish cloths. Clean up spills as they happen. Keep a large pan lid or baking sheet handy to smother a pan fire, and turn pot handles toward the back of the stove.

## Emergency Survival Program (ESP) — Creating a Disaster Plan

The City of Mission Viejo offers detailed emergency preparedness information every month through the Emergency Survival Program (ESP). The guide features monthly topics, historical information and what you can do now to prepare. February's focus was creating a disaster plan.

Recommended steps include:
- Practice "drop, cover and hold on" for earthquakes.
- Identify safe spots in every room, such as under sturdy desks and tables.
- Keep shoes and a working flashlight next to each bed.
- Teach everyone in your household to use emergency whistles and/or to knock 3 times repeatedly if trapped, since rescuers searching collapsed buildings will be listening for sounds.
- Identify the needs of household members and neighbors with special requirements, such as wheelchair or walking aid use, special diets or medication.
- Take a Red Cross first aid and CPR course, and learn who in your neighborhood is trained in first aid and CPR.

More information is on the City's emergency preparedness page.

## Emergency Survival Program (ESP) — Public Health Emergencies

Through the Emergency Survival Program (ESP), June's focus was public health emergencies. These may be related to outbreaks of infectious illnesses (such as pandemic flu and West Nile virus), food and waterborne illnesses like Salmonella and E. coli, other threats to the public's health and safety, and manmade attacks. The City highlights influenza as a special concern because it easily spreads and is constantly changing. The 2009 H1N1 flu virus was declared a pandemic by the World Health Organization.

The City says the simple steps recommended to prepare for and prevent illness from pandemic flu will also help prepare you for other emergencies. Preparedness tips and other essential information are available on the City's emergency preparedness page.

## Emergency Survival Program (ESP) — Wildfire Preparedness

Through the Emergency Survival Program (ESP), November's focus was wildfires. The City advises creating a defensible space around your home: an area free of flammable plants and objects, wide enough to prevent the flames and heat from a wildfire from reaching your home. It also advises preparing a Wildfire Action Plan with all household members, rehearsing it regularly, and keeping it in a safe, accessible place.

The partial checklist includes:
- Create and rehearse a Family Disaster Plan with meeting locations and communication plans.
- Keep fire extinguishers on hand and train your family to use them.
- Make sure your family knows where the gas, electric and water main shut-off controls are and how to use them.
- Plan several different escape routes.
- Assemble an emergency supply kit and keep an extra one in your car.
- Maintain emergency contact numbers.
- Appoint an out-of-state friend or relative as a point of contact.
- Keep a portable radio or scanner available.

The City advises leaving early and not waiting until the fire reaches your neighborhood. Leaving early gives your family the best chance of survival and keeps roads clear so firefighters can move more freely. More tips are in the wildfire guide and on the City's emergency preparedness page.
