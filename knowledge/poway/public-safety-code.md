## Poway Fire Department — Overview

The Poway Fire Department protects a city of just under 40 square miles, including residential communities, retail centers, an industrial park, over 4,300 acres of preserved open space and 78 miles of trails. It holds a Class 1/1X rating from the Insurance Services Office (ISO) Public Protection Classification program. Class 1 is the highest available rating, and the page says it helps lower the cost of homeowners insurance for structure fires.

Services include structural and wildland fire suppression, emergency medical services, emergency medical transport, technical rescue, fire prevention services, public education, and emergency preparedness.

## Fire Operations and Staffing

The Poway Fire Department provides fire protection and emergency medical services 24/7 every day of the year. It has 51 first responders divided into three rotational shifts. Each 24-hour shift consists of 17 personnel. The Deputy Fire Chief oversees day-to-day functions and the three Fire Battalion Chiefs. During a significant emergency response, a Fire Battalion Chief serves as Incident Commander.

The department has a five-story Fire Training Tower in the Poway Business Park. Practical training for at-home emergencies is available to the community through the Poway Fire Department Public Education Program.

## Emergency Medical Services, EMS Records and Ambulance Billing

The Poway Fire Department has provided Advanced Life Support Emergency Medical Services (EMS) since 1976. All firefighters are licensed paramedics. Firefighters provide pre-hospital care and transport for patients with illnesses or injuries believed to be an emergency by the patient.

Individuals who have received care from the Poway Fire Department may request an EMS record from Fire Administration. Ambulance billing is provided by Wittman Enterprises, and payments are not made directly to the City of Poway.

## Emergency Supply Kits

The City recommends three types of kits:
- **Three-minute Under Bed Bag:** one for each family member, for evacuating in three minutes or less. Contents include a change of clothes (long pants and a hat, made of cotton or other natural material), gloves, sturdy shoes, safety glasses or goggles, a face mask or bandana, a flashlight, a whistle, and a thumbdrive with copies of important documents.
- **Evacuation Go Bag:** supports you outside your home for the first 72 hours, using the 6 P's: People & Pets, Papers & Phone Numbers, Prescriptions/Vitamins/Eyeglasses, Pictures & Irreplaceable Items, Personal Computer, and Plastic & Cash. Keep your gas tank at least half full, especially on red flag days.
- **Shelter In Place Stay Box:** includes plastic sheeting, duct tape, dust masks, a manual can opener, and food and water for three days. Include one gallon of water per person per day.

## Evacuation Plans, Orders and Warnings

Have a home evacuation plan with at least two ways out of your home and a designated meeting location outside. For the community, know two ways out of your neighborhood and identify a meeting location outside your neighborhood and an emergency contact outside of the County.

The Poway Sheriff Department issues evacuation orders. An evacuation order requires immediate movement out of the area due to extreme danger. An evacuation warning requires immediate preparation to move because danger is approaching. Evacuation orders are issued through a Wireless Emergency Alert (WEA) sent to every cell phone in range of each affected cell tower. Further information is available through the SD Emergency App and the Genasys Protect App. The Sheriff Department may also use the Hi-Lo Siren.

## Home Fire Safety — Smoke Alarms, Carbon Monoxide Alarms and Extinguishers

- Install smoke alarms in every bedroom, outside sleeping areas, and on every level of the home.
- Test smoke alarms once a month, change batteries twice a year, and replace alarms when they are ten years old.
- Install carbon monoxide alarms in a central location outside sleeping areas, with at least one on each level. Also install them in or near the garage and 15 feet from fuel-burning appliances and fireplaces.
- Keep a fire extinguisher on every level of the home and in the kitchen and garage.
- Most residential extinguishers are multi-class A, B, and C.
- Use the P.A.S.S. method from six to eight feet away: PULL the pin, AIM at the base of the fire, SQUEEZE the trigger slowly and evenly, SWEEP side to side.
- If a fire does not go out or becomes too large, evacuate and call 9-1-1 from outside the home.

## Home Fire Safety — Cooking, Heating, Electrical and Dryer Tips

- **Cooking:** Stay in the kitchen while frying, boiling, grilling, or broiling. For a small grease fire, slide a lid over the pan and turn off the burner.
- **Heating:** Keep anything that can burn at least three feet away from heating equipment. Plug portable heaters directly into the wall outlet, never an extension cord. Never use your oven to heat your home. Have heating equipment and chimneys cleaned and inspected every year.
- **Electrical:** Do not use damaged or frayed cords. Do not overload outlets, and use only one heat-producing appliance per outlet at a time.
- **Clothes dryer:** Clean the lint filter before or after each load. Clean lint out of the vent pipe annually.

## Wildfire Preparedness

The state has designated more than 75% of the City's geography as a Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone. Risk grows in summer and fall when Santa Ana winds are typically present. To reduce the chance of home ignition, reduce flammable items within the first five feet of your home.

Register for San Diego County's Reverse-911 system to receive emergency alerts, including evacuation notifications, at AlertSD.org/Register. The Fire Department offers a Wildfire Safety and Evacuation Awareness Workshop each year in May and August. When dangerous fire weather is anticipated, the National Weather Service issues a red flag warning.

Source: https://poway.org/394/Wildfire-Preparedness

## Fire Hazard Severity Zones — Map and Classification

The Fire Hazard Severity Zone map, provided by the California Office of the State Fire Marshal, designates over 90% of the City's geography as a Fire Hazard Severity Zone. The state identifies three levels: Very High, High, and Moderate. The City has identified additional Wildland Urban Interface Areas. You can search your address using the interactive map on the page.

If a parcel is partially in a fire hazard severity zone, the entire parcel falls under the most restrictive zone identified. The City may be more restrictive than the state but cannot be less restrictive. The City will not change the map produced by CAL FIRE.

Source: https://poway.org/1162/Fire-Hazard-Severity-Zones

## Fire Hazard Severity Zones — Requirements for Property Owners

**Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone:**
- New construction must meet ignition resistant construction requirements (California Wildland-Urban Interface Building Code, CCR Title 24, and PMC 15.24.100) and the State Minimum Fire Safe Regulations (CCR Title 14).
- Real estate transactions must include a hazard disclosure and inspection (CIV 1102.6F).
- Existing homes must meet defensible space requirements for Zone 1 and Zone 2 (GOV 51182 and CCR Title 14 Section 1299.03). Zone 0 requirements will be effective January 2030.

**High Fire Hazard Severity Zone:**
- Effective January 2026, new construction must meet Chapter 7A of the California Building Code (CCR Title 24) and PMC 15.24.100.
- Real estate transactions must include a hazard disclosure and inspection (CIV 1102.6F).

There are currently no regulations for property owners in the Moderate Fire Hazard Severity Zone.

## Fire Permits and Online Submittals

The Fire Department requires and issues permits for a variety of business activities, construction work, operations, practices or functions. Each permit requires payment of the appropriate fee. Work requiring a permit may not be started until the plans have been approved and the permit is obtained.

Use City of Poway Online Services to submit permit applications and plans. Effective June 1, 2022, only electronic submittals are accepted. Some permits, such as places of public assembly and flammable liquids, must be renewed annually. Others are one-time, such as fire sprinkler alterations, tents and canopies, and liquefied petroleum gas tank installation.

Construction permits (for example, moving walls, rack storage, or a ventilation system) begin with the Development Services Department - Building Division.

## Defensible Space Inspection for Real Estate Transactions

Under California Civil Code 1102.19(a), as of July 1, 2021, selling property in a high or very high fire hazard severity zone requires documentation of a Defensible Space Inspection. Only properties in the very high fire hazard severity zone need to comply with Section 51182 of the CA Government Code.

Apply through the City of Poway Online Services Portal. First-time users must register for an account. Click apply and enter defensible space in the search bar. After completing the application, call (858) 668-4470, option 1 to schedule an inspection. For additional assistance, contact (858) 668-4470.

## Wildfire Defensible Space Program

The program is conducted citywide where structures are within 100 feet of highly flammable chaparral. It includes city, state, special district, utility, and privately-owned land. Streambeds, banks, and vernal pools are excluded.
