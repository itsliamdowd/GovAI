## City Council — Members and Structure

The City Council has five members elected to serve 4-year overlapping terms. The Mayor is elected at large by the citizens of Poway. Councilmembers are elected by district. Each year the Council reorganizes and selects a Councilmember to serve as Deputy Mayor. The Council is the city's policy-making body and takes action on all City ordinances, resolutions, contracts, and matters requiring policy decision. It appoints the City Manager and City Attorney and establishes citizen advisory committees.

Current City Council: Steve Vaus, Mayor, at large, term ends Nov-26. Jenny Maeda, Deputy Mayor, District 4, term ends Nov-28. Christopher Pikus, Councilmember, District 1, term ends Nov-26. Anita Edmondson, Councilmember, District 2, term ends Nov-28. Peter De Hoff, Councilmember, District 3, term ends Nov-26.

Emails sent to the Council are not personal communications. They are official public records and may be copied for City Council and staff and made available to the press and public.

## City Council — District Elections and History

On October 3, 2017, the Poway City Council adopted an ordinance establishing by-district elections for council members. Districts 1 and 3 were first elected in the November 2018 General Municipal Election, and Districts 2 and 4 in the November 2020 General Municipal Election. Each district is elected every four years thereafter. The Mayor continues to be elected at large. On February 1, 2022, the City Council unanimously chose to maintain the previously adopted District Boundary Map. The next district review will follow the 2030 U.S. Census.

The City of Poway was incorporated on December 1, 1980. Before 1990, the Mayor was chosen on a rotating basis among the five Councilmembers for a one-year term. In 1990, registered voters approved Proposition K to elect four City Councilmembers and a directly elected Mayor.

## Elections — Running for Office

Candidates must be a U.S. Citizen, at least eighteen (18) years of age on or before Election day, and a resident and registered voter in the City of Poway. A candidate for a City Council District seat must reside within that district.

To run for office, you must pull and file nomination papers during the nomination period of July 13, 2026 through August 7, 2026 by 5 p.m., by appointment only. To schedule an appointment, contact the City Clerk at cityclerk@poway.org or 858-668-4530.

There are no City of Poway filing fees. Candidates who choose to have their "Candidate Statement of Qualifications" printed in the sample ballot issued by the County Registrar of Voters must bear the actual cost of printing and distribution. They pay a deposit when the nomination paper and forms are filed with the Poway City Clerk, and final payment is due upon receipt from the Registrar of Voters (ROV) following the election.

## Low Water Pressure — Troubleshooting

To test static water pressure, thread a hose bib water pressure gauge onto any hose bib with no water running elsewhere in the home, turn on the bib, and write down the reading. A typical pressure regulator is set between 50 to 75 PSI. If your water pressure is above 40 PSI, your "low water pressure" problem is not a water pressure problem but a problem with the flow of water through your plumbing system.

Causes listed include pump problems, clogged pipes, water softener or pressure regulator malfunctions, partially closed valves, aerator clogs or rust, mineral buildup, and a worn out water heater. Pressure regulators typically have a lifespan of 10-plus years. The page advises calling a plumber for pump repairs and for regulator repairs if you are uncomfortable doing them yourself.

## Veterans Park

Veterans Park was dedicated on Veteran's Day (Nov. 11) 2010. It features a raised memorial plaza with monuments to each branch of service, including the Coast Guard, supported by the Wall of Honor of engraved tiles honoring veterans and those currently serving. Tiles are dedicated twice a year: at the Veterans Day ceremony and on the Fourth of July. The park's cannon is fired on ceremonial occasions, including Veteran's Day and Fourth of July.
