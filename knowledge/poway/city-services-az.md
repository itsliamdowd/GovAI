## Mandatory Commercial, Multi-Family and Organics Recycling (AB341 / AB1826)

California State law AB341 requires that commercial enterprises which generate four cubic yards or more of solid waste weekly participate in recycling programs. This also includes multi-family housing complexes of five units or more, regardless of the amount of solid waste generated each week. EDCO Waste and Recycling Services is the City's permitted waste and recycling contractor.

AB1826 requires businesses and public entities that generate organic waste (food and/or green waste) to recycle organic waste, or be issued a City approved waiver, if applicable. Apartment and condominium complexes of 5 units or more are required to recycle organic waste (green waste/landscape materials and nonhazardous wood waste); they are not required to have a food waste diversion program. All generators of landscape waste and clean wood waste meeting the 8 CY/week threshold are required to have a recycling program. Businesses may self-haul, mulch/compost on site (with appropriate state permitting), or contact EDCO for collection services.

For food waste, the City and EDCO use a tiered priority approach: 1. Prevention of Food Waste; 2. Donation of Edible Food; 3. Collection of non-edible food. EDCO is responsible for providing collection services for non-edible food, and waste assessments are available through EDCO upon request.

Uncontaminated used oil, oil filters, antifreeze, and automotive batteries may also be taken to a local certified collection center.

## Wildfire Defensible Space Program

Defensible space refers to managing the vegetation within 100-feet of a structure. It requires cutting and trimming of highly flammable chaparral, removal of dead or dying trees and shrubs, horizontal and vertical spacing between groups of plantings, and limiting the amount of vegetation immediately adjacent to the structure. It does not require clearing, grubbing, grading, or the exposure of bare soil. For more information, call the Defensible Space Hotline at 858-668-4611.

The program includes reduction of flammable vegetation on city-owned land near structures, individualized home assessments, public education, annual maintenance, and inspections. The Fire Department's Fire Prevention Officer conducts the assessments. If you have not been contacted by the city, you may request a home ignition zone assessment by contacting Fire Prevention; assessments are conducted based on fire risk, as determined by staff. A no-fee application must be completed prior to the removal of any protected habitat or vegetation, and city staff complete it during the home assessment.

A structure is a home and any attached garage or other attached building, a building designed primarily for human use, or a building designed to house farm animals; it does not include sheds, storage buildings, or detached garages. Homeowners may do the work themselves and use the city's unlimited green curbside refuse service, or hire a contractor (upon request, the city will provide contractor information for estimates). Wood chips and organic compost are not recommended within five feet of the structure; beyond five feet, wood chips and organic mulch are allowed at a depth of no more than six inches. Work should not be conducted during nesting season. Property owners may appeal using the process established in the Poway Municipal Code.
