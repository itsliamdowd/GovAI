## Construction and Demolition Waste Recycling Program

Under LHMC Chapter 5-48, all new construction, demolition, and renovation projects must recycle or reuse at least sixty-five (65) percent of construction and demolition (C&D) waste. All projects that require a building permit or an encroachment permit and generate C&D waste are "Covered Projects" and must divert at least 65% of C&D waste from landfills.

Projects not required to divert C&D debris, submit a C&D Waste Plan, or pay a security deposit:
- Work for which only a plumbing, electrical, or mechanical permit is required.
- Seismic tie-down projects.
- Installation of pre-fabricated patio enclosures and covers where no foundation or other structural building modifications are required.
- Installation of pre-fabricated accessories such as signs or antennas where no structural building modifications are required.
- Other work that the C&D Compliance Official determines will not produce C&D waste.
- City-sponsored projects.

## C&D Recycling — Security Deposit and Steps

For each building or encroachment permit application for a Covered Project, the security deposit is $0.30 per square foot for residential projects (minimum $100, maximum $3,750). For commercial projects it is $0.50 per square foot (minimum $250, maximum $7,143).

Step 1: Submit a C&D Waste Plan, the Security Deposit, and an Administration Fee ($46.04) with your permit application.

Step 2: During construction or demolition, keep original receipts, weight tags, or invoices for all loads delivered to a recycling facility. They must state that the waste was considered "C&D" or recycled at 65% and originated from Laguna Hills. If documentation designates C&D waste as trash, municipal solid waste, or green waste, the deposit will not be refunded.

Step 3: Submit a C&D Waste Compliance Report and Request for Deposit Refund, with all Step 2 documentation attached, within 60 days of the permit's final inspection date. Otherwise the deposit is forfeited.

Step 4: The City refunds the deposit if you met the State diversion requirement and abided by your Waste Plan. Deposits are also forfeited upon sixty (60) days after a permit expiration date.

Questions: call (949) 707-2676 or (949) 707-2665, or email building@lagunahillsca.gov.

Source: https://www.lagunahillsca.gov/578/Construction-and-Demolition-Waste-Recycl

## Senior Dial-A-Taxi Program

The Senior Dial-A-Taxi Program is funded by OCTA (SMP and M2 Funds). It provides door-to-door taxi service to eligible Laguna Hills residents. The current cab operator is California Yellow Cab. Service within Laguna Hills, Laguna Woods and Mission Viejo city limits (the "Service Area") is for shopping, recreational/social activities, personal business, and standard medical appointments. The Program Guidelines and User Policies (PDF) list satellite destinations outside the Service Area.

Service is for Laguna Hills residents who are 60 years of age or older. It also covers seniors 60 or older who qualify for the OCTA ACCESS program. Participants must have an approved application on file and be issued a Senior Dial-A-Taxi photo identification card. For more information, contact the Program Administrator at 949-707-2681.

To register, complete an Application and Waiver and return it to the Laguna Hills Community Center and Sports Complex, 25555 Alicia Parkway, Laguna Hills, CA 92653. Member ID cards are made by appointment only. To file a complaint, print and fill out the Complaint Form (PDF).

## Water Quality and Storm Drains

Irrigation runoff is prohibited. Report irrigation runoff issues on the Request Tracker system via the "Report an Issue" tab on the City website homepage. The City's storm drain system is separate from the sewer system. Water collected from the streets in the storm drain system goes untreated into creeks and eventually the ocean. Motor oil, paint, pesticides, fertilizers, and cleaners can be blown or washed into the storm drains.

## Overwatering is Out — Runoff Prohibited

Water that runs off your property from excess irrigation, car washing, or hosing down sidewalks is prohibited. It is both a water use efficiency issue and a stormwater issue. Dry-weather runoff flows down streets and sidewalks, picks up pollutants (e.g. litter, oil, dog poop), and reaches catch basins and eventually the ocean. More tips are at OverwateringIsOut.org, an H2OC initiative of the Orange County Stormwater Program.

Source: https://www.lagunahillsca.gov/470/Overwatering-is-Out

## OC Stormwater Program (H2OC)

H2OC is a cooperative stormwater program that includes the County of Orange, Orange County Flood Control District (OCFCD), and all 34 cities in Orange County. It provides resources to residents and businesses to prevent water pollution and works with watershed communities to prevent polluted runoff from entering waterways. More information is at https://h2oc.org/.
