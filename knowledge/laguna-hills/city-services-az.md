## Construction and Demolition Waste Recycling Program

Under LHMC Chapter 5-48, all new construction, demolition, and renovation projects must recycle or reuse at least sixty-five (65) percent of construction and demolition (C&D) waste. All projects that require a building permit or an encroachment permit and generate C&D waste are "Covered Projects."

Projects not required to divert C&D debris, submit a C&D Waste Plan, or pay a security deposit:
- Work for which only a plumbing, electrical, or mechanical permit is required.
- Seismic tie-down projects.
- Installation of pre-fabricated patio enclosures and covers where no foundation or other structural building modifications are required.
- Installation of pre-fabricated accessories such as signs or antennas where no structural building modifications are required.
- Other work that the C&D Compliance Official determines will not produce C&D waste.
- City-sponsored projects.

Security deposits apply to each building or encroachment permit application for a Covered Project:
- Residential Projects: $0.30 per square foot, minimum $100, maximum $3,750.
- Commercial Projects: $0.50 per square foot, minimum $250, maximum $7,143.

## Construction and Demolition Waste Recycling — Steps and Deadlines

Step 1: Submit a C&D Waste Plan, the Security Deposit, and an Administration Fee ($46.04) with the building or encroachment permit application. The plan estimates the C&D debris generated, the quantities reused, recycled, and/or disposed of, and the certified recycling or disposal facility to be used.

Step 2: During the work, keep original receipts, weight tags, or invoices for all loads delivered to a recycling facility. They must state that the waste was considered "C&D" or recycled at 65%, and that it originated from Laguna Hills. If documentation designates the waste as trash, municipal solid waste, or green waste, the deposit will not be refunded.

Step 3: Submit a C&D Waste Compliance Report and Request for Deposit Refund with all documentation attached. It must be turned in within 60 days of the permit's final inspection date, otherwise the deposit is forfeited.

Step 4: The City refunds the deposit if the State diversion requirement was met and the Waste Plan was followed. The deposit is forfeited if the City does not receive the completed report within sixty (60) days after the Building Inspector's final inspection sign-off. It is also forfeited sixty (60) days after a permit expiration date.

Questions: (949) 707-2676 or (949) 707-2665 or building@lagunahillsca.gov.

## Recycling for Businesses — Organics Requirements (SB 1383 & AB 1826)

SB 1383 and AB 1826 require all businesses that generate organic waste (food waste, landscaping waste) to subscribe to and/or participate in a program to recycle that waste. Businesses, including property managers, comply in two ways:

1. Recycle organic material by subscribing to regular organics collection service from CR&R, or by self-hauling to a permitted facility such as a composting or anaerobic digestion facility. A record must be kept of the amount delivered to each facility.
2. Provide organics and recycling service to employees, contractors, tenants, and customers, with an adequate number, size, and location of containers. Container colors are gray/black for trash, blue for recycling, and green for organics, with labels showing materials accepted and prohibited. Education on the legal requirements and on sorting the three waste streams must be provided annually, and for new tenants before or within 14 days of move-in. Containers must be periodically inspected, and employees informed if contamination is found.

Large food generating businesses are required to donate food. Anyone can donate edible food.

## Recycling for Businesses — AB 827, AB 341, and CR&R Programs

AB 827, effective July 1, 2020, requires fast-casual food service establishments to provide customers with disposal containers for food scraps, recyclables, and trash generated from products purchased and consumed onsite.

## Waste Reduction Tips

Residential tips: store leftovers in reusable containers; compost organic waste; use reusable cotton bags for shopping; use cloth rags for cleaning; use reusable utensils, plates, cups and napkins; buy products with minimal packaging; reuse packing containers and bags; use rechargeable batteries; donate unwanted items to a thrift store.

Business tips: use reusable lunch bags; reuse paper for notes; use washable cups, plates and utensils; institute double-sided copying; use voice mail and email instead of paper; use rechargeable batteries; examine production and purchasing procedures; buy products with minimal packaging; buy in bulk or economy-size packages; reuse packing containers and bags; donate leftover food to a food bank or homeless shelter; donate unwanted items to a thrift store.

## Senior Dial-A-Taxi Program

The Senior Dial-A-Taxi Program is funded by OCTA (SMP and M2 Funds) and provides door-to-door taxi service to eligible Laguna Hills residents. The current cab operator is California Yellow Cab. Service within Laguna Hills, Laguna Woods and Mission Viejo city limits (the "Service Area") is for shopping, recreational/social activities, personal business, and standard medical appointments. The Program Guidelines and User Policies (PDF) cover satellite destinations outside the Service Area.

Eligibility: Laguna Hills residents only who are 60 years of age or older. Seniors 60 or older who qualify for the OCTA ACCESS program are also served. Qualified residents must have an approved application on file and be issued a Senior Dial-A-Taxi photo identification card before participating. Program Administrator: 949-707-2681.

To register, complete an Application and Waiver and return it to the Laguna Hills Community Center and Sports Complex, 25555 Alicia Parkway, Laguna Hills, CA 92653. Member ID cards are made by appointment only. Complaints are filed using the Complaint Form (PDF).

## Water Wall at the Community Center

At the entryway of the Laguna Hills Community Center is a water wall representing the ancient Ice Age stream that flowed westward where Alicia Parkway stands today. A petroglyph above the water garden pool is etched with animals that lived in the area about 50,000 years ago: Colombian mammoth, dire wolf, giant ground sloth, long-extinct horses and camels, and saber toothed cats. The artist is John Iwerks, who also painted the three murals inside the Community Center.
