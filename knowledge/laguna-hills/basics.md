## City Council Elections — Schedule and Terms

The City of Laguna Hills consolidates its General Municipal Election with the County of Orange in conjunction with its Statewide General Election on the first Tuesday of November of even-numbered years. The City Clerk oversees the administration of municipal elections and election services for the City. All election services are provided according to the California Elections Code.

City Council Members are elected by-district for a four year term. Terms are staggered, so either 2 or 3 seats are open for election every two years. City Council Members receive an annual stipend, CalPERS contribution, and health benefits.

The City's next election is November 3, 2026, to elect one Council Member for each Council District: 1, 2 & 4. Three City Council seats (Districts 1, 2 & 4) are up for full four-year terms, with one District 2 incumbent eligible to qualify for the ballot.

## Becoming a City Council Candidate

To qualify as a candidate for member of the City Council, you must be at least 18 years old; a U.S. Citizen; a resident of the City of Laguna Hills; registered to vote in the City of Laguna Hills; and not on parole or serving time for a felony conviction.

The City Clerk will issue Nomination Papers and Candidate Packets during the nomination period for the 2026 election, which is Monday, July 13, 2026, through 5:00 p.m. on Friday, August 7, 2026. It is best to make an appointment with the City Clerk as the process takes approximately 30-45 minutes. Call (949) 707-2635 to make an appointment.

There is no campaign filing fee for a candidate of municipal office. You must first be issued a Nomination Petition specifying the office you are seeking, and obtain the signatures of at least 20 and not more than 30 registered voters within the City of Laguna Hills. Other required forms include the Conflict of Interest Disclosure Statement and Campaign Finance Disclosure Statements.

Prior to soliciting or receiving any contributions related to candidacy, you must file a Statement of Intention (FPPC Form 501) with the City Clerk's Office. Additional filing requirements depend on the amount of contributions anticipated. Becoming a candidate means information about you becomes a matter of public record, and documents submitted to the City during the election must by law be available to the public for inspection and/or copying.

## Campaign Contribution Limits

No person shall make, and no City Council candidate or candidate controlled committee shall solicit or accept from any person, any contribution of more than $1,000.00 in any single election. This does not apply to a candidate's contribution of their personal funds, community property funds or domestic partnership funds to their own candidate controlled committee. It does apply to separate property contributions from the candidate's spouse or domestic partner.

## City Manager — Role and Contact

The City Manager is the administrative head of the government of the City under the direction and control of the City Council. The City Manager implements City Council policies, practices, and services and provides overall management of the City. ## Community Center — Facilities and Programs

The Laguna Hills Community Center offers meeting rooms for community group meetings and private gatherings; indoor and outdoor recreational spaces, including a gymnasium, fitness center, and athletic courts; a sports complex that hosts sports leagues and events; and a dedicated arts and culture area with galleries and performance spaces. The lobby has an information desk, comfortable seating, and fossil displays.

Programs and activities throughout the year include fitness classes, art workshops, educational seminars, youth programs, and community events such as fairs, festivals, and holiday celebrations.

## Civic Center Public Art — City Council Chambers Murals

The 3 acrylic-on-canvas murals in the City Council chamber portray events that took place in the Saddleback Valley in 1769, during the 1790's, and in 1847. They are titled "Discovering Orange County" (the Portola expedition, which first entered what is now Orange County on July 26, 1769), "Mining Pecten Reef" (Native-American neophytes cutting limestone blocks from Pecten Reef, in what became Laguna Hills, to make lime for Mission San Juan Capistrano buildings), and "War Brings Change" (a combined force of 600 American soldiers, sailors, and Marines camped at Don Juan Avila's Rancho Niguel between December 29, 1846, and January 8, 1847).

Source: https://www.lagunahillsca.gov/161/City-Council-Chambers-Murals
