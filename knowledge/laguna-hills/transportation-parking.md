## E-Bike Classes and Rules

An "electric bicycle" is a bicycle equipped with fully operable pedals and an electric motor of less than 750 watts (CVC 312.5). A Class 1 e-bike is a low-speed pedal-assisted bicycle whose motor assists only while the rider is pedaling, and it stops assisting at 20 mph. A Class 2 e-bike is a low-speed throttle-assisted bicycle whose motor can propel the bicycle without pedaling, and it stops assisting at 20 mph. A Class 3 e-bike is a pedal-assisted bicycle whose motor assists only while the rider is pedaling, and it stops assisting at 28 mph. A Class 3 e-bike has a speedometer.

No person under 16 years of age shall operate a Class 3 e-bike (CVC 21213(a)). All individuals must wear a properly fitted and fastened helmet while operating a Class 3 e-bike (CVC 21213(b)). Electric bicycles with motors of more than 750 watts are technically motorcycles under the law and require Class M licenses and helmets. An e-bike that does not fall within the listed categories is no longer considered an electric bicycle. It may not be permitted on the roadway without proper licensing and vehicle registration. It is against California law to modify or tamper with an electric bicycle in a way that changes its speed capability, unless the rider also changes the bicycle's classification (CVC 24016(d)).

## Bicycle Rules (California Vehicle Code)

People under 18 years of age must wear a helmet regardless of the type of electric bicycle (CVC 21212). No person operating a bicycle shall leave a bicycle lane until the movement can be made with reasonable safety, and then only after giving an appropriate signal if any vehicle may be affected (CVC 21208(b)).

A bicycle operator shall not allow a passenger, and a person shall not ride as a passenger, on a highway other than upon or astride a separate seat attached to the bicycle (CVC 21204(b)). If the passenger is four years of age or younger, or weighs 40 pounds or less, the seat shall have adequate provision for retaining the passenger in place and for protecting the passenger from the moving parts of the bicycle. ## Residential Parking Permits

Residents should continue using their existing residential parking permits that expire in 2026 until further notice. The City will notify affected neighborhoods by mail once the new online permit portal becomes available and additional instructions are provided. The City is implementing a new online parking permit system. Once it is available, residents will be able to apply for and manage parking permits through the online portal. The Residential Parking Permit Portal will be available soon.

The Residential Parking Permit Program is intended to help manage parking in designated neighborhoods and residential areas where parking restrictions are in place. Information on permit eligibility, permit types, required documentation, and parking regulations will be posted as it becomes available.

For questions, contact the City of Laguna Hills Public Works Department, 24035 El Toro Road, Laguna Hills, CA 92653, Phone: (949) 707-2650.
