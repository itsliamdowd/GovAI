## Building Permits — Basics

A building permit is an official approval to proceed with a construction project. Before applying, you may need approval from the City of Brea Planning Commission or Planning Division. Homeowners planning a major renovation are advised to contact the Building & Safety Division first to find out which permits they need.

To start, complete an application and submit four (4) sets of 24" x 36" plans (minimum). The Construction Permit Application is available online or at the front counter of the Building & Safety Division on the 3rd floor of the Civic Center. Keep the inspection job card at the construction site and show it to a building inspector on request. If plans were required, you will receive an "Approved Field Copy" set of plans, which should also be kept on site.

In Brea, a permit will expire if the work does not begin within 6 months (180 days) and inspections are not requested. It also expires if the work is not completed within one year of permit issuance. The building official may grant, in writing, one or more extensions of time of not more than 180 days each.

## Construction Permit Application — C&D Waste Management and Final Inspection

Email completed building permit applications to building@cityofbrea.gov. The Construction & Demolition (C&D) Waste Management section is required for all building permit applications. Before initial permit issuance and before scheduling a final inspection, applicants must submit their project in the City of Brea's Green Halo portal at https://brea.wastetracking.com/.

Steps in Green Halo: select your hauling method and the facilities you will use from the Authorized Facilities List, then select "Submit Project for Approval". Approval is required before permit issuance. Upload weight tickets throughout the project. At the end, select "Submit For Final" for approval of the Compliance Report. This approval is required before the final building inspection and Certificate of Occupancy. Once the City gives Final Approval, schedule the final inspection with the Building & Safety Division at (714) 990-7668.

## ADU / JADU and SB 9 Two-Unit Developments — Plan Review & Permit Process

Pre-plan check: the City advises hiring a designer, licensed architect, or engineer to design the ADU, and a licensed contractor to build it. The Building & Safety Division handles all building permit intake. Submittal package items include the Waste Management Form, Water Quality Management Plan Checklist (WQMP), Encroachment Permit & Address Change Form, Best Management Practices Form, and Fire Hazard Severity Zones information. If submitting both an ADU and an SB 9 unit, complete Pages 10-12 of the ADU Questionnaire and the SB 9 Checklist & Questionnaire.

To submit, email all documents as a PDF file to building@cityofbrea.gov. Processing can take some time. Wait for an email confirming the application is complete before paying.

## JADU Covenant and Agreement

When a Junior Accessory Dwelling Unit (JADU) is legally created on or after April 16, 2021, a Covenant and Agreement is required under Brea City Code Section 20.52.050E. Review the checklist with a Planner at the Permit Counter. An unrecorded Covenant must be submitted with your plans. ADUs issued a building permit before January 1, 2025 are not subject to any owner-occupancy requirement.

The covenant restrictions include: the JADU may not be sold, transferred, or assigned separately from the primary residence, and may not be rented for a period of less than 30 consecutive days.

## Business License Tax

A business license tax is required of all businesses operating within the city limits, including temporary activities and contractors with offices outside the city. Rates vary by type of business. For general inquiries, contact Customer Support at (714) 886-6314 or brea@hdlgov.com. A service fee appears as a separate line item on the receipt. It is based on the cost charged by HdL, and the City does not retain any portion of it.

Payment of the tax does not constitute zoning, building, or fire code approval. Check with the Community Development Department to find out whether your business can be legally established at your location.

Source: https://www.ci.brea.ca.us/679/Business-License

## Business License — FAQ and Sidewalk Vendor Permits

A business license is required of all businesses operating within city limits, including home-based operations, temporary activities, and contractors with offices outside the City who are doing a project in Brea. The business license tax is an annual non-regulatory licensing fee, due and payable before commencing business. Brea's business license revenue pays for police, fire, and safety expenses as well as other general operating costs. When you apply or renew, the automated system on the Brea Business License portal, managed by HdL Companies, calculates your tax. Tax rates vary based on the type of business activity.

A sidewalk vendor permit is non-transferable and expires on December 31 of the current calendar year. To renew, you must resubmit an application. For questions, email BusinessLicenseInfo@cityofbrea.net. The City responds to inquiries within 48 hours during normal business hours.

Source: https://www.ci.brea.ca.us/FAQ.aspx?QID=61

## Certificate of Occupancy and Restaurant (FOG) Requirements

A Certificate of Occupancy is required from all businesses to occupy a space, except home-based businesses. Commercial or industrial buildings or suites cannot be occupied without one. As part of the process, confirm that the proposed use is consistent with the property zoning. Contact the Planning Division at planner@cityofbrea.net or (714) 990-7674 to verify zoning information.

New restaurants must submit a fats, oils, and greases (FOG) application, in addition to applying for a business license tax and certificate of occupancy.

Source: https://www.ci.brea.ca.us/679/Business-License

## Film Permit

Film crews are required to have a permit before filming anywhere in the City of Brea. Submit a completed application a minimum of two weeks before the proposed filming date. The processing fee is $500, plus a Business License Fee for each day of filming (fees may vary). These fees are waived for non-profit organizations - 501(c)(3). Fees may be paid by cash, cashier's check or credit card. Services such as police and fire personnel are billed as needed. Contact Brea Business License at (714) 990-7686 for fee information.

Submit the form by mail (a check must be included) or hand-deliver it to the Community Development Department, 1 Civic Center Circle, Brea, CA 92821-5732, (714) 990-7674. Two emergency contacts are required. Also submit a Location Release Form signed by the property owner, and a Certificate of Insurance naming the City of Brea as additionally insured. Required coverage is $1,000,000 for each occurrence and $2,000,000 general aggregate in liability insurance. All vendors hired by the applicant must obtain a City of Brea business license before the Film Permit can be approved.

Source: https://www.ci.brea.ca.us/documentcenter/view/9

## Temporary Use Permit (Special Events)

A Temporary Use Permit (TUP) is used for special events such as sidewalk sales, jog-a-thons, or grand openings. Submit the completed application 45 days before the event date to the Planning Division at Planner@cityofbrea.gov. There is a deposit of $850 for large events and a flat fee of $850 for small events. Other fees apply as applicable. The application must include an event description and a site plan. Vendors hired by the applicant must obtain a City of Brea business license, and a vendor list must be provided.

Commercial General Liability insurance of $1,000,000 per occurrence/$2,000,000 aggregate is required. Policies must name the City of Brea as an additional insured, state that coverage is primary and non-contributory, and waive rights of subrogation. Fire Department permit applications must be submitted a minimum of 3 weeks before the event date.

## Development Fees and Plan Submittal Deposits

The Community Development Department charges fees for staff time spent on development projects, based on an hourly rate. Because staff time cannot be known when a project is submitted, the City requests a deposit at the start of processing, based on an estimate. If the project needs more time, an invoice is processed for additional funds. If the deposit is not fully used, a refund is processed as quickly as possible. Deposit + Permit Issuance = Total.

Not all projects use the hourly rate formula. For some smaller one-time items, a flat fee is charged. The City also collects fees for items such as water and sewer connection. Questions about deposits can go to the Brea Fire Department at 714-990-7655 or the City of Brea, 1 Civic Center Circle, Brea, CA 92821, 714-990-7600.

Source: https://www.ci.brea.ca.us/138/Fees

## Planning Commission

The Planning Commission is a five-member commission. It studies proposed developments that may affect the community's growth and environment. It holds public hearings to review plans for compliance with the City's zoning regulations and general plan.
