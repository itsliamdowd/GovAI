## Short-Term Rentals: Ordinance and Permit Status

The City Council adopted the STR Regulations Ordinance on September 23, 2024. The California Coastal Commission certified it on February 5, 2026. The ordinance accommodates Existing STRs that meet the definition in Ordinance Section 30.96.020. The City confirmed there are 150 Existing STRs, and the Existing STR Registry is available as a PDF on the page.

Permits for "New STRs" cannot be issued until there is capacity under the City wide cap of 129 STRs. The permit application period for existing STRs closed on May 1, 2026. For more information, contact the Planning and Community Development Department at planning@delmar.ca.us.

## Short-Term Rentals: Waitlist for New STR Owners

New STR owners are not eligible to apply for a permit due to the citywide cap of 129 STRs. The City maintains an STR Permit Waitlist, and new STR owners can submit their information through the waitlist form on the Short-Term Rentals page. Permits will be issued on a first-to-apply, first-to-be-eligible basis. Owners of New STRs on the waitlist will be notified when STR Permit application opportunities become available.

## Short-Term Rentals: Complaints and Issues

To report an issue with a short-term rental, submit a complaint to the City's Code Enforcement Division. A public facing portal (the STR Public Facing Portal) also lets residents contact the STR designated local contact 24/7 and report concerns.

If there was a failure to respond during non-regular City Hall operating hours (5:30pm - 7:30am), call the San Diego County Sheriff's Department non-emergency line at (858) 868-3200. In an emergency, call 9-1-1.

## Short-Term Rentals: Transient Occupancy Tax (TOT)

Under the STR Ordinance's operating requirements, STR Owners must comply with all provisions of Del Mar Municipal Code Chapter 3.12 on collecting and remitting TOT. The STR Owner is responsible for correctly calculating the TOT based on all fees collected as part of the total rent charged for the STR, for all new bookings made after the City issues the STR Permit. STR Owners must remit TOT to the City through their Rentalscape account. The page links a Step-by-Step TOT Remittance Guide (PDF) and a TOT FAQ (PDF).

## Short-Term Rental Background and Zoning Rules

The City Council directed staff to develop STR regulations that allow existing STRs to continue until the property changes ownership, the STR permit is not renewed, or the permit is revoked due to non-compliance. The Planning Commission unanimously recommended approval of the regulations on August 13, 2024. The City Council unanimously voted to introduce the ordinance on September 9, 2024 and adopted it on September 23, 2024. The ordinance was certified by the Coastal Commission on February 5, 2026.

The background page describes the regulations in place until the new STR regulations are adopted and become effective. Short-term rental operations are only permitted in the Residential-Commercial (RC) zone and the Visitor Commercial (VC) zone, and where otherwise provided for by development entitlement (Wave Crest Time Shares, Hotel Specific Plan, and the 941 Camino del Mar Specific Plan). The page also says new STRs are only an allowed use in commercial zones including RC, Central Commercial (CC), North Commercial (NC), Professional Commercial (PC), VC, Hotel Specific Plan, and 941 Camino del Mar Specific Plan zones, and as provided for by development entitlement (Wave Crest Resort Time Shares and L'Auberge Vacation Villas). STRs are not permitted in residential zones.

## Fair Housing Resources

Fair housing means ensuring that people with similar income levels in the same housing market have the same range of choices available to them despite their protected class status. Discrimination in the sale, rental, and financing of dwellings and other housing-related transactions is prohibited. Per California Assembly Bill 686 (effective January 1, 2029), all jurisdictions in California have a duty to Affirmatively Further Fair Housing (AFFH). Del Mar's Housing Element certifies the City will AFFH.

## 6th Cycle Housing Element — Adoption and State Certification

The Housing Element is a required component of the City's General Plan (Community Plan) and must be updated every eight years. The City Council initially adopted the 6th Cycle Housing Element on March 25, 2021 (Resolution 2021-14). It re-adopted the element on December 13, 2021 and on April 3, 2023 (Resolution 2023-12) in response to feedback from the California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD). The 6th Cycle covers the planning period years 2021 to 2029.

On May 31, 2023, HCD determined the City's Housing Element to be in substantial compliance with State Housing Element Law. The HCD letter confirms the City currently has adequate sites in its Housing Element sites inventory. The page links the final Housing Element chapters 1-4 and Appendices A-I.

The adopted Housing Element includes North Commercial Program 1A as a program to achieve capacity for 22 lower income affordable units. The Border Avenue Rezone option was identified as the contingency plan only if the City cannot rely on North Commercial for Program 1A due to the Referendum in process.

## Affordable Housing Opportunities on the Fairgrounds and City-Owned Sites

City and District consultants studied six potential Fairgrounds sites for affordable housing using Housing Acceleration Program (HAP) Grant funds. Sites 1 through 5 were identified as feasible. Findings for those sites include:
- Sufficient land area for 61-82 units of family housing or 61-128 units of senior housing.
- Sufficient existing water, sewer, and transportation infrastructure.
- Each site is in the floodplain, so dwelling units would need to be built above the first-floor level.

Site 6 was determined to be infeasible because it did not meet the objective criteria and its conversion to housing would cause the intersection at Via de la Valle and Jimmy Durante Boulevard to fail.

Keyser Marston Associates (KMA) estimated financing gaps of -$89,000 for family housing and -$31,000 for senior housing (100% affordable). Harris estimated -$153,000 for family housing and -$72,000 for senior housing. Both studies conclude affordable housing will require local public subsidy in addition to free land contribution.

The Housing Element identifies two city-owned project locations: 10th Street (Civic Center) and 28th Street (North Beach). For the 10th Street site, the City is in process of selecting a development partner (see the 10th Street Request for Proposals).

## NC/PC Zoning Amendment for Housing (5th Cycle Programs 2E/2F)

On September 21, 2020, the City Council adopted Ordinance 971 to amend the Professional Commercial zone to allow housing up to 20 dwelling units per acre (Program 2F). On October 19, 2020, it adopted Ordinance 973 to amend the North Commercial zone to allow housing up to 20 dwelling units per acre (Program 2E). Existing setbacks, floor area ratio, lot coverage, and height limits still apply. The amended NC land use designation identifies the parcels where multiple dwelling unit residential use is allowed and where it is prohibited.

For comments or questions, contact Amanda Lee, Principal Planner, at alee@delmar.ca.us or 858-755-9313.

## 22 in 5 Affordable Housing Study

The "22 in 5" Affordable Housing Program was a City Council Priority Project to develop a plan for 22 affordable housing units in Del Mar within approximately five years (by the end of 2021). The 22 units comprise 12 affordable units (4 extremely low-income, 3 very low-income, and 5 low-income) plus an additional 10 low-income units. The total 5th Cycle RHNA assessment for Del Mar is 61 units, including 20 moderate-income and 34 above-moderate-income units. The page links the June 4, 2018 City Council Meeting and the study report (PDF).
