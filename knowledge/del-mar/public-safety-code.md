## Fire Department — Overview and Contact

The Del Mar Fire Department has nine full-time staff (three captains, three fire engineers, and three firefighters/paramedics). It has one front-line fire engine, one rescue unit and one reserve fire engine. Headquarters are on the southeast corner of the Del Mar Fairgrounds at 2200 Jimmy Durante Boulevard, Del Mar, CA 92014. Emergency: 911.

The department serves nearly 5,000 full-time residents and more than 3 million annual visitors to the fairgrounds. Its service area covers more than 2.5 square miles and includes more than 1,600 structures. It handles fire suppression and protection and emergency medical services, and responds to vehicle accidents, rescue and hazardous materials incidents. It provides mutual aid to Solana Beach, Encinitas and portions of the City of San Diego, and contracts with the City of Encinitas for management services.

Dispatch is 24-hour through the Rancho Santa Fe Regional Dispatch Agency, on an integrated 800 MHz radio system.

## Fire Prevention Division — Plan Review & Construction Inspections

The Fire Prevention Division enforces fire and life-safety codes, reviews development and construction plans, and inspects existing, new, and remodeled buildings in Del Mar and Solana Beach. Services include:
- Building and site plan review
- Fire sprinkler and standpipe plan review
- Fire alarm plan review
- Underground fire-service plan review
- Emergency vehicle access and fire-lane review
- Fire-flow and hydrant review
- Alternative materials and methods requests
- Fire sprinkler, fire alarm, and underground inspections
- Acceptance testing of fire-protection and special-hazard systems
- Knox rapid-entry system coordination
- Construction and building final inspections
- Operational, Tent, and special-event permit review

For questions about plan review, construction inspections, or operational permits, contact the Fire Prevention Division at (858) 720-2415 or fireprevention@cityofsolanabeach.ca.gov.

## Real Estate Defensible Space Inspection — How to Request

If you are selling a home in a High or Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone (FHSZ), California Civil Code §1102.19 may require a defensible space inspection before the close of escrow. The Fire Department provides documentation afterward for the real estate transaction. Request an inspection by completing the Real Estate Disclosure Inspection Request Form on the Fire Prevention page. A fee of 127$ will be invoiced, and payment must be processed before the scheduled inspection date. Requests may take 1-2 business days to process. Appointments are available Monday-Thursday during normal business hours.

The city recommends requesting an inspection at least 2–3 weeks before your anticipated escrow closing date. Expedited service cannot be guaranteed. Most inspections take approximately 20 to 45 minutes. An adult should be available if access is restricted or gates are locked, and the owner must give consent before the site visit. To cancel, contact the Fire Prevention Division at weedabatement@delmar.ca.us or (858) 977-2014 at least 24 hours before the appointment.

## Real Estate Defensible Space Inspection — Results and Scope

If a property does not pass, the inspector provides a written list of deficiencies. After corrections are completed, you may request a reinspection. A compliant inspection only documents that the property met minimum defensible space requirements on the day of inspection. It does not guarantee wildfire safety. The inspection is not a structural, pest, roofing, or general home inspection. It reflects conditions on the date performed, and changes to vegetation or site conditions may require a new inspection.

## Fire Hazard Severity Zones (FHSZ) and Maps

CAL FIRE, through the Office of the State Fire Marshal, released the 2025 Local Responsibility Area FHSZ maps for San Diego County, including Del Mar, on March 24, 2025. The City Council introduced the ordinance adopting the FHSZ maps on June 2, 2025 and adopted it on June 16, 2025. The updated maps significantly expanded areas of the City designated High and Very High, particularly neighborhoods adjacent to open space. Zones are classified as Moderate, High, or Very High. The maps indicate hazard potential, not immediate risk.

The new designations affect Del Mar's local building codes, require enhanced defensible space, and trigger additional disclosure obligations for property owners. New construction in Very High and High FHSZs must comply with Chapter 7A of the California Building Code and Government Code Sections 51175–51189.

To see which zone applies to your property, use the updated 2025 FHSZ layer on the City's Public Map Viewer and click the Fire Hazard Severity Zones tab on the left.

## Wildfire Preparedness

The City's wildfire preparedness page advises maintaining vegetation, creating defensible space, keeping roofs and gutters clear of debris, and preparing homes to withstand wildfire. It notes that embers are one of the greatest threats to homes. Wind can carry burning embers miles ahead of the main fire to roofs, vents, gutters, decks, and other combustible materials.

The page's "Get Ready to Go" steps are:
- READY: harden your home and establish a defensible safety zone.
- SET: make a wildfire action plan and prepare a "go bag."
- GO: use evacuation guides and checklists, including the CAL FIRE guide.

Source: https://www.delmar.ca.us/967/Wildfire-Preparedness-Information

## Emergency Preparedness

Emergency responders recommend being self-sufficient for at least three days after a major disaster, and the City advises being prepared to care for yourself and your family for at least the first 72 hours. The page advises holding a family meeting at least once a year to update your disaster plan, and completing the Office of Emergency Services Personal Disaster Plan. Videos cover emergency alerts (Alert San Diego and Genasys Protect), building an evacuation kit, and utility shutoffs. The page also links resources on earthquake, tsunami (including the Tsunami Evacuation Map - Del Mar) and wildfire preparedness (including the Crest Canyon Evacuation Plan).

During a large-scale emergency, City staff will open the Del Mar Emergency Operations Center (EOC) in the Del Mar Civic Center Town Hall. The EOC serves as a point of information and resource management.
