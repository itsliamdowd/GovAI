## Placentia Navigation Center (Regional Homelessness Effort)

Placentia, together with the North Specific Planning Area (SPA) cities of Brea, Buena Park, Cypress, Fullerton, La Habra, La Palma, Los Alamitos, Orange, Villa Park, Stanton, and Yorba Linda, has taken a regional approach to homelessness in response to a Federal Mandate and Martin v. Boise (2018). The cities identified two Navigation Center locations: one in Placentia with 100 beds and one in Buena Park with 149 beds.

The Placentia Navigation Center is operated by The Salvation Army. It is a referral-only facility with managed and controlled access in and out via transportation only. It provides health resources, counseling, job and social skills, and opportunities to reunite with family and friends.

## Reporting Homelessness Concerns

Placentia PD has partnered with the HOPE Center for homeless outreach services. For non-emergent homeless concerns, or to provide resources to an individual, contact the HOPE Center Dispatch Center at (657) 243-1884. It is open Monday - Friday from 7:30 a.m. to 5:15 p.m. and takes messages when closed. After-hours requests are followed up during hours of operation. For emergency situations regarding homeless individuals, call 911.

The City's mobile app, iPlacentia (App Store or GooglePlay), has a Homelessness Concerns section. It can also be used to report Abandoned Items or Homeless Encampments.

## Short-Term Rentals — Permit Requirements and Registration

A Short-Term Residential Rental is the rental of a residential dwelling unit, or a portion thereof, for 29 consecutive days or less. All STRs must:
- Obtain a Short-Term Rental Operating Permit.
- Submit a site plan and floor plan identifying bedrooms, parking, and occupancy.
- Pass Building and Fire/Life Safety inspections prior to issuance and annually thereafter.
- Maintain a valid City business license.
- Register for and remit Transient Occupancy Tax (TOT) quarterly.

Applicants must register in person at Placentia City Hall and submit a completed Short-Term Rental Registration form with payment of applicable fees. The application must include proof of General Commercial Liability Insurance, a copy of the Grant Deed (if the property was recently purchased), and a Homeowners Association (HOA) approval letter, if applicable. If the application is approved, the City notifies the property owner or managing agency with instructions to apply for a business license. Once the business license is obtained, the STR is considered in compliance with City requirements.

Source: https://www.placentia.org/881/Short-Term-Rentals

## Short-Term Rentals — Operational Standards, Spacing and Permit Cap

Operational standards:
- Maximum occupancy is two persons per bedroom, with children under six (6) years of age exempt, not to exceed ten (10) occupants total.
- Minimum rental period is two (2) consecutive nights.
- The responsible renter must be at least twenty-one (21) years of age and remain on-site during the stay.
- A Local Contact Person must be available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, and respond within one (1) hour to complaints or issues.
- A Good Neighbor Brochure must be provided to all occupants and posted within the unit.

Location and spacing:
- STRs are subject to a 300-foot separation requirement from other permitted STRs.
- For single-family properties, distance is measured from property line to property line. For condominiums or townhomes, it is measured between the exterior boundaries of each unit.
- STRs are not permitted in apartment complexes or in developments where units are not individually owned.
- ADUs may not be used as separate short-term rentals. STRs are not permitted on any property with either an ADU or JADU.

Permit cap: Total STR operating permits are limited to approximately 0.5% of the City's total housing units, as determined annually. No new permits are issued once the cap is reached, except for eligible renewals. Partial-unit rentals do not count toward the cap.

## 2021-2029 Housing Element

The City's 6th Cycle Housing Element (2021-2029) has been certified by the California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD). The City Council voted unanimously to approve it on March 15, 2022 (adopted 3/15/2022). The Housing Element identifies the City's housing needs and conditions and sets goals, objectives, and policies for housing. State law requires it to be updated every eight years.

Comments on the updated document can be directed to Joseph Lambert, Development Services Director, at jlambert@placentia.org or (714) 993-8124. A seven (7) day public review period for the updated 6th cycle document ran from Friday, February 16, 2024 to Thursday, February 22, 2024.

## Annual Housing Reports

The City posts Annual Housing Reports for 2010 through 2025. They detail the City's progress in building new homes and implementing new housing initiatives. ## Prohousing Designation Program (PDP)

The Prohousing Designation Program is a State of California initiative that incentivizes local governments to adopt policies that promote housing development. Designation allows more funding opportunities and infrastructure grants for developers through programs such as Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities (AHSC), Transformative Climate Communities (TCC), and Infill Infrastructure Grant (IIG).

The City asks residents to complete a survey (English or Spanish). For questions or more information, contact Lesley Whittaker, Associate Planner, at (714)993-8149 or LWhittaker@placentia.org. To achieve designation, a jurisdiction must show at least one policy enacted or proposed in each of four categories and earn a minimum score of 30 points. The City's application was revised May 2025.

Source: https://www.placentia.org/1108/Prohousing-Designation-Program-PDP

## City Council Housing, Community and Economic Development Committee

The committee consists of two (2) regular members of the City Council and meets as needed. It serves in an advisory capacity to the City Council on significant developments related to land use regulations, urban design and building standards. Members review issues related to general plans and zoning, housing, land use regulations, development, building standards and economic development policy. The staff liaison is the City Administrator, Director of Development Services and/or designated staff member(s).
