## City Clerk's Office — Services and Contact

The City Clerk's Department makes sure City elections and City Council meetings are open to the public. It prepares and distributes City Council meeting agendas and records and maintains the minutes. It attests, publishes, posts and indexes all resolutions and ordinances, and maintains and publishes the Rancho Santa Margarita Municipal Code. It issues public notices and public hearing notices.

The office also administers and files oaths and affirmations, receives and processes claims, summons and subpoenas, notarizes and certifies documents, and receives and opens all bids. The City Clerk serves as the City's Elections Officer and as the Filing Officer for Campaign Statements and Statements of Economic Interest required by the Political Reform Act. The office manages the City's records and serves as the City's archivist and historian.

Contact: Amy Diaz, City Clerk, phone 949-635-1806. Office phone: 949-635-1800, ext. 6201. Address: 22112 El Paseo, Rancho Santa Margarita, CA 92688. Hours (except holidays): Monday - Friday, 8 a.m. - 5 p.m.

## City Manager's Office

The City Manager's Department makes policy recommendations to the City Council and implements them as adopted. The City Manager serves as Chief Executive Officer of the City and is responsible for its daily operations. Additional duties include:
- overseeing City Hall operations and services to the community
- acting as City Treasurer and Personnel Officer
- pursuing economic development through business attraction and retention
- overseeing emergency management operations
- managing environmental issues
- advocating the City's interests on regional, State and Federal issues

## City Council Meetings — Public Comment and eComments

Beginning July 1, 2026, pursuant to California Government Code section 54953.4 (Senate Bill 707), City Council meetings will include a two-way telephonic service to allow remote attendance and public comment. If a disruption prevents members of the public from attending or observing remotely, the City Council will recess the open session for at least one hour and make a good-faith effort to restore service.

To address the City Council by phone, first submit a "Request to Speak Call-In Form" electronically. Then dial (949) 229-7104 (landline or wireless). The City Clerk will identify you by the last 4 digits of your phone number when it is your turn to speak. Providing your address and phone number is voluntary under the Brown Act (Section 54953.3). Comments on a scheduled agenda item are heard when that item is on the Agenda.

Per Council Policy 4.4, the Public Comments Period for items not on the agenda but within the City Council's subject matter jurisdiction does not exceed thirty minutes. It is divided into two equal segments of no more than 15 minutes each. Each speaker has three minutes. A speaker who addresses an issue in the first segment may not address the same issue in the second segment. The City Council is prohibited by law from discussing or taking action on any matter not on the Agenda.

eComments for City Council or Planning Commission consideration can be submitted through an eComment form. They will not be read verbatim at the meetings. Submit by 4:30 p.m. the day of the meeting, otherwise the comment will be provided at the following meeting.

## City Council Compensation

Monthly compensation provided by the City:
- Salary: $463.50 (established in 2005)

- Transportation Corridor Agencies: $120 per meeting and mileage at the IRS Rate. 1 council member and 1 alternate member attend.

## Fire Preparedness Community Meetings

Fire Season is a year-round reality in Rancho Santa Margarita. The Orange County Fire Authority (OCFA) and the Orange County Sheriff's Department (OCSD) present at community meetings on the basics of wildfires, evacuation methods and tools, and seasonal assessments. The "5th Annual Fire Safety and Preparedness Community Meeting" presentation can be viewed on the City's YouTube Channel.

## About Rancho Santa Margarita

Rancho Santa Margarita describes itself as "a small city with the soul of a village." The City maintains a safe environment through community programs, law enforcement, and fire protection services. Amenities include Lake Santa Margarita, parks, pools, community centers, and pedestrian/bicycle trails.

Most facilities in the City are owned and maintained by private organizations. The City collaborates with public service agencies, Homeowner Associations, and community foundations to improve and maintain public services and facilities.
