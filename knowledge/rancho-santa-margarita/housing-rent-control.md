## Affordable Housing

Housing is considered affordable when a household pays no more than 30% of its gross monthly income for housing including utilities. The City works with the Orange County Housing and Community Services Department to provide affordable housing. Resources linked on the City's page are the County of Orange Affordable Rental Housing List and the United States Department of Housing and Urban Development California Rental Help and Housing Information.

## Rental Assistance — Housing Assistance Payments Program (Section 8)

The City cooperates with the Orange County Housing Authority (OCHA), which administers the Section 8 Voucher Program. The Housing Assistance Payments Program assists low income, elderly, and disabled households by paying the difference between 30% of an eligible household's income and the actual rent.

The county also administers these programs for extremely-low-income persons:
- Family Unification Program: Section 8 assistance to families whose children are at risk of being placed in out-of-home care or delayed in returning from care because of inadequate housing.
- Aftercare Program for Disabled: Section 8 assistance targeted to disabled applicants.
- Shelter Plus Care Program: rent subsidies to homeless persons with disabilities.
- Hope for Elderly Independence: Section 8 assistance to frail elderly persons.

To learn about participating or check eligibility, contact OCHA at 714-480-2700 or visit the OCHA website.

## Fair Housing & Equal Opportunity

The City works in partnership with the Fair Housing Council of Orange County and the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) to ensure unimpeded access to fair housing opportunities. The Fair Housing Council is a private nonprofit organization formed in 1965 and incorporated in 1968. It contracts to serve all of Orange County's local governments with fair housing services for their residents, and it handles more than 250 cases of alleged housing discrimination in the county each year.

The page also links to the Orange County 2025-2029 Regional Assessment of Fair Housing (3-27-25).

## 2021-2029 Housing Element

The City's 2021-2029 Housing Element was adopted in August 2022. The page lists an HCD Certification Confirmation Letter, HCD Letter July 26 2022, and HCD Review Letters dated April 11, 2022 and December 21, 2021. For next steps, see the Housing Element Implementation and Rezoning Project page.

Each California city must update its Housing Element every eight years and plan for its share of housing need, as allocated by the State Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) and the Southern California Association of Governments (SCAG). The draft Regional Housing Needs Allocation (RHNA) for the City is 680 housing units:
- Very Low Income (<50% AMI): 209 units, 30.6%
- Low Income (50-80% AMI): 120 units, 17.6%
- Moderate Income (80-120% AMI): 125 units, 18.3%
- Above Moderate Income (>120% AMI): 226 units, 33.4%

Survey No. 1 (January 29 to March 3, 2021) received 530 responses. Survey No. 2 (February 21 to March 29, 2021) received 236 responses. The City also held five focus groups. The prior Housing Element was updated in 2013 and covers 2013-2021.

## Workforce Housing Overlay

The Workforce Housing Overlay (WHO) would allow housing options for employees to live near their jobs in the Business Park, supporting existing businesses. Its primary goal is to protect the existing nature and character of the Business Park while allowing new housing opportunities close to key employment centers, enhancing workforce retention and reducing commute times. Approximately 38 acres are proposed to be designated with the overlay. The project goals include creating housing that supports the needs of employees and employers in the Business Park, and implementing objective development standards.
