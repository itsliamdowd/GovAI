## Code Enforcement — Filing a Complaint

The Code Enforcement/National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (NPDES) Officer responds to citizens' complaints about observed and alleged violations of the Municipal Code.

- By phone: call 949-635-1800, ext. 6703. You may leave a voicemail 24 hours a day.
- By online report: file a complaint through the City's Community Connection Citizen Request Tracker (CRT).
- By mail: send the complaint to the Code Enforcement Division at City Hall, 22112 El Paseo, Rancho Santa Margarita, CA 92688.

The names of all complainants are maintained in confidence by the City and will not be released except as required by law.

## Code Enforcement — How Complaints Are Investigated and Prioritized

Once a complaint is received, a Code Enforcement Officer will conduct an inspection to determine whether a violation is occurring. If a violation is found, the officer will prioritize it as follows:

- High priority: violations that pose an immediate or readily apparent threat to health, safety or the environment, such as prohibited discharge into the stormwater system.
- Medium priority: violations that do not constitute an immediate or readily apparent threat to health, safety or the environment, but have the potential to do so if left uncorrected.
- Low priority: all other violations, such as signs, shopping carts, basketball hoops, trash cans, etc.

The Code Enforcement Officer will continue the Code Enforcement process until compliance with the Municipal Code is achieved.

Source: https://www.cityofrsm.org/210/Code-Enforcement

## Code Enforcement — Appealing an Administrative Citation

If you have received an Administrative Citation related to a Code Enforcement or Zoning Violation, you have a right to appeal. You may contest that there was a violation(s) or that you are the responsible party. To appeal, complete a Code Enforcement Appeals Form and submit it along with a deposit in the amount of the fine. Appeal forms must be received by the Development Services Director within 15 calendar days of the date of issuance of the Administrative Citation.

## 2025 Fire Hazard Severity Zone Map

On March 24, 2025, CalFIRE and the Office of the State Fire Marshall (OSFM) released the 2025 Local Responsibility Area (LRA) Fire Hazard Severity Zone (FHSZ) Maps for Southern California. The draft maps designate portions of Rancho Santa Margarita within Very High, High, and Moderate Fire Hazard Severity Zones. The map is available for public review and comment through a comment form on the City page, and an Interactive Map Viewer lets residents search their address. The City is required to adopt the map by ordinance within 120 days, or by July 22, 2025.

Properties in a designated FHSZ are subject to specific planning and development standards and disclosure requirements, including building standards for new construction in the Wildland-Urban Interface, defensible space requirements, natural hazard real estate disclosure, property development standards such as road widths, water supply and signage, and consideration in city and county general plans. A local agency may only propose to increase the level of fire severity identified by CalFire; fire hazard severity cannot be decreased. You can look up a property's designation at osfm.fire.ca.gov/fhsz. For insurance questions, call the Department of Insurance hotline at 1-800-927-4357.

Source: https://www.cityofrsm.org/712/2025-Fire-Hazard-Severity-Zone-Map

## Emergency Operations Plan

The Emergency Operations Plan (EOP) is the City's blueprint for preparing for, responding to, and recovering from major emergencies and disasters such as wildfires, earthquakes, floods, severe weather, and hazardous materials incidents. The City updated the plan for 2025/26 following its review of its response to the 2024 Airport Fire. The City retained Jacob Green and Associates for the assessment and update. The plan is required by both the California Government Code and the Rancho Santa Margarita Municipal Code.
